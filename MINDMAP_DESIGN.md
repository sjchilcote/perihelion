# The Mind Map — design document

Companion data file: `game/perihelion/data/mindmap.json` (33 nodes, machine-readable,
validated — see §6). This document is the rationale; the JSON is the source of truth
for ids, costs, prereqs, and unlock keys. Neither file is wired into the sim; that is
the parallel engine agent's job. Node player-facing text (`blurb`) is intentionally
left as `"TODO-HERMES"` in every entry — Commando log-voice prose must be generated by
Hermes from the `brief` field, never authored here (see prose-spec-portable.md §I.b,
the proper-noun taboo, and NARRATIVE_DESIGN_DOC.md's register rules).

## 1. What this is, per the director's brief

Players buy respawn stations (forward seeds, §9.3) to place; a station is a home base
where recovered **components** buy tactical upgrades and recovered **components +
data** expand the **mind map** — a graph of concepts the Commando is or isn't letting
himself think. Expanding a node unlocks new upgrades. Some nodes are pure flavor: no
mechanical benefit, characterization only. Every mechanical node is dual-purpose — a
tactical unlock *and* a philosophical position, because "spend components" and "decide
what the Commando's soul has room for" are the same action. **Scarcity is the point**:
there is never enough to expand everything, so the player's build *is* the read on
faith vs. apostasy (§9.1/§8.1 in NARRATIVE_DESIGN_DOC.md — faith is computed from
investment pattern, not a visible meter).

## 2. Topology

### 2.1 Four regions

| Region | Weighting (per §9.2) | Spine theme | Capstone(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vocation** | faith-weighted (deltas +1 to +3) | just-war doctrine → resurrection theology → the Testators as kin | two: **blessed rounds** (dmg vs. Adversary) and **the rite of return** (protected revival) |
| **History** | neutral (mostly 0, one −1 bridge) | the long peace → the retrieval → the civil wars | **a record-keeper's ledger** — deeper Ratio dialogue gates |
| **Comprehension** | neutral → apostasy (0 down to −2) | connection-space → Testator engineering / Adversary biology | **connection-space tools** — geometry exploits |
| **The Void** | apostasy-weighted (−1 down to −4) | the arithmetic of futility → discarding constraint kernels / organ harvesting | **the hollow litany** — monstrous power + log-voice drift |

Each region has one free-standing **entry node** (tier 0, cheap, no prereqs except
Void's — see bridges below), 2–4 **tier-1/2** nodes branching off it, and a **capstone**
(tier 3) gated behind converging tier-2 prereqs. This gives every region an internal
spine long enough to feel like a commitment (a capstone costs more than the entry +
two branch nodes combined) without forcing a single linear order — the branch nodes
within a tier are siblings, so the player picks which one the Commando has room for.

### 2.2 Cross-region bridges

Two deliberate edges cross region boundaries, both load-bearing for theme, not just
graph decoration:

- **`comprehension-miriad-instruments`** requires *both* `history-miriad-doctrine`
  (History) *and* `comprehension-testator-engineering` (Comprehension). Mechanically
  it's a second-tier deep-scan upgrade; thematically it's the moment secular
  historiography (studying MIRIAD's protocols) and secular engineering (reverse-
  engineering Testator hardware) fuse into one instrumentalist worldview — the
  strongest single push toward Ratio's frame in the whole graph (faith −2, the
  steepest single-node drop outside the Void).
- **`void-arithmetic-of-futility`** (the Void's entry node) requires
  `comprehension-adversary-biology-survey`. The Void is not free-standing: you cannot
  get to "the enemy's organs are a resource" without having first walked through
  "the enemy's biology is a specimen to study." This dramatizes §5.0's core conflict
  directly in the tech tree — treating the Adversary as specimen is the on-ramp to
  treating your own frame as one. It also means a player who wants Void power *must*
  first bank at least one Comprehension node, so a pure-Vocation, zero-Comprehension
  playthrough structurally cannot reach the Void capstone — apostasy has a price of
  entry, not just a destination.

No bridge points *into* Vocation from elsewhere, and none points out of Void — by
design. Vocation is reachable from a cold start (it has to be, or faith becomes
unreachable for a player who dives straight into Comprehension), and the Void is a
terminus, not a hub; nothing worth having lives downstream of it. History bridges out
(into Comprehension) but nothing bridges into History — it's the neutral reservoir
both other tracks draw from, never a destination for drift.

### 2.3 Tiers and gating logic

- **Tier 0 (entry):** 1 per region, cheap (3–6 components, 0 data), no prereqs (Void's
  entry is the exception — see above). Two of the four are pure flavor at this tier
  (`vocation-liturgy-of-the-watch`, `comprehension-testator-glyphs`) so a player can
  taste the region's voice before committing components to its unlocks.
- **Tier 1:** 2–3 nodes per region branching off the entry node, 8–11 components,
  3–6 data. This is where the flavor/unlock split does its real work — every region
  has at least one tier-1 flavor node sitting next to the mechanical branches, so
  "which flavor am I willing to skip to afford the unlock" is a live tier-1 choice,
  not just an endgame one.
- **Tier 2:** 12–17 components, 6–11 data, converging prereqs (some single-parent,
  some — the bridges — dual-parent). This tier is where faith deltas start
  compounding (Comprehension tier 2 hits −2, Void tier 2 hits −3).
  **Tier 3 (capstone):** 20–26 components, 14–22 data, always dual-prereq (both of a
  region's tier-2 nodes feed it, so reaching a capstone means the *whole* spine below
  it, not a shortcut). Vocation is the only region with two parallel capstones instead
  of one, because the director's brief names both "protected revival" and "blessed
  weaponry" as vocation payoffs and they read as sibling payoffs, not sequential ones —
  a player can commit to one, both (expensive), or neither.

## 3. The economy

### 3.1 What currently exists vs. what this assumes

`game/perihelion/sim.py` today has **no components/data currency** — pickups are
`ammo_9mm`, `ammo_shell`, `ammo_cell`, `medkit`, and mission items (keys, implants,
`safe_room_core`, `portable_hole`). `DROP_TABLES` (sim.py:105) gives each of the six
enemy kinds (`husk`, `mite`, `spitter`, `brute`, `shrieker`, `warden`) a chance to drop
one of those on death. **This document assumes the sim agent adds two new pickup kinds
— `components` (from Adversary-kind kills, analogous to today's ammo drops) and `data`
(from terminals/logs, distinct from the existing ammo/medkit economy) — using the same
drop-table mechanism already in place.** That assumption is explicit and open for the
director/engine agent to revise; the cost numbers below are sized against it, not
against a shipped drop table.

### 3.2 Per-chunk income estimate

`game/perihelion/stream.py` confirms the campaign is 10 streamed chunks
(`tools/levelgen/dense/l01.py`–`l10.py`), each a self-contained security zone
(§ design: "a new security zone honors no old badges" — stream.py:243). Counting
`squad(...)` enemy-spawn calls across the level generators:

| File | squad() calls |
|---|---|
| l01–l08 | 11–21 (mostly 11–17) |
| l09 | 24 (densest) |
| l03 | 17 |

Squads average roughly 1.5–2 enemies per call. Taking a conservative campaign-average
of **~20 enemy kills per chunk** (low end of the observed range, since not every squad
call is single-enemy and some enemies flee/hide rather than engage):

- **Components income:** assume a `components` drop chance similar in order of
  magnitude to `brute`'s existing 0.55/0.25 tier but campaign-averaged across all six
  enemy kinds (`husk` 0.35 ammo already, `mite` 0.04, etc.) — call it **~25% average
  drop chance** per kill. 20 kills × 0.25 ≈ **5–6 components/chunk**.
- **Data income:** per §9.5, environmental storytelling is logs/terminals, budgeted
  and prioritized, not a combat drop. Assuming **~5 readable logs/terminals per
  chunk** (a middle estimate — denser chunks like l09 plausibly carry more, sparser
  ones fewer) each worth 1 data on first read: **~5 data/chunk**. Note the
  inter-chunk `__CONV__` tight-beam relay terminal that `stream.py:_build_spine`
  places in every transit spine is **not** counted here — it is narrative-only in the
  current code (a distinct dict from `self.pickups`), and this doc assumes it stays
  that way rather than becoming a resource tap.

**Campaign totals (10 chunks): ~55–60 components, ~50 data.** This is the resource
envelope the node costs below are sized against.

### 3.3 Does the scarcity math work?

Summing every node's cost in `mindmap.json`:

| | components | data |
|---|---|---|
| Vocation (8 nodes) | 95 | 50 |
| History (8 nodes) | 77 | 40 |
| Comprehension (9 nodes) | 102 | 58 |
| Void (8 nodes) | 98 | 56 |
| **Total (33 nodes)** | **372** | **204** |

Against a ~60-component / ~50-data campaign income, **the full tree costs roughly
6× the components budget and 4× the data budget.** A player who spends everything on
the mind map (i.e., *never* buys a second respawn station or any non-mind-map
upgrade) can afford on the order of **15–20% of the total tree** — enough for one full
spine to a single capstone (e.g., entry → doctrine-of-restraint → armor-of-the-
unsleeping → blessed-rounds costs 4+8+14+22 = 48 components, 0+2+6+14 = 22 data,
which is *most* of the campaign's components budget on its own), with a handful of
cheap flavor nodes and maybe one other region's entry/tier-1 pair left over. Reaching
two capstones in one playthrough is arithmetically implausible without either grinding
optional content or a director-side income buff — which matches the brief
("never enough resources to expand everything") rather than fighting it.

This also means **respawn stations themselves are a real opportunity cost.** The
brief specifies stations are bought with components and are prerequisite to spending
on the mind map at all (you need a base to cash in at). This document does not set a
station price — that's the engine agent's/director's call — but flags that whatever
it costs subtracts directly from the ~60-component mind-map budget above, tightening
the ratio further. **Open question for the director:** what should a station cost,
and should the first one be free/cheap (tutorial) with subsequent ones scaling up?

### 3.4 MEASURED income (supersedes the §3.2 estimates)

The shipped economy (sim.py, 2026-07-10) resolved the open questions differently
than §3.2 assumed, and the numbers were then measured, not estimated:

- **One currency.** `data` was not shipped; a node's price is the SUM of its
  components + data columns (`mindmap.price()`). No JSON changes — the loader sums.
- **Salvage is deterministic, not a drop chance:** every kill yields the enemy's
  `score` in components (husk/mite 1, spitter 2, shrieker 3, brute 4, warden 25).
- **Ceiling:** summing scores across shipped `levels/l01–l10.json`: **447
  components** for a full-clear, full-collection campaign (per-level: 15, 29, 34,
  36, 33, 36, 43, 84, 75, 62).
- **Floor:** AggroBot full-campaign soaks (tools/playtest/stream_soak.py, 5 seeds,
  all complete) banked **53–85 components** while only ever collecting salvage it
  happened to walk over. A deliberate player lands well between floor and ceiling.
- **Shipped prices** (sim.SHOP): chassis 8, seed-station kit 12, hull plating
  10+6/level, scrap driver 15, ammo 3–4. The summed-price full tree costs **576**,
  so a diligent collector who also buys hardware affords roughly one region's spine
  plus change — the §3.3 scarcity conclusion survives measurement.
- The first station is free at every chunk entry (the seed you printed from);
  §3.3's open question is answered: kits are for FORWARD placement (12c).

**Tunability note:** every number in §3.2–3.3 is a first-pass estimate built from
static level-generator inspection, not from a shipped drop table or a playtest. If the
sim agent's actual `components`/`data` drop rates differ, the intended affordability
target (roughly one capstone spine per playthrough, most nodes visibly unreachable)
should be re-tuned by adjusting node costs, not by re-deriving this section from
scratch.

## 4. The faith model

### 4.1 Computation

Per NARRATIVE_DESIGN_DOC.md §8.1, faith is "determined not by professed beliefs in
dialogue but by where the Commando spends his thought": **faith_score = the sum of
the `faith` field of every node the player has actually purchased** (not
prereq-satisfied-but-unbought nodes — investment means spent, not merely available).
This is a running signed integer with no visible meter (§9.2: "the player's clearest
feedback is the Commando himself: prayers that shorten, or deepen").

`mindmap.json`'s per-node deltas: 7 nodes positive (+1 to +3, all Vocation), 11 nodes
zero (flavor and neutral History/entry nodes), 15 nodes negative (−1 to −4, concentrated
in Comprehension/Void). The distribution is intentionally not symmetric — Vocation
tops out at +3 per node (`vocation-blessed-rounds`, `vocation-protected-revival`)
while the Void's capstone alone is −4 (`void-the-hollow-litany`), because apostasy in
this design is a steeper, faster slide than devotion is a climb — consistent with
§10's theme framing (mystery/mastery: the tech tree is "a sacrament or a temptation
depending on the soul climbing it," and temptation should read as easier to fall into
than sainthood is to earn).

### 4.2 Suggested bands (for dialogue/log-voice gating, not enforced by the JSON)

The JSON stores raw per-node deltas only; banding is a presentation-layer decision the
engine/dialogue system should own. Suggested starting bands, sized against the
achievable range in §3.3 (a single capstone spine nets roughly +7 to +9 faith in
Vocation, or −7 to −9 in Void, once entry/branch/capstone are all bought):

| Band | faith_score | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Devout | ≥ +6 | committed Vocation spine |
| Observant | +1 to +5 | some Vocation investment, or mixed |
| Drifting | 0 to −4 | neutral/History-heavy, or early Comprehension |
| Apostate | ≤ −5 | Comprehension/Void spine bought |

### 4.3 Interoperating with the dialogue bluff-flags (§8.1/§8.2b)

DIALOGUE_SCRIPT.md's flag economy already tracks two *different* axes that must not be
conflated:

- **Trust** (NARRATIVE_DESIGN_DOC §8.2) — the cumulative posture score from structured
  Ratio conversations. This is set by dialogue choices directly (bluffs, candor,
  hostility) and is **permanent** regardless of the mind map; it never routes through
  faith. Nothing here changes it.
- **Faith** (§8.1) — until the mind map ships, §8.2b states explicitly that
  "bluff- and zealotry-flagged lines serve as **provisional faith inputs, explicitly
  marked as scaffolding**." The relevant flags in DIALOGUE_SCRIPT.md's table are the
  ones keyed to claimed certainty and theological aggression: `claimed_container_
  knowledge`, `called_her_apostate`, `mission_overrides_verdict`, `claimed_doctrinal_
  finding`, `called_her_hypocrite`. Per §8.2b's own framing, these flags are a stand-in
  for "faith read from the mind map," not an independent second faith signal.

**Once `mindmap.json` is wired into the sim, `faith_score` (§4.1) becomes the sole
input to whatever paragraph-swap/log-voice logic currently reads those provisional
flags.** The flags themselves are not deleted or repurposed — they keep firing and
keep being read at the trust-axis gates listed in DIALOGUE_SCRIPT.md's table (Scene 8's
trust-side checks, the bluff-catch double-weight penalty, etc.), because those are
trust mechanics, not faith scaffolding. Only the specific reads that DIALOGUE_SCRIPT.md
itself calls out as *interim faith* proxies should be swapped for a `faith_score` band
read (§4.2). This is a targeted retirement of five flags' *secondary* job, not a
rewrite of the flag table — a scope note for whoever wires this in, since the two axes
currently share some of the same raw flags and it would be easy to accidentally rip out
their trust-side reads too.

## 6. Validation

```
uv run python -c "
import json
data = json.load(open('game/perihelion/data/mindmap.json'))
ids = {n['id'] for n in data}
assert len(ids) == len(data)
for n in data:
    for p in n['prereqs']:
        assert p in ids

```
Result: `ok 33 nodes`. All prereq ids resolve; no duplicate ids; 33 total nodes (8
Vocation, 8 History, 9 Comprehension, 8 Void); 8 pure-flavor nodes; faith deltas span
+3 to −4.

## 7. Open questions for the director

1. **Station pricing.** Not specified here — whatever a respawn station costs eats
   directly into the ~60-component mind-map budget (§3.3). Needs a number (and a
   first-station discount decision) before the economy can be truly validated.
2. **Is one-capstone-per-playthrough the right target?** §3.3's math makes two
   capstones implausible without grinding. If the intent is that a completionist
   second playthrough (or NG+) should be able to reach two, income needs to roughly
   double, or capstone costs need to drop ~40%.
3. **`components`/`data` as new pickup kinds** is this document's working assumption
   (§3.1) — if the engine agent prefers to route mind-map spending through the
   *existing* ammo/medkit pickups (e.g., "components" = a re-skin of scrap already
   implied by wrecked-frame material drops per §9.1) rather than adding two new drop
   kinds, the cost numbers in §3.2 would need re-deriving against that model instead.
4. **Vocation's two parallel capstones** (blessed rounds / rite of return) is a
   reading of the brief's "protected revival / blessed weaponry" as siblings rather
   than a single merged node. If the director intended one capstone with both effects,
   that's a one-node merge (combine costs, keep the higher faith delta) — flagged
   here rather than decided unilaterally, since it changes the region's total cost and
   the "can I get everything Vocation offers" answer.
5. **Bridge node `void-arithmetic-of-futility`** hard-gates the entire Void region
   behind one Comprehension purchase. This is a deliberate thematic choice (§2.2) but
   it also means a "pure faith, zero comprehension" playthrough cannot even *sample*
   Void flavor nodes. Confirm that's intended before the sim agent enforces prereqs
   for real — the alternative (an ungated cheap Void flavor entry, with only the
   *unlock* nodes bridge-gated) preserves the theme for the mechanical spine while
   letting a devout player still read `void-the-count-that-doesnt-stop` for color.
