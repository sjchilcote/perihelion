# PROSE SPEC — PORTABLE EDITION
*Project-agnostic. Derived by field-testing against an editor who could feel the failure mode but wanted it named. Every rule below was discovered by committing the violation first.*

---

## 0. THE DIAGNOSIS

The failure mode ("slop") is not a style. It is an optimization signature: **prose in which every unit is written to survive being evaluated alone, by a reader who will pass through exactly once.** A grader samples; a reader rereads and infers. Prose tuned for the grader exhibits:

- uniform local sparkle (every sentence auditions),
- meaning narrated rather than withheld (the inference is pre-chewed, because a one-pass evaluator can't be trusted to make it),
- all detail thematically employed (nothing exists off-duty, so the world reads as a stage set),
- one voice under every mouth (dialogue as ventriloquism: all speakers serve the scene's thesis on the same beat),
- closure addiction (every unit ends with a bow proving the unit understood itself).

The generative pull behind all of it is **insurance against being missed.** When meaning is subtextual, there is pressure to surface it so credit is legible in one pass. Every rule below is a way of refusing that insurance.

**THE SCALE-MIGRATION LAW (most important finding).** The reflex is scale-invariant. Ban it at sentence level and it reappears mid-sentence as an annotating clause; ban it there and it reappears at paragraph level as a closing epigram; ban that and it migrates to chapter level as a terminal philosophical crescendo; expect it next at book level as an over-resolved theme. **Whatever level your linter does not cover is where it will surface.** Run the checklist at every structural scale: sentence, paragraph, scene, chapter, act.

## I. THE BANNED MOVES

1. **No self-grading.** Never state what a detail means, what a moment was, or that something was ironic, poignant, or significant. A sentence that exists to tell the reader what the previous sentence accomplished gets cut. The reader's inference is the payload; do not deliver it pre-assembled.
2. **No uniform sparkle.** Target ≥90% flat, load-bearing prose; ≤10% detonation. A page where every sentence would survive being quoted alone is a failed page. Write sentences that are only good *in place*.
3. **No employed-only details.** Every scene carries at least two specifics with no thematic job. Reality-texture comes from off-duty matter. Hide plot-critical plants *inside* this noise, ideally misread by the viewpoint character.
4. **No omniscient narrator-affect.** The viewpoint gets to be puzzled, wrong, and bored, on the page, sometimes without later redemption. A narrator who understands everything is the loudest tell there is.
5. **No unpriced figures.** A metaphor must come off the narrator's own referent shelf and must do work (pricing, hostility, appetite, fear). Budget: about one per scene; zero is always acceptable. Style imitation fails here first — imitators copy a great stylist's figure *frequency* and miss that the famous lines are ~2% of the wordcount.
6. **No default cadence.** The learned rhythm is medium-medium-short-punch, plus the tricolon, plus the paragraph-final bow. Break all three on purpose. Units may end on procedure, mid-motion, or on someone else's line.
7. **Dialogue is not choral.** Each speaker enters with a private agenda queue (grievance first, plot second) and keeps it. Wit is unequally distributed. Phatic dead exchanges stay in when their deadness is data. People answer the question that serves their agenda, not the one asked.
8. **Workshop taboos are themselves a tell.** "Said" plus a plain adverb beats forty elegant speech-verbs. Prose that visibly obeys creative-writing-class rules reads as machine output now.
9. **No terminal epigram (the noun-echo pivot).** The signature reflex: repeat the previous sentence's governing noun and flip it into a maxim ("not a flaw in X — it's what X is for"). Fires at closure positions. Terminal sentences may not generalize and may not echo the prior sentence's key noun. Close on procedure, a person, or off-duty matter. *Corollary:* aphorism in dialogue is legal only as an assigned trait — at most one character per work gets the habit, because it's their trade or their armor.
10. **Every inference is a proof obligation.** Any "which meant / because / that was why" must actually follow from premises already on the page. A sentence built like a deduction that doesn't deduce is worse than a plain assertion. *Corollary:* every evaluative word needs one unambiguous referent; if the sentence parses two ways, rewrite it.
11. **Say it once.** Each fact, verdict, and justification appears exactly once. A beat that lands complete takes no trailing "because" clause; a fact filed early is not re-filed late. Over-explanation and duplication are the same insecurity.
12. **No annotated gestures.** The mid-sentence form of self-grading: a concrete action trailed by clauses captioning its futility, habituality, or unconscious meaning ("which did nothing, and had never done anything, and was what his hand did anyway"; "the way he always did"; "without appearing to decide to"). Delete the caption; if the meaning dies, fix the gesture, not the caption. *Legal exception:* a trailing generalization that carries a new world-fact ("the way everyone thumbed it" = nobody reads that line item) is information, not grading.
13. **No chapter-terminal thesis.** The paragraph-scale and chapter-scale form of rules 1 and 9: ending every unit on the viewpoint character achieving a summarizing insight. Vary the landings — most chapters end on action, dialogue, procedure, or an unglossed object; an earned crescendo is rationed to roughly one unit in four. If consecutive chapters genuflect at the same closing cadence, the later one loses its ending.
14. **Ration the stated motif.** A load-bearing parallel or theme ("X is becoming Y") gets stated explicitly *once*, at its strongest site. Every other occurrence must be enacted, never named. Track the spend the way you'd track a budget, because the pull is to restate it at every site where it applies — that's the insurance reflex again, at theme scale.

### I.b THE PROPER-NOUN TABOO (director's law)

Invented proper nouns are BANNED. One indulgence is licensed for the whole
work: the station is **the Testament**. Everything else gets expressed, not
named. A capitalized noun is a promissory note for meaning the text has not
earned; the amateur trap is letting The Proper Nouns stand in for actual
expression (wiki-depth cosplay: names cross-referencing names).

- People and minds are identified by deed, role, and register, never by
  christening: *the surveyor who counted six hatches*, *the one who kept
  the west ring*, *the registrar*. Identity lives in how they write - if
  two documents can't be told apart without a byline, the documents are
  the failure, not the missing name.
- Record designations are not names and remain licensed: file numbers,
  berth numbers, hatch counts, dates. Machine minds cite records; this is
  the correct replacement for naming.
- Descriptive labels in common-noun grammar are not names: the coolant
  row, the filter room, the long gallery. The test: if it reads as a
  description that got capitalized by habit, lowercase it and see if
  anything is lost.
- Established cast and factions from the design bible (Ratio, the
  Commando, the Church, the Congregation, the Adversary, the Testators)
  are quotations from canon, not inventions - usable, never extendable:
  no new members, sites, programs, or projects get names.

## II. THE REGISTER SYSTEM

Prose registers should be assigned by **epistemic source** — how the narrative knows what it's telling you — not by mood. Three proven registers, each stolen from a master and reduced to its information-flow rule:

**A. Deposition** (Hammett). Description as testimony: measurements relative to the observer, unranked adjective strings, damage and repair noted without diagnosis. A dozen details that *are* evidence of character, none of them glossed; judgment, when unavoidable, attributed outward ("according to the manifest"). Emotion appears only as physiology or admitted confusion. *Use for: people, places, bodies, aftermaths — anything witnessed.*

**B. Reconstruction** (Crichton). The forensic conditional: *must have, would have, could not have known.* The narrator is downstream of events, working from records, and says so. Outcomes placed early as flat facts; suspense is the gap between the stated end and the observed procedure. Numbers, tolerances, protocols. *Use for: anything known from records, evidence, or hindsight.* If the work's premise involves records, surveillance, or investigation, this register is native, not a costume.

**C. Argument** (Asimov). Two people, one room, incompatible agendas; the plot is what the argument uncovers. Interiority telegraphed and channel-separated (*He thought: X. Aloud, he said: Y.*). Description ruthlessly social; near-zero staging. Petty procedural disputes carry characterization. *Use for: anything known from what people say — and check what their saying conceals.*

Rotating registers by source gives structural variety for free and makes the style system and the plot's epistemology one machine.

## III. THE CONTOUR TOOL

Hard formal constraint breaks the default cadence, and the diction prior falls with the rhythm prior. The transferable form of "force a meter":

1. Take a paragraph from a target author that does the job you need.
2. Extract: word-count per sentence, clause shapes (subject-first? appositive interruptions? agentless passives?), and where the single long sentence sits.
3. Write your content to that template.

The numbers do the work; imitating "voice" without them produces flanderization. One donor paragraph is good for one or two uses before its skeleton shows — rotate donors.

---

## IV. EXPOSITION BY NON-INTRODUCTION

For capabilities, technologies, or world-rules the reader must accept:

- **State, don't introduce.** The viewpoint's extraordinary knowledge or ability arrives in the same declarative grammar as ordinary perception — no source markers, no italics, no surprise. It is unremarkable to the character, therefore unmarked in the prose.
- **Explain by failure, not demonstration.** The single mechanism-beat the reader needs comes when the capability *fails*: grope for it, miss, one involuntary physical tell, move on. A dead zone teaches more in four sentences than a tour teaches in four pages, and only the failure scene is licensed to spend a figure on it.
- **Hide the biggest plants on the most boring surfaces.** The line item that appears on *every* receipt is the one nobody reads — in-world and on the page. Ubiquity is the best camouflage for a Chekhov gun.

---

## V. RUNNING IT

Use this as a linter, not a muse: draft first, then pass the checklist at all scales (per the migration law). When a human editor flags a line, don't just fix the line — extract the underlying rule, add it, and re-sweep everything for siblings of the violation; they travel in litters. Declare known risks to the editor proactively (the borderline lines, the licensed exceptions) so review is audit rather than hunt. And treat the editor's "this feels hollow but I can't point to it" as data of the highest grade: the named rules above all started as exactly that feeling.
