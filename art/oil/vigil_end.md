# The Long Watch Ends

The chapel is seen down its length in the same corridor perspective as the night's dark paintings, but warm. Bronze walls with ribs fall away to an end wall lit gold by morning. High in that wall, one small arched window holds the far Sun, small and gentle now. Below it stands a bone-white altar with a crimson cloth and four candles. The morning light runs down the aisle as a long warm strip on the floor. Kneeling in that light, before the altar, is the Commando's frame in its torn crimson, still keeping its watch. But now the pews on both sides are full, and everyone faces the altar with him. Docents sit with their bronze egg heads and pale pleated ruffs, and children of the MIRIAD survivors sit in small grey coveralls. Halfway down on the right is Ratio in her little low-poly body, all long black faceted hair and a pale collar, her head turned just enough to show one flat facet of cheek. Candles on stands burn at the pew ends. Warm rims of light catch the tops of every head.

I used the corridor discipline from the dark paintings: a one-point perspective scaffold in Lua and a single light source. The value structure runs the other way from the others. The far end is the brightest place, the walls are graded warm toward it with dithered strokes, and the near pews are darkest. The congregation is simple planes, ellipses and quads in each pew's own depth: a body, a head and a ruff or hood, cut off below by the pew back in front. Lights and rims went on dry paint. One surprise: the near rows were painted on still-tacky pew paint and came out translucent. I repainted them opaque, clipped above the pew backs, once the paint had dried. Still short: the kneeling Commando is tiny at the altar and reads mostly as a crimson shape, the near pews are heavy and boxy, and the far end is a flat gold where a little more architecture would help.
