# Lamp in the Garth

We stand inside the dark bronze walk of the Cloister and look out through three uneven arches (the Testators never made two alike) into its garth. It is lit by a small pale sun on a cord, hanging in the middle bay: a light that is not the Sun, with a turn of brightness spiralling off it. Across the garth runs the far arcade, which is the same walk again, folded; this cloister has only three sides, and you are looking at the one you stand in. A few lamps are lit in its arches: hospitality, computed rather than remembered. Over the arches a frieze of fold-notation runs in worn bone. Each room-glyph holds smaller copies of itself, scaled by the golden ratio and turned through the angle of its loop, with arcs bridging glyph to glyph. Light falls through the arches onto the bronze floor plates in three long pools. In the left pillar the Commando has cut a niche for himself, with a scrap-metal cross over a small lamp, and his bedroll lies in the cleared corner below it. Nothing here hunts him.

How it was painted: a warm knifed ground, the garth and far arcade laid in first as light shapes, then the near arcade's darks clipped around the openings, then the floor pools and perspective seams. The frieze, the garden spiral, the cross and the lamps were stroked one by one. The paint surprised me twice. A glaze I meant as a halo around the hanging light dried as a hard-edged pale disc, and two blending passes softened it into the spiral of light it is now. A low-load white glaze laid for air over the far arcade dried as white streaks; two more blending passes turned it into a veil, but it left pale flecks in the far arches, which I painted over again. Warm touches laid into wet dark simply vanished, and had to wait for the dark to set. With more time I would break up the flat bands of the garth and the upper wall with real form (vault ribs, the garden in depth) and model the near pillars wet-into-wet instead of in flat lay-ins.

## Second pass

The rework went after the flat bands. Overhead, transverse bronze ribs now spring from each pillar and swing up and outward past the frieze. They follow the perspective toward the viewer, with a lit edge on the side toward the garth and a shadowed edge on the far side, and the vault between them is darkened. That puts a ceiling over the near walk. The garth is now a real place. A shade band runs along the foot of the far arcade, and a second step of shade sits under the far arcade's own vault. A pool of warm light lies on the grass beneath the hanging sun. Low plantings are set out on a golden-angle spiral, larger toward us, with lit tops. In the middle bay stands a bone-stone well with dark water and a cast shadow. The sun itself, half-buried by the shade band at one point, was restated with a hot core and a thin tilted bronze armillary ring, with stippled motes of light around it. The floor pools were repainted in three value steps away from the arches, and the perspective plate seams were restated across them, so the light lies on bronze plates rather than floating. The Commando's corner got form. The niche now has a lit bronze sill, a lamp bowl with a pointed flame, the cross's shadow on the niche back, and lit edges on the cross. The bedroll was rebuilt with a cast shadow, a dark body, a lit top and its ties.

What the paint did: every "small" fix had a cost elsewhere. The shade band painted over half the sun. The floor pools buried the bedroll, and their blended seams left stripes until the plate seams went back across them. Working on set paint (waits between passes) is what let the flame, the lit edges and the lit tops of the plants stay crisp. Still weak: the spandrel wall between ribs and arches is a flat field, and the side bays of the far arcade don't step back in value from the middle one as much as the fold deserves.
