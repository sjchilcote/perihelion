# The Crew of Outpost Miriad-7

The photograph the Commando finds on the wall, painted as a Dutch company
portrait. Seven crew in blue MIRIAD coveralls, with silver reflective bands
and amber shoulder patches, gather round a white cargo crate in Dock 4. Its
blue hexagon-and-chevron mark faces us. Behind them, in a warm dark, rises
the docking collar's hazard-striped ring. Three stand at the back:
- a dark-haired man;
- the grey-haired chief in the middle, holding the clipboard against his
  chest;
- a woman in the navy crew jacket with its yellow bands, the same jacket
  that hangs over the rail in *Dock 4, Before the Fall*.
Four sit in front:
- a red-haired woman with the mug, both hands round it;
- a bearded, shaven-headed man and a woman with her hair tied back, both
  resting their hands on the crate the way the Syndics rest theirs on the
  table;
- on the right, a moustached man with the yellow watering can from
  hydroponics on his knee.
The work-light comes from the left. It catches the left edge of every
figure and every face and leaves the right halves in deep blue shadow. The
faces are tired and a little fond, mouths lifting at the corners. A cream
caption strip runs along the bottom in handwriting that cannot be read, and
red tacks hold the corners of the print.

## How it was painted

I made it in 11 chunks on a warm brown ground at 1.5:1. A few things went
wrong on the way:
- I first laid the bodies as flat slabs in bright cobalt, and the crate,
  laid into that wet blue, picked it up. Once they had dried, I painted the
  room back into a Rembrandt dark around proper silhouettes (heads, sloped
  shoulders, arms, the seated figures' legs). Then I repainted the coveralls
  in three blues inside those shapes and repainted the crate on dry paint.
- I built the heads from lit, half-tone and shadow piles, with a narrow
  badger at the turn. Hair, ears, brows, eyes, the shadow side of each nose
  and the mouths went on after two days of drying.
- The first cheek highlights dried as white blots and had to be blended
  down.
- Last, an opaque deeper blue went over the shadow halves of the bodies,
  with bands, patches and props kept clear, and a warm hairline of
  work-light went along each figure's lit contour.

Still rough: the faces are simple and close to masks, and the hands are
small knobs. The coverall blues are stronger than any Dutch painter would
allow. The crate is the brightest thing in the picture, when the faces
should be.

## Pass at 02:58 CDT, 2 Oct 2026

All of this went on dry paint (chunks 12–17).
- **Coveralls.** I repainted them from bright cobalt down to a greyed
  workwear navy: bone black, a little ultramarine and bone brown, in three
  values. The lit side is only a step lighter, and the turns are blended.
  The holes I had left around the old bands and props were filled to the
  new outlines.
- **Reflective bands.** These are dulled to a soft grey, so they no longer
  outshine the faces.
- **Crate.** It is repainted a darker warm grey, lit from the left, and is
  now well below the faces in value. The MIRIAD mark is cut down to a
  small blue hexagon with a few stencil marks.
- **Faces.** Each face has warmer lit planes on the forehead, cheekbone and
  nose bridge, a touch of red in the cheek and ear, deeper sockets and a
  glint in each eye. Each has its own mouth: a smile, a grin with teeth, a
  tight line, a crooked half-smile, a tired straight mouth, a kind one. Some
  have tired lines at the corners of the mouth and eyes.
- **Hands.** Two pairs of hands lie on the crate, with forearms reaching over
  its back edge and fingers curled over the front with knuckle lines. Two
  hands wrap the mug, with sleeves coming in from each side.

What still falls short: the faces are still mask-like, with a hard light
and dark split down the middle. The hands on the crate are stiff and all
alike. The mug hand on the right is lost in the dark sleeve. Some of the
earlier bright cobalt still shows at the coveralls' feet. The crate is
still a large plain block in the middle of the picture.
