# The Tailor

A small warm workroom, lit by one hanging lamp. Its light lies on the back wall as a soft gold disc and on the floor as a pool, and the rest of the room is warm dark. The docent sits on a low stool in profile, hooded in a bronze robe, its bone face bent close to the work. The Commando's crimson tabard is spread across its knees and hangs down in folds. The tabard's bone cross is mended across its middle with small turns of wire. The docent's jointed bone fingers hold a bronze needle, and a thread of crimson lifts away from it in a long arc. On the bench beside it stand four spools of thread (crimson, ochre, bone, verdigris) and a little red cushion bristling with bone pins. To the right, at the edge of the light, the Commando's frame stands without its tabard, helm bowed, arms at its sides, waiting like a man at his tailor's. The lamp catches the plates of his helm, pauldron and arm on the side facing it.

**How I painted it.** I laid the light structure first, as concentric graded steps on the wall and floor, blended at the seams, with the lamp hung over the centre. The figures went in as solid masks: the docent's robe, hood, face and bent arms, the tabard, and the standing frame. The details went in last on set paint: plates on the frame in a mid tone with lit edges facing the lamp, the tabard's folds, the cross and its wire, and lamplight on the docent's crown, shoulders and sleeves. Still short: the wall glow reads as a painted disc rather than light, the docent's hands and needle are small, and the bench is a plain table.
