# The Window Seat, After

The pair to *The Crew's Window Seat*: the same nook, the same eye, the same
objects in the same places, after the fall. The warm wall has gone cold
blue-grey. The fairy lights are dead. Their dark glass bulbs hang on a wire
that sags lower than before, and the left run has come away from the frame
and fallen down the wall to the cushion, dropping a few bulbs to the floor.
Frost feathers the inside of the viewport, growing inward from the rim in
fern-like needles, thickest at the bottom. Through the frost, the Testament
is exactly as it was: bronze plates catching a warm Sun, the only warm thing
left in the picture. The child's crayon drawing has faded to pale ghosts of
its colours, and its lower corner has come loose and curls forward from the
wall. The mug is cold, with a dried brown ring inside its rim and another
stain on the cushion beside it. The paperback lies open where it was, its
page edges stiff with rime. The blanket is the same, frost along the
cushion's piping beneath it, and the headphones' cord trails off the seat
to the floor.

## How it was painted

I made it in 6 chunks on the same ground at 4:3, by replaying my own
Window Seat chunks with their piles changed. The wall's warm ochres became
ultramarine ash and bone black, the seat's greens were cooled, and the
crayon colours were thinned with white. The view outside was replayed
unchanged, so the station is identical in both pictures. The aging went on
last, on dry paint:
- the sagging and fallen wire, with dark bulbs;
- stippled frost and drawn ice needles on the glass;
- the stain rings, the rime on the book, the trailing cord;
- the curled corner.
Still rough: the curled corner reads as a grey lump rather than paper. The
mug is still a strong red, which fights the cold. The wall could fall darker
at the edges for more chill.
