# The Wanderer above the Sun, Unfolded

This is the same place and the same hour as *The Wanderer above the Sun*: the TESTAMENT's bronze dome, the horizon at the same height, the near Sun filling the frame, and the scale-plates spiralling out from the apex toward us. The thing at the apex has changed. Where the Commando stood there is now a centauroid carapace in segmented plates, standing on six jointed legs whose knees rise above its back. The legs carry ringed armour sleeves, and their top edges are lit by the disc. From the front of the carapace rises what is left of the old torso. The helmet still sits inside the white of the Sun like a halo. The crimson capelet hangs from the shoulders as a torn rag, its cross gone. The antler masts grow out of the shoulders and the shell and branch across the disc, so the halo is cut into pieces. A thin cold blue crescent of light leaks round the right side of the helmet, from the visor we can't see. Under the belly hangs a barred cage with violet light and pale eyes inside: the Adversary graft. The same scale-plates seen on the dome are repeated on the creature's rear. The low Sun throws a shadow from every foot back toward the viewer, and the carapace's own shadow is a long dark road down the hull with the plates still glinting in it. The composition, the light and the sublime of the first picture are all still there, but the figure in it has been quietly replaced.

How it was painted: I kept the original's geometry exactly: its sphere-and-camera model of the dome, the Sun at the same canvas centre and radius, and the plates set on a golden-angle spiral and projected onto the sphere. Everything else was repainted from scratch. The corona went down in bands, and two later passes of radial strokes in in-between tones fused them into glow. The plates are drawn one at a time, each a dark under-crescent with a bright lip, and plates nearer the Sun get hotter lips. The creature was laid in as opaque darks from masks. The paint pushed back several times. A dark pass over still-wet pale rim paint dried grey-lavender, so the legs and masts had to be left to dry for days and repainted with a bone-black-led dark. A rim mask run round the whole silhouette made a cartoon outline. Light strokes along the sun-facing edges only, with orange bounce light on the undersides, gave the legs their mass instead. Yellow-orange scumbled over the dark tine tips went olive. One chunk using 60-unit brushes ran past the ten-minute limit and was lost. Next time I would plan the dark silhouette as one dry-then-repaint cycle from the start. I would also break up the big shadow pool earlier and paint the rag deeper and cooler in a single sitting.

## Pass

At the director's request I made one more pass. I repainted the rag in an opaque rose madder, Mars red and bone black crimson, with a thin warmer edge where the light comes through along the shoulders. The grey shoulder pieces are back to dark metal, each with a hairline of the disc along its top. At the antler tips I covered the olive smears with the corona's own colour and refilled the tines as tapering dark ribbons drawn out to a point. Round dabs of slightly different orange are still visible around a few tips. The heavy black pool is gone. The carapace now casts a narrower deep-bronze shadow straight down the hull, and the Sun gets through between it and the leg shadows as warm lit bands. Scale-plates come back through all of it: bright lips with dark under-crescents in the lit bands, and dim lips with occasional hot glints in the shadow. One attempt went wrong. I laid the rays as low-load lead-white streaks on tacky paint. They broke into scratchy stick-slip flames, and a blender turned them into a pale crackled veil over the plates. They had to dry and be repainted as plain lit bronze, which reads more truly as light coming through the gaps anyway.

**Director's decision (00:02):** the 23:40 pass was rejected. The lit bands it
cut through the shadow read as flat orange paths, and the earlier deep shadow
had the stronger value structure. The canonical painting stays the first
version; the pass is kept as `pass_2340_rejected.png`.
