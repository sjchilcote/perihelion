# Hydroponics Torus

We stand in the crew's garden looking along the ring. The floor runs away from our feet, narrows, then rises, and comes back over our heads, so the far end of the garden hangs at the top of the picture, upside down. The whole deck is one hourglass of bed trays, crowding together where the ring turns out of sight. The power is dead. The only light is a viewport in the left wall full of Sun, its heavy MIRIAD window grid black against the glare, and the same grid falls as a skewed patch of light on the far wall. Every bed has gone to pale, etiolated stems, hundreds of them, all leaning toward the window: up from our floor, down from the floor overhead. Where they cross the sunbeam at the turn of the ring they light up pale gold, and their shadows stand on the sunlit wall like a second field of grass. Amber hazard strips run along the feet of both walls. Frost has crept over the near trays on the right, where coolant leaked from the pipes. The only living green left is a scatter of small leaves on the stems nearest the window. For the attentive eye: a yellow watering can lies dropped between two beds at our feet, and the same can lies at the same spot in the beds overhead, turned a quarter of a turn instead of the half turn the ring should give it.

How it was painted: I had fifty minutes, so the picture was built in twelve big passes. The floor's outline comes from one width function; it is narrowest at the waist and widens toward both the top and bottom edges, and the bed rows are spaced by a power curve so they crowd together where the ring turns away. The plants are hundreds of single pointed-round strokes, each leaning toward the Sun by its distance from it. A second pass of hot pale strokes, only inside the beam, made the turn of the ring glow. Mistakes: a warm glow over the blue-gray wall round the window turned olive, so I repainted it with burnt sienna dark. A frost stipple meant as a leak on the far wall read as a cloud, and I painted it out. I meant the near can to stand upright, but it came out upside down. The two cans still differ by a quarter turn, so the detail survives. With more time I would give the light on the far wall a crisper, graded edge, hang the dead grow-lamps over the beds, and thin the stems near our feet so the trays read as MIRIAD hardware.

## Second pass

- **Crisp sunlight on the far wall.** I painted the soft patch out with wall dark, then repainted the light as a hard-edged, skewed quadrilateral, brighter toward the window side. The shadow bars of the window grid are cut sharp, mapped into the skewed patch so they follow it. Sixty crisp stem shadows rise inside it from its foot, leaning away from the window.
- **The outpost's hardware.** Three dead grow-lamp fixtures hang over the beds: two over our floor, and one over the floor overhead, hanging toward the centre of the picture. Each has a dark housing, a violet lens of dead LEDs, hanger rods running toward the ring's axis, and a hot rim of window light along its edge and window-side end.
- **The trays at our feet read.** I repainted the near floor and re-laid the three nearest trays with a dark lower edge and bolts. Each now has about fourteen tall, sparse stems instead of a thicket, so the trays and aisles show between them.
