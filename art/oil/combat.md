# First Contact of the Arc

A MIRIAD prefab corridor, seen from a low eye, the whole scene tipped a few degrees. In the left foreground the Commando backpedals toward us, seen from behind: a dark Church war-body with a grey cross on its back plate and a ragged crusader's tabard, the lightning cannon braced at his hip. The dart has just left the barrel. Its cable whips from the muzzle in an S and is already bursting into plasma, violet sheath and white-hot core, spitting little arcs. From the dart's head six jagged pilot feelers reach for the Warden. The Warden is a hunched, faceted bronze Testator nearly as tall as the corridor, with a bronze nimbus behind it, a sunflower rosette of bone studs around one dark eye, a bone lancet of glyphs down its chest and a lamellar skirt. It reaches its claw toward him, and that hand stutters: a cold violet ghost contour sits displaced beside it. A husk flanks it on each side and mites scuttle across the deck. The discharge is the only light in the picture. It strikes the Warden's near side hard and leaves its far side in shadow. It edges the Commando in thin cold rims, throws his legs' shadows toward us, and lights the near wall and deck panel by panel, while the tunnel behind the Warden stays black, its ribs only just catching the light.

How it was painted: on a violet-dark toned ground I drew the corridor as an octagonal prefab section in perspective. I lit it panel by panel, each plate getting one pile from a seven-step ramp running from dark to cold white, picked by how far it sits from the discharge and which way it faces. My first attempt, concentric light bands, came out as an archery target and blending couldn't save it, so I abandoned it. I modelled the figures as angular part-polygons. On the Warden I painted the half of each part facing the dart lighter, then repainted the far side opaque dark once the paint was dry, because dark laid into wet ochre just stayed ochre. A zinc-and-medium glaze meant to be glow came out as an opaque white hairball. Four passes with a clean brush lifted most of it, and the lacy veil it left now reads as ionised air around the discharge. Rims, sparks, the face rosette, glyphs and studs went on last, over dry paint, with riggers and touches. A yellow hazard band over the cold paint turned olive (as the studio notes warn), so I repainted it as dark stripes. Twice a chunk failed after changing globals and forced a 20-minute replay of the whole log. Next time I'd define every pile before using it, block the figures in with more varied strokes so they look less like flat cut-outs, and give the plasma cable more length and scale, since it is the subject.
