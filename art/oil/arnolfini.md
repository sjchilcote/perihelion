# The Testator Couple

After Jan van Eyck's Arnolfini Portrait: two living Testators stand hand in hand in their room aboard the TESTAMENT, a million years ago. The light comes from a tall leaded window on the left, with three orange-like fruits on its sill. It falls across a room of bronze wall plates and a floor of bronze planks running back toward the far wall, where a crimson bed-canopy hangs from a bronze rail. The husband, in a dark plum great-robe edged with fur, has the long skull of living bone plates, an arc of five small hooded eyes and a closed pleated mouth. He raises one many-jointed hand palm-out and with the other takes his wife's. She is in a deep green gathered robe, her head inclined toward him. Above them hangs a bronze chandelier with one light lit. At the lower left lies a pair of bronze-soled shoes with bone straps, and between their hems stands a small plated creature on thin jointed legs, looking out at us with its own arc of eyes. On the back wall hangs a convex mirror in a ringed bronze frame with ten roundels. In it, tiny, the room is reflected: the window, the couple from behind, a lit doorway, and in that doorway, impossibly, the Commando, a million years later, crimson-tabarded with a bone cross, looking in. Above the mirror, where van Eyck wrote "Johannes de eyck fuit hic", runs a line of fold notation: a room-glyph and its smaller turned copies, ending in a dot.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 38 chunks, building in layers over dry paint. I laid in the room, then the dead colour of the figures; graded the back wall over dry paint; drew the floor in perspective; then did the heads as facets, the eyes and mouths, the mirror (frame, then the reflected room in big wet shapes, then the figures and the Commando over dry paint), the chandelier, the hands, the robe folds, and the objects. The heads at first matched the wall in value. Darkening the wall behind them a step made them the brightest things after the window, as van Eyck's faces are. My first robe folds were bright regular stripes; repainted in middle tones with only a few narrow highlights, they quieted. Several repairs failed before they worked: a patch across the robe at the horizon line mottled, then blocked out against slanted folds, until I re-laid the whole lower robe and its fold planes over dry paint. A pool of window light on the floor came out as a hard polygon and then as blotches. In the end I painted it out with the detail hand, which has been the one reliable way to lay an opaque, flat area over dry paint. A candle glow laid as a glaze went grey, and I replaced it with a few warm touches. Still short of van Eyck: the couple's bodies are simple columns and the joined hands are small and muddled. The bed and the robes are stripes of colour rather than the weight of real cloth, and the room has none of the original's crowd of small exact objects. The mirror, the shoes, the creature and the inscription are the parts I would keep.
