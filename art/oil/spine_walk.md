# On the Spine

Outside, on the back of the TESTAMENT, in light with no air in it. The Commando walks away from us along the top of the spine toward the hub, small, his crimson cloth across his shoulders, a safety line slung from his belt to the rail that runs along the spine's left edge on short posts. The spine is a road of bronze scale-plates, each plate a lit white-gold lip with a hard black crescent of shadow beyond it, crowding finer and finer as the road converges on the hub. The hub is a disc of rings with a black portal at its centre. Down the left flank, vault bays open as black arched recesses with lit rims; the right flank, turned from the Sun, is simply black. Overhead the ring rises from both sides as a vast plated arch, and we are inside its circle. The Sun is behind us: everything faces it and is lit flat white-gold, and every shadow is pure black and sharp. His own shadow runs ahead of him up the road like a needle pointing at the hub. The sky is black, with a few small hard stars only.

**How it was painted.** Value structure first and almost nothing else: a near-black ground, the road, arch and hub laid in as flat gold shapes, the shaded flank pure bone black, the sky re-covered in black. The darks were kept lean (bone black with a breath of ultramarine), and I waited four painting-days before any light went on, so the lips, edges and figure stayed crisp on dry paint. The scale-plates are rigger strokes: a black crescent then a lit lip just in front of it, row by row, spaced by a perspective function so they crowd toward the hub; that one pattern does most of the picture's work. Two things surprised me. The figure's black, painted wet over the gold road, came up a dull grey-green and had to be restated once dry. And with the Sun dead behind us, the walker's back must be lit, not silhouetted; my first figure was a black cut-out, which is wrong in this light, and I repainted his back and limbs in lit steel. Next time I would give the arch and hub their own scale-plates and some thickness, so they read as vast structure rather than flat bands, and make the flank's plating visible in the light.

## Pass at 02:48 CDT, Oct 2

Chunks 9–10, darks first, then lights after four painting-days, on dry paint. **The arch** now has mass. Its sun face carries eight rows of fine scale-plates along its whole length (lit lips, black crescents) between the old segment seams, with a hard white rim on its outer edge and a thinner one on its inner lip. Inside it, a band of pure black is its side face turned from the Sun, set with three rows of tiny warm windows. **The hub** is a drum. Its lit face has five raised ring lips, each lit on top with a hard black shadow under it, and a white rim. Its black side drops below it onto the road, and a deeper portal has a lit far lip. **The left flank** has dim, foreshortened rows of plates, and the vault bays have crisp white rims. **The walker** is about one and a half times larger, with a longer shadow. He is drawn in lit plate (helm with a white crown line, pauldrons, a pack divided into plates, the backs of his limbs) with the black kept at his sides and between plates, the crimson cloth across his shoulders, and the safety line to the rail.

Still short: the arch's black side face reads against the black sky only by its windows and rim, so at a glance the windows can read as stars. The drum's black side cuts the road off where it meets the hub.
