# The Fold Carpet Page

A carpet page after the Book of Kells and the Lindisfarne Gospels, in which the interlace is fold notation. A gold border runs round the page, carrying a red key pattern. Inside it, a great dark cross divides the page into four panels of red, blue, green and red. Every panel is packed with fold glyphs drawn as black-edged bands. Each glyph is a polygon (triangle to hexagon) holding a smaller copy of itself turned by its holonomy, and each is knotted to its neighbours above, below and beside by bowed gluing arcs. Small diamond knots sit between every four glyphs. The colours change from panel to panel: gold bands with blue insets on red, gold with red on blue, bone with red on green, gold with green on red. Down and across the cross runs a chain of larger gold glyphs with red insets, glued end to end. At the centre the Sun is a gold disc with a calm sleeping face, ringed by gold rays and a ring of small bone glyphs in the dark. In the four blue corner squares, little green angular Wardens curl round and bite their own tails.

**How it was painted.** Flat fields first: border, cross, panels, the Sun, the corner squares. After a drying wait, all the interlace went on as straight segments, about 750 band paths, each band stroked twice: a wide black stroke, then a narrower colour stroke down its middle. The first time, I laid the colour while the black was still wet. It went olive and dull, the night's most-repeated lesson. So I recomputed every path and laid all the black first, let it dry, then laid all the colour, which came out clean and bright. The corner squares, painted over the wet gold, came up mottled and were repainted. Next time I'd weave the bands properly over and under at crossings, which real interlace needs. I'd also make the Wardens larger, with clearer heads, and put a narrow bone outline round each panel.
