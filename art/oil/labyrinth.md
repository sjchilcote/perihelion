# The Gauntlet as a Labyrinth

The Chartres floor labyrinth, as the TESTAMENT would lay it, seen from directly above. A great circle sits on a dark tiled floor. Its outer bronze ring carries a hundred-odd lunations, dark teeth with bone cusps between them, cut through at the bottom by the entrance. Inside, eleven circuits of bone path are divided by bronze walls, with turning walls stacked along the four axes. At the centre is a six-lobed rosette, and in it the Sun with a calm sleeping face. The labyrinth is not Euclidean. Four violet seams run along the diagonals, each with a bright thread down its middle and two fold-glyphs on it: a square holding a smaller square a quarter-turn round. In each quadrant the circuits sit a little further out than in the last, so no circuit lines up across a seam. A walker who follows the path round finds it doesn't close the way a labyrinth should. The tiny Commando, seen from above with his shoulders, helm, cold optic, crimson rag and staff, is just stepping in at the entrance.

**How it was painted.** Three chunks in the flat method. First the bronze ring, the bone field and the gold Sun, each followed by a drying wait. Then every wall as short straight segments of a round brush along arcs, each quadrant's radii shifted by 6.5 units from the last. Then the violet seams were laid over the dry walls, and last came the details: lunations, the rosette, the face, the seam glyphs, the floor joints and the figure. Doing the walls, rather than the path, kept it simple and crisp. As a maze it is a likeness, not a true unicursal path. Next time I would design the actual Chartres route, so that a viewer could trace it and find exactly where the seams break it, and I would make the Commando a little larger.

## Pass at 06:22 CDT, Oct 2

Chunks 4–7. **A real route.** I divided the field into a grid of 11 circuits by 16 sectors (four per quadrant) and searched for one path that visits every cell. Its moves are along a circuit, a turn to the next circuit, or a crossing of a violet seam. By the labyrinth's own rule, crossing a seam forward lands you one circuit further out, and crossing it backward one circuit further in. The path starts at the entrance and threads all 176 cells. Its last cell opens back into an earlier one, so it loops forever, and the rosette has no door. I repainted the field bone and derived the walls from the route: a bronze wall stands wherever two neighbouring cells are not consecutive on it, every wall drawn as short segments along arcs. The seams and their quarter-turn glyphs went back on, and a red Ariadne's thread now runs down the middle of the route from the Commando. Wherever it meets a seam, a short white dashed hop shows it landing one circuit out (or in) on the other side, so a viewer can trace it and see why it never reaches the Sun. The entrance moved into the sector just left of the bottom axis, and the Commando moved with it.

Still short: the route is computer-found rather than Chartres's own order. It has the labyrinth's look, long arcs with turns, but not Chartres's famous symmetry. The thread reads as a dashed line rather than a continuous cord.
