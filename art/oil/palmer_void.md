# PALMER, Late

The pair to *PALMER*. The wall, the light from the left, the frontal stance, the floor and the shadow running off to the right are all the same as in the first portrait. What stands there now is the late frame. The old human torso still rises at the front, and it still wears the crimson rag with its bone cross. The palm frond is still etched on the left pauldron, a rosary is still wound round the left fist with the crucifix hanging below, and the right hand still grips the coiled lightning cannon like a staff, with the same cold blue in its muzzle as in the visor. But the torso is now the figurehead of a long, low, plated barrel carried on six jointed legs, three near and three far. Antler-like sensor masts have grown out of the helm and shoulders. Manipulator arms are folded flat against the flank, and a weapon mount rides on the back. Here and there a plate has gone wrong. Pale and turned inside out with a dark lip, it folds into a smaller turned copy of itself, and that into a smaller one. There is one on the flank, one on a leg, one at the shoulder, and one has grown out of the helm itself. Hung beside the first portrait, the rag, the cross, the palm, the rosary and the staff are what make it horrible.

How it was painted: the same method as the PALMER pass, decided before the first stroke. The wall and floor went down around the new silhouette. Every part was drawn as a hard-edged polygon and modelled with a dark core (no white), a steel mid-tone and a pale stroke toward the light. The far legs got a cooler, darker mid-tone so they sit back. After drying, the edges went on: a lit edge on the sides facing the light, warm reflected light on the far edges and a dark seam under each plate. The barrel went wrong first. Modelling each segment separately made a zebra of light stripes, and my first fix put the lit band in the wrong place and painted over the bottom of the torso. It was repainted as one cylinder (lit back, dark belly), the torso was rebuilt over its front, and the segment rings went on afterward. The crucifix also vanished twice into the wall, because a mid-brown on a mid-brown wall has no edge; it only read once it was repainted dark with a bone highlight. Still weak: the barrel's seams are scratchy, the folded arms don't read clearly against it, the muzzle light is a flat blue block, and the wrong plates read a little like bolts at full view.
