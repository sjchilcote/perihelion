# Carceri: the Gauntlet

The Gauntlet as a Piranesi Carceri plate, in warm sepia on pale paper. Two massive piers frame a great vault. Through it you see a second, smaller arch and, beyond that, a far gallery of round-headed arches. In one of them a pale vertical slit opens: the portal seam. Bronze stairways cross the hollow. One flight climbs from the floor to a landing on the right pier. From the same landing a second flight climbs left to a high bridge. A third flight drops from a lower bridge back down past the landing it began from. The lower bridge passes behind the stair that crosses in front of it, so no flight ends where it should. Ropes and a pulley hang from the vault. The same small Commando appears four times around the loop: on the high bridge, on the lower bridge, partway up a flight, and on the floor in the foreground. Each is a tiny dark figure with a rust-red rag.

How it was painted: 6 chunks, 3:4. My first broad "washes" in burnt sienna came out orange and wood-grained, so I repainted the piers and vaults opaque in bone-brown sepia tones over the pale ground. I drew the architecture as ink lines with a pointed round: voussoirs, courses, flagstones in perspective, balustrades, and stairs as stringers with stepped treads. Then I laid hatching (the `hatch` hand) for every mass in shadow: stair stringers, bridge faces, the vault, the piers, and the underside of each flight. I painted the deep shadows under the bridges and inside the far arches solid. Lit tread noses, rope, pulley, the portal seam and the figures went last. The hatching came out coarser and more speckled than an etched line, more like a woodcut or aquatint. The tones lean cool-mauve rather than warm sepia. The upper wall's light hatching reads as a mottled tone rather than as lines. The chunk with all the hatching took six minutes to compute. With more time I would hatch with finer, longer parallel strokes, warm the ink, and add more crossing stairs high in the vault.
