# The Last Testator

A million years ago, the very last of the builders sits alone at a window. Outside, the Sun is huge and close, white at the core and burning orange to the frame. After late Rembrandt, everything else in the room is a warm brown dark. The Testator is my locked design from "A Testator, Living": a long skull of bone plates swept up and back, an arc of small hooded eyes along the line where bone meets bronze chitin, chitin cheek and jaw plates, and a small closed mouth plate of folded bone. Here it is very old. Cracks run across the bone vault, two of the seven eyes are shut, and the sunlight lies thick along the front of the skull and the lit jaw. A thin neck of chitin rings rises from a heavy worn collar. The robe is threadbare and dark, and its folds dissolve into the floor. Its many-jointed bronze hands are on the desk, lit by the Sun: one pinches a bone stylus to the last tablet of the warning, its last lines written and a fold-glyph half-made under the point; the other rests flat and long-fingered at the tablet's far edge. Behind the chair, a docent stands waiting to take it: a bronze egg head with a bone face and one lens catching a spark of the Sun, a pleated bone ruff, and a body that is almost all shadow.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 26 chunks over dry layers. The head is the same plate layout as "A Testator, Living", carried into the new composition by one transform, so the design stays locked, and aged with cracks, closed eyes and duller chitin. The window and Sun went in first as graded rings laid wet together, with their seams blended at once. Most of the repairs were around the neck. A throat patch first jutted into the window, then a dark fill left a black rectangle. Repainting the area with the same background rings, clipped exactly to the space outside the robe, neck and collar, made it disappear. Light fold planes on the robes came out as grey stripes and had to be sunk back toward the robe colour. A sleeve repaint covered the resting hand, which I restored. The writing hand's light finger segments vanished into the bone tablet until I painted them in bronze with dark undersides. Still short: the head is small for the frame and the eyes read a little like beads at full view. The docent is mostly silhouette, and the robes are flat darks rather than Rembrandt's broken, glowing browns.
