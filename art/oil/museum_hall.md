# The Hall of Civilizations

A long, high bronze gallery runs straight away from us between rows of bronze pilasters, its ceiling lost in near-black. Down both sides stand tall glass-and-bronze vitrines, six a side at uneven intervals. Each sits on a dark bronze plinth with a small bone label marked in fold notation. Each is lit from within by a cold lamp-strip under its cap, which throws a soft cone of pale light down through the glass onto one object: a spiral shell-instrument, a crystal lattice, a woven vessel, a mask, a seed. Some cases hold nothing but their label. The things stand dark against their own light, a little cold glint on their upper edges, and the cases' glow is faintly reflected in the polished floor. At the far end, on the axis, stands the last case, newly made: brighter bronze, glass blazing empty and white, its label blank, its cold light spilling onto the end wall. Beside it a docent waits with a stylus raised. In the foreground, left of centre, the Commando stands with his back to us, looking down the hall, edged cold by the cases on either side.

**How I painted it.** I set up a one-point perspective and laid in lean darks. The first wall coat came out too light and needed a second, darker opaque coat. One helper built each vitrine from plinth, cap, side-glass and front-glass masks. Things that went wrong: the first glass, laid with the detail hand in a pale cold mix, came out chalk-white and marbled. I repainted it as dim cold glass with a lamp-strip and a two-step cone of light, which reads, though the cones look rather like light-bulb shapes. The objects, drawn first as thin strokes, vanished into the glass, so I restated them as solid masks painted opaque. The first floor reflections were hard gray slabs. I painted them out and relaid them barely lighter than the floor, though small hard remnants remain at the case bases. Still short: the last case and its docent are small at that distance, so the docent and its stylus are only just legible; the Commando is larger than "small"; and the pilasters are a flat ochre.
