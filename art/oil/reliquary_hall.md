# The Reliquary Hall

We look down the TESTAMENT's great reliquary hall: a long nave under a barrel vault of bronze, its golden-angle ribs receding in a tunnel of warm arcs, the dark skin between them studded with scale plates. Arched side chapels line both walls, and each holds a sealed bronze case in its shadow, the lid catching a thread of light. The floor is a bone mosaic in a sunflower spiral, its tesserae curving in interlocking arcs toward a stepped dais at the far end. There, a single shaft of sunlight falls from the one aperture in the vault that faces the Sun. It strikes the tall bronze-and-bone reliquary, with its one lens, and spills a pool of light across the mosaic before it. Halfway down the nave the Commando is a small dark figure walking into it, long shadow running back toward us. The hall is mid-dark and warm, and the shaft is the only blazing thing in it: a cathedral built by people who knew they would not be there to show anyone around.

**How it was painted.** It took 29 chunks on a warm sienna ground. I laid one-point perspective masses in lean darks (bone black, burnt sienna, raw sienna, no white), then a second, darker coat graded toward the viewer. The ribs are single long filbert strokes along arcs, each with a lit inner edge. The plates are short arcs placed by golden-angle drift between the ribs. The mosaic is about 2,500 flat-brush tesserae placed on a Vogel spiral on the floor plane and projected into perspective. The shaft was laid last, on dry paint, as a glazed body and then as long near-white ray strokes. The paint fought back three times. A blend to soften the floor's shadow glaze dragged pale tesserae across both walls in streaks, and I had to repaint the walls and re-cut the chapels. A dark transparent glaze on the lower walls dried as a flat grey-violet sheet, as studio note 57 warns, and had to be repainted opaque. Blending the shaft's edges built a brown "tube" around it, which I painted out and then had to restore the ribs and plates across. Next time I would keep blends away from the floor entirely, and lay the shaft as rays from the start. The vault plates still read a little as dots rather than a scaled skin, and the light spilling around the aperture is scattered rather than glowing.

## Pass at 02:57 CDT, 2 Oct 2026

The director's note was that the shaft read as a solid golden obelisk leaning over. In this pass (chunks 30–37) I painted the shaft and the scattered flakes around the aperture back out to the dark of the vault and the far wall. The first cover dried too light and pulled the ribs into it, so it took a second, darker opaque coat. Then I restated the ribs across the vault and laid the light again as roughly a hundred long, thin strokes fanning down from the aperture to the dais, with a few thin warm veils under them. They are strongest near the aperture and thin out downward, with the dark showing between them and no outer edge. The strokes broke into a scratchy shimmer on the dry paint, which suits a beam full of dust. The aperture is now a small hard white slot, and the reliquary stands inside the light: a lit bronze flank, bone edges, and a white glint in its single lens. The floor, walls and figure are unchanged.

What still falls short: the painted-out area shows faintly as a slightly olive column on the far wall, and the scale plates were not restored there. Under the beam the vault is ribs only.
