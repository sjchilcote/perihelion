# The Endless Stair

A square loop of bronze steps floats in the dark of the Gauntlet, seen from above at an isometric angle. Four flights of seven, seven, three and three steps run around an open light-well, each flight climbing, and the four close into a loop that returns to where it began. That is the Penrose stair. The well glows violet far below through the centre of the loop. A handrail runs along the outer edge on bronze posts, and a lamp on a post stands at each of the four landings. The same Commando, four times, red tabard and grey cross, climbs each flight in turn, one foot lifted to the next step.

How it was painted: this took three starts. My first layout used flights that do not close a loop, and walkways three units wide modelled as solid towers down to a base. They came out as a pile of bronze blocks, and I painted them out. The second used thin strokes over the ghost of the first and read as a mottled ring, with the well outside the loop. For the third I cleared the canvas fully with two heavy dark passes, then built the honest Penrose geometry. In plan the legs do not close: they miss by a diagonal gap of four units, and the stair climbs exactly four units. In isometric projection, a step along the diagonal and the same rise land on the same point on screen, so the loop closes visually. I painted the well first, then each step as three masked faces (tread and two risers) in depth order, then the rails, lamps and figures as strokes on dry paint. What falls short: the steps are shallow and blocky, so the climb reads less strongly than in Escher. The lamps are starbursts. The figures are small, and the seam where the loop closes (upper left) is a little crowded. The outer skirts of the stair look more like stacked boxes than a building.
