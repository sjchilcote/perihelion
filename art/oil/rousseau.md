# The Sleeping Pilgrim

After Henri Rousseau's "The Sleeping Gypsy". On the TESTAMENT's outer hull, which reads as a pale bronze desert laid in neat, naïve rows of scale-plate arcs, the Commando's frame lies asleep (powered down) on its back. Its helmed head rests on a pale bedroll, and its body is wrapped in a crimson tabard with bone stripes, like Rousseau's striped robe. One gauntlet is folded on its chest, and the cannon-staff is laid along the ground in front of it, like the mandolin. A small bone cross stands at its feet. Over it, from the left, a Warden has come: a crested green-bronze shell on six jointed legs, its head lowered to the sleeper's face, sniffing with a yellow eye, its bladed forelimbs folded back, not attacking. A huge pale Sun sits low on the right, rimmed in gold, as calm as Rousseau's moon. A low grey-blue ridge of the hull lies on the horizon, and the night sky deepens upward with a few precise four-pointed stars.

How it was painted: 5 chunks, about 1.55:1, as smooth flat colour with crisp edges. The sky is five opaque bands blended horizontally while wet. The hull is two flat ochre bands with hand-drawn rows of plate arcs that grow toward us. Every figure is built from flat opaque shapes on dry paint, then edges and details as single strokes. Still short: the dark boot block at the frame's feet reads more as a mistake than as boots. A second cross by the head is half hidden under the Warden's blade. The frame is a little too flat and long, close to a log, against Rousseau's carefully modelled sleeper. The sky's bands are slightly visible.
