# The Wanderer above the Sun

The Commando's frame stands at the apex of a bronze dome on the TESTAMENT's hull, seen from behind, the way Friedrich's wanderer stands on his crag. His right foot rests on the worn central boss, and his rifle is planted like a pilgrim's staff, his hand over the muzzle. A dark rosary hangs from the other hand. A faded bone-coloured cross, rubbed nearly away, is still on the back of his crimson capelet. The Sun fills the picture behind him: a disc so near that its upper limb rises above his head and its glow gives way to the black only at the corners. His helmet and shoulders sit in the white of it, like a halo he did not ask for. The hull's scale-plates are laid in a golden-angle spiral whose centre is under his feet, so every row of scales leads to him. Beyond the near crest the scales flatten into a glittering bronze sea toward the dome's horizon. His three shadows, from his two legs and the rifle, run back across the plates toward us, along the line from the Sun through his feet. Whatever he is looking at, we are looking at it too.

How it was painted: on a warm mid-toned ground. The Sun went down first in limb-darkening zones. The bands only fused when I dragged radial strokes of a half-tone across them; the badger blender would not move thick lead white. The plates are a real phyllotaxis spiral projected onto a sphere. Each scale was painted outer-first, as a dark crescent and a light lip, with a light model of the reflected disc deciding which plates glare. What surprised me was how unforgiving thin wet paint over a dry dark is. Pale scumbles turned into cold grey veils, blending smeared them into rectangles and marble, and yellow glazes over dark went olive. The corona had to be repainted opaque twice. The good passages came from wet-in-wet sessions. The near hull was repainted whole in one sitting, and the cast shadows were laid last over the dry crest and left alone. Next time I would plan every passage as one wet session, keep glazes out of the sky entirely, and stop blending the moment something reads.
