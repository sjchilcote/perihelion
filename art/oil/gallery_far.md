# Two Wings, One Volume

One volume holds two halls, and we see both at once. The first is the Testator east wing: a long bronze hall with pointed ribs on a straight axis, a bone runner, rows of pedestal chairs with egg-shaped backs, and a gold glow at its far end. That gold light touches only its own things: the inner lips of its ribs, the crowns of its chair backs, the Commando. The second is another maker's wing. Its slender pale columns and round arches run on an axis turned about 35 degrees off the first, toward its own far opening of cold blue-white light at the right. Its columns stand in the open floor of the bronze hall. Its round transverse arches cut straight across the bronze vault. Its cold joint-lines are scored across the bronze floor, and its four-legged straight-backed chairs stand in the bronze hall's aisle, a pale one right beside the runner. Its cold light rims only its own columns, arches and chairs. Each hall is coherent in its own perspective, and they share one horizon. The Commando belongs to the bronze hall. He walks its runner toward its gold end, rimmed only by its gold light, his shadow thrown back toward us along its runner. The cold columns pass him without lighting him.

**How I painted it.** I set up one camera with two coordinate systems. Hall A uses the plain one-point perspective. Hall B is the same space rotated about the vertical by 0.62 rad, so its vanishing point sits on the same horizon at the right. I laid hall A in first as warm bands graded by depth and put the ribs in on set paint, stroking each in short reloaded segments. Then I painted hall B's arcade from far to near over it, keeping a mask of every column with its depth. Chairs and the figure were painted far to near and clipped against the B columns that stand nearer than they do, so the two halls overlap in depth rather than one simply lying over the other. What went wrong: the chair-back mid tone, laid into the wet dark, went violet and had to be repainted warm. The first shadow covered the whole runner like a carpet, and narrowing it left the runner's edges as bright yellow stripes, which I then dimmed toward us. The floor still shows hard steps between its depth bands. Next time I would give hall B a light pool on its own floor, and model the column plinths as blocks rather than the rounded ribbon ends they came out as.
