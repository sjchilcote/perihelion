# The Last Docent Rests

The Ninth Gallery is a long, low bronze hall seen down its length, lined on both sides with tall dark exhibit cases on bronze plinths. Each case holds a dim bone object that can barely be made out. High on the right wall, one small window lets in warm light, and it falls on the floor as a single crisp quad split by the shadow of its mullions. On a bench set across that light, a docent sits down for the first time in a million years. It has a bronze egg of a head, a bone face turned a little down, and a pleated bone ruff. Its robe falls over its knees to two feet on the floor, and its hands are folded on an inscribed bone tablet in its lap. Its one lens has dimmed to a small warm glow. A little way off in the dark, the Commando stands in profile with his helm bowed, his facing edges rimmed by the window's light. Everything else is dark.

I painted it in the corridor language: a one-point perspective scaffold in Lua, lean dark bronze passes, the cases placed in depth on each wall, and the window as the only light. The floor light is a hard-edged shape, not a glow, so the quiet comes from its geometry. The figures are flat planes: a dark core, a lighter plane on the side facing the window, and thin warm rims. The lens and other lights went on dry paint at the end. The paint surprised me three times. The exhibits first came out as bright identical discs, so I repainted them as dim varied shapes. My first floor light was a speckled blob, which I repainted as a crisp window-shaped quad. The docent's first body, painted on still-wet bench paint, picked up the bench and came out as a large blob. I cut it back to a narrower robe and painted the old silhouette back into the bench, the light and the floor. Still short: the docent reads more as a bell-shaped robe than clearly seated, and the Commando is a plain column of plates.
