# Last Minutes of the Approach

The TESTAMENT fills the right of the frame and runs off its edges. Its spine rises out of the top of the picture and the near arc of its habitat ring passes out at the bottom, so the station is too big to fit. The Sun is just off-frame to the right and a little behind. It lights the station from that side: a hard band of light runs down the spine, the far inner wall of the ring catches it in pale bays cut through by the spine's shadow, and the near arc comes out of the dark into worn bronze scale-plate armour flecked with verdigris. Everything else is close to black, and the people inside show only as rows of warm lit windows along the dark decks and around the hub. Where the near arc is lit, an erosion break has stripped the plates away to show golden-spiral chambers inside, and the damage spreads into missing plates around it. Through the gap in the ring, behind a silhouetted spoke, the solar glare burns warm white. On the lit flank of the spine sits the tiny gray MIRIAD outpost, a few right-angled prefab modules with amber hazard marks, dark and silent. Out of the empty upper left comes the CUCULUS: a scorched dart with a white-hot retro-burn and a long pale trail, aimed at that outpost. The upper-left third of the picture is left as quiet star field for the intro text.

I built the picture as a scaffold first: a perspective ring, spine and hub in Lua, so that every stroke could be laid along the form. The handling that worked was hundreds of short strokes across the form, each loaded from an eight-step value ramp of earth bronzes. A raking-light model picked the step for each stroke, with some randomness so neighbouring steps mixed. The ramps ran from opaque bone-black darks up to lemon-white highlights. Over that went a golden-angle lattice of scale plates, each a small dark crescent shadow with a cream glint. The paint surprised me three times. Dark glazes looked right while wet, but once dry the ridges of the strokes underneath poked through as bright squiggles, so the shadow sides had to be repainted with opaque darks. An Indian-yellow glaze over the cool gray glare turned acid green, and I had to repaint the glare in opaque warm grays. Wide badger blending of contrasting colours made clouds instead of glows. Next time I would plan the light before the first stroke, putting the shadow sides in opaque darks from the start instead of lighting the whole station and darkening it afterwards. I would also build the glare from opaque warm values on dry paint, never by blending.

## Pass at 00:05 CDT, 2 October

This pass went over the existing canvas (chunks 151–179) and kept the composition, crop and overall values. Block-average values over the station and sky match the previous version to within a few levels, except at the glare.

- **Glare.** The glare is repainted on dry paint in opaque warm grays that run radially from the off-frame Sun, with a solid zinc-and-lead white-hot core. A thin opaque near-black band was laid inside the station's silhouette wherever it meets the glare, and the glare was clipped to the open sky, so the ring and spokes now cut it with a hard edge. The outer front is broken up with short radial strokes in dark blue-grays and a narrow radial badger seam. A few hairline rays are cut off by the spokes. Rays that ran into the upper sky read as scratches, so they were knocked back to faint bands.
- **CUCULUS.** The burn now has a white-hot core touch, a sharper white cone inside a yellow sheath, and a crisper cross-flare.
- **MIRIAD outpost.** It now has panel seams, a row of dead viewports, truss struts down to the hull, radiator ribs, a comm mast with a dish, hairline sunlit edges and crisper amber chevrons.
- **Erosion break.** Each septum has a dark shadow side and a lit edge. The spiral wall is restated as one crisp lit line with a shadow inside it, four chambers have tiny warm lit windows, and there are bent plate shards at the broken lip.
- **Scale plates. This mostly failed.** I re-cut, darkened, wore down or removed a random 16–22% of the lattice plates. The lattices I reused (NEAR_PLATES, SPINE_PLATES) are about twice the size of the visible plates, and their light model still counted the shadowed part of the near arc as lit. The result was polka dots across the dark near arc and the spine. I repaired it by diffing against final_prev.png and laying short strokes along the form, each from a pile picked by the local value of the old version (pile outputs measured on the canvas, neighbouring piles dithered). I then re-laid the window dots and the plate crescents. The dark arc is back to its old value, but the repaired passages are softer and more mottled than the crisp lattice they replace, especially on the spine's lit band and in the shadow-to-light transition of the near arc. What is left of the intended variation is about half a dozen plates near the glare: brown, pale and oversized ones. That reads as somewhat handmade, but the director's request for varied plates is not really met.

Still short: the plate variation needs a lattice that matches the visible plate size and an explicit lit-zone mask, and it should be tested on a scrap easel first. The septa's lit lines make the break read a little like a spoked wheel; curved septa with lit edges on one side only would read more as chambers. Lesson: never reuse a stored plate list without checking its size and lighting against what is actually visible on the canvas.

**Director's decision (00:01):** the 23:00 pass was rejected. Its plate repairs
left the spine and hub mottled where v1 had a crisp lattice. The canonical
painting stays v1, the first 150 chunks of the log. The rejected pass is kept
