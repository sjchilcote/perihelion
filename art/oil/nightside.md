# The Night Side

The TESTAMENT's ring fills the lower two-thirds of the frame as one immense dark curve. Its limb arches across the picture with the Sun directly behind it, so the corona burns along the edge as a thin flaring band: white-hot at the limb, then cadmium and orange, then dull red into a blue-black sky with faint stars. Below that edge the hull is almost nothing. Rows of worn scale-plates are barely visible as dark crescents a shade above black, foreshortened into tight bands near the limb. Window belts curve across the hull: hundreds of dull dark ports and, here and there, a short run of warm lit windows, the lamps the station keeps for guests. Far to the right, on the limb itself, the dead MIRIAD outpost stands in silhouette against the burning edge, a few right-angled boxes and a mast. Its one working beacon shows a small amber point where its low module meets the dark hull.

I decided the values first. The toned ground serves as the sky, and the hull is one lean opaque pass of bone black and bone brown with no white. The corona went in as short radial strokes in four piles chosen by distance from the limb, then a narrow badger blend along the radius only. Plates, window belts, starlight, the limb line, the outpost and the beacon all went on later, onto dry paint. Two surprises: the "faint blue starlight" glints on the plate edges came out far too light, like scattered snow, and had to be painted out; and the beacon's first touches barely deposited on the dry paint, so it took a second, heavier touch to read. The corona came out wider and more flame-like than the thin edge I meant. Next time I would lay it at half the width, and I would put the beacon somewhere it reads against dark at full view, not only up close.
