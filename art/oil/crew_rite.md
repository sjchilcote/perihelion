# Requiem for Miriad-7

This is the hydroponics deck from the same eye and with the same hourglass geometry as *Hydroponics, Before the Fall* and *The Night It Fell*, long after. The grow-lamps hang dead over the beds, and the crop is a scatter of dry stalks. The only light is the far Sun through the gridded viewport and one small candle-LED. The crew's work cart has been wheeled into the aisle. On it, seven MIRIAD crew badges lie in a row, grey slabs with amber stripes. The yellow watering can stands upright again at the end, a small bone cross is propped against it, and the candle burns in front of the row, lighting the cart top in a warm pool. In the foreground, with his back to us, the Commando kneels before the cart with his helm bowed and his shoulder lamp dark. He is built from plates: pauldrons with lames, back plate with a ridge, faulds, and boots soles-up. The candle catches his right-hand edges in thin amber, and a rosary hangs from his left gauntlet with its crucifix just catching the light.

I reused the coordinates from the earlier hydroponics paintings, with the hourglass floor band, the trays, the window and the lamp housings. Values came first: dark walls and floor, the window as the one cool light, and the candle as the one warm one. The figure is plate polygons in a lean dark grey, with the seams and rims added on dry paint. The paint surprised me in two places. My first warm pool, dithered across a large radius, swallowed the cart and broke into speckles on the wall, so I repainted the cart as planes: a dark front, a lit top and a bright near edge. I also tightened the pool to a small radius. The candle's first glow came out as a cartoon flower and then as a muddy disc. It is now a small radial halo with a white-hot core, but it is still a little spiky. Still short: the cart is mostly hidden behind the figure, so the badges are small. The beds directly behind the cart were lost when I repainted the pool area. The window is brighter and bluer than the grief wants.
