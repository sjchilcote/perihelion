# PALMER

A state portrait of the Commando in his first frame. He stands full length and frontal, like a knight's effigy stood upright, against a dark warm wall. One light comes from the left and pools on the wall behind his lit shoulder. He wears steel plate over a crimson tabard with a worn bone cross on the breast and a belt with a brass buckle. His helm has no face, only a visor slit and one cold blue optic on the shadowed side. A palm frond is etched in gold on his left pauldron: the palmer's proof that he came back. A rosary is wound twice round his left gauntlet, and its crucifix hangs below the fist. His right hand grips the lightning cannon like a pilgrim's staff. Its barrel is ringed with copper coils and its muzzle holds the same cold light as his eye. His shadow runs away from the light across the floor to the right. A weapon that knows it is a weapon, standing for its picture.

How it was painted: on a toned ground. The wall went down in two tones, lighter behind the lit shoulder, and the edge between them was softened with the blender. The figure was blocked in as one opaque dark, then modelled part by part (helm, pauldrons, cuirass, arms, cuisses, poleyns, greaves, sabatons). Each part got a lean dark over the whole, a warm steel mid-tone on its lit side and a pale steel stroke nearest the light, all laid wet-into-wet along the part's axis. The tabard was laid in crimson the same way, and its folds were drawn later with a round brush. Plate lines, the etched palm, the rosary, the coils, the optic and the hot highlights all went on after days of drying, followed by warm reflected light along the shadowed contours. What surprised me: any lead white in the steel mixes read pale and lavender, and the first gauntlet and elbow plates came out as flat grey lozenges that had to be repainted lean and dark. Wet-into-wet crimson folds simply merged; only the round-brush troughs and ridges laid on set paint read as folds. Still weak: the frame is stiff and mannequin-like in proportion, the arms are tubes, and the rosary and palm are small at full view. With more time I would turn the figure a quarter toward the light, break the limbs into more lames, and make the cannon heavier.

## Pass at 01:55 CDT (2 Oct)

What changed: I repainted the figure plate by plate as hard-edged polygons, each with a dark core of bone black, ultramarine and burnt sienna (no white), a warm steel mid-tone and a pale stroke toward the light. The edges went on after drying: a pale or hot line on every edge facing the light, a warm reflected line on the far edge, and a dark seam under each plate. The limbs are now broken into parts:
- **Arms:** pauldron with a lame over the rerebrace, a couter with its fan at the elbow, vambrace, a gauntlet cuff and a fist with finger lames.
- **Legs:** cuisse, a poleyn with a side wing, greave and sabaton.

The tabard is shorter so the knees show, with a belt and heavier folds: deep troughs drawn on set paint, lit ridges toward the light, and a lit hem. The pose shifts weight onto his left leg. The free leg's knee comes forward and out, and its sabaton turns outward. The shoulders tilt against the hips, and the helm's visor and optic turn toward the light. The palm frond is larger and runs right across the pauldron. The rosary has bigger beads, a second turn round the fist and a larger crucifix. The cannon is half again as thick, with a breech block below the grip, copper coils, a lit edge down the barrel and a cold-lit muzzle housing. The background was repainted wherever the old silhouette no longer covers it.

What still falls short:
- The contrapposto is too slight to read at a glance. The torso is still frontal and symmetrical.
- The rosary and crucifix are still small at full view.
- The muzzle light is a flat blue block.
- The greaves are long, plain shafts.
- A thin seam of the old silhouette shows in places along the new contours.
