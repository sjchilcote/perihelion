# What It Says (from above)

A second, small take on the remass bay, seen from far behind and high above the kneeling frame. The beam from the oculus is the whole picture. It falls as a broad gold shaft from the top edge, between the dark curve of the sphere tank on the left and the drum on the right, and spreads into a heap of lit rubble on the floor. At the heart of that heap, very small, PALMER kneels with one arm out toward the pale Ark, and the cyan point of its readout is the only cold mark in the picture. Below the light, the floor goes back to dark rubble. Far up on the wall, a few warm gallery openings give the scale.

**How it was painted.** It took 22 chunks on a warm red-brown toned ground. First came two broad, opaque coats of bronze darks, then the beam as body colour along the fall of the light, with a hot core and a pale glow at the top. Flat-brush dabs built the floor's rubble, lit or dark by their distance from the pool. The paint pushed back hard. The first beam was so opaque that it read as a pale pillar. A low-load white veil meant as haze around the beam dried as a grey dry-brush sheet with hard edges, and a warm glaze over it turned orange-olive. Blending over the gold-flanked sphere pulled the gold across the whole tank. I had to repaint the tanks and much of the air with opaque dark, and then melt only a narrow seam of orange glow outside the beam. What finally made the shaft read as light was long single filbert strokes running down the light in gold and near-white, laid over the old flat paint. A mending patch at the drum's shoulder left an orange smudge that is still faintly visible.

**Next time**, I would never veil large dark areas with low-load white, and I would keep every blend to a narrow seam (both studio notes warned me). I would also start the beam as rays, not as a filled column. The beam still ends a little squarely at the floor, the drum's top is rough, and the grey band below the pool is only partly covered.
