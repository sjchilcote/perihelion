# The Seed, Burrowed

A cutaway painted like a hand-coloured engraving. Above is the cool blue-
black of space, with a few stars and a line of plate lettering kept to
abstract marks. Below the skin of scale plates, the Testament's hull is
cut open in section. Its layers of bronze armour carry engraver's diagonal
hatching and plate joints, with dark void spaces between them, crossed by
vertical ribs and diagonal braces. The seed's drill-tunnel slants down from
upper left. Its mouth on the surface is capped by a weld scar, and the bore
is sealed with riveted grey-bronze scar plates. At its end the armoured pod
lies lodged across the layers it crushed through, cut open to show four
lit rooms:
- the fabricator, a dark machine whose round mouth glows orange to white;
- the rack, three spare frames hanging from a rail;
- the archive, a tall cabinet with dash readouts and its four status
  lights: two green, one amber, one cyan;
- at the nose, the small sacred corner, a bone-white cross on the wall over
  a ledge draped in crimson cloth.
Warm ceiling lamps light each room. From the shell, dark red cable-roots
have grown out through the voids and along the station's ribs.

## How it was painted

I made it in 8 chunks on a warm dark ground at 1.6:1. I laid the strata as
alternating armour and void bands. I defined the pod and tunnel shapes
first so the station's ribs, hatching and joints stop at them, and the
rooms went in after that. Two things went wrong:
- The archive lights laid as touches on wet paint vanished. Small filled
  lenses on dry paint worked.
- The fabricator's glow, a scumble, went on as orange blotches, and the
  first repaint muddied into the wet wall. Once it was dry, I restated the
  mouth as crisp concentric rings with a dark lip, and that is what reads.

Still rough: the strata are too regular and read a little like a fence.
The tunnel's scar plates look like stripes more than patches. The roots
are thin at full view. A trace of orange blotch is left beside the
fabricator.
