# The Floor of the Testators

A floor tile for the Gauntlet's Testator halls, seen straight down and lit evenly. It is an 8 × 8 grid of worn bronze plates. Bone-coloured inlay lines run exactly on the 125-unit grid, so the courses carry on across the wrap. Each plate sits a little differently inside its cell: its own inset, its own chamfered and rounded corners, its own brush direction. Verdigris crusts the joints between plate and inlay. Every plate is a different bronze: polished gold where feet have worn it, deep umber where patina has built up, red-brown where the copper shows. Each one carries its own fold-notation, worked out by the golden angle. Some have nested polygon inscriptions (3 to 8 sides), each ring turned by 137.5°. Some have phyllotaxis spirals of studs, and some have both. No two plates match. On pale plates the lines are cut in dark; on dark plates they are inlaid pale, so the ornament still reads at game zoom, where one cell is about 25 pixels.

How it was painted: the ground is an olive-umber imprimatura over a knifed white ground. A verdigris lay-in went into the joints, then I knocked it back with a dark verdigris-umber, leaving the green as a broken crust. The plates went on as four bronze piles with clipped body strokes. Rough-edged patches of light, dark and red bronze were scumbled over them and badger-blended inside each plate, to make the whole-plate wear. The inlay was painted with a flat brush along the grid. The ornament was drawn stroke by stroke with a pointed round and touches of a round. Before drawing it, I measured the value of each plate from a look at the canvas and picked dark or pale paint to suit. What surprised me: a "light" scumble at load 0.3 over the whole field didn't wear the ornament down. It covered everything, including tone variation, ornament and inlay, with one flat bronze, and I had to paint all three again. A medium-rich glaze over two plates did the same thing in streaks; those two plates now show a ghost of their old inscription under a re-cut one. Next time I would only ever wear the ornament down plate by plate, with a dry brush, and never with a pass over the whole field.
