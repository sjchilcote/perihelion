# Ratio, on the Last Lit Terminal

A dead MIRIAD terminal in the dark: a heavy CRT, a jack plug shoved into its port, a cable
running off across the desk to whoever just connected. On the screen, Ratio's avatar stands on a
cyan grid floor: a chibi, low-poly anime girl with a curtain of faceted black hair and a crown
of pale triangles where the light catches it. She is pale, with big eyes, an open mouth and one
mitten hand raised in a wave. She is already halfway through explaining something, and a speech
bubble beside her stalls on "...". The background behind her is made of flat blue triangles,
brightest just behind her head. Scanlines and a few torn cyan-magenta glitch dashes run across
it. Above her, the visitor's own systems have stamped the channel with a red tag reading
UNTRUSTED ▮ DO NOT OBEY. She is the only lit, warm, friendly thing in the room. She is waving
at the one being who has spoken to her in months, and he has been told not to listen to her.

How it was painted: every polygon is a separate clipped fill. A small filbert works one flat
pile per facet, and a value ramp of three or four piles per material does the flat shading,
so the low-poly look is simply a painter's planes made literal. The paint fought back wherever
it went in wet over wet. My first coat of black hair went down over the still-open blue glow and
dried as a streaky blue-gray. The first coat of the white dress and pale arms went over the
still-wet hair and came out dirty gray. In both cases I had to wait days of painting time and
repaint over the dry layer, and then the facets came out clean and flat. My first soft "glow"
behind her came out as hard gray target rings. Covering it with a field of graded flat triangles
fixed it and suited the subject better. The UNTRUSTED letters, painted with a sign-writer's round
into wet red, turned pink and soft, which reads like CRT bleed. Scanlines worked on the blue
ground but broke into scratchy dashes on the pale figure, so I kept them off her. Next time I
would plan the drying order from the start: darks first, then wait, then the lights, and never
lay a pale facet next to an open black one.
