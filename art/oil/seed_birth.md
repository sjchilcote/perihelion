# Inside the Egg

The moment after *The Cuckoo's Egg*, from inside the hull: the dark void between the TESTAMENT's armour layers. Overhead, the underside of the outer armour is lit only around the drill-hole, which glows in a ring of white-hot metal with molten drips hanging from its lip. Below it the seed pod comes down on its own light like a lantern. It is a gold capsule with darker seams, a white-hot waist and a hot point at its nose, struck round with rays of warm dust that fade into the dark. Molten droplets fall past it slowly, each a bright head with a short trail, smaller and redder the further they fall. The station's internal ribs arch through the void from both sides and catch the pod's light along the edges that face it, gold near the pod and dull red further down. Black, gold, one descending light.

**How it was painted.** On a near-black bronze ground, chosen so the load-heavy machine never had to paint a whole field. The ribs, the ceiling and the pod's body were laid as dark shapes, then the lights went on after drying. I relearned the same lesson twice. A shaft of low-load pale strokes meant as light in air came up as a grey striped curtain. Pale warm veils round the pod, blended, came up as a grey disc with a hard edge. Both had to be painted out with opaque dark. Opaque warm-dark zones gave a target of rings, and blending the seams only softened them. What finally worked was the light itself: a few hundred radial strokes struck out from the pod (and from the hole), each fading to nothing, crossing every ring. That turned the target into radiance, something like an icon's mandorla, which suits the subject. Next time I would plan the radiance as rays from the start, keep every pale mix away from the black, and give the ribs and ceiling more of their bronze plating where the light catches them.
