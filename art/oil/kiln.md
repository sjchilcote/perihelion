# The Fabricator

Inside the seed's print chamber, where the Commando's frames are made. The chamber is a round kiln of scorched iron ribs that arc overhead and down the walls, their lips and rivets catching the heat. Hoses of raw material snake in from both walls, banded with couplings. A gantry spans the chamber and carries the print head down into a blaze, and at its nozzle the light goes white-hot. Below it, on a wide iron print bed glowing orange where the heat falls, lies a half-made frame. The legs and pelvis are finished, dark iron with one knee drawn up and lit along their upper edges. The torso is still a lattice of glowing lines, spine and ribs. Where the shoulders, arms and head will be there is only a drift of sparks and dust. The heat falls off from orange-white at the centre to deep red, then to near-black red at the walls. It is meant as a forge and a womb at once, with the frame being made in the light.

**How it was painted.** Value first. The ground was a dark red-brown, and I laid the heat in as zones from black-red to orange-yellow, then blended them radially. That blend raked the zones into a starburst I hadn't planned and kept. The darks went on lean: bone black with a little red. Then the ribs, gantry, bed, hoses and printed legs, and a near-black pass over the walls so the edges fall away. Lights went on last, on dry paint. The centre caused the trouble. White stippled into the blended orange came up as a pale grey-pink disc. Repainting it in yellow made a flat sun with a hard edge, and only radial strokes of the burst's own oranges across that edge broke it. The white-hot point at the nozzle only stayed white when I stippled it after the yellow had dried. Next time I would make the printed frame larger and the print bed smaller, so the half-made body is the subject rather than a detail. I would also give the ribs real breadth and modelling; at the moment they read as thin hoops.
