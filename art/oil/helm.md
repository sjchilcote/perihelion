# The Man with the Golden Helmet (the Commando's Helm)

A head-and-shoulders portrait of the Commando's great helm in the manner of the Rembrandt-circle "Man with the Golden Helmet": a near-black warm ground with a faint glow behind at the upper left, and the helm the whole subject. It is worn steel, cool grey where the window light falls on the dome and sinking to brown-black on the far side, dented and scratched. The Church gilded it: a Latin cross runs up the front of the dome, standing proud enough to throw a shadow, and a broad gold band crosses the brow. Where the light strikes, the gold blazes in thick, granular touches of hot yellow, and around the turn it cools to amber. Below the band, gold rims with small filigree scrolls frame the dark visor slit, and deep in the slit sits one cold blue-white optic, its light just touching the rims. The face-plate falls into deep shadow, with a faint ghost of filigree, rivets down its front ridge, and a warm reflection from the crimson cloth at its chin. At the bottom, a crimson gorget-cloth hangs in heavy folds and sinks into shadow on the far side, and the gilded edges of a steel pauldron catch the light at the left. It is meant as weary and devout: a helm bowed a little, carrying its decoration like a vow.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 40 chunks, building in layers over dry paint as in the Docent and Testator portraits. Toned ground; graded background glow; dead colour; steel facets; then the gilding, filigree, optic, dents and cloth. My first background glow was a hard-edged disc, because a blend over tacky paint does nothing. Repainting it as three graded rings laid wet together, with their seams blended at once, made it soft. The first dome was pale beige, and the gold couldn't blaze against it. Repainting the steel darker and cooler made the gilding the brightest thing in the picture, which is the whole point of this subject. A heavily loaded stipple of hot gold, clustered, gave a granular, encrusted surface that reads as impasto. A near-miss: my first cross had its arms set low and read as inverted. I painted the arms out and set them high on the upright. The optic took three tries, from cartoon blue disc to dull grey to a small cold core with a faint glow on the rims. A squint check showed the pauldron competing with the dome, so I turned its plates down a step. Still short: the helm's silhouette is a plain egg, without the crest or the more complex profile of a real great helm. The bow of the head ("looking down") is only implied, and the crimson cloth is blocky planes rather than heavy fabric.
