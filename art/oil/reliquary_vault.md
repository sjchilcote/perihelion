# The Vault Bays

A long, low barrel vault in the spine's storage bays, seen down its length. Bronze ribs, each lit along its inner edge, recede in an orderly run of arches to a faint warm glow at the far end. Below the springing of the vault, both walls are lined with tiered shelves of sealed reliquary cases. They are bronze with bone lids and a carved bone band, and each carries a small cold blue indicator light. Thousands of them shrink into the distance on six tiers a side. A narrow aisle runs down the centre, lit by a single row of small warm floor lamps, each throwing a little oval of light. In the middle distance the Commando stands tiny in the aisle in his red tabard, lamp raised toward the cases on his left, reading a label. Vast, orderly, quiet.

How it was painted: the tunnel method for the last time tonight. I laid a dark ground with a warm far glow and the aisle first. Then came the ribs as arches built from short reloaded segments, the vault seams, the shelf boards running to the vanishing point, and the cases. Each case is three strokes (a body, a bone lid, a carved band) plus a light, placed in true perspective, all on dry paint. Strokes rather than masks kept every chunk under two minutes. The indicator lights first came out warm white and had to be restated in cerulean and zinc to read cold. What falls short: the rib legs run dry and taper into streaks down the walls, and the floor pools are striped. The Commando's lamp is a small starburst again, and he stands in the aisle rather than right at a case. The cases near us are a little too uniform. The vault's upper surface between ribs is flat dark.
