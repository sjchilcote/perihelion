# The Well

We look straight down a vertical shaft through the TESTAMENT's hub from a narrow bronze catwalk at the top. Gallery after gallery descends in rings that crowd together toward a tiny white-gold glow at the bottom: the core. Each ring is a balcony ledge with a dark balustrade, a lit lip and a string of small warm lamps. Dark doors are set into the wall between galleries. The core's light climbs the walls in warm radial strokes, stronger the deeper the gallery, while the near walls fall into darkness. A bronze spiral stair winds down the shaft wall, ring after ring. A dark octagonal cargo lift hangs in the middle distance on four cables that rise past us out of frame. In the immediate foreground are the Commando's armoured boot-tips at the catwalk's lit edge over the drop, and his gauntlet gripping the rail at the lower right, with a scrap of red tabard.

How it was painted: the tunnel method turned on its end. I laid fourteen perspective sections first on a value ramp from dark (near) to the glowing core, then the galleries, doors, lamps, stair, lift and light, all on dry paint. One chunk that built every gallery from full-canvas masks ran past ten minutes and was rolled back. I redid the galleries as segmented ring strokes, reloading for each arc, after a single full-circle stroke had run dry within a quarter turn. What falls short: the doors read as seed or thorn shapes because the brush tapered them. The core-light strokes read partly as spikes rather than a soft wash of light. The lift platform is a flat dark octagon, a little too big and too plain. The foreground boots and gauntlet are crude and do not convincingly read as his. The vertigo comes mostly from the rings, not from the foreground framing.
