# The Starry Corona

After van Gogh's "The Starry Night". The sky is the Sun's corona and the streamers of the solar wind: thousands of thick, short, directional strokes in ultramarine, cobalt, cerulean and white, all following one flow. Three great spiralling eddies turn in white, pale blue and lemon yellow across the middle of the sky. Eight haloed stars ring out in concentric strokes around yellow cores, and the Sun in the upper right, where van Gogh's moon is, is an orange-gold disc of circling strokes in a white halo. Below roll dark blue hills, laid in strokes that follow their slope. In the valley the ring lies low and dark like the village, its windows small and warm and quiet, small dark roofs above it, and the reliquary's chapel spire rising thin among them. In the left foreground the TESTAMENT's spine stands as the cypress: a dark green-black flame-shaped tower of upward-curling strokes rising into the swirl.

How it was painted: 6 chunks, 4:3, on a mid-blue ground. After a broad underlayer laid along the same flow field, the sky is about 5,600 hand-placed dashes. Each is a short three-point filbert stroke that bends with a flow field: gentle waves plus three vortex eddies. Its colour depends on whether it falls in an eddy. The stars and Sun are rings of short arcs. Their first cores went green, because yellow touched into wet blue mixes, so I restated them opaque on drier paint. The Sun's first restatement was a flat graphic disc, and circling strokes over it brought it back into the handling. The cypress is about 700 upward-curling strokes, widened in a second pass. Short of the brief's upper range: the cypress is more mottled green-brown than van Gogh's black flame, the village is small and reads mostly as a ring of lights, and the hills' strokes are a little checkered.
