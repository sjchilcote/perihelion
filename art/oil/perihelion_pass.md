# Perihelion

This is the closest pass. The TESTAMENT is inside the corona, seen from slightly behind and above. The Sun is beyond the right edge, and everything there is white plasma. The sail stands ahead of the station as a vast dark oval shield, and its rim burns white where the full Sun strikes it. Behind it, the shield casts a long band of shadow that streams away to the left. The plasma inside it runs dark, from near-black by the sail to a deep crimson downstream, and the band thins and dissolves at its edges into the streaming corona. Inside that shadow the station sits as the only still, dark shape in the picture: a stepped spindle of tiers widening to a great ring amidships. Its exposed lips glow cherry-red, hottest on the leading tiers and fading into shadow toward the stern. A few plates tear off the leading corners and stream back as bright sparks. All around, the corona rushes past as a storm of white, gold, orange and red streamers, all running with the flow. The picture is meant to feel sublime, terrifying and survivable.

**How it was painted.** It took 20 chunks on a warm orange ground. My first lay-in made the shadow a solid black cone, and painting crimson streaks into it only made a brown solid. I threw that design out. Instead I repainted the whole field as about 1,700 long streamer strokes running with the flow. Each took its colour from a seven-step ladder, from near-black up to white-hot, chosen by where the stroke lies: deep in the shadow band, in its widening penumbra, or out in the light, which grows hotter toward the Sun. The shadow then became paint moving with the plasma rather than a cut-out shape. The sail's rim is a white crescent eating into the black edge, with an orange fringe and glare strokes bleeding off it with the flow. The station was redrawn as a crisp stepped silhouette. Its glowing lips first came out as an even red outline, so I broke them: runs knocked back into black at the trailing end, hot dashes on the leading tiers.

**Still short:** the remains of the first cone's slanted edges can still be faintly seen at the upper and lower left. The station's glow still reads a little as an outline rather than as heat. The peeling plates are small.
