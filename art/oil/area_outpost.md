# Coolant Row, Two Panes and Three

*Establishing shot for the MIRIAD outpost (first stratum): oil on simulated linen, 16:9, 65 chunks.*

We look down COOLANT ROW, a one-point corridor of the dead outpost. On the left is a march of identical gray prefab pump modules: the same louvred vent, the same printed placard and the same amber-and-black hazard band, module after module, with coolant risers climbing to conduits along the ceiling. The third unit is a mismatched olive replacement with a "3" painted on by hand. The right wall was never finished: it is the Testament's own bronze scale-plate hull, warm and impossibly old. Gray struts and cable rails are bolted straight over it, and a patched prefab panel carries a printed machine-language placard plus a red arrow somebody painted on with a brush. The power is gone. The only light is a ceiling viewport full of Sun, which drops a hard white patch on the deck. There is no air to carry a beam, so the shaft shows only as a drift of frozen-coolant glints. Bounce from that patch catches the lowest bronze scales nearby. At the far end, under a hanging wayfinding board and inside a hazard-striped hatch lit by an emergency lamp, one of the outpost's own security machines stands looking back with a single cold eye. Torn cables sag from the ceiling, one fixture hangs loose, rime has spread where a pump leaked, and a cargo case sits abandoned in the foreground. The one wrong thing is for the attentive eye only: the viewport has two panes, and the light it throws on the floor has three.

How it was painted: I set up the corridor as five perspective planes from a small projection helper, then built every module, strut, placard and stripe as a clipped mask in that space and filled it with real passes, keeping the darks lean (bone black, ultramarine or ash, a little burnt sienna). The paint surprised me in several ways. Dark piles laid over still-wet gray came out lighter and marbled, so I had to wait for layers to set before restating them. A lead-white veil for the light shaft came out as an opaque slab even at low load. I lifted it with clean scumble passes and repainted the planes behind it, which turned out to be the right call physically as well, since a beam has no air to show in. A warm "bounce pool" on the floor dried as a hard ochre puddle and had to be painted out. The biggest single improvement came near the end: one dark glaze over the whole dry canvas, loaded heavier away from the light and then blended twice inside the mask. It left grime-veins that read as wear and pulled the values together around the sun patch and the amber hatch. Next time I would lay the room darker from the start and keep the light-gray panels smaller, so the two light sources have less competition.

## Second pass

The director asked for the dead outpost to be truly dark, with most of the room in the bottom third of the value range. Only three things should give light: the sun patch, the amber hatch with its security machine, and the one live status lamp. v1 is kept as final_v1.png. In v1 the room sat at a uniform mid-dark, with a median gray of 54/255 and almost everything between 40 and 70. In v2 the median is 38, and 98% of the picture is below 85/255. The squint now reads as the viewport, the patch and the hatch, and nothing else.

How it went: first a near-black glaze, loaded more heavily the farther a spot was from the three lights, then blended twice. It only took the room down to a uniform, veined 42. Next, an opaque near-black body pass, weighted by the same distance, overshot badly: the whole room fell into a void, and the pumps, the olive "3" and the patched panel disappeared. So I brought the structure back out of the dark as bounce-lit planes. Each pump side that faces the corridor near the sun patch was repainted as two panels split at its existing seam, with the lower panel lighter because it is closer to the lit deck. On those sides I restated the louvres, seams, hazard stripes, the dim printed placards, the hand-painted 3, and a hairline on each module's corner that brightens toward the floor. The panel on the hull went back as dark slate with its placard and a dim red painted arrow. The live lamp got a hot bead, a small warm halo, and a lit lip on the dead screen above it. Around the sun patch I added the deck-plate seams and the amber deck dashes as thin lit lines, and a soft glow on the deck that I lifted and blended down from a failed starburst of strokes. The far end is now black except for the hatch. Two lessons: any lead white in a "dim" mix still jumps out against near-black, so the bounce piles had to be nearly white-free. And darkening by glaze only flattens the room; giving it real darkness took opaque paint, then putting back only what the light would actually reach.
