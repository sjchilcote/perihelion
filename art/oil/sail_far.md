# The Sail, from a Mile Off

The Sun fills the left edge of the picture. It is a white-hot disc with a warm limb, ringed by a streaked cream corona and then a step of paler blue before the night begins. Across the dark to the right hangs the TESTAMENT's new solar sail, fully spread for the first time: a huge, thin oval mirror ruled with radial and concentric panel seams and edged by a hoop. Its rim is lit on the side facing the Sun, and a dark lower lip shows how thin it is. At the centre of the mirror, the Sun's own image burns as a pale starburst. From there a straight beam of reflected light is thrown back across space, a widening shaft running to the lower-left corner. Below the sail, seven tethers converge on the station itself: a small bronze ring and spine, lit on the Sun side, with warm windows and a little verdigris. It is tiny beside the thing it built. Everything else is plain dark sky with sparse stars.

I decided the sky first this time: a dark ultramarine and bone-black ground serves as the whole sky, so no big sky pass was needed. The Sun went in as three opaque zones on that dry ground, and the sail as one large body pass. I worked the sail's value down away from the reflection with a mid-silver scumble and blended that seam into a glow. Seams, rim, blaze, tethers, the station and the stars all went onto dry paint. The corona is where it went wrong. Low-load glazes, meant to read as light in air, came out as a broad streaky fan that swallowed the sky around the sail. I cut it back by repainting the dark sky over it, which left a hard outer edge. A transitional band laid to soften that edge became a third flat ring. The result is a stepped, graphic halo rather than a falloff. Next time I would paint the corona as a few opaque value steps on dry paint, check each one, and stop. I would also make the sail sheet darker overall, as a mirror reflecting black space, so that the blaze and the hoop carry the light.

## Pass at 01:05 CDT, 2 October

Chunks 15–22 changed the following:
- **Sail.** It is now a dark mirror. I repainted it opaque in a near-black blue, the black of space it reflects. It warms to a sheen and then a pale glow toward the Sun's reflection, and narrow radial blends join the three zones. On dry paint I then added a white-hot blaze touch with a specular streak along the film and a cross-flare, faint pale seams catching light, a hard bright hoop on the sunward rim, and a faint cool edge on the far rim.
- **Corona.** I repainted it as short radial strokes graded in five piles, from warm cream at the limb out to the sky colour, and blended it along the radius only.
- **Beam.** It is now strokes along its own length inside a crisp-edged wedge. It is white-hot at the mirror, fades through warm and cool grays as it widens, and is blended along its length.
- **Station.** It is repainted in bronze (bone brown, burnt and raw sienna), with a pale bronze lit rim on the sunward side, verdigris, and larger warm windows. The tethers and stars were restated.

Still short:
- A soft step remains where the corona meets the sky, about 300 units out from the limb. A second softening band, mixed partly from the sky blue, dried lighter than the sky and made a new hard ring. I painted it back to sky, which left the earlier step.
- The radial blend softened the Sun's limb, so the disc no longer has a crisp edge.
- The seams on the sail read a little too evenly, like a globe grid.
- The windows are too small to read at full view.
