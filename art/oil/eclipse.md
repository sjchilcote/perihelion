# The Sail Eclipses the Sun

The view is from the deck of a MIRIAD supply tug standing off from the station, far off. The sail passes exactly across the Sun and makes a perfect dark disc, ringed by the corona's full crown: long pearly streamers flung out in every direction, dense and white at the rim and thinning into the dark. A few small red prominences flicker at the disc's edge, and the stars have come out in the sudden dark. Below the sail, the TESTAMENT hangs small and dark on its tethers: a spindle with its ring edge-on, outlined in pearly light. In the foreground lies the tug's prefab deck, cool grey panels in a strict grid with amber hazard markings along its edge. On it, two small crew children in suits stand with their backs to us, holding hands and looking up. Their helmets catch the corona's light, and their long shadows run back across the deck. The mood is reverent.

**How it was painted.** It took 13 chunks on a blue-black ground. The corona is about 500 strokes running out from the disc. The long streamers were laid in two segments with fresh paint, the longest along a tilted axis, with a dense ring of short bright strokes hugging the rim. Then I restated the disc in solid black. What went wrong:
- The first TESTAMENT was too small and vanished among the streamers. I painted it larger and drew its ring in plainly.
- The first children were grey on the grey deck and read as nothing. I painted them out and drew them larger, so their helmets rise against the night sky.

**Still short:**
- The prominences are small.
- The corona has no soft pearly glow between its strokes.
- Faint ghosts of the first children show on the deck beside the second.
- Canon note: the viewpoint was first briefed as the MIRIAD outpost's hull, but the outpost is bolted onto the TESTAMENT. It is now the deck of a MIRIAD supply tug standing off from the station.
