# Both Factions Look Up

The Sun takes up the upper-right corner as a white-hot rim with a frayed, rayed corona around it, and the rest of the sky deepens to an ultramarine night scattered with stars. Small against all that space, the TESTAMENT hangs in the corona's blue fringe. Its new solar sail is spread in a pale gold oval, rimmed white on the side facing the Sun, and the bronze ring of the station hangs below it on tethers, a few windows warm. Faint, broken rings spread out from it across the sky: the two witnesses' testimony going out. Below, two fleets hang motionless across an empty gap. On the left in the dark are MIRIAD's gray, square-cut hulls with amber bands. On the right in the bluer light are the Church's crimson cruciform ships with bone cross-arms and spire prows. Their lance and gun barrels lie cold along their flanks, and every ship, near and far, has turned its nose toward the same small light.

I painted it fast and broadly. The sky went in as concentric zones of five blue-to-warm piles laid in long strokes around the Sun. I had to repaint the outer field darker, and narrow radial badger passes over the seams turned the hard bands into the frayed corona, which I kept. The ships come from two small helper functions, a MIRIAD hull and a Church cruciform, called with a fresh position and scale for each ship. Each ship is aimed at the station, and its sunward side is lit with a hairline rim. The paint caught me out twice. A thin warm glaze laid as a halo around the sail on still-tacky sky broke into a ring of speckles. Later, a `lose` pass meant to soften the halo's edge on dry paint broke into dark thorny streaks. Both times I had to repaint the area opaque with the sky pile. The halo is now a broad, soft, slightly lighter disc. Next time I would let the sky dry fully before any glaze and build the glow from opaque value steps. I would also spend more time on the ships, which are still diagrammatic up close.

## Pass at 00:37 CDT, 2 October

Chunks 12–24 changed the following:
- **Sky.** I painted out every old ship, then laid a band of short strokes in four blues (corona blue to night) across the old hard boundary, choosing the blue for each stroke by its distance from the Sun with some randomness. A tangential badger pass followed. The night now falls off gradually into the corona's blue instead of breaking at a toothed edge. The cost is that the middle of the sky is lighter and more mottled than before.
- **Testimony rings.** Most of the rings are painted out. Fragments survive of the two inner rings only, fading outward. A faint dark ghost of an outer ring is left on the left, where a covering stroke came out darker than the sky.
- **The TESTAMENT.** I repainted it at about twice the size. It has a dark bronze ring band, lit warmer on the side facing the Sun, with a crisp sunward rim, a hub, a spine running through it, verdigris on the shadow side, a row of warm windows, and tethers up to the sail.
- **Ships.** I repainted all 22 from scratch. Each pile was laid as one pass over the union of every ship's parts, which was much faster than painting part by part. The nearest three in each fleet are larger and carry more detail: seams, window rows, an antenna, signage, and cold barrels along the dark flank. The far ships use piles grayed toward the sky blue. Every ship has a lit edge on its sunward side, a dark edge on the other, and a faint cold blue-white drive light at the stern. The Church hulls are solid crimson with bone cross-arms, and the stripes are gone.

Still short of what I wanted:
- Rigger lines laid on the Church hulls while they were still tacky (cadmium red and rose madder dry slowly) broke into small white ticks beside the near ships. I painted most of them out with sky, which leaves a soft shadow beside those hulls, and a few remain on the nearest ship.
- The mottled middle sky is busier than the quiet field it replaced.
- The corona's own frayed outer edge is unchanged.
