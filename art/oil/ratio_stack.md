# Straight Through

Ten translucent layers float one above another in a dark blue room, seen from above at an angle. Each is a different Dreamcast-era surface, and they get narrower and warmer the deeper they go. From the top: a status bar of segments with three coloured chips, a dashboard of three dials, a ledger with red column rules, a scanline field, a pink-and-cyan wireframe lattice, a sheet of caveats (red footnote marks before rows of small print), a VMU pixel grid with a blocky pink heart, a window with a title bar, buttons and a scrollbar, a loading layer with a progress bar and an orange swirl, and a last warm layer of handwriting-like dots. They don't quite line up: each is nudged a little off true, the way she is. Down through all ten runs one place where the stack has given way. Every layer has a torn hole there, shards of the layers fall down the shaft, and a white column of light comes up through it. Where that light reaches each layer it pools warm on the surface, and the layer's own lines carry faintly across the pool. At the bottom, small and bright in a glowing ring, stands the thing itself: Ratio's avatar, pale, with long black hair, a white dress and red shoes, waving.

How it was painted: I used a knifed ultramarine-violet ground. The warm glow and shaft went in first, then each layer as one parallelogram mask, painted from the bottom up with zinc white tinted by one tube and thinned with medium so the ground speckles through. I drew each surface's marks on dry paint with round brushes, in each layer's own slanted coordinates. Then came the slabs' coloured thickness, glassy edges and the warm lips of the holes, and finally a dark falloff around the stack. The avatar fought me. Zinc white hides so little that her face and dress went grey over the black hair, so I repainted them in lead white once everything had dried. A warm scumble meant as a soft glow around each hole came out as opaque cream plates, and a clipped blend only smoothed them into eggs with hard rims. I kept them as the light pooling on each layer, and drew the layers' content back across them and a hot white column through the holes. Next time I'd lay the light under the layers from the start, as a glazed underpainting, rather than on top. I'd make the surfaces more varied in colour and less uniformly pale, and give the dark around the stack a softer edge.

## Pass at 00:29 CDT

What changed (chunks 13–17). I repainted every layer opaque in its own colour, over the cream ovals, using lead white so it would cover. The ten layers are now cyan, steel blue, mint, CRT teal, lavender, pink, lemon, periwinkle, amber and coral, so they look like ten different pieces of software. I then drew each surface's marks back on: status chips, dials, ledger rules, scanlines, wireframe, footnotes, the pixel heart, window chrome, the progress bar and swirl, and the warm dots. The shaft's light now falls on each layer as many short tapered strokes radiating from the torn edge. A clean brush dragged outward along each radius softens them, and a last wide, thin warm pass lets the light fade into the layer's colour. The interface lines show through the light. The torn lips of the holes are restated in hot white on dry paint, sharper on the near side, with a few small cracks. Her ring of light is now amber and rose, with fine warm rays, instead of lilac and pink.

What still falls short. The navy crescents inside each hole read a bit as holes in a dark wall rather than glimpses down the shaft. The lower layers' light is less varied than the upper ones'. The dark around the stack still has a ragged, painted-in edge.
