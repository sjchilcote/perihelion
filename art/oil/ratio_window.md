# Ratio Looks Out

The pair to *Where Ratio Lives*, in the same room: the same one-point geometry, pastel walls, cream-and-teal checkered floor, slab-book shelf, faceted lilac beanbag (empty now), and the beige CRT on its low table, still showing the Commando's helm. But the window no longer shows a pixel sky. Through the camera feed he has given her, it shows the real outside, painted in a completely different manner: loose, dark, oil-thick strokes. Black space at the left, the Sun's corona swirling round a white-hot disc, and the curve of the TESTAMENT's bronze hull below, its scale-plates catching the light, with a hot line along its limb. Ratio stands at the window with her back to us, her arms up and her palms flat on the glass. She is still low-poly, but the real light is on her: a warm rim down the sunward edge of her faceted black hair, on her raised sleeves and on her hands. That light falls into the flat room as a warm skewed pool across the floor, and her flat cool shadow runs back toward us through it. The fog in the far corner has retreated to a thin dither, and the scanlines run over the room but stop at the glass.

How it was painted: the room was rebuilt from the first picture's own geometry and palettes, every facet a clipped flat fill. The window alone was painted the other way, with broad and body strokes laid wet into wet along the corona's swirl, a blend through the glow, and plate lips stroked on the hull, so the two kinds of image meet at the mullions. The paint pushed back on Ratio. My first figure was too small and its arms were lost behind the hair, so I painted a larger one over it, arms clear, and painted the old hands out of the sky. Black hair laid over her wet white skirt came out grey-blue twice before it covered, after days of drying. Still weak: the warm pool turned the teal tiles olive, a little of that grey still shows at the hem of her hair, and the shadow under the beanbag is a separate flat shape as in the first picture.
