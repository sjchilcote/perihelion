# Impression, Perihelion

After Monet's "Impression, Sunrise". A hazy field of close blue-greys, painted in quick, broken, visible strokes. The sky, made of slanting dabs, warms to peach and pale gold round a small hot vermilion Sun. Below a soft horizon, the hazy middle ground (light on the sail's film) runs in horizontal strokes of slate, blue-green and pale sage, darkening toward us. A broken streak of orange reflection breaks up and scatters down from beneath the Sun. Seen at a distance through the haze, the TESTAMENT's spine stands like a mast at the left, ringed by its thin grey-blue ring, with low structures at its foot like a quayside. Fainter spars rise like cranes on the right. In the foreground, small and dark, the CUCULUS rides like Monet's rowboat, with a hunched pilot and an oar, and a fainter second craft lies further off.

How it was painted: 5 chunks, 1.3:1, on a pale blue-grey ground. My first round of free strokes left the ground showing between separate dabs, so I laid a full broad underlayer wet and then about 800 more quick filbert strokes into it (slanting in the sky, horizontal below), which gave the broken, close-valued Monet surface. The silhouettes went in wet, so they soften a little. The Sun, its reflection and the boat went on top. The first reflection was too regular a ladder of dashes, so I broke it with wider, uneven slashes. My first boat read as a fin, and a hunched pilot and an oar turned it into a rowboat. The spine and ring are a little too thin and crisp for Monet's mist, and the Sun could be a touch larger.
