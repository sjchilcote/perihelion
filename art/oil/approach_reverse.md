# Someone Is Coming

This is the reverse shot of "Last Minutes of the Approach", seen from inside the TESTAMENT at the one window that faces the Sun. It is the Last Morning's window again: a tall round-headed bronze opening with golden-angle spiral tracery in its arch, set in a dark wall. Through it the Sun stands high in deep blue-black space, its corona thrown out in long gold strokes. Below it the station's own ring curves across the bottom of the window, studded with tiny warm windows, as in the approach painting. Crossing the edge of the glare is a tiny blue-white spark trailing a thin line: the CUCULUS's braking burn, the first visitor in a million years. A docent stands at the sill. It is a tall, slender bronze machine, robed in silhouette, with its back to us and its gold rim catching the light. Its single lens holds a pinpoint of the spark, and its hand is raised flat against the glass beside it. The cold spark against all that gold is the hope and the dread at once.

**How it was painted.** It took 16 chunks on a warm dark ground, reusing the Last Morning's window code for the frame, plates and tracery, and its corona method: about 260 long strokes running out from the Sun, laid on dry paint. What went wrong:
- The first halo bands came out grey and blotchy, and had to be painted back to deep space before the rays went on.
- The yellow glow glaze dried as a round, olive-crackled disc. I broke its edge with dark-blue strokes running outward between the rays, but the crackle is still visible inside the glow.
- The frame and the outside ring came out too light and needed a second, darker coat.
- The spark first vanished into the glare and had to be restated larger, with a cross-flare and a cool halo.
- The docent's first silhouette had square shoulders, which a sloping mantle softened.

**Still short:** the plates on the frame read as dotted beads, the glow has the olive crackle, the spark is small at full view, and the docent's lower body is a plain dark column.
