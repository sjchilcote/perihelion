# Ratio Speaks

A minute after Terminal 0, in the same corridor, at the same terminal and from the same viewpoint. The Commando has typed, and the green protocol header has scrolled away; only its last line clings to the top edge of the CRT. On the screen now is a small window, and in it Ratio's low-poly avatar looks out: pale flat-shaded face in two planes, long black faceted hair, big dark eyes with square highlights, mouth open mid-word, one hand raised in a tentative wave. Below the window her words run in warm white, still framed by the Commando's amber braces. Her light is a soft warm white-pink, not green: it tints the housing's rim, the tops of the keys, the faint spill on the wall and floor, and the edges of the gauntlet, which has stopped just above the keyboard. On the wall, beside the hand-painted red arrow, there is now a second, smaller arrow in the same red, with its own drips, that nobody drew.

I rebuilt the scene by re-running my own Terminal 0 chunks with the same coordinates, swapping every green pile in the light model for warm rose-madder piles and leaving out the old text. Then I painted the window and the avatar on dry paint, polygon by polygon, as flat facets, the way her Dreamcast-era model would render. The paint surprised me once: the skin laid over the still-wet hair came up grey and speckled. It took two more passes on dry paint, with a firmer filbert, to get clean flat skin. Still short: the warm spill on the wall around the housing is blotchy, and the side corridor is still schematic.
