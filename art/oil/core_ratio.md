# Days Alone

The outpost's hardened core is the last room with power. On the left a bank of identical
server modules in a cool gray MIRIAD frame holds rows of status LEDs. Most of them are dark,
and a scattered few still burn green or amber. On the right is the round blast door, bolted
and frosted along its upper rim. Its lower half is blackened by fire, and pale claw gashes run
through the soot where something tried to cut its way in. Frost hangs at the top edge of the
vault. Between the rack and the door stands a maintenance terminal. Its small amber screen is
the brightest thing in the room. On it, under a header, Ratio keeps a ledger of tally marks:
the days she has been alone. The screen warms the dark wall behind it and throws a faint
rippled reflection onto the floor plates. The eye goes to that one warm window in a cold, dead
building.

How it was painted: I used 24 chunks on a dark, knifed ground of bone black, ultramarine and
burnt sienna. I blocked in the big shapes first, then cut the module grid in by working dark
faces inside a frame. The screen, LEDs and tally marks went on after the paint below had
dried. The big surprise was the glow. A low-load lead-white and Mars-orange glaze over the
dark laid down opaque yellow streaks. I tried to blend them, and the badger dragged them into
hard-edged clouds, so I had to let everything dry and repaint the middle third of the picture
in opaque colour. In the end I painted the light as warm-dark colour (a warmer wall pile
around the terminal, a dark ochre pool on the floor) instead of glazing it. That worked much
better. Stippled soot on the door read as gravel until a scumble pulled it together. Claw marks
done with a rigger over the dry scorch worked on the first try. Next time I would plan the
warm air from the start as graded opaque piles, and keep the blender off anything larger than
a seam.

## Second pass

At the director's note I repainted the top band. The vault ceiling is now opaque dark above a
hard steel lip, and only a thin rime of pale crystals hangs along that edge, where before a
cloud-like frost band filled it. Faint warm rigger lines now pick out the right edge of the
nearest rack column and the left rim of the blast door, so the screen's light reaches them.
