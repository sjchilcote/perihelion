# The Rack

The rack runs down the inside of the CUCULUS seed, a ribbed, hot tube of a room lit from one side by the fabricator's mouth. In that glow the next body is being printed bottom-up, its legs done, the print head laying the hip. The spare frames hang from a single beam in the order the mind will use them, and you read the row from left to right. Nearest is frame I: the Church soldier, ivory armour, bowed helm with a cruciform visor, a crimson cloth thrown over the hook and a rosary of bone beads running round the helm to a small brass cross. Frame II has a sensor housing where the head was, a mast, a second pair of arms (a long barrel on one, a three-fingered claw on the other) and reverse-jointed legs, and it still wears a cross, though the paint is worn. Frame III is centauroid, slung by two chains, with a cannon along its back, a vestigial torso at the front and only a rag of crimson left at its waist. Frame IV is a hunched barrel on six legs, bristling with masts and guns, with a cold violet organ grafted into its flank. Past the last rib of the hull hangs something with no number: bigger than it should be at that depth, its masts like antlers, a cluster of pale lenses, bruised organ-light, and arms that hang down past where the floor ought to be. The light gets colder and the votive marks thin out down the row until there are none. An old helmet lies on the floor in the foreground.

How it was painted: 45 chunks on a warm umber ground over a pale knifed priming. The room went in as dark perspective planes, then the fabricator glow and its halo, then the frames, each built from tapered limb ribbons with a lit flank, a shadow flank and a hot rim, and then dried and reworked. The paint kept surprising me in the same way. Dark mixes laid thin over dark came out lighter and warmer than they were on the palette (one floor pass went caramel), and only bone-black-heavy piles at heavy coverage gave real darks. Scumbles at low load still went down as opaque slabs. My worst mistake was masking a scumble and a `lose()` by "the wall", a region that contained the nearest figure. The figure was buried and I had to repaint it from its own code. Next time I would subtract every figure from every room mask from the very first chunk. I would also build the room's light falloff in one graded passage before the frames, not patch it in zones afterwards, and model the frames more with strokes than with half-plane value masks, which make them look like wooden mannequins.

## Second pass

The director wanted the frames to read as machined war-bodies instead of mannequins, so I re-armoured them in chunks 46–54. Every armour plate on frames I and II is now its own small shape, painted from the back of the body to the front. Each one gets a dark thickness edge that falls on whatever is behind it, a midtone, a lit crescent toward the fabricator and a core shadow on the far side, all fused wet inside the plate. I then put the core shadow and the light back narrower, added a warm bounce line on the shadow edge and a hot rim on the lit edge. Frame I now has stacked three-lame pauldrons, a cuirass of two pectoral plates meeting at a keel, overlapping belly lames and faulds, knee and elbow cops over exposed steel pistons, segmented gauntlets, umbilical cables from the yoke into the back of the helm, scorch stippling and bright scratches. The helm was restated in front of the crimson cloth, and the rosary was laid back over it. My first plates came out pale and pillowy: the badger evened everything to a midtone, and the shadow pile dried light over wet ivory. The fix was to rebuild each frame's plate shapes as one mask, without painting, and lay an opaque warm-black shadow down the right of every plate after a day's drying. Frame II got the same plating in cooler, dimmer gunmetal with a cold black shadow side, and kept its worn cross. Frames III and IV got more machinery with warm rims so they read against the dark: an over-under gun pair, a ventral cannon, clawed manipulators and a dish mast on III, and a rotary gun, clawed arms and masts rimmed warm on one side and cold on the other on IV. The plates still have softer, rounder corners than I'd like, and the rim lines on III's legs ended up a few units off the legs, so they read as ghost edges.
