# The Ambassadors (after Holbein)

Two living Testators stand full length on either side of a two-tier shelf, envoys of their civilisation before it died. Behind them hangs a green damask curtain, and at its top-left edge it parts on a small bronze fold-glyph, as Holbein's curtain hides its crucifix. The left envoy wears crimson with a broad fur stole and a bronze chain with a fold medallion. The right envoy wears violet-black with a flat bone collar, a row of bronze studs and a gold hem. Each rests a many-jointed bronze hand on the shelf's patterned cloth. Their heads are long skulls of living bone plates, with arcs of small hooded eyes and closed pleated mouths. The shelf carries their instruments of learning. On the upper tier: an orrery of the Sun, its outer orbit carrying a tiny ring-and-spine, the TESTAMENT; a bronze astrolabe with its scale and rete; and a sealed reliquary case with a bone panel and a red seal. On the lower tier: a bone stringed instrument with one broken string curling off its body, a fold-notation tablet and a closed book. The floor is an inlaid pavement of roundels and lozenges, crossed by the envoys' shadows. Lying across it on a long diagonal is a cold shape in inverted greys and violet, smeared out to the right, with an edge catching the window. From the front it reads only as an unaccountable slab. Seen from the low left side, along its axis (see anamorph_side_view.png, made from the painting by foreshortening it), it resolves into the Adversary's fold: a hexagon folding into a smaller turned copy of itself, five deep, down to a point.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 33 chunks, building in layers over dry paint. The anamorph is the fold glyph laid out in a frame stretched 4.5 times along a diagonal, so it is a drawn shape, not a distorted image. Each nested hexagon ring and gap is a separate masked passage, painted solid. My first attempt to smear it, with a clean brush dragged along the axis while the paint was wet, wiped it nearly away, leaving streaks. I let that dry and re-laid the whole shape solid, then added a smaller smear only at the far end. Each time, I checked the result by foreshortening the saved canvas along the axis. The curtain's first pass was loud stripes. I toned the light bands down, and the damask came out as dust-specks until I laid it as masked motifs: a lozenge holding a smaller turned one, the Testator mark. The inlaid floor came out too loud, and the envoys' cast shadows, laid as opaque dark floor with dim lozenges inside, quieted it. Still short of Holbein: the bodies are simple bells of cloth with few folds, and the cast shadows are stiff wedges. The instruments are lively but few next to his crowded shelves.
