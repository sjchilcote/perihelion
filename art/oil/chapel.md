# The Hours, Kept in a Borrowed Chapel

A round Testator chamber deep in the TESTAMENT: bronze ribs rising into a dome
gone dark, a frieze of bone fold-notation glyphs (each a polygon holding a
smaller copy of itself turned by the golden angle), concentric inlay rings at
golden-ratio gaps around an off-centre dais. The MIRIAD crew dragged their gray
modular benches in here — identical legs at identical spacing — because the
room awed them. Now one shaft of sunlight falls from a nested heptagonal
oculus, and the Commando's frame kneels in it, back to us, praying the hours.
On the dais, a cross made from two scraps of outpost metal.

How it was painted: ~48 chunks, by the studio's director, alongside the six
commissioned painters. Mostly a record of learning the paint: unclipped broad
strokes overrun a mask by their own length; a dark glaze laid over a darker
passage comes out *lighter*; a violet glaze meant to neutralise a too-yellow
shaft turned it magenta; the light shaft was painted out and repainted three
times before a low-load blended lead-white veil, warmed with Mars orange, read
as light in air. Rim lights laid into wet bone black dissolve; they stuck after
six days' drying.

## Pass at 23:55 CDT, 1 Oct 2026 (chunks 49–61)

What changed:
- **Dome and ribs.** I repainted the whole wall opaque in four values. It goes near-black up into the dome and warms only in a band along the shaft and around the oculus. The pale cloud-shaped patches round the shaft were knocked back so the warm wall stays close to the beam. The eleven ribs are now dark bronze. The half of each rib turned toward the light got a mid-bronze, and the edge facing the shaft got a lit line whose strength follows the light, from hot near the beam to dim, and nothing high in the dome. The dome now reads as a ribbed cage.
- **Shaft and pool.** I painted out the old grey-bordered, green-yellow shaft and the green halo round the oculus. In their place are lean lead-white veils, then a thin Mars-orange warm veil, blended along the fall with softened edges. It reads as warm light in air, with the ribs showing through. The heptagonal oculus got a bronze frame. A hot white-yellow pool sits at the frame's knees.
- **Floor and rings.** The floor is dark, lit from the pool outward. A quieter round platform holds the inlay rings, which are restated as dark grooves with bone inlay. They are now legible.
- **Frieze.** The frieze is a real dark-bronze course between two lit mouldings. The fold-notation glyphs are restated cleanly: nested polygons, each turned by the golden angle, in bone that brightens near the light.
- **Benches.** They are grey MIRIAD modules. Each has a lit top rail that warms toward the aisle, a dark back turned from the light, panel seams on the module in line with the legs, and a lit aisle end. A narrow warm path runs down the aisle, with the bench ends' shadows darkening its edges.
- **Frame and cross.** The frame now has a helm, pauldrons, a back plate with spine and waist seams, folded feet, rim light from the shaft on the helm and shoulders, and pool light on its soles. The cross is two scraps of metal, lashed, with sunlit edges.
- **Lamp.** I put the dead work lamp back.

What still falls short:
- **Shadows.** The benches don't throw visible cast shadows from the shaft. They stand so close together that no floor shows between rows.
- **Bench backs.** They are blocky in texture.
- **Frieze placement.** The frieze follows `foot()` rather than the actual wall/floor line, so near the centre it arches over the floor.
- **Seam in the shaft.** A faint horizontal seam crosses the shaft where the wall meets the floor.
- **Cross.** It sits in a soft haze where an earlier box was painted out, and its cast shadow was lost.
- **Lamp.** It stands in a dark rectangle.
