# The Ingestion Maw

We look out from inside the receiving chamber, down an enormous ribbed bronze throat that recedes in true perspective. Its ten rings crowd closer and closer together toward the mouth. The walls between them are painted as surfaces: lean dark near us, warming through bronze to a pale gold where sunlight falls on the lower-left wall near the opening. Every rib carries a row of grinding teeth, bronze plates each the size of a house. Each plate is lit on one face, with a dark seam on the other. In the mouth, a faceted chunk of comet ice nearly fills the circle of black space. It is pale blue-white on the facets turned to the Sun and deep blue in shadow, scored with crevasses and glittering. Four heavy braided cables run from the throat walls to the ice to draw it in. White gas streams off it and out of the mouth. The Sun sits just past the rim. One hard white edge lights the rim's teeth on the right, and glare spikes cross the ice's upper edge. A dark catwalk on two struts crosses the lower foreground. On it the Commando, tiny, with his red tabard, stands at the rail looking up the throat.

How it was painted: this restart followed the plan from the first attempt. I laid the tunnel first as eleven perspective sections, with the spacing compressing toward the mouth. Each wall band got its own pile on a ten-step ramp from lean dark to warm gold. A shadow side and the sunlit sheet came next, then the ribs and teeth ring by ring, from far to near. The ice, cables, gas, sunlit rim, catwalk and figure all went on dry paint as strokes. My first sunlit sheet came out as a wedge-shaped pie slice, and the second as a crescent with hooks. I repainted it until the lit wall matched the bands. The near-frame darkening I laid at the end barely registered. What falls short: the ice is still a little too clean-faceted, like a gem. The upper-right shadow region is blotchy, and the near teeth repeat mechanically. The gas reads as scribbles rather than vapour. Still, the throat now has real depth, and the scale reads from the tunnel itself, not only from the figure.
