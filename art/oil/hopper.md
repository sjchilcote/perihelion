# Nighthawks at Miriad-7

After Hopper's "Nighthawks". The MIRIAD mess late on the night shift, months before the fall, is seen from the dark corridor outside through a long window that runs off the right edge in perspective. Inside, under three hard fluorescent tubes, everything is flat and lit: a pale green-yellow back wall, a cream counter top, an oxblood counter front, a steel dispenser, a coffee urn and a stack of trays. Three crew in navy coveralls do not talk. One sits hunched over a mug, head down. One sits upright with his head turned away toward the dispenser end. Behind the counter a third stands with his back to the others, reaching for the dispenser. Two chrome stool posts show through the lower glass. The window's dark frame and mullions cut the scene, and the light falls out across the corridor's deck grating in a long olive-green trapezoid. Two dark bars cut it, the mullions' shadows, and it ends abruptly in the dark. To the left, in the corridor wall, a round viewport shows black space, a few stars, and the TESTAMENT as a faint bronze arc.

How it was painted: 6 chunks, 16:9, on a dark green-grey knifed ground. I laid flat opaque planes with masks (wall, floor, spill, ceiling, back wall, counter), then the figures as simple navy volumes with lit shoulder tops, then the frame, the mullions and their shadows, and the grating. The first figures read as navy eggs. I squared their shoulders and tapered their backs to the counter by painting the wall colour back in, and restated a head that had smeared into wet paint. The server was first cut in half by a mullion, so I moved him. A stipple meant to soften the floor's dark edge made a speckled band, and a middle-tone step ran over the corridor's shadow wedge. I repainted both as clean flat bands. Short of the brief's 8–20 chunks: the figures are still stiff and doll-like next to Hopper's weighty people, the floor's fade goes in visible steps, and the urn reads a little like a capsule.
