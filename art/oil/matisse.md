# Icarus

After Matisse's "Icarus" from "Jazz". A deep ultramarine sheet, cut slightly crooked, lies on white paper. Through it the Commando dives head-first as a flat black cut-out. His legs are flung up in a V, his arms float out sideways in wavy, flame-like ribbons, and the only colour on him is a crooked red cross on his chest, the tabard's cross as his heart. Below him, cut off by the bottom edge of the sheet, a huge jagged yellow Sun bursts upward. Ten jagged yellow star-bursts of different sizes are scattered around him. Everything is flat colour with crisp, scissor-cut edges and no modelling.

How it was painted: 3 chunks, 3:4. First the blue sheet as one opaque masked field, dried. Then the Sun, stars and figure as flat opaque masked shapes: the bursts are polygons with alternating long and short irregular radii, and the figure is head, body, legs and wavy arms as smoothed polygons. The red cross went on last, on dry paint. This was the quickest of the night, because flat cut-out shapes are exactly what masks and opaque fills do well. It is under the brief's 6–10 chunks, but there was nothing left to add without losing the simplicity. The legs are perhaps a little long and thin next to Matisse's chunkier forms.
