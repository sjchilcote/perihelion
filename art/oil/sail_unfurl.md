# The Sail Unfurls

The view is from low on the TESTAMENT's hull, looking up. Out of a bay on the hull's crest, the solar sail erupts for the first time in a million years. Thirty-four pleated sheets of thin dark mirror-film fan up and out across the black sky until they run off the canvas. Each crease catches the Sun as a long white-gold line, hottest on the folds facing the Sun at the upper left and dimming to a cold grey on the far side. The pleats' tips stand ragged against the stars. Small dark docents stand along the bay's lip with their arms raised, directing it or praying, each with a hairline of light on the side facing the Sun. In the foreground the hull's scale plates are hard-lit, with a bright upper lip and a black shadow under each one. To the right of the bay, the sail's own shadow falls across the hull, flat black, with the plates only glimpsed in the cold light thrown back by the film.

**How it was painted.** It took 11 chunks on a blue-black ground. The darks went first: black sky, dark film, dark bronze hull. The fan was cut into wedge-shaped facets that alternate between two near-blacks. My first facet mixes (with a little white and ochre) came out as pale blue and ochre stripes, like a sunburst flag, and had to be repainted opaque in darks only a step off the sky. The crease lines were laid last on dry paint as long round-brush strokes running out from the bay. The first pass ran dry and faded within a third of the length, so I laid each crease again in segments with fresh paint, so that the light reaches the tips. The plates were laid row by row in perspective with the same body, shadow and lit-lip strokes as in the other paintings. The shadow is opaque black over them, and its edge is notched by the pleats.

**Still short:** the lit half of the hull is a uniform warm orange pattern that could be darker and more varied, the film facets have no sheen of their own between the creases, and the docents are very small at full view.
