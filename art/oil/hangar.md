# The Docking Hall

The TESTAMENT's great docking hall: a vast bronze barrel vault whose ribs recede in lit arches to a far round door open on black space, with a hard white sliver of the Sun at its rim. The glare runs in a pale shaft down the deck toward us. Rows of empty cradles line both sides, each a pair of curved bronze arms big enough for a ship, every one empty. Gantry towers with their jibs stand against the walls. All but one cradle is empty. In the near-left cradle, enormous around it, lies the CUCULUS, a small dark seed-pod hull with warm light on its back and a hatch light. Cables run down to it from the gantry above: the only ship in a million years. The Commando, tiny, walks away from it into the hall with his shadow behind him.

How it was painted: the tunnel method, a last time. I laid ten perspective bands first, horseshoe-shaped for the vault and walls, ramped from lean dark to warm gold, then a separate deck plane. On dry paint came the ribs with lit inner edges, the deck seams, the cradles and gantries placed by depth, the ship and cables, the sun and its glare, the figure, and strokes along the deck to grade its bands. What falls short: the vault bands still read as flat nested arches between the ribs. The near cradle and its ship are a smeary mass, and the ship is too small and too low in the frame to read clearly as the focus. The gantries are plain dark posts, and the dark foreground band is empty. The deck grading strokes look like streaks.
