# Vanitas: the Logbook

A still life in the manner of Claesz and Heda, on the bridge console of the
dead outpost. A single cold light falls from the left, from a heavy round
porthole in the warm-grey wall. Through it we see black space and the
Testament's bronze scale-plate hull curving past, frost rimming the lower
edge of the glass. On the console the crew's paper logbook lies open. The
left page is full of handwriting. On the right page the entry stops
halfway along a line, and the pen lies dropped across it, nib to the
words. A white mug lies tipped on its side, its dark mouth toward us and a
brown stain spreading from it. At the back stands the snuffed candle of the
picture, a dead indicator lamp: a frosted bulb on a dark collar, its
filament cold. In front lie a MIRIAD crew badge, with its photo square and
blue mark, and a wristwatch stopped on its strap. The skull of the picture
is the Commando's small bone cross, set down in front of the book while he
read. Every object throws its shadow to the right. Frost creeps along the
console's front lip, thickest under the window.

## How it was painted

I made it in 9 chunks on a warm-grey ground at 4:3. The paint fought me in
three places:
- The wall's first value bands left hard stripes. A badger over them only
  made wider stripes. On dry paint I repainted it in twelve finely mixed
  steps, from lit warm grey to near-black, laid diagonally, and that gave
  the even fall of window light.
- The pages, laid wet over the wet cover, came out mottled. I repainted them
  on dry paint and then wrote the lines with a fine round.
- A glaze meant as the window's light on the console dried as white streaks,
  so I repainted it as two opaque paler zones with blended edges.
The objects themselves are all built from dried layers: base, shadow side,
lit side, and then details and highlights last.

Still rough: the objects are small on the canvas and drawn plainly, more
diagram than Heda. The pages are too warm and bright for the quiet palette.
The bulb's light and shadow halves meet in a hard line, and the badge is
crude.
