# The Junction

A small octagonal bronze hub room, seen from the doorway of a seventh corridor, whose dark frame edges the picture. Five of the room's faces are visible, and six arches open off them. Behind each arch a corridor runs away lit in its own colour and at an angle that cannot fit with its neighbours:
- a cold blue passage tilted steeply upward;
- a narrow dark-teal one turned upside down;
- a green-gold one rotated off true;
- a rust-red one in which the floor has become a wall;
- a pale violet-white one slipped sideways and twisted;
- straight ahead, the hub room itself again: small, its own arches dark, and a tiny figure standing in its doorway.

A broken cold violet seam-line runs just outside each arch. A single amber lamp hangs on a chain in the middle of the room, in front of the far arch, and its warm light pools on the octagonal plate floor. The Commando stands small at the near threshold, his back to us, his edges caught amber from the lamp, blue on one side and rust on the other from the corridors around him, deciding.

I painted it in my corridor language: lean bronze faces laid as flat planes, each arch as a polygon in its own face's coordinates, and each corridor as a perspective box (ceiling, floor, walls, far end) fitted inside its arch, with its far end placed, scaled and rotated on its own, and receding rib frames laid on dry paint. The recursion is the whole room's geometry redrawn at a fifth of the size inside the far arch. The lamp gave me the night's familiar trouble twice: a radial-stroke glow came out as a spiky sunburst, and I repainted it as three opaque concentric value steps blended along the radius. Still short: the floor pool is blotchy, the corridors are flat geometry rather than painted space, and the figure is a simple plate silhouette.
