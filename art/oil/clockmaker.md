# The Clockmaker

A tall, dim clock-room. High on the back wall hangs the TESTAMENT's great clock, a bronze face nearly the width of the room inside a heavy dark bezel. The face is laid in nested rings with an outer band of fold glyphs (small polygons, each holding a smaller turned copy of itself), a ring of sixty ticks counting down, of which the last few, nearest the top, are lit bright, and a turned-hexagon inset at the hub. Its single hand stands just short of the top: a few marks from perihelion, after a million years. A ladder leans against the wall beside the face with a lantern hooked at its top. That lantern is the only light. It warms the upper right of the face and the wall around it in a soft falloff and lays a pool of light on the floor below. Near the top of the ladder the docent stands in its bronze robe, bone face turned to the clock, both bone hands on the crossbar of a long bronze key set in a keyhole in the face, winding it. The lantern rims its hood, shoulders and the edge of its robe. At the foot of the ladder the Commando stands with his back to us, both hands raised to the rails, holding it steady, and the light from above catches his helm, pauldrons and arms.

**How I painted it.** Light structure first: the wall in three soft steps around the lantern, then the face. My first face was split hard down a diagonal into lit and dark halves, and its ring grooves sank into the wet paint. I repainted it in three graded steps by distance from the lamp, blended, inside a heavy bezel, and on set paint restated the rings with lit lips on the lamp side, the glyphs, the ticks and the hand. Then came the ladder, the lantern, the keyhole, the docent and key, and the Commando. Their edge light went in on dry paint. Still short: the wall glow outside the face reads as a broad blotchy disc, a few dark arcs left from the first ring grooves cross the lower right of the face, the floor pool is an oval, and the key is small for the scale of the face.
