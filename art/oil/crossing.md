# The Crossing

A long Testator corridor runs straight away from us. The near half is ours: dark bronze walls with plate joints, a near-black ceiling, and heavy bronze ribs with thin warm edges. Past a clean, straight seam across the whole corridor, the same corridor continues in cold, inverted colour. Its ceiling is the brightest thing in the picture, its walls a pale blue, and its floor a deeper blue. Its ribs are dark lines now, and each is smaller, turned a little further and slipped a little off-centre, so the corridor folds into ever smaller copies of itself. Across the seam walks the Commando's late frame, a centaur-shaped body. Its rear half, the haunches and hind legs, is still bronze and dark on our side of the seam, rimmed warm, with the torn crimson rag over it. Its upper body and masts are already past the seam and have gone pale and cold, outlined in inverted dark. One band of the torso has slipped sideways, and faint smaller copies of its head and masts are folding away into the tunnel. Where the rag crosses the seam it turns from crimson to cyan. At the near end, on the bronze floor, lies the rosary, set down in a loose loop with its crucifix.

I built it in the corridor language from the earlier paintings, with one one-point perspective scaffold for both halves. The value structure was decided first: a dark warm near half and a bright cold far half, split at the seam. The cold half is three opaque inverted piles, and the folding rings are a sequence of rotated, shrinking, drifting cross-sections drawn as dark strokes. The figure was laid in as two separate parts, the rear half in lean bronze dark and the front half in cold inverted grey, with rims, outlines and the rosary on dry paint. One surprise: the rag's crimson came up speckled over the haunch, so I had to repaint the haunch dark around it. Still short: the frame's upper body reads a little like a mannequin and could use more mechanism, and the ghost copies are faint.
