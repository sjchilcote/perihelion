# The Stolen Sunbeam

The Testament's night side, seen across its curve. Worn bronze scale plates run in golden-offset rows into near darkness under a black, sparsely starred sky. A few warm windows show between the plates. At the right edge the hull turns into the sun: a narrow band of hot bronze, and on it a small dark oculus with eight notches at golden-angle steps and a central boss. Out on the dark plates, far from any light, lies a hard-edged patch of full sunlight in exactly that oculus's shape. The boss shows as a hole of shadow and the notches as teeth of dark. The patch is not laid over the plates like a decal. Its edge steps from row to row, and every plate inside it throws its own short lip shadow and catches sun on its lip, as real light falling on that surface would. Nothing else in the picture is wrong. The light is just somewhere it cannot be.

**How it was painted.** It was a 40-minute piece in 10 chunks. I knifed a dark ground, laid space and the hull in two bands, then painted the limb and terminator in three graded strips and blended them at the seams. The plates are about a thousand lips, each a dark U stroke with a faint warm hairline, with rows spaced to foreshorten toward the limb. A dark glaze in medium over the dry night hull did the job of pushing it into darkness, but it buried the lips and turned violet, so I painted them again on top. The oculus and the patch come from one notched-shape function: once on the limb as a dark aperture, and once as a sunlit patch with a small step at every plate row. I gave the patch two coats over dry paint so the dark could not come up into it, then gave the plates inside it hard lip shadows and lit edges clipped to the patch. That is what makes it lie on the surface. With more time I would soften the hard vertical strips of the terminator into a real curve of light. I would also fix the window rows, which landed mostly off the frame or too small to read, and add a very faint warm bounce from the patch onto the plates around it.

## Second pass

The terminator is no longer a set of vertical stripes. It is now the hull rolling into the light plate by plate. Each plate face is painted in one of six steps from night bronze to sunlit gold, staggered by golden-ratio offsets so the light advances row by row instead of in straight bands. Every plate then got a shadow crescent and a lit edge, and a thin rim of light runs along the horizon, brightest toward the sun. My first try laid a wet graded underlayer and the plate faces dissolved into it. The fix was to let that underlayer set for a painted day and lay the faces on top. Inside the stolen sunbeam, the plates now have the same golden faces and dark crescents as the real sunlit strip, so the two lights match. The plates just outside the patch catch a faint warm bounce. Three rows of larger warm windows at golden-ratio gaps, each with a dim glow, now make the night side read as inhabited. The limb's aperture had to be restated twice, because face strokes ran over it.
