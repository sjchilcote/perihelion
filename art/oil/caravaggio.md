# The Calling

After Caravaggio's "The Calling of Saint Matthew". The dead outpost's mess is dark. From the upper right, a hard diagonal shaft of pale light falls from a terminal screen across the back wall and onto a table, and from the screen Ratio's small pale hand reaches out, index finger pointing straight at the Commando. At the table on the left, the three MIRIAD crew in navy coveralls are frozen. One is hunched over the table, unaware. One turns toward the light with a hand at his chest. One at the end of the table, on a stool, turns his lit profile toward the screen. Among them the Commando's armoured frame sits, its helm raised to the light, its face plate and shoulder lit pale steel. Everything the light touches has a hard edge, and everything else is black. The shadow of a window-frame cross lies on the lit wall, the light's own geometry.

How it was painted: 7 chunks, 1.4:1, on a near-black ground. The beam is one masked polygon. The wall inside it is graded in three warm greys and blended only inside the beam, so the beam's edges stay hard. The figures are flat dark silhouettes, and each lit plane is that silhouette cut by a hand-drawn polygon on the side facing the light, so every light edge is a crisp line. Lights laid on tacky darks mottled and had to be restated after a longer wait. The window-shadow cross first ran over a figure and was painted out. Still short: the figures are pawn-like simplifications rather than Caravaggio's weighty, gesturing bodies, there is little drama in the poses, and Ratio's pointing hand is small and far from the group, so the call reads weakly.
