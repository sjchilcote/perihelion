# The Cable Bursts

The Commando fills the left half of the corridor. He backpedals away from what is chasing him: his far foot is planted behind him, his near knee is lifting, and his torso is twisted so that the chest plate and pauldron face the threat while his hips still point the way he is running. A red crusader's tabard with a grey cross hangs below the heavy housing of the lightning cannon, which is braced at his hip. His visored helm is turned toward the Warden. The cable leaves the muzzle as a dark line and whips up and over in a long arch. Along its length it is already bursting into a string of white-hot plasma stars on a ragged violet sheath, and the bursts grow toward the dart. The dart is a black barb at the end. From it, jagged pilot sparks leap onto the Warden's face, shoulder and reaching arm. The Warden is a hunched bronze Testator mass with a dim bronze nimbus behind it. It is lit hard and warm only on the side facing the discharge, with a bone rosette around its socket, and the rest of it falls away into darkness. Two husks stand between the two fighters, rimmed cold from above, with mites on the deck. The discharge is the only light. It catches every armour plate that faces it, casts a faint pool and a few rib arcs in the tunnel, and leaves the corridor's edges near-black.

How it was painted: as the brief asked, I drew and painted the Commando first. I made a chalk underdrawing on a mid-violet ground, then an underpainting of every armour part. That first pass came out far too pale and even, like a mannequin. I rebuilt him on dry paint: the shadow side in opaque slate (bone black, ultramarine and a touch of Mars brown, no white), and the planes facing the cable as separate lit shapes, each graded with small tapered strokes. Plate seams, lames, knee cops, the visor, rims and glints went on last, over dry paint. The paint surprised me twice. A "neutral dark" scumble with a little zinc white turned the whole shadow side khaki, as the studio notes warn, and I had to repaint it. Later, a vignette pass with `edge="lost"` ran past its clip and buried his back leg, so I repainted that too. On the cable, a violet sheath that I blended came out as a solid purple slug. Lost dark strokes made it thorny. Radiating hot strokes laid over the dry sheath finally gave it the look of bursting plasma. The Warden kept turning into an even gold outline around a dark shape. Breaking the rim low down and adding warm halftone planes near the light helped, but it is still the weakest passage. Next time I'd block the Warden's lit planes as broad shapes from the start instead of a contour band. I'd also give the Commando's lit planes more than three values and keep the blender away from the plasma sheath.

## Pass at 23:53 CDT

What changed (chunks 42–57). **The light now falls on the room.** I repainted the deck as a dark floor with a dim, ragged pool of cold light under the discharge, and added the Commando's and husks' shadows thrown across it. The ribs of the corridor catch the light as continuous bands, strongest nearest the cable and fading away from it, so the tunnel reads as lit by the event. **The husks** are now boxy outpost machines with sensor heads and cold eyes, piston legs and a raised arm, lit on their top faces. Before, they were blobs. **The Warden** is now built from flat bronze facets that turn from a hard-lit pale gold on the near side, through warm halftone, into dark core shadow. It is darker toward the floor. A golden-angle rosette of bone studs sits on its breast, with a line of studs down the arm. A cold, displaced second outline of its head, crown and reaching arm shows the corruption. The nimbus is now a solid bronze band, lit on the side that faces the discharge. **The Commando's lit plates** each run in four steps, wet into wet, from core shadow to a hot edge facing the discharge. Over the dry paint I then restated the seams, a rim that varies with the form, and hard glints.

What the paint did. My first deck pass laid down a pale slab with a hard top edge and a target-like pool, and blending only made it flatter. I buried it under an opaque dark floor and scumbled the pool down. The husks, painted into the still-wet deck, came out pale grey (note 54 again). They needed a third coat over dry paint, and a faint ghost of the old blobs still shows on the deck. Rib arcs made of short segments came out as dashed stitching until one continuous stroke along each arc joined them up.

What still falls short. The Warden's reaching arm is still a thin limb. Its facets read a little as a stack of blocks rather than a hunched body. The ghost contour is subtle. The Commando's gun housing remains a flat dark box.
