# Harmonia Testamenti

A plate after Andreas Cellarius' *Harmonia Macrocosmica*. It is a hand-coloured celestial chart in a red frame ruled in gold, with a row of fold glyphs round the border, each a polygon holding a smaller turned copy of itself. In the pale blue field, deepening to ultramarine at the edges, the Sun sits at one focus: a gold disc with a calm sleeping face and wavy gold rays. Round it the planets' circles are washed in pale ochre, rose, green and lilac, each inked at both edges and carrying its planet. Across them sweeps the TESTAMENT's long orbit, a great gold ellipse inked on both sides and ticked by the day, with a heavier tick every sixth. The station is drawn small as a ring and spine at perihelion, close by the Sun, and again smaller at aphelion at the far end. A dotted line of apsides joins the two. In the four corners, docent cherubs with gold curls and pale wings sit on banks of cloud, each holding out a scroll of abstract lettering. Across the top, a banner carries the plate's title in the same unreadable capitals.

**How it was painted.** In the flat illuminated method of the night. First the frame and sky, with a darker band at the edges as in a hand-coloured plate. Then, on dry paint, the pale planet rings, the Sun, the clouds (a grey-blue underside with white lobes over it), the cherubs, the scrolls and the banner. After drying, every line went on as short segments: inking, the orbit's gold band and its day-ticks, the stations, faces and lettering, and the frame's glyphs. The ink drawn as short segments with small ramps reads a little dashed, like engraving, which suits a printed plate. Next time I would give the cherubs real bodies and drapery, add the zodiac ring Cellarius always draws round the outside, and lay a few hatched shadows on the clouds.
