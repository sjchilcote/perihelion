# The Shrieker

A low-caste Testator turned herald, caught mid-cry where a bronze corridor meets three doorways that will not agree on which way is up. One stands straight ahead, one leans out of the left wall, and one lies on its side high on the right. Its body is a broken church bell: cast bronze, ringed at shoulder, waist and sound-bow, studded with bone in a golden-angle spiral, cracked up from a chipped lip, with a bone clapper hanging beneath. Through its front is cut a person-shaped absence, edged in cold light. On a thin bone neck the mask is thrown back, the mouth a long dark gape lit cold at the rim, and a crown of bronze spikes bristles behind it. The arms are flung up. The cry fills the corridor as broken, notched arcs of fold notation, warm and cold by turns. Where the rings enter a doorway they continue inside it, but turned and darkened, as if the sound came out of another room. Each doorway's light shivers, and in each the summoned are arriving: a tall shape with long hands on the jamb in the centre, thin limbs with splayed fingers reaching out on the left, and on the right another bell, small and dark, with a bone face.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 11 chunks in about 20 minutes of real time. I used a dark, cool-brown ground and laid the corridor in with broad passes in perspective, then filled the three doorways with violet portal air, lighter toward the middle and blended. The cry is round brushes along broken arcs with golden-count notches. Inside each doorway the same arcs are rotated about the door and clipped to it. The paint fought back in three places. Strokes over the dry bell barely showed. A badger blend across the bell's seams flattened it into one ochre cone, so I re-laid it as hard facets (lit flank, sheen, middle, shadow, dark core), which reads better as cast metal. And a low-load violet "spill" on the floor came out as white blotches and had to be painted out. Unclipped surround strokes also crossed the bell, which taught me to clip everything near the figure. If I did it again, I would give the head and hands more drawing and paint the portal glows less saturated and less cloudy. I would also give the corridor real wall ornament rather than plain seams.
