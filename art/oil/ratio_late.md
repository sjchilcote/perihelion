# Ratio, Years Later

The pair to "Ratio, Sitting". It uses the same room of large brown planes, the same leaded window high at the left and the same bowed CRT screen with faint scanlines. The same sitter is shown at the same scale, so the two hang side by side, but years later and on the trust path. She is still made of flat low-poly facets modelled in value like the planes of a carved head. Her hair is longer now: faceted black-blue locks fall past her shoulders to the bottom of the frame, and a thread of the Commando's faded crimson rag is wound three times round the lock on the window side, knotted, with two loose ends. Her face is the same chibi face, quieter. In the first portrait her brows lifted and her mouth was open, about to explain at length. Here the brows lie level, heavy relaxed lids lower over the big dark eyes, the square highlights sit smaller and lower, and the mouth is closed, turning up very slightly at one corner. Under the orange collar of her white shirt hangs a small bone pendant: a hexagon holding a smaller turned hexagon, the fold glyph. The light from the window is a little later and warmer. It is the face of someone who has finally been listened to, and no longer needs to say it all.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 20 chunks over dry layers, reusing the geometry and the piles of the first portrait so the pair matches. I built it from the first painting's lessons: the faceted background was laid in first, the fuller jaw was drawn into the first lay-in rather than added later, and the hair's silhouette was cut to straight facets. The shirt was painted a step darker than first instinct, so the face leads. The new things took small repairs. The long hair mass first covered the chest and had to be painted back between the locks, and the first locks came out too blue and patchy until darker piles brought them back to the first portrait's black-blue. The crimson thread and the pendant glyph, first laid as brush strokes, were too thin or went round. Restated as masked bands and hexagons, they read at full view. It is short of 25 chunks because it converged quickly on the first painting's method. Still short: the near eye's lower lid facet is faint, and the far locks are slightly more regular than the first portrait's.
