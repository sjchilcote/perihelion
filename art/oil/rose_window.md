# The Rose Window

The TESTAMENT's chapel rose, seen from inside with the Sun behind it. A great circle of glass is set in near-black bronze tracery on a dark stone wall. At its heart is the medallion: the Sun as a gold disc with a calm sleeping face and a ring of orange rays, inside a ring of blue glass divided by leads. Around it twelve petals, ruby and blue in turn, each tell one beat of the story in a couple of flat shapes, read clockwise from the top:
1. The CUCULUS crossing.
2. The seed burrowing into the gold scales.
3. The new frame in the tabard.
4. The dead outpost.
5. Ratio on her green screen.
6. The docents under their arcade.
7. A Warden.
8. The lightning.
9. The sail spreading.
10. Perihelion.
11. The station sailing on.
12. The seed as an ark.

Beyond the petals the outer ring is blue glass holding twelve roundels, gold and green in turn, each with a fold-notation glyph leaded into it: a polygon with a smaller turned copy inside. Small ruby and violet bosses sit between them. The tracery is heavy and dark, ringed by one thin warm line where the bronze catches the glow. Below the window and to either side, patches of ruby, blue and gold light fall on the wall.

**How it was painted.** Glass first, as flat saturated body colour laid straight onto a dark knifed ground, in two passes with drying waits between. The tiny scenes went in next, then the coloured light on the wall, and after another wait all the leading and tracery: thin round-brush leads, then the heavy bronze bars with a flat brush, every arc broken into short straight segments so nothing could smooth into a loop. The glass reads as jewel colour against the black, but it is flat. It does not glow as glass with light behind it does. Next time I would vary each pane's value (lighter where the light is strongest, toward the centre), let a little halation bleed over the leads near the Sun medallion, and lay the wall's light patches as softer, slanted shapes, angled the way the light falls through the window.

## Pass at 05:20 CDT, Oct 2

Chunks 4–5, on dry paint. **The glass is now lit from behind:**
- The inner band of every petal is a full step lighter: pale ruby-coral or pale blue.
- The main panes get lighter and brighter toward the Sun through a feathered stipple. I kept each scene clear, which leaves it sitting in a small roundel of the original deeper glass.
- The blue ring around the Sun is now pale and luminous.
- The Sun's disc is hotter at its centre.

I restated the leads and the Sun's face where the new glass covered them. Small white flecks sit over the lead circles nearest the centre, where light bleeds round them. The bronze tracery stays near-black, so the inner rings blaze against it. **The light patches:** the blocky rectangles are painted out with the wall's dark. In their place, slanted ruby, blue and gold shapes with lost edges are thrown down and to the right across the wall and floor.

Still short: the outer blue ring and roundels are still flat. The thrown light reads as broad brush-strokes more than as soft light.
