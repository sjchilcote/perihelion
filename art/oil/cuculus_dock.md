# The Cuckoo's Egg

Close on the TESTAMENT's hull at the moment of burrowing. The hull curves away under a black sky, a field of bronze scale-plates in overlapping rows, each plate's lip catching the Sun, which rakes in hard from the left. The CUCULUS lies across the picture like a tick: a long, dark, armoured body, a hard white light along its upper edge, plate seams across it, Church crimson bands at its nose and waist, a bone cross on its flank. Its clamp legs bite into the plates, and it throws a long pure-black shadow across the hull to the right. Its drill is buried in the hull. Around the hole the plates are wrenched up and splayed outward, their torn tops glowing. The pale shoulder of the seed pod shows in the black of the hole, ringed with white-hot metal. Molten gold and sparks fly up and out in long fans, so the wound looks like a nest. It is a violation, and also a cuckoo coming home to someone else's nest.

**How it was painted.** Value first: a black sky and a mid-dark bronze hull, then the plates as hundreds of small rounded shapes. On this loaded machine that one chunk took over 18 minutes of compute, which is the main reason the picture is as simple as it is. The darks then betrayed me in the usual way. The lander, its shadow and the hole, painted over the still-wet bronze, came up a pale mottled olive-grey, and I had to restate them opaque once the hull was dry. Everything bright went on afterwards, on dry paint, as strokes: plate lips and their shadows, the lander's lit edge and seams, crimson, the cross, the torn plates' edges, and the spray. The first spray was drawn with too much gravity and fell back into a nest-like tangle round the hole. I kept it, since it suits a cuckoo, and threw a second spray up and out. Next time I would paint fewer, larger plates (a few big rows close to us, a texture beyond them), lay the darks before the bronze rather than over it, and model the lander's body with a turning half-tone rather than flat black.

## Pass at 03:43 CDT, Oct 2

Chunks 8–15, after a 23-minute replay. **The lander** now turns. I first laid a half-tone too pale, and blending it dragged it over the crimson and the cross. So I restated the body opaque dark, with a narrower dark blue-grey half-tone along the upper middle and a warm bronze reflected light along the underside, where it faces the lit hull. I repainted the crimson collar and band and the bone cross. On dry paint came a hard white sunlit edge along the top, plate seams across the body each with a lit lip, and a few rivets catching light. **The plate field:** a warm glaze at the left where the Sun rakes in and a darker glaze toward the right and the horizon. Both dried streaky, as dark glazes over light do here. I also added two new near rows of large plates, overlapping the right way at last (the row behind laid over the row in front, so the rounded lips show), each with a black shadow under its lip and a sunlit left half. This makes the scale grow toward us. **Torn plates:** three more, wrenched up higher near the hole, with white sunward edges and white-hot torn tops. **The jet:** one thick rope of molten gold thrown up and back from the drill against the black sky, with a white core and spitting sparks.

Still short: the middle distance is still the old chequerboard, which I laid with the overlap the wrong way, and the glaze streaks sit over it. The top of the new near rows makes a straight line across the field. The lander's half-tone is a stripe more than a turning surface.

**Director's decision (03:45):** the 03:43 pass was rejected. The jet was
good, but the new near rows made a hard block line across the field, the
glazes dried streaky, and the half-tone read as a stripe. v1 (7 chunks)
stays canonical; the pass is kept as `pass_0343_rejected.png`.
