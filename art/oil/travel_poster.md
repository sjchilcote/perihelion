# Visit the TESTAMENT

A 1930s Art Deco travel poster for the station, after Cassandre and Broders. We look up from below at the TESTAMENT. Its spine rises from the bottom of the picture as a tall tapering bronze needle, pale and gleaming on its sunlit left side and deep bronze on its right, ringed by thin dark bands. Its great ring is seen tilted from below as a bronze ellipse with a dark rim and a gleaming lip, passing behind the spine at the top and in front of it at the bottom. Behind it the Sun is a huge flat gold disc. The sky falls in flat stepped bands from deep navy at the top through blues and a pale lilac to peach and gold at the horizon, and thin rays fan out from under the Sun. From the spine's tip the solar sail streams off to the right like a liner's funnel smoke. At the upper left a tiny dark CUCULUS comes in on a thin fiery trail. Across the bottom a navy band, under a gold rule, carries TESTAMENT in big gold block capitals, with a short line of pale abstract marks beneath.

How it was painted: the flat method. Every shape was its own area of the page, painted side by side on the bare ground, never over another wet colour. The sky bands, sun, title band, spine halves, ring rim and face and sail all went down in one chunk. The first ring face and the spine's lit side were too close to the Sun's gold and vanished into it. After four days' drying they were repainted, the face darker bronze and the spine's lit side pale cream, so both stand off the disc. The gleams, rays, sail fold, CUCULUS and lettering went on last, on dry paint. The title was first drawn with a round brush and came out thin and wobbly. It was restated with a wide flat brush as straight segments, which gives the heavier Deco block. Still weak: the lettering's strokes have rough ends rather than crisp Deco terminals, the S is still a little soft, and the rays are faint.
