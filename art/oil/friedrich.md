# Monk by the Sea

After Friedrich. The Commando stands tiny on the TESTAMENT's outer hull with its back to us. The hull is a thin pale band of worn bronze scale-plates across the bottom of the picture. Beyond it lies a flat dark band of deep space. Above that, filling four-fifths of the canvas, is a nearly empty sky: the Sun's corona rising from below as pale gold and cream, cooling through grey and slate into blue-black at the top. Nothing else is there. The figure is a few strokes no taller than a fingernail, and a crimson rag lifting from its shoulder is the only saturated mark in the picture.

How it was painted: 6 chunks, about 1.72:1, on a warm grey knifed ground. The sky went in as six horizontal bands, blended wet, but they dried still visibly banded. I repainted it in ten finer overlapping steps and blended it twice horizontally while wet, which gave the continuous gradient. The dark band of space and the hull band are broad ruler-straight strokes. Scale-plate arcs drawn row by row on the hull read too regular. A pale scumble meant to quiet them turned the band into camouflage blotches, so I repainted the hull opaque, pale and blended, and then laid sparser, lower-contrast arcs. The figure went last on dry paint. A rectangle I held back around it showed as a pale box, which I painted over and restated. A faint plain patch is still visible there at 1:1. Next time I would paint the hull band right the first time, as one pale wet passage with the plates barely hinted, and leave the sky even emptier.
