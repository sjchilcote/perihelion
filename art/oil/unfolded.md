# The Empty Vault

The last shot of the Unfolded ending. We look straight into the Ark's vault. The Testator bronze wall is laid in golden-angle scale-plates that spiral toward a tall arched niche, and away from the niche the scales sink into darkness. Inside the niche, under one cold shaft from above, stands a bone cradle on a bronze plinth: a shallow basin with four ribs curled up like cupped fingers, and nothing in it. The brightest thing in the picture is a ring of frost in the hollow, the outline of the container that lay there. Everything else in the room shows what happened. A double trail of many-legged gouges runs from the foot of the plinth out of the frame. A scrap of crimson cloth and a dropped rosary with a small bone cross lie on the floor. A dead MIRIAD terminal, bolted to the alien bronze where Ratio once lit up, has a black screen. Most of the lamp rows high on the wall have gone out, and only a few warm points remain. There is no figure.

How it was painted: a mauve-brown toned ground, then dark lay-ins of wall, niche and floor, and about 600 pairs of strokes for the scale-plates (a shadow crescent and a lit lip each) placed on a phyllotaxis spiral. The light was the hard part. A lead-white glaze for the shaft went down as bright streaks, repainting it in graded piles gave hard bands, and blending made a cloudy panel with ruled edges. What finally worked was broken vertical strokes of the niche darks dragged over the edges, which turned the shaft into a cold glow between dark curtains. The floor light pool did the same thing: a scumble came out as an opaque blotch, and I rescued it by brushing floor color in over its rim. Dark accents laid wet into pale (the shadows under the bone ribs) came up pale, as the studio notes warn. The final passes were subtractive: broken dark strokes over the outer and middle rings of scales put most of the lamps out and quieted the wall. With more time I'd model the cradle further, break up the crimson scrap's flat red, and make the tracks heavier gouges rather than bead-like dots.

## Second pass

At the director's request I repainted the wall, the cloth, the tracks and the cradle, keeping the composition and the cold shaft. The scale-plates are now about 530 real plates on the same golden-angle spiral. I painted them from the outside in, so each inner plate laps over the one behind it. Each plate has a dark underside, a bronze body and a thin lip of dim light on the edge facing the niche. The plates grow larger and darker outward through three bands of mixes, until the outer ring sinks into the dark. I painted them twice: the first time they came out as separate warm studs, so I went over them again with bigger brushes and darker mixes until they overlapped. Six surviving lamps sit among them as tiny warm points. The crimson scrap is now a larger folded cloth: a dark madder-brown base, dark folds with lit ridges laid into it wet, then dust and broken dark strokes on top, so it reads as a dulled, crumpled rag. The bead-like tracks are painted out under floor colour, which left a faint scuffed path, and replaced by about 150 gouges in the dust, each a dark tapered furrow with a thin pale lip on the plinth side. The bone ribs got a cooler body, a shadowed inner side and a cold crown highlight. The basin got a light top band, a shadowed belly and a cold rim. The plinth got cool lit lips on both steps and a contact shadow at its foot.
