# The Stack Has No Floor

*Establishing shot: the Depths (RECURSION STACK / THE FOLD / TESSERACT DECK). Oil on linen, 3:2.*

We look straight down a corridor in the Testament's deep decks. It is a tunnel of thirteen hexagonal bronze frames. Each frame is a handmade copy of the one before it, turned about nine degrees and shrunk by about a quarter, and no two are exactly alike. So the corridor twists as it recedes, and none of its six faces stays the floor for long. Between the frames, every facet is shelved with bone record-tablets that run back into the dark: a records stack with no end. The last generation abandoned its bodies, and there is no furniture left. The ornament is pure fold-notation. Cartouches show a hexagon holding a turned, smaller copy of itself, which holds another. A frieze of small nested triangles, squares and pentagons runs around the bands like script. The light comes from the bottom of the recursion, a cold white hexagon with one more turned hexagon ghosted inside it. Down the axis runs the only level thing left, a narrow bone causeway, and on it walks the pilgrim, three times. The near one throws a long shadow toward us. Further in he is smaller, and further still he is a speck against the glare. The corridor recurs, and so does he. For the team, the visual language is: nested rotated polygons as both architecture and ornament, bronze turned cold and olive, violet-black void, bone used only for writing and for the one walkway, light that sits at the bottom of the repeat instead of coming from a source, and a human-sized figure as the only thing that is not geometry.

**How it was painted.** The geometry was built first: a helper made each frame with fresh jittered vertices. Then the walls were painted facet by facet, each facet's value set by its depth and by which way it faced. The thickness of each frame (its "reveal") was laid in lit from the core. That turned a flat op-art target into a space. The tablets were short flat strokes in shelves, and the first attempt merged into pale blobs that had to be painted out. The rigger smoothed my first hexagon glyphs into spiral rings, so they were covered and redrawn edge by edge. A dark cool glaze over the outer zones, blended inside its mask over dry paint, pulled the eye to the centre and left radial streaks like rushing air, which I kept. The bronze faces caused the most trouble. A pale scumble came out as cheese-yellow blotches, and lifting it with a clean brush spread it into marble. An opaque repaint came out green. A burnt-sienna glaze then went orange over the dark halves, and lifting it while wet left a usable olive-bronze tint. The lesson: at this easel, decide values with opaque paint and don't count on glazes to fix colour. The causeway's near end was the other weak passage. Lead-white mixtures and tacky paint underneath kept pulling it light, and a long blend left polished streaks with a seam. With more time I would lay that whole passage dark-to-light in one session from the start, and vary the record-tablets more (gaps, a few fresh-white ones), so the stack reads as handmade and not as a pattern.
