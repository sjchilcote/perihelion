# What It Says (II)

*A close-up variant of "What It Says": oil on simulated linen, 3:2, 35 chunks.*

This is the same moment as the original, but now we are down on the rubble beside the Ark. The human seed-bank lies across the picture as a pale, dented slab. Its faded red band and a worn mission patch are human-made, and a stencilled "SEED VAULT 07" has frosted half away. PALMER's gauntlet reaches in from the upper right and lays four plated fingers and a thumb flat on the shell. The vambrace carries an inlaid bone cross, and a dark rosary is wound round the wrist with its loop and small cross hanging onto the shell. The warm beam from the oculus crosses the picture diagonally: a band of gold rubble above and below, and a warm blessing of light across the shell's middle, which stops just short of the hand and gilds only its fingertips. Outside the beam the shell is cold slate furred with rime and frost feathers. On its front face the diagnostic readout is the one cold note. Its glyphs can't quite be read, and its cyan breathes out into the frost around it and runs along the underside of the thumb. Hope and diagnosis meet in one hand: the gold falls on its fingertips and the cyan on its palm side.

How it was painted: the palette and light logic are carried over from the original. Grounds and darks are bone black, bone brown, burnt sienna and ultramarine. The beam is raw sienna, ochre and Indian-yellow whites, and the readout is the only cerulean and viridian in the picture. The shell, beam and gauntlet are flat planes with clipped passes; the rubble is stippled and graded by distance along the beam. Three things fought me. A frost stipple over the whole cold half of the shell turned it light gray, and in a squint the beam no longer read as the light. I repainted that half opaque slate and put frost back only at the edges and corners. A dark glaze with a badger over it went navy and ate a stencilled letter. Twice I tried to paint the readout's glow on the shell and failed: blended strokes left a hard-edged ellipse, and radial strokes became a cartoon starburst. Lifting that wet starburst with four clean scumble passes was the surprise of the session. It left a fine, broken, cold shimmer in the frost round the screen, much better than anything I had aimed for. Next time I would set the cold half's value first and leave the frost for last.
