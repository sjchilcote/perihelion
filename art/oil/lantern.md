# The Lamplighter

A long, low bronze gallery runs straight away from us under transverse ribs, set at uneven depths. Small lamps hang down its right side on chains: bronze cages around bone shades. Behind the lamplighter they are lit, a receding row of warm points that warm the right wall in bays of gold, lay a warm band along the floor, and brighten toward the far end. Ahead of it, nearer to us, the lamps are still dark gray shades in their cages, and the near gallery is dark. The docent comes toward us in a dark bronze robe, hooded, its bone face and single cold lens in shadow because all the light is behind it. Its arms are raised and its long pole reaches up to the next dark lamp, where a small flame at the tip has just caught. Far behind, small and dark in the lit reach of the gallery, the Commando follows at a respectful distance.

**How I painted it.** I wrote a one-point perspective and painted the walls, floor and ceiling in four depth bands from near-black to warm bronze. My first band masks were cross-sections, and they made boxes, so I repainted them per surface. I put ribs on three of the band seams so the steps hide behind them. Ribs laid as flat strokes into wet paint broke into flames, so I restated them as solid masks once the paint had set. The wall pools under the lit lamps went in as soft two-step ellipses. The floor pools came out as hard ovals, and I replaced them with one long warm band along the right side of the floor. That band went lumpy and now reads more like a lit kerb than light on a floor. It is the weakest passage. The docent laid into wet paint came out mottled, and I restated it on set paint. Its warm back-rim is faint. Still short: the floor band, the docent's stride (it reads as standing), and a dark lamp clipped at the top right corner.
