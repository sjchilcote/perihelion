# Bay Seven

The MIRIAD cargo-bay floor, seen straight down and lit evenly, built on the 125-unit grid. Sixteen 250-unit load panels sit on seams that lie exactly on the grid and the canvas edges, and the solid panels are split again into 125-unit plates. Five panels are open steel grating over a deep blue-brown dark. Each has a steel frame, bearing bars every 15.6 units and cross rods every 31.25. The rest are solid load plates in the same cool MIRIAD gray family as the corridor deck plate: gray-blue and olive-gray plates scattered, brushed along the direction of traffic. Use shows in a few places, all kept small and well inside the tile: a worn amber load-zone box, an amber edge line beside one grating, a single stencilled "07", eight tie-down rings set at plate corners, a few dull fluid stains, and gray forklift-tire scuffs that all run in one direction (y), spread evenly and kept off the gratings. Mean value is about 119/255, so it sits at mid value under the game's lights.

How it was painted: knifed gray ground, plates laid in clipped body strokes from three gray piles, pits in a lean black-umber-blue, then the gratings ruled in with a flat brush stroke by stroke and the seams cut in with a detail round. The wear fought me. Single dry-brush strokes of black and raw sienna dried as dark olive worms. A body pass meant for tire tracks came out as flat pale bars. Glazed stains came out as black blobs. So I painted every plate and pit again over all of it, then tested each scuff pile on one plate before the full pass. The gray that worked is lead white and bone black 1:1 with a little medium, at load 0.1–0.18 and half pressure. Two repairs re-ruled grating bars where a test stroke and a stain had landed on a grate. Next time I'd keep the panel map in front of me before placing any test mark.
