# The Wanderer above the Sea of Corona

After Caspar David Friedrich's "Wanderer above the Sea of Fog". The Commando stands alone with his back to us on the dark, jagged summit of the TESTAMENT's highest spire, one foot forward on a higher crag and a staff planted beside him. His long dark coat has pauldron edges, his steel helm catches a thin warm rim of light, and a crimson rag lifts from his shoulder in the solar wind, the only saturated mark in the picture. Below and beyond him stretches a vast sea of the Sun's corona, pale gold and cream streamers lying like fog, and out of it rise the pale blue-grey tips of the station's other spires, fading with distance. The sky above cools to pale blue. The picture is romantic, solitary and still.

How it was painted: 5 chunks, 3:4, on a pale warm ground. The sky and the corona-sea are broad graded bands blended along a gentle wave field (one six-minute chunk), then distant spires as flat cool shapes, then about 140 long pale streamer strokes blended horizontally so the peaks rise out of the fog. The spire summit and the figure went on dry paint as flat dark masked shapes with warm rim lines. The first facets on the rock read as teeth and the first summit as a soft mound, so I painted them out, broke the outline into crags and repainted the head as a helm. Still short: the rock near the figure is a flat, slightly blocky dark mass, the sail's rim arc vanished into the fog, and the figure is less finely drawn than Friedrich's.
