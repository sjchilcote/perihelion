# A Book of Hours: Perihelion

A calendar page in the manner of the Limbourg brothers' *Très Riches Heures*, for the TESTAMENT's month of perihelion. At the top is the lunette: a blue half-moon rimmed in gold, its rim ticked red and blue like a calendar, with the station's orbit marked in gold. Around the orbit run twelve fold-notation glyphs as the signs of the zodiac, each a small polygon with a smaller copy inside, turned. Inside, under scattered gold stars, the Sun rides in its chariot: a gold disc with a calm sleeping face on a red car with gold wheels, drawn by two white horses on gold reins.

Below, in a gold frame ruled with red, is the labour of the month. On the pale gold film of the solar sail, ruled with furrows running back toward the horizon, seven docents in white robes and jewel-coloured hoods work in a staggered row like reapers. Each sweeps a long brush across the film and leaves a polished bright strip behind. In the distance, against the black sky, the TESTAMENT rises like a castle: pale towers and twin spires tipped in gold, its ring a curtain wall with a row of small warm windows. At the left stands the MIRIAD outpost, a grey prefab box with amber hazard stripes, cyan windows and a mast with a red light. Three tiny crew in grey stand by it with amber helmets, watching the work like peasants watching the Duke's harvest.

**How it was painted.** As the icons were: flat shapes of jewel colour on a vellum-tinted ground, a drying wait, then all the drawing as fine segments and arcs. The pale shapes laid into the still-wet black sky came up mottled and had to be restated on dry paint, the same lesson as all night. The figures are tiny and simple, closer to an illuminator's first lay-in than to the Limbourgs' precision. Next time I would give the docents more varied poses and folds, add the calendar's lettering and month numerals in the lunette band, and put the castle on a rise with a little atmospheric step-back.
