# The Long Gallery, with Good Chairs

An establishing shot of the Testament's shallows, looking straight down THE LONG GALLERY toward MIRROR WARD. It is a tall bronze hall with a barrel vault on ribs. The ribs and pilasters stand at uneven intervals. The south wall (left) has four arched crossings and the north wall has three, yet the two walls are the same length. Each crossing opens onto a warm lit room and throws its own pool of light across a floor of worn bronze. The nearest pool also catches a dusty shaft of light. Down the middle runs a bone inlay runner set with golden-angle stud rosettes. The chairs are pedestal seats with lens-shaped seats and tall paddle or egg backs, and no two stand the same way. One faces a blank pier, one waits for you in the right foreground, and others face the far end. At the end of the gallery a tall arched mirror shows the gallery behind the viewer, but with the counts swapped: three crossings on the left, four on the right. The chairs nearest the mirror still have silver on their backs. A small chair is half out of the glass, and silver has run out onto the floor. Fold-notation in bone, a polygon holding smaller copies of itself each turned by the golden angle, sits above the mirror, and on the south pier where the fourth crossing folds into the wall. The piers carry golden-spiral medallions of scale plates around bone bosses, each a different size. The palette is bronze, bone and warm light. The only cool note is the mirror's silver.

**How it was painted.** I built the space on a hand-written one-point perspective (P(X,Y,Z) in metres), then laid it in dark to light over a toned ground. The order was the vault, the walls in depth bands, the floor, the lit crossings and their reveals, and then the pools of light. I worked out each pool by projecting its opening along its own light direction. Most things that went wrong were edges and veils. A `lose()` pass round the pools grew a fringe like grass. Scumbles bounded by rectangle masks left hard horizontal seams and wiped out the vault ribs. A dry-brush "brushed bronze" sheen came out looking like tree bark and had set before I could lift it, so I repainted the walls. A lead-white glaze meant as a light shaft went grey and mottled. I lifted it only partly, then warmed it with a Mars orange and rose glaze and blended it three times inside its mask. The result is a hazy beam of dusty light, and I kept it. Yellow glazes over the dark floor turned olive, as the studio notes warned. One surprise: the detail hand laid almost nothing over tacky paint, so I painted the chairs with a small filbert instead. Next time I would test every veil and sheen on the scrap canvas first. I would also put the floor plate seams in before the chairs and shadows rather than after, and put the mirror ward closer or larger so the chair coming out of the glass reads at a glance.

## Second pass

The director's notes were about the light shaft, muddy values, flat chairs and the chair in the mirror. I painted the glassy light shaft out completely, rebuilding the wall, pier, crossing and floor underneath it, and let the light live on the floor instead. Each pool of light is now graded in three or four steps, from a near-white lead and cadmium yellow beside its crossing to a dull ochre at the far edge. The hottest patch is beside the first south crossing. Once everything was dry, I glazed the walls, vault and far wall with a warm dark (burnt sienna and bone brown in a lot of medium) and blended it, giving the middle and far distance a second coat. The bronze now steps back into warm dark with distance. The seven gold crossings and their pools are the lights, and the bone runner leads the eye down to the mirror. Glazing worked here, though it failed in the first pass, because the paint underneath was fully dry and the glaze was dark over light, not pale over dark. Each chair now has a cast shadow that falls along the light of its own crossing, a dark shadow side and a thin lit edge. The story beat is now a big egg-backed seat stepping out of the mirror. Its back is still silver, outlined cool against the bright glass. Its seat and pedestal are already bronze, with a bright line where they cross the surface of the glass. It throws a shadow on the gallery floor and leaves silver drops in front of it.
