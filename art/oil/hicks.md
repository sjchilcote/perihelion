# The Peaceable Station

After Edward Hicks' "The Peaceable Kingdom". On a terrace of the ring, whose far floor curves up into a pale sky behind, a gentle crowd gathers in the foreground under a great bronze pillar standing in for Hicks' tree. A Warden lies curled asleep in the middle with its eyes shut and a small smile, its crest of bone blades and banded shell like a tortoise. Crew children in navy, with round faces, rosy cheeks and dot eyes, lean on and sit atop it. Small dark scuttlers lie quiet along the front among little red, white and blue flowers. The Commando's frame sits at the left in its crimson tabard with a bone cross, its visor glowing softly, with Ratio's little printed body (black hair, pale face, sky-blue dress) on its knee. A tall bronze-and-bone docent stands at the right. In the middle distance, small, as in Hicks' treaty scene, crew, docents and the Commando shake hands across a white-clothed table, with a scroll and a little red flag. Everything is smooth, flat and earnest.

How it was painted: 4 chunks, 1.4:1, on a warm pale ground. I laid flat landscape bands and the pillar, then flat opaque figures, then faces, crests and flowers as single touches and strokes on drier paint. The treaty scene first sat behind the Commando's head and was painted out and moved to the open middle ground. Still short: the Commando and the docent are big plain silhouettes without the folk painter's careful patterning, the sky and plain are empty where Hicks would add trees and water, and the crowd is smaller than Hicks' tightly packed one.
