# The Docents' Primer (Plate of Seven Folds)

A lesson plate from the School's exhibit halls, seen straight on: a square bronze tablet with a bone-inlaid border, turned and polished like an astrolabe. The seven lessons of fold notation sit in round recessed wells, placed like seeds in a sunflower. Lesson *n* lies at angle n × 137.5° and radius 118·√n from a central boss, which is itself a Vogel spiral of bone seeds. The rings the lessons ride on are engraved grooves, and a limb of 89 ticks set by the golden angle runs between the two outer grooves. Each well has n bone seeds beside it for its lesson number. Going outward: (1) a room-glyph with one, two and three notches, each set ending in a chevron; (2) a square and a corridor joined by a bridge between their notched edges; (3) a room that opens onto itself, with an upright solid inset at 0.618 scale (a quiet loop); (4) the same lesson with a room whose inset is turned a quarter (a twisted loop); (5) a room, a bar, and beyond the bar its reflected copy, drawn in outline only (a mirror join); (6) a corridor whose solid insets shrink by φ and turn by the golden angle at each step, spiralling along a faint scribed guide into a dot (recursion); (7) a hexagonal heart with six petals placed by the golden angle, each petal bridged to the heart and to its neighbours and each holding a tiny copy of the heart (the Fold). The eighth slot on the spiral has no glyph. It is a ragged hole cut into the plate, with a warm-black floor, one narrow lit wall and chisel marks. The grooves stop at its edge. One bridge leaves the Fold, runs most of the way round the plate in its own channel, and ends at the lip of the cut: the Breach, shown as an absence.

How it was painted: 29 chunks. First the bronze was laid in broad strokes over a toned ground. The wells and frame went in dark bronze and the grooves as overlapping round strokes. Every glyph edge was painted as its own straight stroke so the corners stay sharp, and the solid insets are clipped detail fills. These are deliberate, liftable shapes for decals. The paint fought back in some useful ways. The first grooves came out dashed because the brush ran dry. A self-bridge bowed the wrong way and crossed into its own room, so I repainted the room's interior and laid the bridge again outside it. The Breach went through three versions: a navy sticker, then a cartoon donut (an ochre hatch laid over wet paint came out opaque), and finally a dark hole with one lit wall. Both a scumble and a glaze I tried for polish failed (camouflage blotches and a cold grey veil). I painted them out with the field's own pile, which disappears into itself. What finally made the bronze read as metal was opaque near-masstone strokes laid off the grooves and dragged together with a tangential badger, plus thin gold highlights on the grooves' lit walls. The radial burnish wedges kept hard steps at their edges because clipping repeats the mask's edge. Next time I would lay the sheen before engraving, with no clip, and blend it while everything is still open.

## Second pass

The director asked for three fixes, in chunks 30–32.
- **Sheen:** the blocky burnish wedges are gone. I repainted the whole disc face, everywhere except the groove lines and the bridge channel, in five angular zones graded toward the light axis, from dark bronze to a pale gold, and laid them all wet together. One tangential badger pass over the whole face then pulled the zones into one smooth turned-metal sheen. The lesson is to keep the whole passage wet at once and never clip a blend to a zone.
- **Lessons 1 and 2:** I painted out lesson 1's cramped notches and laid them again larger, with golden-gap spacing and wide chevrons. The first restatement went down while the cover patch was still soft and came out grey, so I restated it again once the patch had set. Lesson 2's chevrons are now larger where the bridge lands.
- **Verdigris:** the pale speckle went under the new face and under repainted well and cut lips. Verdigris now sits only as thin dark-green runs in the groove bottoms, heavier on the shadowed lower right. I also restated the groove walls (gold on the lit wall, dark on the shadowed one), the limb ticks and the lesson-count seeds.
