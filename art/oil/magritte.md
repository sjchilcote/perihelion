# The Empire of Light

After Magritte's "The Empire of Light". At the bottom it is night. A quiet terrace of three Testator houses on the ring stands in darkness between two great black domed trees. The houses have low domed roofs with finials, bone trim on the eaves and pilasters, round doors in bone surrounds, and arched windows, all dark except two that glow warm. A single bronze lamp-post stands lit before the right-hand house. Its globe glows pale yellow, and its light warms the facade around it in a soft pool. The wet street below reflects the lamp and one window in broken gold streaks. Above all this is a perfect daytime sky, clean cerulean shading to pale near the rooftops, with soft white clouds. Small and exact in that blue, the TESTAMENT's spine crosses it as a thin, straight, ringed rod, so the sky is inside the station.

How it was painted: 9 chunks, 4:5, on a pale ground, as flat clean paint. The sky is eight graded opaque bands blended horizontally while wet. My first try used too much white and also a vertical blend, which left drips, so I repainted it stronger and blended only horizontally. Each full-sky blend took about five minutes. My first long stroke for the spine ran dry after a short distance, so I restated it in ten reloaded segments. The trees muddied the wet spine where they crossed it, and dabs meant to give them leafy edges came out as bead chains, so I enlarged the domes to swallow them. The lamp's light was the hard part. Lighter rings blended against the dry facade left a hard disc, twice. The fix was to repaint the whole facade wet with the glow rings inside it, blend it all at once, and then restate every window, door, pilaster and the lamp on dry paint. Still short: the clouds are cream and a little cut-out, without Magritte's soft grey undersides; the trees are plain domes; the spine is faint; and the doors read as ovals.
