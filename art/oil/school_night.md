# The Long Gallery at Night

This is the Testament's Long Gallery from the same viewpoint as the day painting, with the night cycle on. The seven crossings (four on the south wall and three on the north) are dark blue-black voids. The bronze walls and the ribbed barrel vault have sunk into warm near-black. Only two lights remain. One is the cold silver glow of the mirror at the far end. It shows the gallery behind us with the counts swapped: three openings on its left wall, four on its right. Its glow sits on the stone around it, picks out a cool lower edge on the far ribs, and lays a silver tongue on the floor in front of it. The other is a row of small bone-white lamps set low beside the runner, alternating sides at golden-ratio gaps (long, short, long, long, short…). Each lights only a small spot of floor and puts a glint on the runner's bone edge. The Commando walks the runner toward the glass, small and dark against it, with a cold edge on the helmet and pauldrons. The mirror throws his shadow back toward us. It is long and widening, and runs down the runner and off the bottom of the canvas, darkening one bone line. The over-solved pedestal chairs stand where they stood by day. Here they are dark shapes with cold rims on their backs and seats and a little lamp warmth underneath. A small bone fold-notation pentagon hangs over the glass.

**How I painted it.** I rebuilt the day painting's one-point perspective (the same vanishing point, focal length, wall and crossing positions) on a 3:2 canvas over a dark blue-umber ground. I laid it in with lean darks and no white, and kept the lights for last. The lamp light took three tries. Full pools of light made of graded rings came out as big, ringed, olive "manhole covers", even when laid wet into a wet floor and stippled at the edges. In the end I painted them out and let each lamp light just a small spot. The first floor sheen from the mirror read as a grey rug and had to be painted out as well. Thin mixes of black with raw sienna laid in narrow masks dried ochre, the same thing that happened in the Seam pass, which left the first ribs and chairs as brown ghosts. Heavier coats of bone black with burnt sienna and ultramarine fixed them. The first shadow was a saturated navy wedge, and a neutral near-black reads better. Next time I would test the lamp glow on the scrap canvas first, and I would build the shadow's soft edge, the warm lamp bounce on the walls and some texture on the bronze walls rather than leaving them plain.
