# Months in Flight

The CUCULUS seed-projectile, months out, is seen close from behind: a squat,
scorched armoured dart whose rear plate and burnt-bronze engine ring face us,
its body tapering away toward a single bronze speck with a cross-glint in the
upper right — the TESTAMENT, dead ahead and very far off. The Sun is a hot
blown-out glare cut off by the upper-left corner, throwing pale splinters of
light across the dark. The only warmth on the dart is the sun-side flank,
where the light rakes along the plates toward the nose; the shadow side is
streaked with soot blown back from re-entry-like heating. The Commando inside
hears the brief and sees none of this.

How it was painted: six chunks on a 1000x667 canvas. A knifed black-blue
ground, then a broad filbert pass of bone black and ultramarine for space.
The sun went in as a lead-white/orange glaze, a scumbled cadmium-yellow ring
and a zinc-white core; the ring came out hard as a coin, and a clipped badger
blend softened it into glare while the glaze strokes stayed as splintered rays,
which I kept. The dart's dark, laid into the still-wet first pass, came out
plum and pale (note 54 again); a three-day wait and a heavier opaque repaint
only partly cured it, so the dart reads lighter than the void. Lit plate
strokes, soot streaks and a rim highlight went onto dry paint after a second
wait and held crisp. Next time I would lay the dart's near-black first, before
any space paint around it, and keep the engine ring duller from the start.
