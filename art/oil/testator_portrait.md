# A Testator, Living

A bust portrait of one of the builders, painted the way a Flemish master painted a scholar at his desk: three-quarter view, one window light from the upper left, a dark warm wall that glows faintly near the window, and the sitter's soft shadow on the wall behind. The Testator's long skull sweeps up and back, a vault of living bone plates. The plates facing the window are pale, and those turning away step down into shade. Across the brow runs a bony crest, and under it an arc of seven small eyes set on a golden-angle curve, largest at the front and shrinking toward the temple. Each eye sits under a heavy, half-lowered lid with a warm glint deep inside and the window's highlight on its surface. The cheeks and jaw are bronze-coloured chitin in large plates, each ringed with fine growth lines like tortoiseshell. The mouth is a small closed plate of folded bone pleats, its seam quiet. The neck is stacked chitin rings, wrapped in a heavy folded collar of dark plum cloth. Its two hands have too many joints, each finger a chain of four plated segments. One pinches a bone stylus to a tablet; the other steadies the tablet's near edge. On the tablet the Fold is half-written: a heart glyph and six petals, each holding a smaller turned copy, bridged by arcs, with a column of script down the margin. The expression is meant as grave kindness: someone careful, unhurried, looking up from a warning they are writing for strangers.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 44 chunks, building each layer over dry paint as in the Ratio and Docent portraits. First a toned ground, the wall graded over dry paint in three piles around the window corner, and dead colour. Then every plate of the head as its own facet from short bone and bronze ramps. Then the eyes, mouth, tablet and hands, which this time were in the plan from the first chunk, not added late. A squint check showed the back of the skull outshining the face, so I turned those plates down a step and the eyes now lead. The same paint lessons came back. Two sleeve patches laid onto paint only two days dry mottled, and came out clean after five days. Strokes over fully dry paint skipped and left dashes, so the brow light and the under-eye shade went on as narrow flat facets instead of strokes, which also suits the plated style. My first mouth, seven pleats in a row, read as a barcode; one soft plate with faint fold lines reads as a delicate closed mouth. Still short: the robe is a few blocky planes rather than real heavy cloth, the neck rings on the lit side are a little regular, and the face is still more carved mask than living flesh. A next pass would warm the chitin with a breath of rose in the shadows and give the robe deeper, rounder folds.
