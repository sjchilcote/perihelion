# The Stylite

The Commando keeps his watch on the far side of the breach. He's a small hooded frame with a staff, standing on a pillar in front of the last piece of the TESTAMENT: a ring of bronze scale-plates in two courses, with an unreadable line of bone script cut into the outer course. Through the ring comes the only warm light in the picture, ordinary space, home, and he stands black against the brightest part of it with his back to it. One cold point of light marks his optic. Around him the Adversary's space turns as one slow vortex in violet, plum, teal and sickly cyan, and nothing in it holds still. The ring's lower-right arc has broken open. Its plates drift outward along the swirl and turn edge-on until they become specks, and where the ring is missing the gold light leaks out and sours to green in the cold. In the top corner the rigid lattice of ordinary space is still whole, but its lines bend and curl as they reach the swirl. The pillar is plumb and coherent where it holds him up. Lower down its drums slide off the vertical, their tops catching the gate's light, and its skin peels away in hinged strips that dissolve into the dark. Faith keeps the one straight line and the one warm light, and everything else unfolds.

**How it was painted.** I toned the ground cold violet-grey and laid the space in with broad swirling strokes that follow a vortex field. The glow went in as concentric zones wet-into-wet and was blended radially and in circles. That first gave a bullseye, and the blender then mixed the white core down to amber, so the core went back on later as stippled lead white blended over dry paint. The ring caused the most trouble. Its first lay-in came out as flat tiles and then as something like wood grain. A scumble went on far too heavy and turned the whole ring khaki. I kept that as a dead-colour underpainting, glazed it back to bronze, and then repainted each plate in radial bands (dark, mid, cold olive light, dark) blended along the curve. After a dark glaze on the side turned away from the light, it finally read as metal. Speculars, verdigris and the bone inscription went on last. The paint kept surprising me in two ways. Paint laid by a blender stays exactly inside its mask, so a soft-looking mask still leaves a hard disc. And blending a mistake while it is wet often rescues it: the folded "blades" of cold light I first painted looked like a quilt, and once blended into the swirl they became the luminous cloud-banks. Next time I would plan the ring's modelling from the start rather than glaze it out of trouble, and I would keep the small geometric props (unfolding cubes) out of the space. They read as satellites, so I smeared them into the vortex.
