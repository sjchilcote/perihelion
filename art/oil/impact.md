# Impact

The TESTAMENT's hull is a single bronze cylinder that fills the right half of the frame and runs off the top and bottom, so it is too big for the picture. Its near limb is one long diagonal. The Sun is off-frame to the right. Where the scale-plates turn toward it they are hard gold, with a crescent of black shadow under every lip and a white glint on top. Toward the limb they roll into near-black, and there the hull shows only as rows of tiny warm windows. The rows are parallel to the axis and crowd together as the surface turns away. A few plates are missing and a few are crusted with verdigris. The small gray MIRIAD outpost is bolted to the dark limb, where the Sun doesn't reach it, so it is lit only by its own destruction. The CUCULUS has just gone into its outer cargo module, and its faint trail runs back out of the upper left. At the wound a white-hot core sits in a ring of torn skin petals with incandescent edges, and a thin starburst comes off it. Straight streaks of burning debris fan out into the black, white-yellow near the wound and cooling to orange and red farther out, and a few torn panels tumble along them with hot leading edges. A plume of escaping atmosphere freezes into a glitter of ice that is dense at the vent and thins into a pale cloud. The flash throws orange light onto the plate lips nearest the wound. The rest of the hull doesn't react to it at all.

I laid the hull in as about 3,200 short strokes along its axis. Each stroke took its paint from an 8-step bronze ramp, chosen by its distance from the limb. Over that went a lattice of plates: shadows, glints, then per-plate gold bodies, then shadows and glints again on dry paint. My first lit bronzes had too much lead white and went chalky cream, so the per-plate Mars yellow and ochre dabs were what turned the hull into metal. The paint surprised me twice and saved me twice. A halo of radial dabs around the flash dried as a cartoon flower with a gray outer ring, and clean blender passes lifted it into a soft golden halation. A "low-load" scumble veil for the plume went down as an opaque gray blanket. I lifted that to mist, and then my attempt to carve its shape with opaque navy wiped out the glow by the wound. Lifting again straight away rescued it, and a glitter stipple tied the plume back to the vent. Next time I would paint the plume as glitter and dry-brush from the start and never lay down a veil, and I'd plan the verdigris as dry-brush in the recesses rather than as solid plates. I found the green plates afterwards by measuring the saved image's pixels and dry-brushed bronze back over them.

**Second pass.** I re-plated the whole hull. The new lattice offsets each row by the golden ratio rather than by half a plate. Plate size drifts from region to region, every plate is tilted slightly, and the rows bow along the curve of the cylinder. Each plate's value now comes from a crisp terminator whose edge wavers, plus a wear field. Past the terminator only the raised lips catch the sun, and they fade into the dark. Shadows get longer as the light rakes toward the terminator. The lit field has light and dark halves on each plate, dry-brushed tarnish, verdigris only in the shadowed crescents, a few missing sockets and some re-cut plates in bone or new bronze. I repainted the dark side near-black, which brings the windows forward. The windows are now rows of decks running around the cylinder: short dashes in curving rows across the axis, with runs, gaps and a few brighter lights. The plume's far end was a solid gray blob. I tried to break it up with a dry navy scumble, but its "load" setting didn't make it thin and it buried the far end again. Two clean blender passes turned that into a faint blue cloud dispersing into space, and a sparse glitter of ice ties it to the vent. Lesson: at this easel a low `load` does not give a thin film. A thin film has to come from lifting.
