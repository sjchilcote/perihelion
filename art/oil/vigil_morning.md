# The Long Vigil: The First Night

This is the pair to *The Long Vigil*: the same two at the same spot on the
TESTAMENT's hull, centuries earlier, when the watch began. The Sun is still
far off. It is a small white star with a short-rayed corona hanging in a
deep blue-black sky full of stars. The bronze scale plates catch only a dim,
cool light, with a thin silvered line along the hull's horizon. The frame is
seen from behind, left of centre, just as it is in the late picture. It is
new: there is no repair plate yet, and a whole crimson tabard hangs down its
back with a bright gold-white cross on it. The sun's light picks out its edges
in a thin cold line. Its hand rests beside the same small lantern. Ratio's
window is lit and throws a warm pool across the plates, and the near hand
takes that light too. The pool is the only warm light in the picture. On the
right, the solar sail hangs whole and taut between yard and boom on its
lattice mast. It is a sheet of silver-blue film with one long blaze of the
far Sun reflected down its middle, a great mirror that will one day be torn
to tongues against a Sun that fills the sky.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 16 chunks on a dark blue-black ground at 1.6:1. I reused the
late painting's horizon curve, mast position and frame silhouette so the two
line up as a pair. I laid the sky in four horizontal piles, from bone black
and ultramarine at the top to a greyer violet-blue near the horizon. The hull
is cool bronze bands with about 1,400 scale lips drawn by a helper. Each lip
is a dark under-edge with a dim crest. The paint fought me in three places.
A stippled band of the galaxy came out as a hard pale stripe, so I painted it
back out. My first corona, built from soft glazes, dried as a hard pale disc.
Then a radial badger dragged the light into a toothed, cog-like halo. What
finally worked was graded rings laid along the circles and blended only at
their seams, as on the late Sun. My first sail, concentric rings around a
hotspot, read as a target, and my second came out as an hourglass. The third
is vertical bands of silver-blue around one reflected blaze, with rigger
streaks laid on dry paint. My first crests on the scale lips were far too
bright, so I scumbled them out and redrew them dimmer.

What still falls short: the corona still shows faint rings. There is a
lighter wedge in the lower sky, right of the sail, left over from the galaxy
repair. The cool rim runs all the way around the frame instead of only on
the side facing the Sun. The lamp pool's edge is ragged. Next time I would
test the sail's mirror sheen and the corona on a scrap easel first, instead
of painting them three times on the canvas.

## Pass at 00:28 CDT, 2 Oct 2026

What changed (chunks 17–22):
- **Corona.** The stepped rings are gone. I painted the glow again, smaller:
  short radial strokes in four piles graded by distance from the core, out
  to about 44 units, then sky paint brought back in around it. The piles are
  fused only along the radius, at narrow seams. The core is restated, with
  eight thin rigger rays, four of them long. My first try blended the whole
  annulus radially. That dragged the light out of the centre and left a
  mottled blue disc, so I repainted it once more over tacky paint with
  seam-only blends.
- **Sail.** I repainted it over dry paint in long straight flat-brush
  strokes graded silver-blue toward the edges. One long clean zinc-white
  blaze, laid on dry paint, runs down its middle. It has a lit top hem,
  darker side and bottom hems, and new rigging: a stay to the masthead and
  sheets down to the hull.
- **Rim light.** The frame's cold rim now stays only on the side facing the
  Sun (up and to the right). The far side and the lower edge are painted
  back into the frame's dark.

What still falls short: the sail is smoother and brighter but still not a
clean mirror. The flat brush's fill dabs left a horizontal brushed-metal
hatching across the bands. A faint lighter circle of repainted sky still
shows around the Sun at about 1.2 times the old halo's radius, and the
inner glow keeps a trace of stepping. The lighter wedge of sky right of the
sail, left over from the galaxy repair, is still there.
