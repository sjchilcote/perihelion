# The Crew's Window Seat

The best seat in the outpost, months before the fall. A round viewport in a
heavy riveted frame looks out on the Testament's bronze curve, its scale
plates catching the Sun. The Sun itself burns white-gold in the upper part
of the glass, inside a warm swirl of glare. Someone has strung small warm
fairy lights round the frame on a thin dark wire, swagged loosely, with one
loose end drooping toward the seat and a few red bulbs among the gold.
Beside the window, taped up crooked, is a child's crayon drawing of the
station: a big orange ring with a pole through it, a yellow sun with rays,
a tiny figure and a row of green grass. Below runs a padded green window
seat with buttoned cushions. On it are a rust-and-ochre knit blanket,
folded and spilling over the edge, a blue paperback left open face-down
like a small tent, a red mug with a thread of steam, and a pair of
headphones. No one is there. Warm, small, human.

## How it was painted

I made it in 8 chunks on a warm ground at 4:3. I graded the wall in nine
mixed piles in rings round the viewport, brightest at the window, then
blended only the seams. The view outside has sky rings round the Sun,
bronze bands and scale lips, all as in the other paintings of the set.
The objects went on in flat masses, then their details on dry paint. Two
things went wrong:
- A scumble meant as window light on the cushion dried as a lacy, blotchy
  oval, so I repainted that part of the seat opaque in two graded piles.
- The blanket's cast shadow came out as a black blob, and I painted it back
  to the seat front.

Still rough: the wall's rings show as a faint target round the window. The
blanket is a stiff block rather than soft wool. The sky in the window is a
swirl more than space.
