# The Handshake

After Klimt's golden period. The Commando and Ratio stand side by side on a flat gold ground, at their first contact. The Commando is a tall dark column filled with Klimt's rectilinear pattern: packed upright oblongs of black, steel-silver, crimson and gold, crowned with a row of gold bars over a visor slit. Ratio's avatar is a tall pale column of the curvilinear pattern: gold and blue discs and fine spirals. Her long black hair falls down one side, a gold diadem set with blue sits on it, and her pale face is turned slightly toward him with closed eyes and red lips. Between them, on a small dark terminal, his gauntlet plugs a connector into a glowing port, and her bare hand reaches from the other side, index finger extended, not quite touching. Where the arms meet, the patterns cross over: a few of his oblongs appear on her sleeve and a few of her discs on his. The gold ground carries spirals and fold-notation circles in darker gold, and a strip of small flowers runs along the bottom as in The Kiss. Only her face, his gauntlet and her hand are modelled; everything else is flat ornament.

How it was painted: 7 chunks, 0.9:1, on a gold ground. I laid flat opaque fields first, then the ornament as brush marks: flat-brush oblongs for him, round-brush discs with a second colour at the centre for her, and drawn spirals for both and the ground. The meeting point took the most work. My first gauntlet was a grey mitten and the first hand a pale cone. I cleared the hand back to gold and rebuilt it finger by finger, each with a shadow and a light, and rebuilt the gauntlet as segmented steel fingers wrapped round the plug. The terminal's gold frame vanished into the gold ground. The figures are stiff, column-like silhouettes rather than Klimt's interlocked embrace, and the meeting point is small in the composition. With more time I would have them lean together so the robes overlap into one ornamental mass, and make the hands larger and more central.
