# Sixteen Rooms, One Room

The view through a doorway into TESSERACT DECK. One bare room of cool bronze and bone, with no furniture, a dark ceiling and a pale floor. It has three doors besides the one we look through, and every door shows the same room again. Through the back door the room recurs smaller and turned a little further at each step, falling away to a point. Through the two side doors it recurs at a steeper turn, one to the left and one to the right, and each of those rooms has its own back door holding the room again. The Commando is the dark small figure in those rooms. He is seen from behind through the back door and side-on through the side doors, because each room is a different step around the 4-cube. The floor of each room carries its address in fold notation: the room-glyph, a notch on each edge whose bit is set (our room is 0000, so it has none), and a smaller copy of the room turned by the deck's twist. Each door flips one bit. Over the back door a bone fold-glyph shows the room with two arcs bridging back to itself and a golden-ratio inset turned inside it.

## How it was painted

Four chunks, in a hurry at the end of the session. The room is painted once, as a function: five faces, three door voids, cold hairline edges, bone door frames, the floor glyph and an optional figure, all in the room's own coordinates and passed through a transform of centre, scale and turn. A recursive helper calls it again inside each door, clipped to the door's opening, darker and colder at each step. The surprise was the walls. A dark glaze of medium, laid over them to cool and deepen them, dried as olive camouflage blotches, and blending did not save it, so I repainted them opaque in a cool dark bronze. With more time I would model the light falling off across the room, give the turns more depth steps, and set the Commando's figure more crisply against the bone floors.

## Second pass

One light now: the floor's own address glows cold, and the room falls away from it. The floor is repainted in four bands by distance from the glyph and blended, pale and cold at the glyph and dark bronze-gray toward the walls and the near edge. The walls are repainted opaque in two dark, worn bronze bands, which leaves a low pool of cold light where they meet the floor near the doors. They now carry plate courses and joints converging on the vanishing point, and a fold-notation inlay on each side wall. The glyph itself is restated in zinc white. The three nearest Commandos are repainted in dark armour with a thin cold edge, so they read against the rooms beyond. The lesson came twice: in this box a thin dark layer over light bronze, whether glaze or scumble, dries as camouflage mottling. Only opaque repainting in graded bands gave a clean falloff.
