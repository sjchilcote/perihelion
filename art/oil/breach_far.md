# The Far Side

Deep in the Adversary's space, far from anything that holds still. The Commando, in a later and heavier frame, crosses a bridge of tiles laid over nothing. He is broad and hunched, with a spur of plate jutting from one shoulder, his crimson rag torn into strips and the bone cross on his back. The tiles narrow as the road runs in, and some lift at their edges. Some are missing, and loose ones tumble down into the void below. Ahead, the whole space turns slowly round an eye, and around it hangs the invaders' staging-space. It is a mass of hinged, tiled planes in cold inverted colour, ice-blue, lime, magenta and violet, each unfolding into more of itself: big and bright near at hand, smaller and closer to the dark further in, until they are only specks round the eye. They read as organs of one distributed thing. The structure's cold light runs down his front and his staff. Far behind him, in the empty upper left, is the TESTAMENT: one tiny gold point, the last warm thing, with the faintest warmth on the side of him that faces home.

**How it was painted.** The visual language is the one I built for *Through the Breach*: flat hinged quads of body colour that only become floors once dry tile joints and bright hinge lines are drawn over them. This time I grew them from one small helper that lays a plane on a hinge and unfolds children off its far edge and its side, smaller and turned each time. I called it from far roots first and near roots last, so the near overlap the far and fainter piles carry the distance. The background was one body pass along a vortex field, blended along the same field, and the blend made a turning eye I hadn't planned and kept. The paint caught me twice. The warm stipple meant as the gold point's glow came up as a grey disc over the dark, and covering it left a faint cool halo, so the gold glow is mostly the core and its rays. The figure's dark legs laid over the light tiles came up pale, as dark over light does here, and had to be repainted heavier once the tiles were dry. Next time I would lay the figure's darks before the tiles under him. I would also keep the structure's joint lines inside each plane, because drawn across the overlaps they read a little like wireframe.
