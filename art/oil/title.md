# The Reliquary at the Limb

The Sun takes up the lower right of the picture. It is a white disc so close that only an arc of its edge fits on the canvas, with a looping red prominence on its rim and a short line of sunspots. Its light spreads out in rings: white-gold, then cadmium and Indian yellow, then vermilion, then a madder red that turns into a violet-black. That dark fills the upper-left third and is kept quiet so the word PERIHELION can sit there. The TESTAMENT lies across the edge of the Sun, lit from behind and almost entirely in silhouette. Its habitat ring is tilted like a halo. Its spine runs from a ribbed, flared maw in the upper left, past tanks covered in bronze scale-plates, to vault bays that point down into the Sun. The only lit surfaces face the Sun. The inner face of the upper ring arc shows as a crescent of bronze catching fire. Thin gold and orange rims run along the hull, and rows of small windows, a few of them lit warm, give the scale. The far end of the spine, which enters the light first, has fire licking along its underside. In the dark lower left a single cold spark, the Cuculus seed, trails toward the station. I wanted a Turner vortex with one of John Martin's architectural wonders sinking into it: a tomb with its lamps still lit, falling into a star.

How it was painted: I laid the glow on a toned brown ground as wet concentric bands, painted along the curve and blended. I painted the disc in lead white and let everything dry. I then cut the station in over it as one dark silhouette and built it up with golden-angle plate touches, rim light graded toward the Sun, ring seams and window rows. I tested each mix and technique on scrap replays before putting it on the canvas. Several things surprised me. Lead white reads as ivory, so zinc white with a breath of lemon chrome was the only way to make the disc look white. Every clipped pass through the glow left its mask's outline as a visible shape. One unclipped blend dragged wet glow across the dry station, and I had to repaint a whole section of it. A zinc veil over a dark turned it a dead cold grey, and an amber glaze laid on top of that outlined the old silhouette. Rim strokes laid on tacky paint broke up into stick-and-slip flames, which I kept. Transparent glazes over the dry glow (Indian yellow inside, rose madder outside) were what pulled the rays and an olive band into one Turner-like atmosphere. Next time I would plan the glare on the far spine from the start instead of finding it through repairs, and I would never let a blender near the station.

## Second pass

The director said the composition was right but the painting read as unfinished, so I kept the composition and worked on the surfaces. The biggest change is the corona. In v1 it was a glazed glow crossed by pale, glassy ray strips. I repainted all of it opaque, as radial streamer strokes in eleven graded bands from white-gold at the limb out to violet-black. Each band went wet into the next, and then a single badger pass along the radius pulled them into one gradient while keeping the streaks. The strips and the leftover glaze rectangles are gone, and the light now looks like it bursts out of the Sun. I painted out the dotted row of sunspots and put one group in its place: a large spot with a radial penumbra and a few small spots trailing it. I restated the prominence over the new corona and added a second, lower loop. I repainted the limb as a gradient laid along the curve of the edge. On the station, every scale-plate on the tanks now has a crescent of light on its sun-facing lip, so the tanks read as overlapping armour rather than spots. A dim warm bounce from the corona runs along the shadowed edges, so the silhouette reads as solid metal. The Cuculus spark and a few stars, buried by the repaint, are back. What surprised me: a soft clip mask let the outer corona fade into the dark the way an edge should, and a radial blend kept the stroke direction where an ordinary blend would have flattened it. The streaky limb and the spiky sunspot are what I'd still refine.

## Third pass

This pass went into the station, which still read as a flat black cut-out. The inner face of the far arc of the ring faces the Sun across the hole. It is now a broad band of bronze, graded from deep sienna where it turns away to a hot cadmium-ochre where it faces the light squarely. It has plate courses and rows of windows, and a broken hot lip where it turns into the hole, so the ring finally has thickness. The near arc, which lies against the disc, is no longer black. It is a dim warm bronze lit by light bouncing off the corona, with dark edges, so it reads as a rounded band in shadow, and new window rows give it scale. Each tank has a crescent of light on its sun side that is wide where the form faces the light and thins away from it, with a core shadow inside the crescent. Where a neighbouring part blocks the light, the crescent breaks off. The spine is modelled as a cylinder with a dim bounce down its middle. Its joint rings catch light on their sun-side ends, the spokes are lit along one edge, and the far arc has one cool dark where it faces the void. I also relaid the limb band in long strokes along the curve, softened the sunspot and faded the seed's trail into the corona. Two things I learned again: a yellow glaze over a cool brown goes olive (I repainted that face opaque), and seams drawn on tacky paint break into ragged relief. I kept the second because at full size it reads as carved, worn bronze.
