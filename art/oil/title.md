# The Reliquary at the Limb

The Sun takes up the lower right of the picture. It is a white disc so close that only an arc of its edge fits on the canvas, with a looping red prominence on its rim and a short line of sunspots. Its light spreads out in rings: white-gold, then cadmium and Indian yellow, then vermilion, then a madder red that turns into a violet-black. That dark fills the upper-left third and is kept quiet so the word PERIHELION can sit there. The TESTAMENT lies across the edge of the Sun, lit from behind and almost entirely in silhouette. Its habitat ring is tilted like a halo. Its spine runs from a ribbed, flared maw in the upper left, past tanks covered in bronze scale-plates, to vault bays that point down into the Sun. The only lit surfaces face the Sun. The inner face of the upper ring arc shows as a crescent of bronze catching fire. Thin gold and orange rims run along the hull, and rows of small windows, a few of them lit warm, give the scale. The far end of the spine, which enters the light first, has fire licking along its underside. In the dark lower left a single cold spark, the Cuculus seed, trails toward the station. I wanted a Turner vortex with one of John Martin's architectural wonders sinking into it: a tomb with its lamps still lit, falling into a star.

How it was painted: I laid the glow on a toned brown ground as wet concentric bands, painted along the curve and blended. I painted the disc in lead white and let everything dry. I then cut the station in over it as one dark silhouette and built it up with golden-angle plate touches, rim light graded toward the Sun, ring seams and window rows. I tested each mix and technique on scrap replays before putting it on the canvas. Several things surprised me. Lead white reads as ivory, so zinc white with a breath of lemon chrome was the only way to make the disc look white. Every clipped pass through the glow left its mask's outline as a visible shape. One unclipped blend dragged wet glow across the dry station, and I had to repaint a whole section of it. A zinc veil over a dark turned it a dead cold grey, and an amber glaze laid on top of that outlined the old silhouette. Rim strokes laid on tacky paint broke up into stick-and-slip flames, which I kept. Transparent glazes over the dry glow (Indian yellow inside, rose madder outside) were what pulled the rays and an olive band into one Turner-like atmosphere. Next time I would plan the glare on the far spine from the start instead of finding it through repairs, and I would never let a blender near the station.
