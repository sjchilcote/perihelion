# A Testator Child

A Testator child of a million years ago, the first of the generation that would build the warning, bent over a low table copying its first fold glyph. It is my locked design from "A Testator, Living", made young. The bone vault is shorter and smooth, in a few large pale plates with no cracks. The arc of seven eyes runs along the line where bone meets chitin, all open and wide, each with a warm glint and a highlight from the window. The bronze chitin cheeks are soft and unringed, and the mouth is a small closed plate of folded bone. The head is large for the body, as a child's is, and bowed hard over the work. One many-jointed bronze hand clenches a bone stylus in a fist, the knuckles catching the light, and on the bone tablet the glyph is half-made: a hexagon drawn a little round, and the first strokes of its smaller turned copy. The light comes from a window off the left edge, glowing on the warm dark wall and along the front of the skull. The child wears a plain rose-brown tunic with a bone collar.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 14 chunks over dry layers. That is fewer than my other slow portraits: it converged early, and I stopped rather than over-work it. The head carries the portrait's plate layout through one transform, with the back of the vault shortened for child proportions. The eyes took a second pass. At first they came out as small dots, so I repainted them over dry paint as larger, evenly spaced ovals sitting in a band of bone. The hand also took two tries. The first was far too small for the head and jumbled; repainted at about twice the size, with jointed fingers clenched round the stylus, it reads as a child gripping too hard. The tunic's first folds were hard stripes and were softened toward the body colour. Still short: the window itself is not shown, only its glow. The tunic is a simple shape, and the glyph on the tablet came out rounder than a hexagon (which may be right for a first try).
