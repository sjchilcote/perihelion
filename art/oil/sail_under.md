# Under the Sail

Noon near perihelion, on the hull, standing in the sail's shadow and looking up. The sail fills the sky as a vast dark film. Behind it the Sun shows through the thin material as a soft gold disc, with rays of light leaking through the film all around it. The film's pleats and seams lie silhouetted across that light: long dark lines radiating from the hub straight overhead, crossed by two concentric seams. Along the far rim, where the sail's edge arcs across the top of the picture, light leaks past in a thin white ring and flares into the black. Below, the hull is in deep shade, its plates only faintly visible by the warm glow from the film. On its crest stand the Commando and four docents, small dark silhouettes, faces turned up. The light catches only the tops of their heads, the docents' lenses and the Commando's visor. One docent has lifted a long arm toward the sail, and the crimson rag stirs at the Commando's shoulder.

**How it was painted.** It took 11 chunks on a warm dark ground. My first sail was an ellipse with space all around it, so it read as a floating saucer. I repainted everything below its edge as film, so that the sail fills the sky and only the top corners stay black. The glow first came out as concentric target rings. About 220 long radiating gold strokes laid over it, and then the dark pleat lines, each laid in three segments with fresh paint, turned it into light seen through a pleated film. The figures went on last, in near-black, lit only from above.

**Still short:**
- The docents read a little like chess pieces.
- The rim's flare strokes look like short spikes.
- The concentric rings still faintly show around the glow.
- The shaded hull is a large, plain area.
