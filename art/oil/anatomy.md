# The Anatomy Lesson (after Rembrandt)

Seven living Testators gather in a dark, warm room round a bronze table, as Dr Tulp's surgeons gathered: six crowd in a pyramid at the left, and the lecturer sits at the right. On the table lies the first captured Adversary organ, a pale violet thing in nested folds, each fold a smaller turned copy of the one around it. The lecturer reaches across with a dark sleeve and a bone cuff, and lifts its outer fold with long bronze tongs held in many-jointed fingers. The nearest onlooker throws up a hand of jointed bronze fingers, in wonder or alarm. Light from a window at the upper left falls on their long skulls. Every head uses the design from "A Testator, Living", painted large enough to read at full view: a long skull of pale bone plates swept up and back, darker at the back; an arc of five small hooded eyes along the line where bone meets chitin; bronze chitin cheek and jaw plates ringed with growth lines; a small mouth plate of folded bone with a dark seam. The jaws nearest the organ catch its cold violet light. Each wears a pleated bone ruff, lit on the window side, over a black robe. An open book lies at the table's near corner.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 23 chunks over dry layers. After the director's note on The Last Assembly (faces read as brown human faces with white grins), I locked the design before painting. Each head is the Testator portrait's head built at large scale from one plate layout: six bone plates, four chitin plates, an eye arc and a mouth plate. It is transformed per figure (position, scale, facing, bow), with each plate's value set by its facing to the window, so no figure ever gets a flat face oval or a light mouth stroke. The skulls first all blazed equally white and competed with the organ, so I turned the back-of-skull plates down a step. The organ, laid as wet nested lobes, went muddy; repainted as separate rims over dry paint, it reads as folds within folds. Repainting the robes covered the onlooker's raised hand, which I restored. A grey patch from the dead colour hid under the table's edge until I traced it to the table mask. Still short of Rembrandt: the robes are flat dark bells, and the light is even across the group rather than falling hardest on the organ and the nearest heads. The eye arcs are small at full view.
