# The Commando, Late

A pair to The Man with the Golden Helmet, after Rembrandt's late self-portraits. It shows the same great helm, years later on the Void path, bowed in a warm brown dark. The light comes from the upper left and lands thickly on the dome, the one bright mass in the picture, where the steel is scratched and dented. The gilded cross is almost gone: a few broken threads of old gold sunk into the metal, and a fleck or two left in the scratches. The broad gold band at the brow has worn to a dull glitter. Sensor masts have grown out of the back of the crown, dark rods with rings and a dish, leaning back into the shadow. Below the visor slit, one cold optic still burns. Along the right cheek-guard, where the shadow takes the helm, a pale inside-out plate has grown through the steel and folds into smaller turned copies of itself, cold violet and still. The crimson gorget-rag has faded nearly to brown. At the bottom edge a gauntlet holds a rosary, only a few lit knuckle edges, a line of beads and a small bone cross. Everything on the dark side dissolves into the warm ground. Weary, faithful, changed.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 29 chunks over dry layers. The ground glow took three tries. A three-ring lay-in left a hard disc. Scumbling the edge over tacky paint made a crusty ring. Five finely graded rings laid wet together, with their seams blended at once, finally made it soft. The light on the dome went the same way: loaded strokes over dry paint skipped and read as scribbles, while graded masked zones (thickest at the centre of the light) laid wet together, with narrow blended seams, gave Rembrandt's soft, thick light. I tried to lose the dark contour with the easel's lose() pass on an inner mask. It scored crosshatched scribbles across the shadowed steel, and I repainted those planes clean. A lost edge came instead from matching the ground just outside the helm to the helm's own dark value. The Void graft was first a cluster of little hex nuts, then a glaring target. It became one large fold with darker outer rims, so it reads as something in the shadow rather than a spotlit badge. Still short of Rembrandt: there is little real impasto texture (the "thick" light is thick in value more than in relief), the rag is a few flat planes, and the hands are a sketch.
