# The Tower of Babel

After Bruegel's "The Tower of Babel". The TESTAMENT is being raised a million years ago, as the Testators' last act. A colossal tiered tower rises out of a warm plain under a huge pale sky: seven drums stacked on a cone, each tilted a little upward to the right, as if spiralling. Each drum is wrapped in rows of piers and dark arches that crowd and foreshorten toward its edges. Light comes from the left, so the left face is pale ochre and the right turns into burnt-sienna shade. Pale ledges run round each tier, and tiny workers stand along them. The top two tiers are unfinished and ragged. They carry fresh, brighter bronze scale-plate cladding, a cage of scaffolding, and treadwheel cranes with long jibs, and a long low cloud drifts across them. Three dark gates open at the foot, and figures stream along the road. On the left, the solar sail lies furled in a long cradle like stacked timber beside a little town of steep red roofs. On the right is a harbour where small craft arrive with cargoes of bronze plates, and haulers line the quay. A Sun much larger than ours hangs pale in the sky to the upper right.

How it was painted: 6 chunks, 1.4:1, on a pale ground. The sky is four graded bands blended horizontally while wet, and the land is three flat bands. The tower's tiers are masked polygons laid in three vertical lit, middle and shaded zones. Over them, each tier is repainted as a drum: a flat-brush pier for each step of angle round the cylinder, foreshortened by sine and shaded by its angle to the light, with round-brush arch openings, two storeys on the lower tiers. That turned a stepped pyramid into a round tower. Scaffolds, cranes, workers, ships, the town and the sail's cradle are each a few strokes. Still short: the tower is a smooth cone where Bruegel's is a rough, rocky, half-collapsed spiral. The arches are bristly blobs rather than crisp openings. The furled sail reads as a pale smear. The sky is very empty, and the whole is more toy-like than Bruegel's dense swarm.
