# The Observatory, Years Later

The same observatory, years later on the trust path, at night. The bronze dome still closes overhead, with its ribs, its rings of latitude and its star-map of golden-angle bone spirals and fold-notation circles. The slot is now open on night: deep blue-black, thick with small stars. The Sun is far behind now. The only light in the room is a small lantern set on the floor beside the great telescope's drum. It warms the floor in a soft pool, the right side of the telescope's banded bronze tube, the edges of its fork and drum, and the figures nearest it. The starlight leaves only a cold glint on the objective ring. The docent is still at the eyepiece in its high-backed chair, bone face lifted to the glass. Now a crew child in small grey coveralls sits on its knee, held there by its arm, face lifted beside the docent's to share the eyepiece. On the floor two more children lie on their backs across the rail ring, faces up to the painted sky of the dome. The one by the lantern is lit warm along its side.

**How I painted it.** I replayed the first painting's own chunks for the room: the canvas, the value structure, the dome's ribs, the orrery spirals and the scattered stars. The slot, telescope, figures and light are new. The telescope is modelled in zones across its width as in the earlier pass, but now lit from below and to the right by the lantern rather than by the Sun. On dry paint I added the bands with lit lips and the objective ring. Things that went wrong: a pale stippled galactic band in the slot came out as a loud diagonal stripe, so I repainted the slot and restated the stars. The lantern's pool, laid after the lantern, half covered it, and I restated it. The floor children were first placed too low and cut by the frame, so I painted them out and moved them up. Still short: the children are small flat silhouettes, the fork arms read as dark shapes with little modelling, and the docent's left side has little light.
