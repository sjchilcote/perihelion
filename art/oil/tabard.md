# Taking Up the Tabard

This painting closes the trilogy after The First Death and The Second Frame. It is the same MIRIAD corridor, under the same amber hazard lamp, with the seed's warm glow at the far end. The wreck is gone, hauled to the seed for parts. Where it lay, a sooty, body-shaped burn is left on the grating, with a few spent casings. The new frame stands in the middle of that burn with its back half-turned to us, facing down the corridor. It is built as armour: helm, gorget, pauldrons with lames, back plate, faulds, cuisses with knee cops, and greaves. With its right hand raised, it is pulling the old crimson tabard up over its shoulders. The tabard is torn and scorched at the hem and holed in two places. The bone cross on it is broken across the middle and mended with crossed wire. The rosary is already wound three times round the left gauntlet, with its crucifix hanging below. Its long shadow lies back across the burn toward us, and its optic just shows as a warm point past the visor edge.

I rebuilt the corridor by re-running my own Second Frame chunks with the same coordinates and without either frame. This time I drew the figure as plates from the start, as the brief asked. Each plate is its own polygon, cored in a lean dark grey, with a narrow mid plane on the side facing the lamp and thin amber only on the edges that face it. The tabard went on in deep crimson on fully dry paint, and its folds, scorch, cross, wire and hem went on after that. The burn mark gave the most trouble. It came out first as a crisp outline that looked like a chalk crime-scene outline, then as a solid black pit, then as grey grit. A floor scumble wiped it out, and a final soot scumble left it as a dark body-shaped burn. It is still heavier and more literal than I would like. Still short: the figure stands inside the burn rather than walking clearly away from it. The raised arm reads a little like a wave. The armour is clean but simple.

## Pass at 02:54 CDT, 2 October

Chunks 20–28, all on dry paint.
- **Burn.** I painted out the old figure, its shadow and the body-shaped burn, using the wall's own ground mix on the wall and the floor mix on the deck. Then I restated the ribs, rail, edge and grating. In place of the burn there is now a soft, irregular soot patch with no body shape, the grating laid back through it, a few soot and heat-blue streaks along the slats, and six spent casings, each with an amber glint.
- **Stance.** I redrew the frame further down the corridor at 0.86 scale, mid-step, with the scorch behind it. The left foot is planted, and the right foot trails with its heel up and its sole showing.
- **Arm.** The right arm now bends back so the hand grips the tabard's corner at the right shoulder.
- **Back plate.** The tabard hangs lower on the left, so the back plate shows above it with its central ridge, two shoulder lames and the pauldron lames. The tabard is again deep crimson laid on dry paint. It is scorched and holed, and the broken bone cross is mended with crossed wire. The rosary is wound on the left gauntlet with the crucifix hanging.

Still short:
- The soot patch is blotchy rather than smoothly graded, and the heat-blue streaks barely show.
- The patched wall where the old figure stood is a shade lighter than the rest.
- The figure's lit bands still lean toward an outline in places.
