# Hydroponics, Before the Fall

This is the pair to *Hydroponics Torus*: the same garden deck from the same
spot, months earlier, while the crew were alive. The floor runs away from
our feet, narrows at the waist and comes back over our heads, so the far end
of the garden hangs at the top of the picture. Now every tray is full. Dense
dark-green crop rises from our floor and hangs down from the floor overhead,
lit yellow-green and pink-white along its crowns. The trays' front lips are
clean and grey with water glinting on them, and pale irrigation spray stands
in rows over the beds. Three grow-lamps blaze in the same places where the
dead painting has three dark fixtures: two over our floor, one over the
floor overhead. Each is a heavy housing on rods with a white-hot lens edged
in magenta, and pink light falls from it onto the leaves. The window on the
left shows only deep space, a scatter of stars and a small, far Sun behind
the same heavy MIRIAD grid. Amber hazard strips run along the feet of both
walls, irrigation lines climb the right wall, and a printed placard in an
amber frame hangs there. Nobody is in sight, but someone was just here. A
grey work cart with a flat of seedlings stands at the near beds, a pair of
yellow gloves is left on a tray lip, and the yellow watering can stands
upright at the same spot where the dead painting has it dropped. The same
can sits overhead, properly half-turned.

## How it was painted

I made it in 13 chunks on a grey-green ground at 1.6:1. I reused my own
geometry from the dead painting: the hourglass floor from one width
function, the bed rows spaced by a power curve, and the same window. That
way the two hang as an exact pair. Some things went wrong along the way:
- My first plants were single stems with four leaf dabs and read as
  seedlings. I laid a dark-green mass over each bed, far beds first, then
  stippled lit leaves on it, then put the tray lips and lamp-lit leaves back
  on dry paint.
- The masses overran the walls in stepped rectangles, so I repainted the
  walls along the floor's edge.
- My first lamp blooms went on as solid pink lozenges. A badger made them
  worse, flattening them into bigger opaque ellipses. I painted them out,
  wall, crop and tray lips included, then built the lamps again: heavy
  housings, magenta lenses with a zinc-white core, and a broken pale scumble
  for the light falling below.
- An early lead-white glaze for the lamp light dried as rows of vertical
  white streaks. I kept them as the irrigation spray.

Still rough: the walls are large flat lilac areas with too little incident.
The lamps light the crop but not the walls. The placard's white went yellow
over the wet amber frame. The near edge of the crop is a hard horizontal
line.
