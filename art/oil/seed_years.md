# Compline, Years Later

The same seed, the same view down its tunnel of heavy dark ribs, years later on the trust path. The fabricator mouth is banked low: its throat a deep red-orange and a small gold glow lying at the bottom of it. The rack at the left edge that once held spare frames now holds a child's faded blue coverall, with a red patch on one knee, and a bone tablet on a string, scratched with lines. Taped to a rib on the right is a crayon drawing: the big knight in his red tabard, a small figure beside him and a little sun. In the sacred corner the scrap-metal cross, the crimson cloth and the candle-LED are all as they were, the candle still lit. On the crate where the frame sat at Compline there is now a little bed: a pale pillow, a red blanket the colour of the old rag, folded over a small sleeping shape with dark hair. The Commando's frame sits on the floor beside it with his legs folded and his helm bowed. His one cold optic is turned toward the child, and the rosary hangs from his gauntlet, the low glow catching his edges. Warm, quiet, kept.

How it was painted: the geometry is the earlier painting's own, the same hull rings, ribs, deck and fabricator collar. This time every pale thing (the coverall, tablet, drawing and pillow) was set aside before the hull went down, so it was painted later on bare ground and dry paint, and stayed clean. The deck, mouth and darks were painted around the figures. The pale things, the warm edges and the small marks came last, after days of drying. Still weak: the frame is a simple dark silhouette with a few seams rather than modelled plate, the coverall reads a little too pale for the gloom at the edge, and the deck shows a step at the left where I repainted a strip.
