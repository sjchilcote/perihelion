# Deck Plate, Gauntlet Issue

A tileable floor of MIRIAD prefab deck plate, seen straight down under flat light. It is a 4 x 4 grid of identical 250-unit plates (seams on 0/250/500/750/1000, so they wrap), each with the same ring of 28 rivets set 14 units in from the seam. Four plates are treadplate with diamond lugs, one is cut into a four-pane hatch on the 125 subdivision, and one is a mismatched replacement plate in a greener gray, patched with two welded strips with a heat-tinted halo. Two more welded patches sit on other plates. The grays run cool with a faint verdigris cast, and each plate's mix is slightly different so the floor reads plate by plate at game zoom. The wear is spread evenly: grime showing through where the top coat is broken, boot-polished streaks, dark scuffs, hairline scratches, and rust blooming at about one rivet in five plus a dozen bigger rust stains at seams. Nothing large crosses the canvas edge except two small rust stains, and each of those got a matching piece on the opposite edge. There is no hazard striping, because it would repeat in every room.

How it was painted: a toned gray knife ground, then plate fills with filbert strokes along alternating grain. After that came a grime layer, which went badly. Noise-driven scumbles laid opaque camouflage blotches, a clean brush could barely lift them, and the badger flattened whole plates into gray soup, along with the first treadplate lugs and sub-seams. The fix was to use that mess as an underpainting. Once it was dry, I brushed each plate's own gray over it at partial coverage without blending, so the grime shows through between real strokes. That is the best paint in the picture. The seams went through a similar cycle: grime laid into them while they were still wet smeared them into wide tan bars, so I repainted a lip of plate color around every plate and left a 10-12 unit dark gasket. Lead white in the rust and scorch mixes turned them salmon and violet, so they were knocked back with leaner darks once dry. Next time I would lay the grime field thin and lean first, under the plate color, and never blend whole plates.
