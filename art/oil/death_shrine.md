# Where He Fell, Years Later

This is the same MIRIAD corridor, from the same eye and with the same coordinates as The First Death, The Second Frame and Taking Up the Tabard, years later on the trust path. The corridor is warm and clean. The walls are a lit ochre with dark ribs, and the grating floor runs in perspective to the far doorway. Where the amber hazard lamp hung on the right wall, a warm working light now burns in a plain housing and lights that wall gold. There are no figures. Where the first frame fell, a small shrine sits on the grating:
- a bronze plate set into the floor, with rows of cut inscription, a cross at its end and a lit lip along its far edge;
- a candle-LED burning at its edge;
- beside the candle, a grey tin of white bone-flowers.

On the left wall above, the first frame's broken visor is mounted on a bracket, cracked through, with a scrap of crimson tied to it. Beside it is a child's taped drawing of a knight in a red tabard with a white cross, a sword and a yellow sun.

I rebuilt the corridor by re-running my Second Frame chunks, which themselves rebuild The First Death. I swapped the dark blue-black piles for warm ochre and raw sienna, so the light model that once graded an amber hazard lamp now grades a warm working light. I left out every figure, and the amber lamp became a light panel. Then I laid the shrine on dry paint. The working light's first housing came out with a ragged dark surround, which I repainted as a clean frame with a soft radial bloom. The inscription first disappeared into the plate, and I cut it again deeper. Still short: the bronze plate reads a little like a mat lying on the grating rather than set into it, the visor is simple, and the left wall is a flat warm field.
