# The Bandage and the Wound

Looking straight down PERIHELION CORE. The Testament's last structure is an iris of handmade bronze scale-plates. Each one is the size of a district, with rows of windows, and they are laid on a golden-angle spiral that closes inward on the Breach. The defenses face the hole: bone-ringed turret sockets aim their barrels at it, and three tiny Testator sentries stand on the rim. Each stands on its own plate with its feet toward the station, so the one at the top hangs head-down over the void. That is locally coherent and globally wrong. Through the hole the colour is inverted. Where space should be black, the Breach is a pale, cold, breathing vortex of zinc-white, cerulean, lavender and rose. Its stars are dark specks. Ghost arches of a corridor that runs into itself turn down toward a black sun with a white limb, and dark tendrils swirl out of that sun toward the right side of the rim. There the bandage is failing. The plates are cut by straight lines and their inner parts are turned inside out: pale bodies, dark lips, warm windows turned to cold indigo. Shards break off, shrink and drift into the hole. Over them lies a warped grid, the sterile lattice that the Adversary perceives ordinary space to be. The visual language for the team: warm, worn bronze and gold windows mean the Testators; pale-for-dark and cold-for-warm mean the Adversary; straight cuts, repeated shards and a lattice mark the places where one becomes the other.

## How it was painted

43 chunks on a warm dark ground (bone brown, burnt sienna, ultramarine). The bronze was laid in with broad tangential strokes. The breach went in as graded pale piles, blended inside its mask. I painted each plate with a helper that lays dark axial strokes, then two lighter arcs wet into wet near the rounded tip, then a cold lip. The plates were laid on a log spiral from the far inner ring outward, so nearer plates overlap farther ones. What surprised me, and cost the most, was the breach mask. It still covered the plate tips that had since been painted over the rim, so both a zinc veil and a later swirl repaint flooded the inner plates. Each time, four passes of a clean scumble-blender over the wet paint lifted it off the dry plates almost perfectly. My first idea for "rooms that connect to themselves" was thick navy ribbon arches with white cut-strips, and it read like a sticker, so it is now only thin ghost lines in the vortex. Warm windows laid into a wet dark stroke went olive; laid straight onto dry bronze they stayed gold. Next time I would build a mask of what is actually visible before any pass near an edge. I would also give the far bronze more variety in light, because the outer third is a little monotonous.

## Second pass

The director asked for massive plates instead of scallops, a Breach that falls away, and inside-out plates that are visibly the *same* objects. I repainted all 119 plates with a new helper. Each plate gets its own size, tilt and tone from a deterministic hash. Each casts a dark shadow just past its free edge onto the plate below, which gives the plate thickness. Each has a dark base, a mid-tone turn, a sheen arc and a cold lip. Plates on the infected side get a straight cut, and the inner part is repainted with the same helper in an inverted palette: pale body, pale "shadow", dark indigo lip, cold windows. That inverted paint has to go on after the bronze has set. Laid wet-on-wet, the first attempt came out beige. A dark glaze, graded by distance and blended over the tacky near plates, pushes the plates toward the frame into shadow, so the outer third reads as nearer and deeper. The windows follow each plate's curve, two rows on the big plates. The gold lights went on dry in a separate pass that recomputes the same positions, because gold laid into a wet dark slot goes olive.

The Breach is now six bands of deepening cold violet spiralling down to the black sun. The arches of the self-connecting corridor are pale frames with shadowed reveals, getting smaller and turning as they fall away. A spiral of streaks, blended along its own direction, gives the vortex motion. Things that went wrong: verdigris crevice dabs scattered as small green flecks rather than lodging in the recesses, and a `lose()` pass across the swirl's edge tinted the sentries, which then had to be restated.
