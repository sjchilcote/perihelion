# The Fall of the Testament

A companion to "Perihelion", in the register of Turner's Snow Storm and Slave Ship. The same vortex has turned violent. Gold, vermilion and orange wheel around a white eye, and a ring of bruised storm weather in bone brown and violet-gray closes in on it, heaviest at the upper left and lower right. From the lower left, the cold violet of the Breach tears up through the gold toward the eye as a pale, veiled wedge, edged by one crisp cold line and one dark one. Caught at the vortex's left edge, the station is breaking: a heavy dark stern still holds a row of warm lights, which go out toward the break. The bow is snapped off and tilted harder into the vortex, already being eaten by the glare, and torn spars fly along the curve.

How it was painted: 9 chunks, 3:2, on a pale lead-white ground. Almost everything is free filbert strokes drawn along a vortex field (no masks): about 400 warm strokes, then the breach, the storm ring and the wet station, each picked by where it falls. Then full and partial badger blends ran along the vortex. The first warm layer was graphic, like a target. Dragging a clean badger by hand did almost nothing, and only a full blend() fused the stripes into weather. Once the paint was dry, the dry-brushed violet tear came out as a flight of blue dashes. Blending it wet inside a band left the band's straight edges, so the Breach now reads as a veiled beam more than a rip. The station's hard notes went on over dry paint as heavy filbert strokes, and gold and storm scumbles along the vortex then broke the bow apart. The warm window lights were partly lost under them. With more time I would lay the violet in the first wet layer, so it tears through the gold instead of veiling it, keep the lights crisper, and go to the 15–25 chunks the brief suggests.
