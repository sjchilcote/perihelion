# The Particle Storm

Close to perihelion, on the TESTAMENT's outer hull. The Sun is a white blaze at the left edge, and its hard light rakes across a field of bronze scale-plates, each with a lit lip on its sunward side and a black shadow under it. The Commando stands braced on the curve of the hull, clipped by a line to a rail that runs across it. He is a black frame against the lit bronze, rimmed hard white on every edge that faces the Sun, with one cold point at his visor, and his long black shadow is thrown to the right. No wind moves him. The storm is light and charge. St Elmo's fire streams up off his helm crest and pauldrons in tangles of thin white filaments. Smaller violet filaments flicker off the lip of every plate, off the rail and round his outline, and gather at rivet points. Above, the sky is alive with streaming aurora: sheets of green and violet hang in curtains over the black.

**How it was painted.** I used the lessons of the night. The ground was near-black, so the sky needed no pale field. The aurora is saturated green and violet strokes with no white in them, laid as sheets and then hanging rays, so nothing could go grey. The hull went in as two bronze values, the plates as rigger strokes (a black crescent, then a sunward lit lip) with the scale growing toward us. The figure and his shadow are lean bone black, restated once the hull was dry. The discharge is the radiance technique from *Inside the Egg*: several hundred thin rigger strokes on dry paint, each starting at a source and wandering off in four jittered segments to nothing. Whitest and longest off him, violet and short off the plates. One thing went wrong. I tried to light the whole sunward half of his figure, and the light mix came up pale and blotched and split him down the middle. Repainting him wholly black with hard white rims was both simpler and truer to vacuum light. Next time I'd give the discharge on the sunlit plates more violet saturation so it carries against the bronze, soften the Sun's ragged rim into a true blaze, and model the figure's armour more.

## Pass at 04:22 CDT, Oct 2

Chunks 10–17. I painted the sky back to black around a **clean, hard-edged white sun disc**, and repainted the hull under the old scribbles, then redrew its plates and the rail with posts. **The figure** is now a war-body in black:
- a great helm with a crest and a cold point at the visor slit, and broad pauldrons;
- a breastplate, faulds, plated thighs and greaves;
- a braced, wide stance with the left gauntlet down on the rail.

A hard white edge runs along the sunward contour. **The St Elmo's fire** is now concentrated at a few points only: the helm crest, both pauldron tips, the gauntlet on the rail, the five rail posts and a few rivets. Each has a white core, a violet halo and a few crisp branching filaments reaching up and out. **The aurora** took three tries:
1. Opaque curtains, too heavy.
2. Black taken up into them, which erased them.
3. Broad blended sheets. The blender dragged everything into a flat block with cut mask edges, so I painted it out.

The final version is three folded curtains, green, violet and green, each a hem of soft rays rising and fading to nothing, with no blending.

Still short: the curtains read a little combed rather than smooth and translucent. The figure's interior white lines from the first rim pass sit inside the silhouette like plate seams. The helm is blocky.
