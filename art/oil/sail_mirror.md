# In the Mirror of the Sail

The Commando stands on the sail itself. The dark mirror-film stretches away to a faintly curved horizon and reflects the black sky, so he walks on a field of stars. The Sun blazes above him and again beneath his boots. He is a small war-body silhouette striding toward the light, rimmed gold on his sunward side, with the crimson rag at his shoulder. Upside down beneath him walks his reflection, a shade cooler, its rim fainter. A seam of the film runs from the near corner away to the horizon past his feet as a thin bright line. Far off along the curve, the TESTAMENT hangs small on its tethers, and it too stands on its own reflection. The two halves are nearly the same, broken only by the film's slightly bluer black and the line of the seam.

**How it was painted.** It took 12 chunks on a blue-black ground. Each Sun was painted separately with the long corona strokes: a disc, a dense inner corona of short strokes, broad soft rays and long spikes. The reflected Sun was laid with its own fresh strokes, and the reflections of the figure and the station were drawn again by hand beneath them, rather than computed as flips. The film is a breath bluer than the sky, so the horizon holds without a drawn line. Two fixes were needed:
- My "faint sheen" strokes on the film dried as flat grey bars. I repainted the whole film and laid the lower Sun again.
- The figure first came out brownish and the reflection nearly vanished. A second coat of true near-black fixed both.

**Still short:** the Suns read as gold starbursts more than as soft glare, the film has no sheen of its own now, and the figure is small at full view.
