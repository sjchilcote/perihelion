# The Long Hall of the Spine

We look down the TESTAMENT's main artery from one end: a single straight tube-hall of bronze ribs crowding toward a tiny white-gold vanishing point miles away. The walls between the ribs are painted as surfaces on a fourteen-step ramp, lean dark around us and warming through bronze and gold toward the glow at the far end. Each rib has a warm lit inner lip, with a fainter seam halfway between each pair. Every other rib carries a lamp-ring: a ring of small warm lights in bronze housings near us, shrinking into a long string of pearls into the distance. Dark arched openings to the vault bays line both walls and recede in step with the ribs. A rail runs down the centre of the floor on dark sleepers, and in the middle distance a cargo sled sits on it, a small dark box lit along its top edge. In the foreground the Commando stands tiny on the rail line with his back to us: red tabard, grey cross, a warm rim on his helm, his shadow running down the rail toward us.

How it was painted: the maw method, exactly. The tunnel went in first as fourteen perspective sections whose spacing compresses toward the vanishing point, each band with its own pile. After it dried came the ribs (dark ring, lit lip, half-seam), then the lamp-rings, arches, rail and sleepers, the sled and the figure, all on dry paint. One surprise: the nearest lamps first came out as starbursts, so I gave them bronze housings, an amber lens and a hot core instead. What falls short: the large side arches at mid-height read as flat tombstone shapes that do not follow the wall's curvature. The floor reads less clearly than the ceiling, and the near-frame darks are a little empty. Still, the depth and the sense of an endless orderly hall come from the space itself.
