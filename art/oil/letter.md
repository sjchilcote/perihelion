# The Letter Home

A crew bunk in the dead MIRIAD outpost, as its owner left it. The blanket is still rumpled on the narrow bunk and the pillow still dented, and a pair of worn slippers waits on the floor below. On the fold-down desk sit a mug, a small pot of dried stalks that was once a plant, and a handheld reader still lit. On its warm screen are four lines of a message, the last one stopping short at a cursor. Above it, taped to the bulkhead, is a photograph from home: a sunlit beach, blue sky, a grown-up, two children and a dog on the sand. Its colours are the only daylight in the room. The only lights are the screen's warm glow, which spreads in a small pool up the wall to the photo, and a cold strip of emergency light along the ceiling at the upper left, which catches the tops of the blanket's rumples. From the right edge an armoured gauntlet rests on the desk's lip, fingers curled, barely touching. The Commando is reading it.

How it was painted: value structure first. The dark cabin, a warm pool and a cold wash were laid in on a dark ground, then bunk, desk and objects as lean darks, then the lights last on dry paint: screen, photo, cold rims on the blanket, warm edges on the gauntlet's fingers facing the screen, and the mug rim. The paint pushed back in three places. My first warm pool dried as a hard-edged disc, then as a target of rings. It only became a glow when I repainted it in three tones with blends across each seam. The dark under the photo and the screen came up through the first pale coats, which suits the photo (it reads as old and faded) but made the screen blotchy, so the screen was repainted and the message written again. A blend near the cold wash dragged warm paint into a brown rectangle, which had to be painted out with rough-edged dark. Still weak: the blanket front is a flat slab, the slippers almost vanish, the desk-top glow is gritty stipple, and a faint ghost of the patched rectangle remains near the strip light.

## Pass at 02:56 CDT (2 Oct)

What changed:
- **Upper left.** I painted out the speckled grey-blue patch under the strip light in the room's dark. The cold light is now two soft horizontal bands, only slightly lighter than the dark, fading down the wall from the strip, with a cold line just under the strip housing and a cold glint along the top of the bunk frame.
- **Photo.** Repainted clean on dry paint: a pale blue sky, a saturated blue sea, a pale sand band and a white border. On the sand are a grown-up in red and blue, two small children in yellow and green, and a ginger dog. It is now the only daylight in the room.
- **Blanket.** It has lit fold ridges catching the cold strip.
- **Slippers.** Repainted a little lighter, with warm toes, so they read on the floor.

What the paint did: blending the first cold wash dragged warm paint into a brown rectangle again. A pass meant to lose the warm pool's left edge into the dark dragged strokes across the desk, mug, pot and reader. All of those had to be repainted from dry, and the screen, its message and the gauntlet's fingers were restated after them. My first cold-light strokes read as icicles hanging from the strip, so they were painted out and replaced with the soft bands.

What still falls short:
- A faint seam of the old patch shows at the right end of the cold bands.
- The blanket's lit ridges are a little stark and white.
- The dark under the desk shows a faint boxed edge.
