# Ratio, Sitting

A bust-length portrait of Ratio as an old master would paint a sitter, except that every form is a flat low-poly facet. She sits in three-quarter view in a dim room of large brown planes, lit by one window high at the left. That window is the brightest shape in the picture, and its light falls across her: the far cheek and brow take the palest facets, the middle of the face a warm mid-tone, and the near jaw turns away into a darker plane. Her long black-blue hair is a carved helmet of locks. The planes facing the window catch a cool grey light, a hairline of warm light runs down its outer contour, and it ends in pointed tips over the shoulders. Her eyes are big dark hexagons with square highlights, and the lower half of each iris is lit by the window. Her brows lift at the inner ends. Her mouth is small and just open, and there is a faint blush facet on each cheek. She looks out with the earnest, slightly worried attention of someone about to explain something carefully, at length, because she wants you to understand. She wears a white shirt in dull planes with a folded orange collar. The whole picture sits behind faint scanlines in a rounded, bowed CRT screen with a small glare of curved glass at the corner.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 47 chunks, building in dried layers. First I toned the ground and laid the dead colour: the dark hair mass, a middle skin, a pale shirt. Then I painted facet by facet, each plane chosen from a five-step skin ramp and a four-step hair ramp, the way a painter assigns values to the planes of a carved head, rather than from any computed shading. Most of my corrections came from one rule, studio note 54. Every time I laid a facet into paint that was still wet (the collar twice, the shirt band, the hair's silhouette ring), it came out mottled and muddy. Laid over dry paint, the same pile came out clean, flat and exact. That is what low-poly facets need. A value-and-squint check partway through showed the white shirt was the brightest mass and was competing with the face. I repainted it a step darker, keeping light only on the window-side shoulder. A first pass at the hair came out too pale and blue, so I repainted it darker, with light only on the narrow top planes. The chin facet once covered the mouth, which I repainted lower. A smooth arc of the old background still ran round the hair, and I painted it out so the silhouette is the facets themselves. If I did it again, I would design the neck and jaw facets before laying them, because the neck still reads a little long for a chibi. I would also let the background dry before painting anything against it.
