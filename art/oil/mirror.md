# The Other Bed

*The Mirror Ward, concept for the game area. Oil on linen, 3:2.*

We stand at the near end of a Testator ward in the shallows. It is a bronze hall with ribbed walls, and low couches line it for convalescent creatures. Each couch has a bone pad and a curled bone headrest. The far wall is not a wall. It is a mirror join: an arch outlined in a single thread of bone, and through it the room simply goes on. The couches, ribs and floor seams continue without a break, and at the end there is a warm open doorway. That doorway is the one behind us, seen the other way round. The Commando's frame stands in the aisle with his back to us and his arms at his sides. His reflection faces us across the join, backlit, and it has raised one hand toward him. He has not. Two things in the reflected room are not in ours. A tall Testator nurse stands beside the far couch, with a bone face-disc ringed by petals set at golden-angle steps. On the reflected couch that matches the empty one at our left, a pale shrouded patient lies asleep. Above the arch, the Testators carved the warning in fold notation: the ward's glyph, a bar, and across the bar the same room reflected, its inset outlined in bone, with an arc bridging the two. The same diagram is inlaid large in the floor at our feet, the room's address, so the player reads it twice before reaching the join. For the team, the visual language is: a mirror join is an opening, never glass, framed only by a hairline of bone; the space through it is fully built and lit by the light behind the viewer; one small gesture or occupant is wrong; and the notation diagrams the join at the threshold and on the floor.

**How it was painted.** It was built as a one-point perspective box. Walls, ceiling and floor were each painted as one clipped body passage. The reflected room was painted the same way but smaller, inside the arch mask, so it reads as a continuation and not as a picture on a wall. The couches were stacked masks (side, front, top, then pad and headrest painted after drying). My first attempt at the pads came out as speckled cheese, because I painted bone into wet bronze. Waiting a day and laying them in opaque fixed it. Painting the wall ribs ran them straight over the couches, and I had to repair those in vertical strips. A rim of light laid as a shrunken-mask band made a cut-out outline round the Commando, so I repainted him dark. The rim light stays only on the backlit figures in the reflection, which also helps say which side is lit from where. The warm glow round the far doorway, scumbled and blended, came out as a sunburst of streaks, which I kept. A dark warm glaze on the back wall dried as flame-like graining. Lifting it with a clean brush left a fine crackle that reads as patina, so that stayed too. With more time I would shape the figures properly (they are still simple silhouettes), darken the near floor so the light gathers at the join, and give the reflected nurse a clearer, stranger silhouette.

## Second pass

At the director's request I went back in with three aims: give the figures form, push the eye to the join, and clean up the glow. The scribbled burst round the far door was painted over opaque. My first try used three rings and a blend, and it left hard ring edges. The second used nine close steps from warm white to the wall's bronze with one light blend, and it now reads as a glow on the wall. The near floor now sinks into shadow in four graded bands. My narrow blends along the band seams smeared into fuzzy strata, so I repainted each seam as an in-between step instead. The floor seams and the inlaid diagram were restated in a dimmer bone, so they stay legible in the shadow, and the near couch pads were dimmed too. The Commando's frame was rebuilt as armour. Light strokes down each limb first came out as flat grey pills. Working into them with a clean brush fused them into mottled, worn metal. Over that went plate seams with lit lips at the elbows, knees and belt, curved pauldron rims, a lit helmet crown, and the warm rim from the join on his contour. The reflection got the same pauldrons, the same bone cross, and the same belt and knee seams, so the raised hand is the only difference. The nurse is now handmade: a bronze robe lit on the side toward the door, bone ribs across its chest, a bone collar, bone studs at golden spacing down the robe, and bone hands.
