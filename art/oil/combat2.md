# The Cable Opens

A MIRIAD corridor gone black, its ribs showing only as cold arcs where the discharge reaches them. The Commando fills the left foreground, a dark war-body leaning back as he backpedals. His far leg reaches behind him, his lit side faces the light, his tabard tails fly toward the enemy, and the lightning cannon is braced at his hip. From the muzzle the cable runs as a thin dark line, already flickering violet. It arches up over the corridor, then turns down and bursts. In the second half of its length it becomes a churning cloud of white-hot core and ultramarine-violet sheath, with ruptures tearing outward, and it swells largest where the dart bites. The dart is a small black barb at the heart of the white. Jagged pilot sparks jump from it to the Warden's bronze. The Warden fills the right half: a hulking, faceted Testator body with a bronze nimbus behind its head, a golden-angle rosette of bone studs on its chest, and lamellar seams that read almost as a grin. A violet ghost of its head and reaching arm sits displaced from the real contour, as if part of it won't hold still. A husk stands in the lit deck between the two of them with mites scattering at its feet, and a second husk lurks in the dark at the right edge. The discharge is the only light. It catches the Warden's near facets hot and gold and leaves the rest of the picture in violet-black.

How it was painted: on a knifed violet-black ground I laid the corridor in broad dark passes, then a light falloff that came out as a radiating grey hairball. I buried most of it under opaque dark, keeping only a pocket of light around the discharge and a pool on the deck. The figures went in as part-polygons painted with strokes along each limb. Small amounts of zinc white in dark mixes surprised me twice: 0.35 parts turned the Commando powder-blue and 0.5 parts turned a "dark" wall lavender. I had to repaint both with white-free darks. The cable was the struggle. My first sheath read as a slug. A low-load glaze laid opaque dabs instead of haze. A violet glaze striped the result with hard ultramarine, and perpendicular "rupture" strokes made a fishbone. What saved it was a clipped badger blend over the wet ruptures and dabs while the sheath underneath was set. That churned everything into a convincing plasma cloud, and I restated a thin hot core, the dart and the crisp sparks over it once it had dried. Next time I'd test every dark on the scrap easel before committing, model the Commando in more, smaller planes so he doesn't read as one blue plank, and paint the deck's light pool once and leave it alone.

## Second pass

At the director's request I went back in on three things. First, the Commando. I painted out the blue plank and rebuilt him as armour forms: a helmet with a visor slit, a pauldron, a breastplate with a centre ridge and a waist break, and thigh plates, knee cops and greaves on both legs. Each form went down dark, then got a lit plane facing the burst laid into it wet. A narrow badger pass, clipped to each form, turned that into a mottled halftone. Over the dry paint I then painted each form's far side back to opaque dark and gave it a crisp cold edge (filbert, then rigger) on the side facing the discharge. Now he reads as a wide-stanced armoured body leaning back over his trailing leg. Second, the cable. The stretch from muzzle to burst is now one continuous dark whip. It carries a violet sheath along both edges, and three plasma beads grow in size toward the burst, each a violet bloom with a hot core spitting short sparks. The core line now runs from the whip on into the burst. Third, I painted over the Warden's belly seams in dark bronze, so the grin is gone. The blend surprised me: it went to cloud just as the studio notes warn, but over a dry ground that cloud made a usable halftone, and opaque dark plus a rim restored the form.
