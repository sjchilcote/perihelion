# Perihelion

A study after late Turner. The TESTAMENT falls into the Sun and is nearly gone into light. A vortex of pale golds, Indian yellow and lead white turns around a white-hot heart, right of centre. Ochre and orange arms of corona wheel through it, a rose warmth gathers below, and pearl-cool notes sit in the far upper-left and lower-right corners. At the vortex's left edge the station survives only as a few dark bronze strokes: a leaning spire, the stub of the hull and a dragged keel. A thin crisp arc of the ring sweeps up from the hull and is lost in the glare. Feathered strokes stream past it along the vortex like wind, and they are the only drawing in the picture.

How it was painted: 11 chunks, 3:2, on a pale warm lead-white ground. I laid broad semi-lean washes of ochre, gold and pale yellow along a swirl field and blended them wet. The corona's arms came out stringy and the pearl patches hard-edged, so I swept the whole field again wet along the vortex, and that gave the Turner-like dissolve. The hard part was the station. Every masked pass kept its mask's edge: a blended blob, then a crusty pale patch, and once a flat gold disc. The fix was to drop masks entirely. Free filbert strokes drawn along the swirl itself, plus a clean badger dragged by hand along the same arcs, broke the edge and became the streaming strokes. The final darks went on over dry gold, as a few heavy strokes and some lean, broken ones. They read as a dark vessel, close to the Temeraire's tug, so I kept them. The ring's arc and the white heart came last. The one warm window I touched into the hull is lost in the dark and barely shows. Next time I would lay the station's dark wet into the first washes, so it dissolves with them from the start.
