# The Seam

The MIRIAD outpost's prefab corridor runs straight at the viewer in one-point perspective. It is dead and dark: grey-violet ribs, a taped-over replacement panel and a dead light fitting in the ceiling, cables lying loose on the deck. It ends at an amber-and-black hazard collar bolted against a wall that nobody on the station built, a field of worn bronze scale-plates laid in a golden-angle spiral around the opening. The researchers have cut a rough rectangle with rounded corners through that wall. The cut faces are raw bronze, and they show the chambered growth inside the hull plating. A tripod sensor is aimed through the hole, a scraper rig is bolted up one side, there are clamp-on pods, a hand-lettered warning board held up with tape, and a printed hazard sign. Through the cut is the Testament's interior: a warm, vaulted nave with rows of well-made curved chairs facing down a central aisle, and small porthole niches along the walls, a few of them lit. The arches go on for longer than they should and slowly twist as they shrink toward a far white glow. The Commando's frame stands a few metres in, a dark silhouette with its back to us. Its shadow and one research cable run back toward the corridor, where the light spills over the sill and fans out across the deck.

How it was painted: the canvas has a grey lead-white and bone-black ground. I built the geometry as masks (the corridor's perspective quads, the rib frames, the cut and its reveal faces) and laid it in broadly. Each scale-plate was then painted with one helper: a single filbert stroke for the body, a dark arc for its shadow and a rim glint whose strength falls off with distance from the cut. The chairs and the nave arches were also drawn by small helpers with per-instance variation, so that no two are the same. The paint taught me three things the hard way. First, any lead white in a dark mix pushes it far lighter than I expected, so I repainted almost every dark passage at least twice with much leaner piles. Second, the badger blends everything that is wet inside its mask: two graded passages (the vault and a soft shadow band) were turned into flat ochre by one blend pass. After that I graded with separate piles and blended only narrow seams. Third, a dark glaze laid over light paint left a crackle of light lines in the weave, which survives on parts of the walls. If I painted it again I would start the corridor much darker and leaner, keep the bronze wall's darks separate from the plate passes, and save the badger for the far glow alone.
