# The Observatory

We stand near the rim of the Testators' observatory and look across its floor. The bronze dome rises and closes overhead. Its ribs run up to a zenith out of frame, and three rings of latitude cross them. The lowest course of the dome holds a warm band of bronze, and the rest darkens upward into near-black. The inner surface is a map of the sky they tracked. Seven spirals of bone studs are laid on the golden angle, each with its own size and density, and each is ringed by an inscribed circle holding nested, turned polygons of fold notation. Between the spirals lie scattered single stars, denser along a band, a few of them cold. A slot is cut up through the dome. In it, against deep blue-black space, the Sun's limb crosses as a hard white disc edge with a thin pale chromosphere, the only hot light in the room. It lays a hard stripe on the floor and a white edge down the sunward side of the great bronze telescope. The telescope stands in the middle aimed through the slot: a heavy banded tube in a broad fork on a drum, its carriage set on a bronze rail ring studded with bone. At its eyepiece, in a high chair, a Testator docent sits with its bone face lifted to the glass, still watching. Far off on the left, at the foot of the dome, a low doorway glows dim and warm, and the Commando is stooping through it, tiny.

**How I painted it.** I built the value structure first. The dome went in as three darkening bands with blended seams, then the floor, then the black space in the slot. Next came the ribs as ribbon masks (heavy near the rim, thinning overhead), the orrery spirals and circles, the stars, and last the lights: the Sun's limb, the sun stripe, the hot edges. The first telescope was painted too slight to be "great", so I restated it twice as broad over the first. Its hot edge went partly into wet paint and had to be laid again on set paint. My first meridians, stroked with a round brush, came out as hairlines, and drawing them as ribbon masks gave them their weight. Things I'd change: the corona band reads a little too wide and ivory beside the hard limb, the doorway's floor spill is a hard little oval, and the remnant of the first, slighter fork still shows beside the tube.

## Pass at 02:17 CDT, Oct 2 (chunks 10–14)

- **Telescope as polished bronze.** I modelled the tube in zones across its width: a warm reflected left edge, a dark core, a warm mid-tone, and bright bronze toward the sun, blended only at narrow seams. Once it was dry I added four banded rings with lit lips, an unbroken hard white edge down the sunward contour, and an objective ring at the top whose far rim catches the white light, with a cold glint on the dark glass. The fork and drum were repainted broad and dark, with lit edges and trunnion bosses. The remnant of the first, slighter fork is painted out.
- **Docent.** It is now a legible seated figure. A high-backed chair, a robed body leaning up toward the eyepiece, its bone face lifted to the glass with a dark eye-slot, and one bone hand on a brass wheel on the tube. The slot's light rims the crown, brow, shoulder, robe and chair.
- **Sun band.** A hard pale band now crosses the floor from the instrument over the rail ring toward the lower left.
- **Doorway spill.** I painted out the old hard oval and laid a dim tongue reaching toward us. The first try came out as a large warm mound and was painted back to floor; a faint trace of it remains.

**Still falls short:** the doorway spill could be softer still, and the drum is a heavy dark mass.
