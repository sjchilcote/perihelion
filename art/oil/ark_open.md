# The Ark Opened

This is the next moment after "What It Says". The Ark is open: its lid is a thin pale slab lifted at the back, and the inside is a seething white. Out of it rises the warning itself. It is not text but light: hundreds of thin gold lines climbing out of the box in a slow fountain, white-hot at the root and gold to orange as they rise and fan across the near-black bronze vault. Carried up along them are the glyphs of fold notation, each a small polygon holding a smaller turned copy of itself, tiny near the box and larger and sparser as they rise. A few drift free across the vault overhead. The light lands as a warm pool on the rubble around the box. PALMER's frame kneels beside it with one knee raised and one hand still on the rim, rimmed gold, its helm tilted up. Its visor is a slit full of the gold reflection, with one glyph caught in it.

**How it was painted.** It took 11 chunks on the same warm dark ground as "What It Says". The fountain is 200 long round-brush strokes, each laid in three segments with fresh paint (hot at the root, gold, then deep gold), which I learned from the sail's crease lines. The glyphs come from a small helper that lays one glyph: a polygon, a smaller turned polygon inside it, and a third inside the larger ones. I called it about 200 times along the fountain's paths. What went wrong:
- The scale-plate rows in the vault came out as loud bead-like dots, so I painted the vault back to near-black and let the light carry it.
- The first lid read as a solid yellow block and had to be repainted as a thin slab.
- The frame was too crude until a raised forward leg, a sloping mantle and a narrowed visor were added.

**Still short:** the frame's lower body and near shin are a plain dark mass, the box's front is a flat grey without frost or lettering, and the vault has lost its plates.
