# First Steps

The last of the Ratio series. One of the TESTAMENT's grand halls runs away from us under a procession of dark bronze ribs to a warm far wall. A high window in the right wall throws a beam of hazy gold light across the hall onto a floor of golden-angle mosaic. Ratio walks toward us down the middle in her small printed body: white shell and bronze legs, mid-step, her arms out wide for balance, a stiff printed lock of black hair swung out by the step, big dark eyes with square highlights and a small determined smile. She is still flat and pastel against the painterly dark of the hall. A step behind her comes the Commando, huge, crimson rag and bone cross on his chest, cannon-staff in his right hand. His left hand reaches forward and hovers just behind her back, not touching. In an alcove in the left wall a docent, a tall bronze egg of a head with a bone face-plate, has turned its single amber lens to watch them pass. It is the first new thing it has seen in a million years.

How it was painted: the hall was laid in broad and dark with a warm pool down the floor, then the ribs and pillars as receding arches, then the mosaic as golden-angle touches in perspective. The light beam went wrong first. A white glaze dried as streaks, as the studio notes warned, and blending it gave a hard-edged muddy quad. It was painted out on dry paint and relaid as feathered stipple, densest down the beam's axis. The Commando was built plate by plate, with warm edges on the window side and dark seams. Ratio was built as flat clipped fills, darks first, then the pale parts after days of drying. A floor-shadow pass painted over her body, and she had to be repainted on top of it. Still weak: the beam reads as a sandy haze rather than light in air, the Commando is blocky, Ratio sits a little close to the bottom edge, and the mosaic is sparse dots rather than a laid floor.
