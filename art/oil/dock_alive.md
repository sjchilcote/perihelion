# Dock 4, Before the Fall

This is the third pair, to *Dock 4 — Sweep*: the same storage ring, from the
same eye and with the same geometry, months earlier with the lights on.
White work lights burn along the ceiling of every section, round the bend
and out of sight. The docking collar on the left is sound and shut, its
hazard ring bright and a ring of green status lenses lit around its flange.
The bay doors are freshly stencilled in amber, with hazard bands crisp over
each one. They read 7 on the near door, then 8 and 9 going away, and round
the bend, plain as day, 1. Nobody noticed that either. Shelving with
cargo pods runs down the inner wall beyond the bend. A pallet stands
half-unloaded on the deck. Its big white MIRIAD crate, marked with a blue
hexagon and chevron and a stencilled code, is still strapped on, and two
orange cases have already been set down beside it. A crew member has thrown
a blue jacket with a reflective band over the handrail under the 7, and a
clipboard hangs on a hook on the near frame. There are no figures.

## How it was painted

I made it in 8 chunks on a light grey ground at 16:9. I reused Dock 4's
projection exactly: the ring rises, and past the near frame its centreline
bends left. The doors, ribs, racks, stencil shapes and collar are drawn by
the same helpers at the same coordinates, so the 7-8-9-1 sits where it sits
in the dark picture. Three things didn't work first time:
- The green status lights first went on as single brush touches and
  vanished into the flange paint, so I painted them again as small lenses in
  dark sockets.
- My first crate mark looked too much like a real transit logo, so I painted
  it out and made a mark of MIRIAD's own.
- A pool of work-light on the deck came out as a pale rectangle, and
  blending it only spread it. It reads as a deck mat.
Still rough: the room is evenly lit and pale, with little value structure.
The jacket is stiff. The near ceiling lights throw no glow.
