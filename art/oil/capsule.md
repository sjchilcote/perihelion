# Against the Sun

*Store capsule, vertical key art (2:3). Oil on linen.*

The Commando stands on the curved crest of the TESTAMENT's hull with his back to us, a small dark figure against the white disc of the near Sun. Behind and around him, the station's vast habitat ring passes across the Sun as a tilted band of dark bronze, pricked with rows of tiny warm windows. Where its near arc crosses the disc, the glare eats it away, so he stands against pure light. The Sun's corona breaks outward in streaks of yellow, orange and red earth into a violet-blue night. The hull below him is worn bronze laid in scale-plate rows, lit on the crest and falling to dark, with a few lit windows. The top third is calm night with a few stars, kept for the logo. At thumbnail size it reads as four masses: dark sky, the blazing Sun, the dark ring and hull, and one small figure at the point where they all meet. Pilgrim, tomb and Sun, in one image.

**How it was painted.** It was kept big and simple to fit the forty minutes. The Sun went down as nine opaque concentric bands from zinc white to chromatic black, then got one radial pass with a blender. That turned the hard bands into a burst of flame streaks, which suits a Sun this close, so I kept it. The ring is one ribbon mask, thicker on its near side, painted dark. The windows are single touches strewn along it. The glare "eating" the ring is opaque white painted over the band with a roughened edge, kept clear of the figure, so his silhouette stays the sharpest edge in the picture. The scale-plates are rows of small arcs that grow and darken toward the bottom. With more time I would break up the even ring of corona streaks at the top, give the hull plates more varied bronze and some verdigris, and add a faint rim of light along the Commando's shoulders.

## Second pass

The director asked for two changes: fuse the stepped corona bands into one radiance, and put a hot rim on the figure. I repainted the whole corona as radiating strokes, 40 to 90 units long. Each of seven piles was graded by distance from the Sun, from lemon-white through cadmium, Indian yellow, Mars orange and red earth to the chromatic dark. The strokes are anchored in their own band but allowed to run across the neighbouring ones, so no band has an edge. Then there was one blend along the radius only. A first try left the outside of the corona as a visible disc edge against the sky. Short radial strokes of the sky's dark, crossing that edge, dissolved it into rays. Fifteen long, tapering pale streaks at uneven angles replace the old fringe of many short ones, though they are thin and only faintly visible. The Commando now has a thin hot rim of zinc white and lemon along his helmet and shoulders. I also painted out a grey smear across his waist, left over from the first glare pass.
