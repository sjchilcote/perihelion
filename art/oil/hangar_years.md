# The Docking Hall, Years Later

The pair to another painter's *The Docking Hall*, with its geometry and its
first six chunks replayed: the great bronze barrel vault, its lit ribs
receding to the far round door, the rows of cradles and gantries down both
sides. Years on, the cradles that stood empty for a million years hold
ships. Four white MIRIAD supply craft lie in their cradles, two on each side.
Each carries an amber-and-black hazard band near the stern, the blue hexagon
on the flank and a dark canopy at the bow. Black and amber cables and hoses
run down to them from the gantry jibs, each jib with a warm work light
burning at its end. In the near-left cradle the CUCULUS lies where it always
did, its back lit warm now, with a working light on it. Through the far
door, against black space and the sliver of the Sun, another white ship is
coming in, nose-on with its red and green running lights, and its landing
lights lie as a pale line down the deck. Small figures work on the deck:
crew in blue coveralls with reflective bands, and two tall bronze docents
with a cold blue eye each. A small cart of crates stands between them. The
station is finally receiving visitors.

## How it was painted

I made it in 9 chunks at 16:9. I replayed the original painter's chunks for
the hall, leaving out the lone walking figure. My first ships were small
pebble-shaped hulls that read as eggs. I repainted each one as a long hull
lying along its cradle in depth: a lit upper flank and a shadowed lower one,
a seam between them, a lit spine, a hazard band, a hexagon and a canopy.
The cables, work lights, incoming ship, figures and docents went on last,
on dry paint.

Still rough: the ships are foreshortened into short white pods rather than
long craft. The figures are tiny and few for "busy". The near foreground
band is still the original's dark, empty floor.
