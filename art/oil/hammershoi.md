# Interior with an Open Door

A Testator residence aboard the TESTAMENT, a million years empty, painted after Hammershøi. A grey-warm wall is divided by a pale chair rail and skirting. Left of centre, an open door with a bone-white architrave looks through into the next room, where the door leaf stands swung back. In that room's far wall is a second doorway, and beyond it a third, lit pale at the end of the sequence, so the doors recede into doors. A good, plain chair stands against the wall to the left, and a bare table stands between the door and the window. On the right, a tall window of pale northern light, crossed by thin mullions, throws a soft parallelogram of light down across the bare floorboards toward us. A single docent stands there with its back to us, tall, robed and still, dark against the glass. Its shadow lies across the light on the floor. The values stay in a narrow warm grey range, the paint is thin, and nothing moves.

How it was painted: 6 chunks, 4:3, on a warm grey knifed ground, each chunk dried for days before the next. My first lay-in was too pale and flat, so I repainted the wall a mid grey and the floor a warm grey-brown, and added the architraves and dado. The through-view first read as a target of nested rectangles. Adding a shadowed jamb, the swung door leaf, the next rooms' floors and a trimmed second doorway turned it into recession. The first docent was a shapeless bowling pin, and the first light patch on the floor was too bright with ragged edges where a blend crossed dry paint. I painted both out and restated them: a tall robed figure with a small bowed head and square shoulders, and a quieter light shape with the figure's shadow lying in it. The chair, table and mullions came last. Next time I would keep the wall's brush texture smoother, as Hammershøi's surfaces are almost still, darken the corner away from the window slightly so the light has somewhere to fall from, and soften the hard little shadows under the furniture.
