# The Docent Who Keeps the Lamp

A Testator docent stands in a dark niche, close enough to read. Its body is a bronze bell, a hooded cope gone smooth with handling. Its face is a bone plate tipped forward, with closed eyes and a closed grille mouth: the bow of a teacher waiting for a student to finish reading. In both sleeves it holds a small lamp, and above the lamp a lesson plate, so the flame lights the lesson from below. The plate carries rows of fold-notation in a script that never repeats itself. A golden-angle rosette is inlaid on the brow. A long crack runs down the right side of the face from crown to chin, and someone mended it with six bronze staples that have gone green. A chip is missing from the crown of the hood. Lower on the bell a worn bronze band repeats one short phrase in fold-notation, the way a lesson gets recited by rote. The only light comes from the lamp. It makes a warm, uneven halo in the dark around the figure, and the bell's foot falls into shadow.

How it was painted: I worked fast, in 26 chunks over about ten minutes. The canvas has a dark earth ground covered with a violet-dark body coat. Over that I blocked in the bronze bell, the bone face and the lamp, then modelled the forms with strokes that follow them, and let the paint dry between layers (with `wait`) so the cracks, staples, rosette and notation could go on crisp. The paint pushed back several times. A dark chest pass ran over the wet face and I had to repaint it. A low-load warm glaze I meant as glow left long pale stripes like wings, and I had to paint them out with opaque dark. A scumble over dry paint landed as a solid orange slab, which I turned into the lesson plate (the best accident of the session). A mask edge marked "lost" stayed hard. What finally softened the halo was a band of mid-value paint laid across the seam and blended while wet. With more time I would do colour swatches on a scrap canvas first, model the bell's pleats with wider soft strokes instead of thin light sticks, and add more variation to the large flat passages on the face and plate.
