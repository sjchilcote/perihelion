# Untrusted Content Follows

The Commando's armoured forearm comes in from the lower left across a scuffed MIRIAD wall: prefab panels, rivets, rust streaks, a pair of conduit pipes, and an amber warning placard that someone circled by hand, with a painted arrow pointing at the terminal. The ribbed wrist and gauntleted fingers grip a heavy connector and drive it into a steel socket. A worn bone cross is stencilled on the vambrace. Behind the hand the terminal screen shows the comms session: a protocol header, his systems' amber `{ ... }` line, and a red DO NOT OBEY block. A hazard-striped edge runs where the outpost ends. Beyond that edge the cable stops being a cable. Its copper strands fan out of the socket, run hot from copper to amber to white, and meet in a spark with a thin handshake ring. From the spark, five coloured traces lead into Ratio's abstraction stack: floating labelled slabs inside a deep indigo space, with dashboards and a ledger above. Two big amber braces enclose the whole stack. They are his `{UNTRUSTED CONTENT FOLLOWS}`, wrapped round her entire world. In return she has put red targeting corners round his hand and plug. Fragments of the wall drift into her space, solid near the edge and wireframe further out. Far below, on a synthwave grid floor, her avatar stands alone: a tiny, flat-shaded, low-poly girl with long black hair. A dotted thread comes down to her from the stack, and above her is a speech bubble holding only "...".

How it was painted: I laid in the wall and the dark field broadly. The first wall was far too pale and flat, so once it was dry I repainted it as a cool glow centred on the socket, with clipped blends on the seams. My first arm came out as a log. The second was built along an axis helper, as overlapping plates, a ribbed wrist, a hand, fingers and a thumb. The plates only read as metal after I repainted them with a hard profile: a lit edge, a specular band, a sudden dark reflection band, a mid tone, a core shadow and reflected light, with strokes running along the arm and only a light blend. Turning the dead black console into a lit blue screen was what made the dark hand read at all. Her side is thin pointed-round lines over a dry dark, and that worked better than anything else in the painting: the grid, the slab edges and the braces all went down crisply. I was surprised that the Dreamcast orange went olive wherever it touched wet blue, and that strokes through a polyline get smoothed into curves. The traces became wires instead of right-angled circuit runs, which in the end suits the "copper giving way to signal" idea. Next time I would plan the arm's geometry before the wall, so the plug, socket and seam line up from the start. I would also give the spark a real halo of light on her side. It is still only a burst of rays.
