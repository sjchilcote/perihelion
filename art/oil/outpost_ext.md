# Bolted On

The MIRIAD research outpost, seen close up from outside. A hasty cluster of gray right-angled prefab modules sits clamped with crude brackets onto the TESTAMENT's hull. The modules have dead dark window slots, amber hazard bands and small placards of machine glyphs, and they carry a dish array, a mast, a truss boom with a dish at its end, and one antenna bent over. The hull is worn-bronze scale-plates in staggered, overlapping rows. The plates shrink toward a bright limb, where the hull curves away against deep navy space, and they run off every edge of the frame. Hard raking sunlight from the right catches each plate's sunward flank in a gold crescent and throws the outpost's shadow down and left across the scales. The far end of the station is the scorched module: a torn hole blown in its front, a black soot plume across it, and one peeled lip catching the sun. The whole base is about the size of five of the hull's plates. The lower left of the hull turns away from the sun into shadow, and that keeps the light and the eye on the outpost and the lit plates to its right.

How it was painted: There were 9 chunks in about 12 minutes of easel time, on a 16:9 canvas with a dark warm knifed ground. Space and the hull base went in first as opaque fields. Every plate is laid by one helper function, called about 1,650 times with fresh positions and sizes, nearest rows first so the farther courses overlap them like shingles. Each call lays a filbert body in one of four bronze or verdigris piles, a dark shadow arc under its rounded edge, a soft sheen band, and a gold or hot rim on its sunward flank. My first sheen was a short stroke, and it printed every plate with an orange polka dot, so I repainted those rows with the sheen as a band following the edge. The modules first marbled whitish because I laid them over a still-wet, oily shadow glaze. I repainted them opaque and darker once it was dry. The first cast shadows were thin slivers along the surface, and the shadows only read once I projected the box tops down and left. A lean dark glaze over the lower left reads as the hull turning out of the sun. With more time I would break the regularity of the plate rows (golden-angle spirals with real curvature), give the outpost more modules and a larger soot trail onto the plates, and draw the brackets big enough to read as clamps.

## Pass at 00:26 CDT, 2 Oct 2026 (chunks 10–21)

What changed:
- **Hull.** I covered the hull below the limb with a dark base and laid new plates over it, about 830 of them, set on a golden-angle (Vogel) spiral laid out on the hull's surface and foreshortened toward the limb. The rows now curve and cross as spirals instead of marching in coin rows. Each plate varies in size (1.25–1.6×), shape and tilt, and has a dark underside, a soft worn sheen and a lit lip on its sunward flank. The bronze, worn-ochre and verdigris patinas are mixed, about 1% of plates are missing (dark sockets) and a few are re-cut with a straight scar. A second pass over the dry plates restated every plate's dark underside and lit lip, strongest where the sun rakes.
- **Terminator.** Lip brightness and body value follow a terminator band running from the upper-left limb down to the lower middle. The plates on the far side were re-laid in dark bronze with dim lips.
- **Outpost.** I repainted it heavier: deeper modules (more side depth), lit vertical corners and roof edges, dark underside skirts, panel joints, roof vents, dead windows, amber hazard bands and glyph placards. Seven struts brace the modules onto plate lips with U-shaped clamp jaws and bolts. The plates around each clamp are scraped bright and creased dark. Three cables sag away across the plates. The mast, the bent antenna, the dish array and the truss boom with its dish were repainted thicker. The scorched module has a heat-browned face, a torn hole with lips peeled out and lit, and soot up its face and streaked down across the plates below. Its long shadows fall down and left across the scales.

What went wrong, and what still falls short:
- **Plate size.** The first spiral courses were too small and pale and read as scattered coins. Repainting them larger over still-wet paint muddied the bodies, and the accent pass is what rescued the read.
- **Terminator.** The first night-side glaze went to a hard black wedge. Re-laying the plates there turned it into a muddier, olive band that is still not a clean roll from gold into shadow.
- **Outpost.** It is still small and light against the hull and could carry more weight and darker values. The scorched module reads as a rusty box more than a blown-out one, and the modules' fronts carry pale smears from earlier layers.
- **Limb.** The thin limb band keeps the old regular tiny plates.
