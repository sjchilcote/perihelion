# Perihelion Core

The heart of the station, seen from the bridge that crosses the middle of a vast spherical chamber. The chamber's inner surface is ribbed in latitude rings of bronze, cut by meridian seams, converging on the far pole behind the core. The rings are lit warm near the centre and fall away to dark toward the edges. At the very centre, held on seven heavy radial struts, hangs the core: a sphere of white-gold light. Its surface buds into smaller copies of itself, and those into smaller ones still. Light sprays off its rim along the struts. The bridge runs straight to it between two rails. The Commando walks along it, tiny and dark against the glare, and his long shadow falls back down the deck toward us.

How it was painted: perspective first. The shell is eleven latitude bands whose screen radius follows the tangent of their angle from the far pole, on a value ramp from white-gold to near-black. Over it, on dry paint, came the meridian seams, the bronze ribs with lit lips, the struts, the bridge and rails, the core in three nested layers of light, the budding copies, the corona, the figure and his shadow, and a lean dark falloff at the edges. What falls short: the budding copies read as small curled marks rather than spheres growing out of the sphere. The final dark falloff left a hard-edged oval that looks a little like a porthole. The shell rings are evenly lit all round, with no shading from the core's direction.
