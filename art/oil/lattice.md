# The Lattice, from the Other Side

The picture shows ordinary space as the Adversary experiences it. In the middle stands a corridor of the TESTAMENT, rendered as a rigid lattice. Nested square frames recede to a single vanishing point, and every wall is ruled into identical plates and windows. The ceiling glares white and the floor and right wall are a hard, saturated ultramarine, with cold white seams ruled exactly. All around it is their home: warm-dark space in madder, violet and ember that keeps folding into more of itself, with no edges and no straight line anywhere. Four soft, rose-coloured organs press against the frame from outside, and each goes pale where it touches the rigid edge. At the lower-left corner a seam has opened, and the warm folding pours in over the blue floor. The inversion is the point: here the clean geometry is the horror, and the soft, dissolving place is the relief.

**How it was painted.** It took 14 chunks on a madder-and-bone-brown toned ground. The outer space was laid in with broad strokes whose direction followed a warped noise flow. I then added long streamline strokes of lit ridges and dark creases and blended them while wet, which turned them into folds. The lattice was ruled with straight two-point strokes after the glare body had dried. My wall glazes were far stronger than I meant: the ultramarine came out saturated and hid the ruling, so I re-ruled the grid on top, dark lines on the pale walls and glaring white lines on the blue ones. That accident is what made the corridor feel offensive. One chunk computed for over twenty minutes, because I passed `lose()` a `where` function that grew a whole-canvas mask at every sample, and I had to restart the session. The blends taught the same lesson again: a blend flattens whatever is wet inside its mask. The organs' first blend left them flat, and a blend over the opened seam flattened it into one brown wedge until I laid folds back into it. A rectangular blend mask also left a rectangular haze, which I had to dissolve afterwards.

**Next time** I would make the organs larger and more numerous, and actually deform their soft forms against the frame. I would also give the pour far more space and light, so that it reads as relief rather than as a stain. The organ edges are still a little too sticker-like.
