# Farewell to the Sun

Weeks after perihelion, from the outer hull. The Sun is small and far behind now, low in the upper left: a white point inside a modest corona of short gold and rose strokes, with the stars coming out across the rest of the sky. On the right the sail is furling back into its bay, a narrowing fan of dark pleats drawn down into the hull, each fold a long gold line and the ragged pleat-tips edged in light. The hull's crest curves across the lower third, its scale-plates gone to dusk, each one catching the last warm light on its upper lip — thickest toward the Sun, thinning away from it. On the crest, small against the sky, the watchers stand together looking back: tall robed docents with long necks and single lenses, the Commando's war-body among them with the crimson rag lifting from his shoulder, and three crew children between them, a docent's hand on one child's shoulder and a child's hand in the Commando's. All are dark, rimmed warm on the side that faces the Sun, their shadows running long behind them along the hull. Calm, grateful, an ending.

**How it was painted.** 15 chunks on a blue-black ground. My first warm afterglow round the Sun read as a swirling planet and was repainted back to night, leaving the Sun as a small disc in a corona of short strokes. The sail is the Sail Unfurls' pleated fan made small, its creases laid in three segments with fresh paint. The hull plates first came out an even, too-light pattern; a dark glaze sank them into dusk, and the warm lips went on afterwards on dry paint. The watchers first read as a row of posts and were painted out and drawn again larger, with robes, necks, heads, children's legs and linked hands. Still short: the sail's fan floats a little above the bay at its foot, the plates' lips are a uniform crescent mark, and the figures are small at full view.
