# The Perihelion Tapestry

The whole story of PERIHELION in one embroidered strip, after the Bayeux Tapestry. It is laid on a linen ground in four wools (terracotta, ochre, green and blue-black, with a little cream and grey) and read left to right between stylised trees:
1. Under a stitched sun, the CUCULUS crosses the waves of space, a dark ship with a crimson keel trailing its wake.
2. It dives nose-first into the TESTAMENT's scale-plates in a fan of sparks.
3. The first frame lies dead on the ground, and the second stands over it in the terracotta tabard.
4. The dead MIRIAD outpost, a grey box with its doors dark.
5. Ratio as a face on a screen on its stand.
6. A frame stands at the door of the docents' museum, where two white docents wait under a gold arcade beside a relic on a plinth.
7. The Commando levels his cannon and lightning leaps at the angular green Wardens.
8. The great sail spreads from its mast.
9. Perihelion: a huge terracotta Sun ringed with rays and the station small before it.
10. The station, ring and sail, rides away over the waves off the right edge.

Every shape is outlined in dashed stem-stitch. Above runs a band of abstract stitched capitals divided by little crosses, and over that a border of small beasts in slanted compartments. Below, a border of fold-notation glyphs, each polygon holding a smaller turned copy of itself.

**How it was painted.** Three chunks. First every shape was laid as flat wool colour with the "hatch" hand, short parallel stitches in a direction chosen per shape, which reads as laid-and-couched embroidery. After a drying wait came all the stitching. Every contour, rule, border, beast, glyph and letter is drawn as short dashes with small gaps, like stem stitch, from one helper. The flat method held up well, and the dashes are what make it read as embroidery rather than painting. I meant to alternate beasts and glyphs in both borders. The counter I used put all the beasts above and all the glyphs below, which happens to work. Next time I would fill the empty linen above the scenes with captions and flying birds, as the original does, make the figures larger and more animated, and stitch the trees' crowns as interlace.
