# The Dormitory

Deep in the TESTAMENT is a long, low bronze gallery, seen straight down its length. Tall arched alcoves line both walls between bronze pilasters, and in each one a Warden sleeps upright, folded in on itself. Each has a crested, banded shell, bladed forelimbs crossed over its front, and jointed legs tucked in at its sides. A single cold eye on each is dimmed to a pinpoint. The eyes recede in two rows toward the far end, where one small amber hazard lamp burns on the end wall. Near the far end on the left, one alcove is empty, with nothing in it but a few scored marks. Down the middle of the floor, the Commando walks very slowly away from us. He is a dark figure built from plates, his shoulder lamp turned low to a small warm pool on the floor ahead of him, his right hand raised to his helm as if holding his breath. Almost everything is dark.

I built it in the corridor language from the earlier paintings: a one-point perspective scaffold in Lua, with the floor, ceiling, walls and end wall as lean dark bronze passes on a dark toned ground. Each side's alcoves are placed in depth independently with small irregularities. The Wardens were drawn in each alcove's own wall-plane coordinates, so they foreshorten with the wall. Their shells went in as one pass and were then shaded darker below. Bands, crest, blades and legs went on dry paint, and the eyes and lamps came last. Twice the paint surprised me. The figure painted in the same chunk as the warm pool picked up the pool's wet paint and came out bluish and transparent, so I repainted it on dry paint. The far lamp's first wash was a flat amber disc, which I painted out and rebuilt as a small lens with short radial strokes. Still short: the pool is a little blotchy, the Wardens' crest lines are brighter than the dread wants, and the figure is small.
