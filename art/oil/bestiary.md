# Bestiarium Adversarii

A page from a bestiary of the Adversary, as the Church's monks might have drawn it, made as a companion to the Mappa Testamenti. The page has a gold ground inside a blue border dotted with gold, a title scroll across the head, and four framed panels on alternating blue and red diapered grounds, each with a scroll of abstract lettering and a red initial beneath its beast.
- **I. The Warden:** a long violet crested shell with segment bands, a row of pale spikes, many legs, and two pale bladed forelimbs raised before a head with three white eyes.
- **II. The Shrieker:** a broken white bell, cracked down one side with a red chip out of its lip, with a person-shaped hole cut clean through it. Violet arcs of its scream spread from its crown.
- **III. The small scuttling things:** a swarm of pale many-legged bodies laid out in a golden-angle spiral, all crawling different ways.
- **IV. The Fold:** a pentagon holding a smaller turned copy of itself, and that another, eight times over in pale, violet and bone. Every edge is ruled in ink with the care of a monk who does not understand what he is copying.

In the lower margin two little pilgrims, one in crimson with a cross and a raised sword, one in brown thrusting a staff, are fighting a small Warden.

How it was painted: as flat illumination, like the Mappa. Border, gold ground and panel grounds went down at full coverage, then the diaper dots, then each beast as clipped flat polygons over dried paint. The ink contours, scrolls, lettering and margin figures came last. The Shrieker's person-shaped hole, first laid over the wet white bell, came out grey and had to dry and be repainted black. The swarm, built as hundreds of tiny clipped fills in one chunk, took sixteen minutes to compute; I should have stroked the legs instead. Still weak: the Warden's blades are clipped by its panel edge, the lower margin's beast and pilgrims are cruder than the panels, and the lettering is uniform ticks.
