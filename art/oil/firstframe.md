# The First Frame

The CUCULUS seed lies where it landed, diagonally across the dead MIRIAD cargo bay. Its scorched nose is buried in a wave of torn deck plating at the left, and its tail runs off the right edge under a ragged hole it punched in the ceiling. Through the hole there are a few cold stars. Its flank has split open like a husk. The upper armor flaps are peeled back, showing their starlit exterior. The lower flaps fold down toward us and glow on the inside. In the opening is the fabricator womb, a lens of white-hot to orange light ringed with ribs. A new frame stands in front of it in black silhouette. Its joints are still glowing from the printer, umbilicals still tie its back to the womb wall, and one hand is braced on the lip of the husk while it finds its feet. Two jointed fabricator arms reach out of the seed to cut into the outpost's deck, pulling glowing strands of raw stock back into the husk. Everything belonging to MIRIAD is cold and dead, lit only by the intruder: the gray wall panels with warm glints on their seams near the glow, a stenciled "07", a printed placard with a hand-painted red slash, stacked crates with hazard corners, an amber-and-black hazard band, and a caged hazard lamp still blinking cold blue-white. The cuckoo's egg hatching in the enemy's nest.

How it was painted: I used a 16:9 canvas on a dark violet-gray knifed ground. I laid the darks in first and then put the glow down wet in concentric zones. Blending those zones with the big badger merged them into one flat orange. Restating the core with small filberts over setting paint gave a radial starburst instead, which I kept, and a late stipple of zinc and lead white gave a hazy white-hot center. Most of the metal is built from many short strokes laid across the form, each one dipped into a different pile from a small value ramp depending on where it sits (cool and starlit on top, dark in the middle, warm bounce underneath). Blending did not work for that. The badger crosses its strokes, and the first time I tried it on the hull it left a crosshatched lattice. The frame is about twenty hard-edged part shapes, with rim lines set slightly inside the edge so they would not vanish into the glow, and its joints are flat ember strokes with a white core. Several things surprised me. Even 0.2 parts lead white makes a bone-black mix read as a mid gray. Glazes over value-1 darks lift them into blotchy veils even at 94% medium, so the light spill had to be drawn as warm glints on panel seams instead. And `edge="soft"` ignores the clip, which carried a shadow onto the frame's legs. Some passages had to be covered: the first petals read as a flower, and a ring of "torn bulkhead" tongues came out as stegosaurus plates. If I painted it again I would draw the husk's torn edges as fewer, larger, more irregular tears from the start. I would also keep the lower flaps' inner faces cleaner and less olive, and give the crumpled bulkhead a real passage of its own.
