# The Stylite: the First Night of the Watch

The pair to *The Stylite*, painted from up close. We stand at the top of the pillar, level with him. The Commando kneels in profile on the small bronze disc of the pillar's top with his face toward home. Through the gate the gold of ordinary space burns brightest just behind his helm, and he holds the rosary up to his face-plate. The bone cross hangs below his gauntlets and catches the light. His armour picks up the gold along every edge that faces the gate, while the cold of the Adversary's space lies along his back, his jaw and the crimson hood and cloak that fall behind him and pool on the disc. One cold point marks his optic. The gate's bronze lip curves past him like a doorway. Past the disc's edge, to the right, the violet and teal turn, and a few bronze plates come loose and drift away into it. In the first picture he is a speck against the light. Here he is a person at prayer, and the warm and the cold meet on him.

**How it was painted.** The machine was heavily loaded, so a single background pass took seven minutes, and the picture had to be made in 14 chunks inside an hour. That is far fewer than it needed. I laid the gold in as concentric zones and the cold as broad strokes following a swirl, then blocked in the figure, the disc and the drifting plates as painted shapes. The paint surprised me twice. The first armour pile, with a little white in it, came up pale and see-through over the gold. Later, a wide band of "lit plane" brought the armour up to the value of the gold behind it, and the silhouette vanished. I had to take that band back down to a warm dark bronze-steel and keep only a narrow lit edge. Rim lights laid on tacky paint broke into thin scratches. The gold set too fast to blend out its target rings, and the strokes I laid across the seams made a brighter ring. A feathered pale stipple, densest behind his head and blended radially, finally made the core the lightest thing in the picture. Next time I would make the figure much bigger, for real intimacy: the head and hands should fill a third of the canvas. I would also draw the frame's armour as real plates from the start rather than one silhouette, and keep the gold loose so it can still be blended.

## Pass at 01:03 CDT, Oct 2

Replaying the 14-chunk log took about 25 minutes, so this pass is chunks 15–21. **The figure rebuilt as armour:** I painted each piece as its own plate with a dark body, a gold band on the side toward the gate and a cold teal band on the far side. The pieces are a domed helm, a face-plate and cheek-plate, a gorget, a pauldron, a backplate and breastplate, three fauld lames, the rerebrace, couter and vambrace, a closed gauntlet, two cuisse plates, a knee cop, a greave along the disc and an upturned boot sole. After the plates dried I laid dark seams between them, gold glints on the gate edges, cold glints on the far edges, a visor slit with a cold optic, finger lames across the fist with gold knuckles, and rosary beads over the fist and hanging below it. The bone cross is now larger and clear below the gauntlets. **The cloak** got dark and light fold strokes blended along the fall. **The gold:** the speckled ring is painted over and blended out.

Still short: the plates were drawn as smoothed polygons, so up close they read as rounded pods rather than hard-edged armour. Each plate's light band runs round its top and bottom as well as its gate side, so they look outlined. Wet-into-wet, the gold and cold bands greyed toward each other. The cloak folds are faint. The gold, now that its speckles are gone, still shows soft concentric bands. At full view, though, the figure reads as an armoured body kneeling at prayer, gold-lit in front and cold behind.
