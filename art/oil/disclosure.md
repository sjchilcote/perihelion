# The Disclosure

Two reliquaries face each other under one arch. The left half of the arch is a beaded bronze
rib and the right half is a cyan wireframe, and they meet at the apex. The floor beneath also
changes halfway across: iron plates on his side, her glowing grid on hers. On the left stands
his casket, iron and gothic, its doors thrown open. The inner faces carry his oath as rows of
short bone-coloured lines like hand-tuned assembly. In the niche burns a cross built from those
same lines, stacked and uneven, the rules that are him. On the right is her low-poly shell. It
has split open, and pieces of its facets drift away, showing her core: a faceted sphere of pale
gold light with a small curled human seed inside, which is what her values are for. Between the
two cores runs a bridge of light, an amber thread from him and a cyan thread from her braided
around a fine white seam. Each side's check mark lands on the other: he has read her and she
has read him, and it holds. His rosary hangs from the open door, runs across the seam in the
floor and ends with its small cross on her grid. In the foreground lie the red braces that
fenced every word between them, { and }, fallen on the floor and no longer needed.

How it was painted: I laid the four quadrants in broadly, then painted the casket and the shell
as flat clipped facets. The paint's drying order ran the picture. Both cores, painted into
blacks that were still setting, came out dull and greenish. I painted those areas over with a
faster-drying dark, waited about eleven days of painting time and repainted them bright. My
first braces, drawn as brush strokes, smoothed into orange worms. Painted as calligraphic shapes
with varying width, foreshortened and given contact shadows, they read as braces. A lean
lead-white scumble along the bridge, softened with a small badger, turned the thin threads into
a beam, and I painted the threads again on top once it was dry. Next time I would put a little
warm light from the cross onto the iron floor, and paint the vault as a true pointed gothic
arch. Mine came out round.

## Second pass

The bridge is now a clean, hard-edged bar of light, the brightest thing in the picture. It glows
amber on his half and cyan on hers, around a near-white core, and the two threads braid along it
as continuous strokes. I could only get those crisp lines once the bar underneath was fully dry.
Painted on tacky paint, the threads broke into dashes, and the lean scumble I tried as a wash of
light on his door turned into a tan blot that I had to paint out. Light from the bridge now
falls as a warm lit strip on the niche's inner wall and lit edges on his door. Her side gets a
few pale cyan facets on the shell beside her opening, and a lit lip where the bridge enters.
The striped seam in the upper void is gone: warm black meets cool black in one clean vertical
edge, with no blending. His half of the arch is now a pointed bronze rib that rises into the
apex at an angle. It meets her round wireframe half there, two different structures holding up
one vault.
