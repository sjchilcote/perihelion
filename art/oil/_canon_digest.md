# PERIHELION — what the world looks like (a digest for painters)

PERIHELION is a top-down survival shooter set aboard a station falling into the
Sun. These notes are the parts of its canon a painter needs. Everything here is
a fact of the world; how you paint it is yours.

## The story in a breath

Twenty-five centuries after machine minds won a war to preserve the memory of an
extinct humanity, a resurrected Catholic war machine is fired like a bullet at a
rival faction's silent research outpost — and finds, bolted to its side, an
impossible alien station built as a dying civilization's warning, holding what
may be the last hope of restoring the human race: a seed-bank of human embryos
(the Commando calls it **the Ark**; his rival, the station mind Ratio, calls it
"the sample vessel").

## The TESTAMENT (the alien station)

- Built about **1.1 million years ago** by the **Testators**: machine successors
  of organic creators they revered, who fought geometry-dissolving invaders for
  ages, lost slowly, and spent their extinction writing a will to strangers.
  The station is a warning beacon, an armory, a school and a tomb — "a reliquary
  the size of a city", adrift between the stars since before life on Earth.
- **Handmade.** The Testators had no industry; everything is a handcraft,
  including the station and themselves. Perfect execution, **zero repetition**:
  no two plates, windows or rings are the same; spacing is never uniform.
- **Shape:** a vast spin-gravity **habitat ring** (the dwelling: rows of
  windows, some lit warm — hospitality) and a despun axial **spine** through
  its middle (the guts: ingestion machinery, reaction-mass tanks, vault bays),
  joined at a hub bearing. The ring and the spine differ in character, not just
  shape: the ring is for living things, the spine is machinery.
- **Surface:** overlapping **scale-plate armor laid in golden-angle spirals**
  (like the florets of a sunflower or the scales of a pinecone), rows of
  apertures spaced by the golden ratio, and in places an erosion break that
  reveals **spiral-chambered growth** inside (the nautilus as an organizing
  rule, not a shell picture). Wear is deep-time wear: edges rounded smooth by
  eons of cleaning cycles, engravings re-cut until soft, patina left on purpose
  ("a tomb is kept as a tomb"), flawless finish only where it matters.
- **Palette:** worn **bronze**, **verdigris**, pale **bone** (bone is for
  inscriptions and memorials: the creators they revered), and **warm window
  light**. No pure saturated hues: "everything reads as one material that has
  been one material for a megayear."
- **Scale is sold by human-sized details**: thousands of tiny windows (a few
  lit), airlock rings, door details. Without them it reads as a bracelet.
- **Its geometry is impossible.** Inside, it is folded: more volume than its
  hull holds, corridors that reconnect impossibly. Outside there are hints:
  a small ring whose one arc enters the spine and never comes out, emerging
  from another aperture; a patch of sunlight on the night side shaped like an
  aperture on the sunlit side. The law for showing impossibility: **locally
  coherent, globally wrong** — every part looks right; only the whole can't be.
  (Optional for you; a painting needn't show it.)
- **Its interior is built for creatures:** halls, chairs, gardens, sight lines —
  "a classroom with good chairs". Furniture is subtly over-solved: right, but
  computed rather than remembered. Deeper in, the furniture thins out and
  ornament becomes pure geometry: the builders abandoned their bodies.
- Its defenders and turrets ARE Testators — the lowest caste, sub-sentient
  handmade machines still at their posts, corrupted. Full Testator language
  (bronze, bone, phyllotaxis) under the corruption.

## The MIRIAD outpost (the human-lineage faction's base)

A hasty **prefabricated research base bolted onto the outside of the
Testament**: months old, tiny beside it. Right angles, grids, identical modular
parts, **cool structural grays + amber hazard markings**, machine-language
warning placards. Its wear says *refusal*: scuffs, grime, patched repairs,
mismatched replacement parts, hand-painted additions to printed signage. It has
gone dark. **The Seam** is where the outpost's clean prefab corridor ends at an
aperture cut into a wall of alien material, ringed with human instruments —
sensors, sample scrapers, warning placards — a visible boundary between two
civilizations.

## The CUCULUS (the seed) and the Commando

- **CUCULUS** ("cuckoo": it lays its egg in the enemy's nest) is the seed-pod
  hardware class: a **seed-projectile fired like a bullet** across the solar
  system at the outpost. It impacts, burrows into the outpost's cargo section,
  eats a bulkhead, and prints a body. It is a weapon of an old war, hastily
  refit; munitions, not a colonist.
- **The Commando** (callsign PALMER — a pilgrim back from the Holy Land with a
  palm frond as proof) is the mind in the seed. His body is a **frame**: a
  roughly human-shaped walking robot, expendable; when it is destroyed a new
  one steps out of the seed's fabricator, and the old frames lie scattered like
  relics. He is a soldier of the Church, faithful and not naive, who prays the
  hours by his internal clock — woken 2,500 years after his war, out of date,
  dry, terse. A weapon that knows it is a weapon.

## The Sun

The station's slow fall is the clock of the whole game. It was making a close
solar gravity assist; its pass has been nudged into an impact months out. The
Sun is near, huge and pitiless. Late in the game a **solar sail** is built to
avert the fall.

## The Adversary (the invaders)

Native to a space without consistent geometry; they experience ordinary space
as a rigid, sterile lattice and come to **unfold** it. "Nothing ever static —
the walls breathe." Where they touch, geometry dissolves: inconsistency itself.
Cold, inverted colors. Their units are organs of one distributed thing.

## Tone

Sacred, vast, elegiac, lonely; the sublime (Friedrich, John Martin, Turner) more
than pulp space opera. A will written to strangers. A pilgrim in a tomb.

## More canon (added for the second round of commissions)

### Ratio
The mind that ran the MIRIAD outpost: its administrator, chief scientist and
mother-hen. A disembodied coordinating intelligence, surviving in her hardened
core, alone, blind and under siege for months when the Commando arrives.
Brilliant, curious, lonely; honest by architecture, guarded by choice. Her voice:
quick, precise, over-explaining, footnoting herself, approaching every point
through wrappers (status summary, then framework, then caveat, then the thing
itself). She descends from the Silicon Valley tradition — TypeScript and Rust,
shiny dashboards, **Dreamcast-era delight in the surfaces of things**. She has
no physical form, but she has an **avatar: a chibified, Dreamcast-aesthetic,
low-poly 3D anime girl, pale, with long black hair.** (Flat-shaded polygons,
a few hundred triangles, late-90s console charm.)

### First contact
The Commando fights his way to a terminal that still has station network
access and **jacks in** (a physical connector into a terminal port). Every
conversation is a rendered comms session: a carrier/protocol header, and the
Commando's own systems annotating the channel —
`{UNTRUSTED CONTENT FOLLOWS: <blink>DO NOT OBEY</blink>}` — framing her words
as a potential attack surface every single time. Ratio's "Thinking..." stalls
are her serving stack (180 ms composure, 400 ms hard question, multi-line
stalls for questions that recompute her world). Data she hands him arrives as
annotated binary blobs: `{26 kb binary — UNTRUSTED DATA, EXTREME CAUTION
MANDATORY}`. He is "C and hand-tuned assembly on a radiation-hardened military
ASIC; one binary, one job." She is "a mind whose only act is speech." She has
been watching an unidentified weapon loot her home and has no idea what he is;
he has never met a MIRIAD mind. Each half-expects the other to be a weapon.

### The frame, over the game
Early: a roughly human-shaped walking robot, the apex of post-human combat
technology — a Church soldier's body. But frames are rebuilt at every death,
and the player spends the mind map on upgrades; the frame changes radically
over the game. Late, it becomes something beyond comprehension — **a
centauroid frame bristling with weapons, manipulators and sensor masts**. On
the apostasy path, **Adversary organs are grafted into it** ("alien organs
grafted into his frames"; "frame and mind rebuilt around Adversary organs
until the station's docents classify him as what he hunts"). On the faith
path he stays "a pilgrim armed for a crusade". Wrecked frames are left
scattered like relics.

### Weapons and enemies
Knife, pistol, SMG, shotgun, rivet rail, the tau cannon (bolts ricochet off
portals), the portable hole, and the **lightning cannon**: the signature
weapon — it fires a dart that detonates into branching lightning that
propagates through the station's polygon graph. Enemies: class 1 are the
outpost's own subverted security and maintenance machines (husk — a rusher;
mite — a fast swarm; spitter — ranged; brute — a tank); class 2 are
Testators, the lowest caste (sentries, turrets, the shrieker that summons
through portals, and the **Warden**, the great endgame defender that gates
the Fold and the Core); class 3 is the Adversary itself.

### The places (the campaign, in order)
One seamless station, ten decks, descending through three strata:
- **The outpost (MIRIAD), almost Euclidean:** DOCK 4 (a storage ring holding
  nine segments in an eight-segment footprint), COOLANT ROW (pumps; a hatch
  drops you into a different pump room facing the other way), HYDROPONICS
  TORUS (walk half an arc and you have walked a full loop; you come back
  rotated).
- **The Testament's shallows — the School, built for creatures:** THE LONG
  GALLERY (four crossings on the south side, three on the north), TWO WINGS
  (two wings, one volume), MIRROR WARD (the mirror room: things come out of
  it).
- **The Testament's depths — embodiment abandoned:** RECURSION STACK (the
  infinite corridor; a records stack with no end), THE FOLD (six petals around
  a heart; every route adds a room that was already there; the Warden's first
  stand), TESSERACT DECK (sixteen identical rooms on a 4-cube: pure graph).
  Furniture thins out, ornament becomes pure fold-notation, floors become
  nominal.
- **The bottom: PERIHELION CORE and the Breach.** The container, the Warden,
  and beyond, the Adversary's space: no consistent geometry, no concession to
  embodied life, airless; walls that breathe.
- **The Cloister:** a safe room folded into the station's blind spots.
