# The Fold: the Warden's First Stand

We stand in the doorway of one petal and look into the heart of the Fold. It is a round bronze chamber under a ribbed dome, its walls laid with scale-plate lips. Three of its arched petal-doors face us and glow warm. In each side door, at the back of the petal-room, the same heart appears again, small and impossible: a lit round chamber with a tiny haloed warden standing in it. Every route adds a room that was already there. The Fold's notation is inlaid in lit bronze in the floor: a heart hexagon, and six petals around it. Each petal holds a smaller copy of the heart, and each is bridged to the heart and to its neighbours. One more bridge runs from under our own feet into the nearest petal, notched in golden-ratio steps. In the centre, in a pool of light, the Warden stands guard and faces us. Its spear is grounded, its halo is up, and one palm is raised: halt, peace or welcome. The other arm hangs at its side as a flat, cold ghost with a double contour and one finger too many. One sector of the halo has broken out and floats displaced, lit cold from the wrong side. The Warden is backlit by the centre door, so it reads as a silhouette with rim-lit edges against the brightest shape in the picture. Nothing in the room repeats exactly: the petals differ in size and turn, the side doors in width and height.

## How it was painted

I painted it in 23 chunks in about half an hour of real time. I used a warm, dark toned ground and laid the big shapes in broadly: the door frame, the dark dome, the mid-bronze wall, the dark floor and the glowing doors. Then I built the architecture and the Warden with small tube strokes for every limb and lame, which I had learned on the Warden painting, and drew the inlay with paired groove-and-lit-line strokes along the hexagons, projected onto the floor's ellipse. The paint gave me two surprises. A light shaft glazed with lead white came out as a heavy, streaky white curtain over the figure. I lifted most of it with a clean flat brush wiped after every stroke, but it left a gray hatch, and I had to repaint the Warden and its surroundings opaque over the dry residue. Blending the floor reflections of the doors mixed them with the dark floor into olive blocks, so I painted them out and replaced them with a few tapering streaks. If I did it again, I would make the beam with a much leaner, shorter glaze tested on a scrap first, or leave it out. I would also give the near floor and the frame more design: the frame's outer band went navy, and the lower third of the floor is still mostly empty polish.
