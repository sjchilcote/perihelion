# The Letter, Before

The pair to *The Letter Home*: the same crew bunk with the same geometry,
months before the fall. The cabin is warm. A reading lamp on a wall arm
hangs over the desk, and its light glows in soft rings on the cream-and-
ochre wall. On the desk, the reader shows the first line of the message
that will never be finished, with a cursor after it. Beside it are a
steaming blue-white mug, a pair of glasses folded on the desktop, a
half-eaten snack bar in its torn red wrapper on a napkin with crumbs, and
the plant in its terracotta pot, green and alive. Above, the same beach
photo of the family is taped to the wall. Beside it a second photo is
pinned, just arrived: a green garden, someone with a child on their
shoulders. The bunk's red blanket is thrown back off the white sheet,
which is still creased where someone just got up. The felt slippers wait
on the floor. No one is there, and no gauntlet.

## How it was painted

I made it in 8 chunks on a warm ground at 4:3. It reuses the other
painter's geometry exactly: bunk, frame, pillow, desk, brackets, reader,
screen, photo, mug, pot and slippers are all their shapes. The wall is
graded in ten warm piles in rings centred under the lamp. The paint
fought me in one place: a light cone onto the desk went on as a flat pale
trapezoid. Widening it with blended edges only made a bigger block, and a
flat wall-colour repaint didn't match. What finally worked was repainting
that patch with the wall's own ring piles, clipped to the patch, so the
lamp's light reads as the wall's own glow.

Still rough: the wall's rings read as a faint target. The lamp arm
passes in front of the beach photo. There is a small dull patch behind
the plant. The bunk is a flat block.
