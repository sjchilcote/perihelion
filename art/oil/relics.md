# Relics

A MIRIAD corridor sinks away into blue-black, with one cold lit hatch at its far end. Against the bulkhead on the right sits the wreck of the Commando's previous frame. Its crimson tabard is still bright and its bone cross is still whole, but the chest is torn open into a dark ragged hole. Spent brass lies scattered on the deck at its feet. The new frame kneels beside it with the same crimson on its back. His head is bowed, and the narrow beam of his helmet lamp falls on his own gauntlet where it rests on the dead body's chest. The only warmth in the picture is that small pool of light holding the two of them, one body twice, a pietà with no mother. The eye moves from the lamp, down the beam and the reaching arm, to the hand on the wound.

How it was painted: the canvas was knifed with a dark bone-brown and ultramarine ground. The corridor planes were blocked in with broad, clipped passes, and the two figures were laid as opaque silhouettes before the tabards went on. The warm lamp glaze surprised me. I laid it over the figures while they were still wet and badgered it, and it clouded the whole group into fog. I won it back by repainting the darks opaque, letting them dry for several painting-days, and only then laying rims, the cross and the brass with a pointed round. I had to repaint the head to bow it, which left a few small seams near the hatch. With more time I would model the armour plates with small tapered strokes instead of flat silhouettes. I would also light the floor pool with stippled touches rather than a glaze, which left stripes.

## Second pass

The director said the figures read as soft blobs, so I built armour over both bodies. The wreck now has a helm tipped forward onto its chest, mismatched pauldrons and buckled breastplate edges. Its right arm has fallen in segments down to a gauntlet on the deck, and it has knee cops and greaves. The kneeling frame got a bowed visored helm with the lamp at its brow, a near pauldron with lames, and a segmented reaching arm. I also gave him a clearly bent knee: a thigh plate, a knee cop and a greave that drops to his foot. I laid the plates as small opaque shapes in lean darks and let them dry for several painting-days. Then I drew their edges with a pointed round: warm lamp-coloured rims only on edges facing the light, dark seams between segments, and cool rims on the kneeling frame's back where the far hatch reaches him. Smoothed polygons painted rounder than I drew them, so the plates are chunky rather than crisp. The edge lines are what make them read as armour.
