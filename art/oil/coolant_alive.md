# Coolant Row, Before the Fall

This is the pair to *Coolant Row, Two Panes and Three*: the same one-point
corridor of the MIRIAD outpost, from the same eye, months earlier with the
power on. Cool white strip lights burn down the centre of the ceiling,
including the ones that hang dead in the later picture. The march of
identical grey pump modules runs down the left. Each has the same louvres,
the same clean printed placard with its red header and the same
amber-and-black hazard band. Each one's amber status lamp is lit. Their
coolant risers climb to the ceiling furred with frost, and frost dusts the
nearest pump tops. On the right the Testament's bronze scale-plate hull
stands under its grey struts and cable rails, warm and clean, with a prefab
panel bolted over it carrying a fresh printed placard. Nobody has painted a
red arrow on it yet. The far hatch is shut and lit, its hazard frame bright.
Amber walkway dashes and deck-plate seams run down the floor. The two-pane
ceiling viewport is full of Sun. Below it lies the hard patch of sunlight
on the deck, and it has three panes. It always had; nobody noticed. In the
foreground, on the cargo case that will later be abandoned, a crew member
has left a red tool case and a white mug with a thread of steam.

## How it was painted

I made it in 8 chunks on the same cool grey ground at 16:9. I reused the
dead painting's projection helper and every coordinate in it: the five
room planes, pump spacing, strut positions, viewport, floor patch, hatch,
panel and cargo case. That way each element sits exactly where its dead
twin sits. The scale plates were drawn with the same U-lip helper, lit
evenly instead of by the sun patch. Two things went wrong:
- My first attempt at room shading, a loaded dark scumble, went on as an
  opaque band full of pale specks.
- The pale "light pools" under the fixtures dried as crackled white discs.
I repainted both as solid graded planes with seam blends. Still rough: the
room is evenly lit and a little flat. The edge of the near floor's shadow
is abrupt. The status-lamp glows are blotchy, and frost spilled from the
nearest pump top onto the module behind it. With more time I would let the
strip lights throw cooler and warmer bands down the corridor and give the
bronze more sheen.
