# The Room That Is Its Own Neighbour

A square bronze room seen from its doorway, in Stratum Three. Its far wall is not a wall but a door into the same room. Through it we see the room again, with the Commando standing in that doorway with his back to us. Through that room's far door is the room again, smaller, and again: an infinite regress converging on a point. Each copy is turned a little further than the last, so the regress twists like a corkscrew. Each copy is also colder, the bronze walls cooling through mauve to a pale violet at the vanishing point. The plate seams of the walls and ceiling and the grating of the floor run continuously through every doorway, so it is visibly one room glued to itself. Every doorway has a thick dark jamb with a lit inner edge, and in each of them the same small figure stands, red tabard and grey cross, looking at himself. In the near doorway he stands closest, back to us.

How it was painted: the tunnel method with a twist. Each copy of the room is the space between two squares, each scaled by 0.46 and rotated a little more. I laid all eight rooms' four faces first, then the continuous longitudinal seams, the joints and grating bars, the doorway jambs, and one figure per doorway turned with its room. Last, I repainted the walls of the deeper copies on a ramp from bronze to violet over dry paint and restated their lines and jambs. The first jambs ran dry partway along each side, so I redid them in reloaded quarter-lengths. What falls short: the floor is the same mauve in every copy rather than cooling with the walls. The rotation per copy is subtle in the first two rooms. The figures beyond the second doorway are too small to read as him, and the near room's walls are a little flat.
