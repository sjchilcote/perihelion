# Holding the Stair

A wide shot from high and to the side. A broad bronze Testator stair curves down from a lit landing at upper left toward a violet fold torn open at its foot, lower right. The Commando holds the landing, braced wide with his red crusader's tabard and grey cross, cannon levelled down the flight. Three Wardens climb toward him. The nearest, halfway up, is a hulking bronze mass with a hooded head and a claw reaching for him. The cannon's cable arcs out of the muzzle, high over the flight and down into it, beaded with bursting plasma. Three impact blooms burn white on the Wardens' chests, and lightning crawls over the leading Warden's hide and down its legs. Balusters along the outer rail are shattered where the fight has passed. Small pale Adversary things with violet bellies and many legs climb out of the fold among the Wardens. Bronze lamps hang in the dark hall. The flight is warm and gold at the top and turns cold violet near the bottom, where the fold's light seeps up the lowest risers.

How it was painted: I built the stair as twenty-six graded tread quads along a curving centre line, with risers, a stringer dropping into the dark, and two rows of balusters spaced by the golden angle. The figures were laid as dark silhouettes and then given lit planes with small strokes once the dark was dry. Pale paint failed four times on this canvas:
- A cold "seep" scumble went opaque and buried the lower flight.
- A lit-side fill turned the Commando olive.
- A glow glaze around the blooms dried as cream ovals.
- A dust scumble and the first lamp glows came out as flat tan and yellow discs.

Each one had to be repainted opaque over dry paint, which cost the stair its first crisp structure and buried the Wardens' first modelling. What falls short: the Wardens are still rough masses rather than designed creatures, and the smoke and dust the brief asked for barely survived. The repainted lower flight is patchy, and the lamps read more as stars than as lamps. Next time I'd lay every glow and haze as a stroke structure on dry paint and never as an area of light paint, and I'd design the Wardens' silhouettes before laying any effect over them.

## Pass at 02:37 CDT

What changed (chunks 29–33), all on dry paint with strokes rather than pale area paint. **Wardens:** I painted out the rough masses back to stair and hall. Each Warden is now a designed silhouette: a hunched, crested carapace on six jointed legs, with two raised forelimbs ending in blades. I modelled each one in stroke bands: overlapping carapace plates lit warm along their upper edges, violet along the underside and back edge from the fold, bone knuckles and a bone stud rosette on the crest, a cold eye, and hot blade edges. The leading Warden has branching lightning crawling over it, and the impact blooms were restated as stroke bursts. **Lamps:** the starbursts are gone, painted out with dark, and replaced by small hanging lanterns: a chain, a bronze cage lit on one side, an amber body and a hot core. **Commando:** I drew plate edges and braced knees on both legs, added cold sabaton edges and tabard tails flaring behind him, and laid red folds into the tabard. **Small things:** I added seven more low, pale, many-legged things scuttling on the lower stair. I also painted out the tan smear by the cable on the upper steps.

What still falls short: the Wardens are now smaller and read as giant beetles more than as hulking defenders. The new scuttlers are tiny dashes, and the older round pale ones look like eyes. The cable's base near the muzzle is lost in a dark patch. The lower stair is still patchy from the first version's repaint.
