   walk links two hops deep, harvest quantitative detail, cite only what it

# Research candidates

## LANE G — Wave composition and role allocation: what mix comes out of a gate, and what decides it
> **CRITIC — lane-level warning.** LANE G — single-source concentration. Candidates 4, 5 and 6 (frontage-keyed mix, inverted mix at contact, disturbance-type key) all come from ONE fetch, the J Insect Sci 2024 battles review, which the lane itself calls 'the highest-yield fetch of the lane'. That is 30% of the lane resting on one document, and two of the three (4 and 6) extract design rules from single qualitative sentences rather than from measurements. The lane also burned its search budget before running the two sub-questions the brief specifically named (task-switching rates; home-defence vs expedition ratios), so both remain unanswered.

### G1. Return-traffic gate — the breach pours only while agents that went out are coming back
**What the source says.** In a distributed forager-dispatch system with no commander, the decision to send another agent out is driven by the CONTACT RATE with agents returning through the same opening, not by any demand estimate. Direct in-nest filming (4 colonies, 22 trials over 9 days) measured: return rate 0.18-2.29 agents/s across colonies; contact rate experienced by an outgoing agent 0.69 +/- 0.29 to 2.68 +/- 0.99 contacts/s (low vs high return periods, all three colonies significantly higher in the high period, e.g. colony 977 t53 = 6.23, P < 0.0001). An outgoing agent leaves 3-8 s after its contact rate steps up, with the step ('change point') a mean 7.3 s before departure. The size of the ready pool waiting at the opening adjusts on the timescale of MINUTES, not seconds. When returning agents were blocked, blocking for 3 min vs 10 min gave different recoveries: after 3 min, dispatch resumed faster (t20 = 2.4, P = 0.029) and the outgoing agents came from the shallow pool at the mouth (t4 = 2.43, P = 0.038); after 10 min both the outgoing count and the mouth pool refilled at the same rate (t5 = 1.34, P = 0.21), i.e. everything had to come up from deep storage. The authors state the stabiliser explicitly: positive feedback (more returns -> more contacts -> more dispatch) checked by negative feedback (more dispatched -> fewer available to be stimulated). Sibling result in the same system: scouts' SAFE RETURN is what starts the main dispatch at all; if scouts do not come back, nothing comes out.
**Proposed game rule.** Give the marrow a per-breach ReturnCredit counter instead of a spawn timer. Any agent that re-crosses a breach INTO the hub adds +1 credit (decaying, half-life ~8 s). The breach dispatches the next agent only when credit > 0, with a 3-8 s latency after the credit step. Two pools behind each breach: a mouth pool (small, ~4-6 agents, refills in seconds from the guardian ring) and a deep pool (the marrow reserve, refills over minutes). If return traffic stops for < ~45 s the next dispatch draws from the mouth pool and resumes fast; if it stops for longer, the mouth pool sinks back into the deep pool and the next surge is slow AND arrives as one clump instead of a stream. Wire the credit signal to the existing Muster field so no new coupling is needed: a re-crossing agent emits Muster on the hub side.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who camps the breach mouth and kills every single agent as it emerges (nothing ever returns) throttles that breach to near-zero output and it seals on its own timer — grinding at the mouth is a WINNING containment play, not an infinite farm. PREDICTS: a player who falls back and lets agents chase him past the mouth, then kills them deep, keeps return traffic alive and the breach floods. PREDICTS the measurable signature: dispatch interval vs time-since-last-return should show a knee, and a 3-min lull recovers visibly faster than a 10-min lull. FORBIDS: constant-rate spawning; forbids the breach being a fixed faucet whose output is independent of what the player does at its mouth. Verifiable in a real run by logging (crossings-in, dispatch times) per breach and checking the correlation is positive with ~3-8 s lag.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 bounds reinforcement rate only by the finite reserve, the 45 s uncrossed-timer, and the one-transmitter-at-a-time rule — all of which are player-independent throttles. This adds a throttle the player controls with his positioning, and it makes the existing 'a crossing is anything crossing the line, either direction' rule do double duty: crossings already gate the timer, now they also gate the pour rate. It also finally supplies the missing answer to 'when is a breach no longer useful to the agents the hub spawns' — when nothing is coming back through it.
**Source.** https://web.stanford.edu/~dmgordon/articles/other/animal-behaviour-86/Animal%20Behaviour%2086%20(2013)%201-11.pdf

### G2. Depot dispatch thresholds that reinforce — wiping a class permanently rewrites the mix (hysteresis)
**What the source says.** A full threshold-reinforcement model of role allocation with no commander. Each agent i holds a threshold theta_ij per task j; commitment probability T_ij(s_j) = s_j^2 / (s_j^2 + theta_ij^2), so T = 0.5 exactly when stimulus equals threshold. Thresholds are NOT fixed: theta decreases by xi*dt while the agent performs the task and increases by phi*dt while it does not, bounded to [theta_min, theta_max] = [1, 1000]. Simulation parameters given explicitly: alpha = 3, beta = 1, p = 0.2 (per-unit-time probability of dropping the task, so mean stint = 1/p), xi = 10, phi = 1, sigma = 0.1; theta(0) = 500 for all agents, x_ij(0) = 0.1; 'specialist' means theta < 100, 'non-specialist' theta > 900. Regimes are sharp: with xi + phi = 11 and N = 100, phi < 0.4 -> every agent becomes a specialist; 0.4 < phi < 2 -> genuine differentiation into distinct role groups; phi > 2 -> no specialization at all. Along the other axis, p < 0.04 -> all specialists; the specialist count collapses just past p = 0.04, then rises again across 0.04 < p < 0.42; p > 0.42 -> all specialists again. Specialists self-limit their own activity to hold the stimulus under threshold: in the worked example, agents specialised on the low-demand task ran at x = 0.55 while agents on the high-demand task ran at x = 0.8. THE KEY PREDICTION: remove 50 of 100 task-1 specialists for a duration Tr and reintroduce them — 'the colony does not recover the same state as that prior to the perturbation, and the difference between before and after the perturbation is more strongly marked as the time between separation and reintroduction increases.' There are TWO threshold values of Tr: below the first, nobody re-specialises; above it, replacements appear (N_n rises) but revert on reintroduction; above a second, larger Tr, the replacements have fixated and the returning originals cannot displace them (N_f rises too).
**Proposed game rule.** Give the MARROW (not the field agents) a threshold vector theta[class][role] over ~3 combat roles: SCREEN (mite, husk), RANGED (spitter, shrieker), BREAKER (brute, warden, flicker). When the hub dispatches class c into role r, theta[c][r] -= xi*dt; every tick it is not dispatched into r, theta[c][r] += phi*dt, clamped to [1,1000]. Pick a dispatch by sampling P = s_r^2/(s_r^2 + theta[c][r]^2) where s_r is the role-demand scalar the hub derives from its own signal reads (RANGED demand rises while the player holds a chokepoint at distance, BREAKER demand rises while player structures stand). Tune phi into the 0.4-2 band scaled to game time so differentiation actually occurs within a 10-minute run rather than collapsing to all-or-nothing. Set the two Tr knees at roughly 60 s and 180 s of a class being absent from the field.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: if the player systematically kills every spitter for ~3 minutes, shriekers (and then husks at range) get pushed into the RANGED slot, and when spitters become available again the mix does NOT snap back — the run's composition histogram is permanently different. PREDICTS a short wipe (< ~60 s) has no compositional effect at all, so the player only gets the payoff for sustained, disciplined target priority. FORBIDS: a static per-run spawn table; forbids composition that is a pure function of run time or difficulty. Directly verifiable: log the per-breach class histogram in 60 s buckets across a run with an enforced class-kill-priority bot vs a random-priority bot; the histograms must diverge and must NOT reconverge after the culled class returns.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 lists 'adaptive counter-biasing — learn how this intruder fights, bias later reinforcement mixes toward counters' as a target with no mechanism and no numbers. This is the mechanism, with published parameter values and, critically, published REGIME BOUNDARIES (phi in 0.4-2, p in 0.04-0.42) so the tuning is not blind. It also adds hysteresis, which the spec does not have anywhere: an irreversible consequence of player behaviour that is not a scripted unlock.
**Source.** https://static.ias.edu/pitp/archive/2012files/18.pdf

### G3. Idle reserve with one-way replacement — only over-killing thins the enemy permanently
**What the source says.** Across 20 groups totalling 1307 agents (mean group size 65.35, median 56, SD 33.01), agents spent a mean 0.607 of observed time inactive (median 0.628, SD 0.146) and only 0.170 active (median 0.154, SD 0.071); the paper states 'upwards of 50% of their workers inactive at any one time'. Role census: inactive 605 (40.8%), outside-task 145 (9.8%), interior-task 249 (16.8%), transit 483 (32.6%). Removal experiment, 20% of agents removed per group (5 groups active-removal, 9 inactive-removal, 6 random): removing the MOST ACTIVE 20% -> group-level activity 'was reestablished within 1 week of removals', with no significant deficit at either 1 or 2 weeks; the idle pool supplied the replacements. Removing the LEAST ACTIVE 20% -> no replacement even 2 weeks later; group inactivity simply dropped and stayed down. The authors' conclusion is the asymmetry: mechanisms exist for maintaining a set number of ACTIVE agents but not for maintaining a set number of idle ones. Independent corroboration in a different system: of 1142 marked agents in 11 groups (4 large, 147-233 members; 7 small, 27-100), 52% (large) and 57% (small) never performed ANY of the four tracked tasks.
**Proposed game rule.** Model the marrow reserve as TWO numbers, not one: ACTIVE (agents eligible to be dispatched now) and IDLE (~45% of total roster, never dispatched while ACTIVE is at quota). Killing an agent in the field decrements ACTIVE; the hub promotes IDLE -> ACTIVE to restore quota over a fixed window (game-scaled equivalent of the 1-week restoration: ~40-60 s). Player kills therefore do NOT reduce pressure until cumulative kills exceed the ACTIVE quota within one restoration window — the moment they do, ACTIVE cannot be refilled and pressure drops and STAYS down, because there is no mechanism that regenerates IDLE except the slow core regen. Show the state diegetically: the guardian ring around the CORE visibly thins as IDLE drains.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a hard, discoverable breakpoint: sustained kill rate below quota/window produces literally zero reduction in incoming pressure (the honest 'you are accomplishing nothing' state the design wants); above it, pressure collapses non-linearly. PREDICTS that a player who trades evenly all run feels no progress and is correct to feel that. FORBIDS: linear attrition where every kill helps a little; forbids the idle pool being replenished by anything except CORE regeneration. Verifiable: plot incoming-agents-per-minute against cumulative player kills; the curve must be flat then knee down, not monotonically declining.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has a single scalar marrow reserve that only exhausts and slowly regenerates. Splitting it into ACTIVE/IDLE with one-way promotion is what makes the spec's own thesis — 'containment, not extermination, is the practical strategy' and 'the reinforcement stream itself is the fight' — actually TRUE as a consequence of the simulation rather than as a hope. It also gives the reserve-exhaustion window (the player's invasion cue) a visible physical correlate: the guardian ring thinning.
**Source.** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0184074

### G4. Frontage-keyed mix — the depot sizes its units to the width of the approach
**What the source says.** Review of inter-group combat. On force composition under terrain constraint: army-ant assault forces are 'overwhelmingly medium-sized workers, allowing a greater total number of ants to occupy the front line' — they deliberately do NOT send their largest class into mass attacks. On the underlying combat mathematics, the review surveys Lanchester's linear law (restricted terrain, only a few units can engage at once) versus the square law (open field, numbers compound), and reports that most studies of this system support the LINEAR law in restricted terrain (fire ants, acacia ants), while a recent study in an open arena fitted the SQUARE law better; results are summarised as 'Linear, but variable with arena type'. Scale of the fights involved: several thousand dead on both sides in a single large nest assault. The review is explicit that specific casualty figures and defence-versus-expedition ratios are otherwise scarce in the literature.
**Proposed game rule.** At the moment the hub opens a breach, it already knows the target chamber. Compute a FRONTAGE number for that chamber = (portal count) x (mean portal width) normalised by chamber area — pure geometry, cheap, needs only the polygon/portal graph that already exists. Low frontage (a corridor-like chamber, one or two narrow portals): the pour is weighted toward FEW, HEAVY units (brute, warden, flicker) because only 2-3 bodies can engage at once and cheap bodies are wasted frontage. High frontage (a wide chamber with many portals): the pour is weighted toward MANY CHEAP units (mite, husk) plus spitters at the back, because numbers compound when everyone can engage. Same total reserve cost either way — the hub is spending its reserve on the mix that the geometry pays for.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that the same player, same run time, same reserve level gets a qualitatively different wave depending on which chamber the spotter transmitted from — and that this is legible: 'they breached the hall, so it is a swarm; they breached the corridor, so it is two brutes.' PREDICTS the player learns to fight in the frontage that suits his loadout and to place turret cordons at low-frontage crossings knowing what will come. FORBIDS: one global wave table; forbids a spotter's choice of transmit location being tactically neutral. Verifiable: run the same seed with breaches forced into a wide chamber vs a narrow one and compare class histograms — they must differ by class, not just count.
**Beyond the current spec.** This is the single biggest thing the spec is missing. DESIGN_7_25 has an extremely unusual asset — a continuous portal-space map where 'the map is polygons in continuous portal space' — and currently nothing about wave composition uses it. The spec says multiple breaches 'split enemies evenly', which is geometry-blind. This makes the level's own topology the primary composition input, which no other candidate does, and it costs one scalar per polygon computed once at load.
**Source.** https://academic.oup.com/jinsectscience/article/24/3/25/7697941

### G5. Inverted mix at contact — the roster at the depot is not the roster that shows up
**What the source says.** Same review. On which classes actually appear at a fight: for a two-class system, heavy-class agents are deployed disproportionately to combat sites even though the light class is the overwhelming majority of the group — 'at battle sites, the major workers outnumber the minors (although the reverse is true for the colony at large).' Separately, in a large leafcutter system, during induced battles 'media ants participated in the bulk of the fighting' while 'large numbers of minima ants were recruited to the battle site, though these mostly did not participate.'
**Proposed game rule.** Two rules. (a) Deliberately make the hub roster and the delivered mix INVERSE: the marrow's standing population is ~70% mite/husk, but a called breach delivers a mix in which brute/warden/flicker OUTNUMBER mite/husk at the point of contact. The light classes are the depot's bulk and its garrison-seeding stock, not its strike package. (b) Add non-combatant arrivals: a fraction (~25-35%) of each pour is mites that arrive, mill inside the Muster field, harass buildings, and do NOT close on the player. They inflate the apparent size of the wave, they occupy frontage, they die to splash — and per DESIGN_7_25 mites are the one class that does not destroy buildings on contact, so a wave that is 30% mites is genuinely less dangerous to a firebase than it looks.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the tension the design wants ('outnumbered and undergunned'): the player sees twenty bodies and reads twenty threats, when eight are the threat. PREDICTS that a player who learns to read the mix can spend far less ammunition per wave — a real skill differential from perception, not reflexes. PREDICTS that a hub invasion feels different from a breach fight: inside the marrow the player meets the TRUE roster (mostly light chaff plus the guardian ring), not the strike mix. FORBIDS: sampling waves from the hub's standing population; forbids every arriving agent being a committed attacker. Verifiable: class histogram of hub population vs class histogram of a pour must be inverted, and a fraction of arrivals must be loggable as never having entered the player's chamber.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 treats the hub as a spawn source whose composition is unstated, and treats the hub area as 'an entire game area unto itself — to be designed'. This gives the hub area a designed identity that is derived from the combat system rather than invented: it is the place where the real, unflattering demographics live. It also makes the flocking rule ('keep-apart weighted above bunch-together, loose formation with visible gaps') read as tactical rather than cosmetic, because the gaps are full of chaff.
**Source.** https://academic.oup.com/jinsectscience/article/24/3/25/7697941

### G6. Disturbance-type key — the mix is chosen by WHAT hurt us, not how much
**What the source says.** Same review, on threat-specific composition: in a large leafcutter system, 'significantly more soldier class... were recruited to simulated vertebrate disturbances than to disturbances by other ants' — i.e. the class mix committed depends on the CATEGORY of the disturbance, not merely its intensity. The review also notes the response is spatially conditional: agents of one desert genus show increased aggression closer to their own nest, and proximity affects the decision to engage at all.
**Proposed game rule.** Do not add a new field. Add a 2-bit TAG carried alongside the existing Distress scalar per polygon, set by the last thing that harmed an agent there: {STRUCTURE (turret/sentry beam), SUSTAINED_FIRE (rail/autogun), BURST_AOE (Tau detonation)}. The tag propagates with Distress hop-by-hop and decays with it, so it obeys the existing constitution exactly (one scalar plus a tag per polygon, sampled only locally and one hop out). The marrow reads the tag at the breach target and biases the pour: STRUCTURE -> spitter-heavy (projectile classes prioritise buildings per spec) plus flicker to close distance; SUSTAINED_FIRE -> brute/warden-heavy (mass absorbs a firing line, and knockback is mass-scaled per spec, so heavy bodies are the correct counter); BURST_AOE -> mite/husk swarm dispersed via the flocking keep-apart weight, so a single Tau cannot clear the wave. The tag is also the natural key for the spec's 'evidence relay'.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the spec's stated counterplay reward is measurable: a bot that fires only one weapon type all run should face a mix that gets progressively worse for that weapon, and switching weapons should measurably slip the counter within one or two waves. PREDICTS a turret-heavy firebase specifically draws spitters, which is exactly the pressure that makes the pheromone scrubber and repair-station placement matter. FORBIDS: composition keyed to a single scalar 'aggro' number; forbids the counter being invisible (the player must be able to name why brutes started showing up). Verifiable in a real run: force a bot to use only Tau for 5 minutes and confirm mean inter-agent spacing in arriving waves increases and mite/husk share rises.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 names 'adaptive counter-biasing', 'evidence relay', and 'counterplay reward' as three separate unimplemented targets. This collapses all three into one 2-bit addition to a field that already exists, with no new system and no cross-system coupling — it goes through the shared substrate exactly as the architecture rules demand. Candidate 2 supplies the slow, hysteretic version of the same idea at the depot; this is the fast, per-breach version.
**Source.** https://academic.oup.com/jinsectscience/article/24/3/25/7697941

### G7. No field re-roling, and an off-ratio penalty that rewards selective culling
**What the source says.** A direct manipulation of class ratio. Groups were built at 10, 25, 50, 60, 75 and 90% heavy-class; the natural composition was measured at 10-12% heavy class, matching field values. Two results matter. FIRST, no compensation: 'Minors did not compensate for their large depletion in the colony by performing more social behaviors', and the heavy class showed 'no significant changes... regardless of the composition of the nest population'; at every ratio tested the observed behavioural repertoire of the heavy class matched the rarefaction-curve prediction, i.e. neither class expanded its repertoire to cover the other's absence. Per-class activity was flat across all ratios: light class 70.1% +/- 10.8 active, heavy class 50.8% +/- 17.3 active at the natural 10% ratio, with the heavy class holding between 40% and 50% activity at every ratio. Task frequencies stayed class-fixed (interior-tending relative frequency 0.205 light vs 0.104 heavy — roughly 2x). SECOND, an off-ratio penalty: total group-level social behaviour dropped significantly as soon as the ratio departed from natural, with groups at 25% or more heavy class averaging 9-17.8 behaviours per scan against a higher baseline at natural composition.
**Proposed game rule.** Two hard rules. (a) FORBID field re-roling outright: a husk never learns to act like a spitter, a brute never picks up a shrieker's marking behaviour. Class behaviour is fixed; only the DEPOT's dispatch mix changes (Candidate 2). This is a constraint, and it is the cheap one — it deletes a whole category of AI complexity the design was drifting toward. (b) Track the live field ratio against a per-world natural ratio (start at ~10-12% heavy = warden+brute share, mirroring the measured natural value). Compute deviation and apply it as a coordination-efficiency multiplier on the enemy's collective behaviours ONLY — the Muster spread coefficient and the room-danger threshold-holding group size — never on individual damage or hit points. At natural ratio, agents mass properly before crossing a dangerous threshold; at 40% heavy, they trickle in and get killed piecemeal.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that a player who kills ONLY spitters, or only wardens, degrades the entire response's coordination rather than just removing one threat — targeted culling beats even trading, and the reward shows up as enemies attacking in ones and twos instead of as a mass. PREDICTS the inverse trap: a player who kills only the cheap chaff drives the ratio heavy and also degrades coordination, but faces individually harder units — a genuine choice. FORBIDS damage/HP scaling as the expression of the penalty (per the director's 'rebalance via kinematics, not damage inflation'). Verifiable: log mean group size at threshold-holding events against live class ratio; the curve must peak at the natural ratio.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 says removing any one agent 'thins the response without stopping it' but gives no mechanism by which WHICH agent you removed matters. This makes composition, not headcount, the thing the player attacks — and it does so with a measured natural ratio (10-12%) and a measured no-compensation result, so it is grounded rather than invented. It also pre-empts a design mistake the spec is one step away from: 'adaptive counter-biasing' read as 'agents adapt their behaviour', which the source says does not happen.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3014662/

### G8. Vulnerability-keyed thresholds — the class that responds first is the class the current threat hurts most
**What the source says.** A proposed replacement for arbitrary threshold distributions: an agent's response threshold for a task is set by that agent's own SUSCEPTIBILITY to the hazard the task addresses, so the agents most damaged by a condition are the ones that act first to relieve it — weakness causes specialization, not the other way round. Direct test in a thermal-load system: two groups were heated from 29 C to 34 C over 4.5 minutes; 42 agents that performed the cooling response and 41 non-responding controls were collected; placed in a 48 C bath, the responders died significantly sooner than the non-responders (p = 0.02, linear mixed-effects model with source group as random effect). The authors flag the framework as conceptually novel but empirically preliminary — this one experiment is essentially the whole direct evidence base.
**Proposed game rule.** When the marrow samples P = s^2/(s^2 + theta^2) for a class (Candidate 2), do not draw theta from a flat random distribution — set theta[c] inversely to how badly class c suffers from the CURRENT tagged threat (Candidate 6). The class most vulnerable to what is happening has the LOWEST threshold and pours first. Concretely: sustained rail fire is worst for low-HP mites -> mites lead the wave; the Tau AoE is worst for tight-packed husks -> husks lead; the sentry beam is worst for the slow-turning brute -> brutes lead. The heavier, tougher-versus-that-threat classes are held back and arrive second.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a fully legible, learnable tell: the composition of the FIRST rank through a breach tells the player what the enemy currently believes is hurting it, and it is always the wrong unit for the job — the enemy feeds its most vulnerable class into the threat first. PREDICTS the exploit and therefore the skill ceiling: an advanced player deliberately establishes a threat profile in one chamber to pull the class he most wants to farm, which is exactly the spec's 'a truly advanced player will intuit how the system communicates with itself and manipulate it to his advantage.' FORBIDS uniform-random per-agent thresholds. Verifiable: histogram the first 3 arrivals of each pour versus the last 3; they must differ systematically by weapon used in the preceding 30 s.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25's quorum rule uses a 'per-agent-varying threshold' with the variation unspecified — implicitly random. Random variation produces a cascade but carries zero information for the player. Keying it to vulnerability makes the same cascade READABLE, which is the difference between an enemy that feels noisy and one that feels like it is thinking. Flagged honestly: this is the thinnest evidence base of any candidate here (one experiment, n = 83), so treat it as a design idea the source inspired rather than a well-supported natural law.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783416/

### G9. Three-clock separation — dispatch in seconds, availability in minutes, roster in the long run
**What the source says.** Three independently measured timescales in the same class of commanderless system. SECONDS: an agent commits and leaves 3-8 s after its contact rate steps up. MINUTES: the size of the ready pool at the opening adjusts over minutes, and interrupting return traffic for more than about 4-5 minutes drives the ready pool back into deep storage; the 3-min vs 10-min interruption experiments recover differently (t20 = 2.4, P = 0.029). LONG: in a separate system, group-level activity after removal of the most active 20% is reestablished 'within 1 week', and the idle pool that supplied the replacements is never itself replenished. A third system gives the maturation clock: role stages run cell-cleaning at emergence, interior tasks days 2-11 or 4-12, receiving/middle stage days 11-21, external tasks from about day 20, against a lifespan of roughly 1 year; the retreat direction is heavily restricted — when the youngest agents were removed, only about 20% of external-task agents could reacquire the interior role. The same review notes the acute/sustained split explicitly: under an acute resource pulse all middle-stage agents should be shuffled to external tasks fast, but under sustained scarcity they should AVOID the transition because it is physiologically one-way and shortens life.
**Proposed game rule.** Set the marrow's three constants at strictly separated orders of magnitude and never let a designer collapse them: DISPATCH latency 3-8 s (Candidate 1); READY-POOL refill ~45-90 s, with a hard fallback to the deep pool past ~90 s of no return traffic; ROSTER regeneration on the couple-of-minutes scale the spec already sets for core-kill recovery. Add the one-way rule: at most ~20% of agents committed to a forward garrison can ever be recalled to the hub; the rest are spent. And add the acute/sustained switch: while Distress is SPIKING the hub empties its ready pool (fast, cheap, disposable mix); while Distress is high but FLAT (a turtling player), the hub deliberately withholds — it stops feeding the ready pool and accumulates, because spending into a stable firebase is throwing agents away.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that a fast, aggressive player is met by fast, thin, cheap waves, and a slow grinding player is met by silence that ends in one large mass — which is exactly the pressure that should push a turtling player over the extreme-Distress direct-connection threshold rather than letting him farm a steady drip. FORBIDS the failure mode the spec already recorded ('a sparse stomp — the enemies just don't have any chance to react'): if the three clocks are within 2x of each other the system reads as one clock and nothing feels coordinated. Verifiable: hold a bot in a fortified room emitting constant Distress and confirm arrivals become bursty (increasing inter-wave gap, increasing wave size), not steady.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec sets exactly two timing constants (45 s uncrossed-breach seal; 'a couple of minutes' of downtime after a core kill) and no dispatch or availability clock at all. It also has no acute-versus-sustained distinction, which means a turtling player currently gets the same treatment as a charging one until the extreme-Distress threshold fires — a cliff with nothing before it. This fills the gap between ordinary play and that cliff.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2810364/

### G10. Specialists are not more efficient — a class's edge must be access, not throughput

### H1. Denominator gate — an agent wakes on the ACTIVE FRACTION it can sense, not on absolute signal level
**What the source says.** Rosenthal, Twomey, Hartnett, Wu & Couzin (PNAS 2015) reconstructed who-can-see-whom in schools of 154 fish and fitted the probability that agent i reacts given that agent j has already reacted: P(i|j) = (1 + exp(-b1 - b2*LMD - b3*AR))^-1 with fitted coefficients b1=0.302, b2=-1.421, b3=-0.126, where LMD is log metric distance and AR is the rank of j by how large it looms in i's field of view (rank 1 = largest). They then simulated three spread models on the reconstructed networks (5,000 parameter sets, 50 replicates each): simple contagion (link weight only), NUMERICAL threshold (absolute count of active neighbours), and FRACTIONAL threshold (fraction of visible neighbours active). Verbatim: 'We find that the transmission of behavior in our fish schools is best described by a fractional contagion process'. The mechanism is explicitly inhibitory: 'if an initiator has a relatively low rank (i.e., there are many individuals that subtend higher areas on the retina that do not respond), the focal individual is inhibited from responding', and agents 'near the group center ... are more inhibited from responding (thus inhibiting the cascade) because they tend to have a large number of nonresponding neighbors.' Spontaneous unprovoked reactions occur 'approximately once every 3.3 h per individual'; waves propagate at 'approximately twice the maximum swim speed of any individual' and decelerate with distance/time; frames in the cascade figure are 167 ms apart.
**Proposed game rule.** Replace the wake test 'Distress+Noise > my threshold' with a fraction test. Each idle agent, at wake-check time, uses the find/senses API to enumerate agents it can actually see in its own chamber plus one hop, splits them into ALERTED and CALM, and wakes with probability sigma(w0 + w1*log(dist_to_nearest_alerted) + w2*rank_of_alerted_by_apparent_size + w3*(alerted/(alerted+calm))). Calm visible neighbours subtract. Keep the scalar fields as the long-range term but let the local denominator gate the commit.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: cascades ignite at garrison edges and thin pickets and crawl inward, never from the middle of a packed garrison; the same Noise value produces a cascade in a 3-agent corridor and nothing in a 12-agent hall. PREDICTS a heavy-tailed distribution of cascade sizes from one shot (most shots wake 0-2 agents, rare ones wake a wing). PREDICTS a measurable wave: log the tick each agent wakes; the alert front should move outward at roughly 2x agent top speed and DECELERATE with hop distance. FORBIDS the current spec's failure mode where one loud event flips an entire dense room at once, and forbids a lone screaming agent dragging a big garrison out of position — in a dense group its rank is low and it is ignored.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 §Quorum specifies exactly the model this dataset REJECTS: 'idle agent wakes when combined Distress+Noise crosses a per-agent-varying threshold' is a numerical/absolute rule with no denominator. Adding the calm-neighbour denominator is the single highest-leverage change in this lane: it makes group size, formation spacing (which the spec already has via keep-apart flocking) and chamber occupancy into automatic modulators of wake sensitivity, with zero new signals and no new coupling.

### H2. Two-knob commit curve — nerve (T) and discipline (k) are separate tunables
**What the source says.** Sumpter & Pratt (Phil Trans R Soc B 2009, read in full) define the quorum response as p_x = a + (m-a) * x^k / (T^k + x^k), where x is the number already committed, T is the population at 50% commitment, k is steepness, a and m are floor and ceiling probabilities; they state 'A quorum response occurs if only if k>=2' — below 2 it is not a quorum response at all, just amplification. Verified verbatim from a second targeted fetch of the same page: '75.5 per cent of individuals choose X for a shallow response, while 83.3 per cent do so for the steep quorum response' and 'a steep quorum function (k=9) yielded a more accurate decision, but the time taken for all individuals to choose was longer on average (307.8+/-71.0 time steps, mean+/-s.d.) than when k=1 (253.7+/-64.0 time steps).' So steepening bought +7.8 percentage points of accuracy for +21% decision time. The review also reports that under urgency colonies 'made dramatic increases in acceptance rate, and smaller decreases in T', and 'ants also used a significantly steeper quorum function when accuracy was emphasized under low urgency'. Their fitted parameters for a shelter-choice dataset were S=40, theta=0.01, rho=1667, alpha=2.
**Proposed game rule.** Implement wake/commit as an explicit Hill curve p = a + (m-a) * x^k/(T^k + x^k), with x = the fraction-gated activity estimate from candidate 1. Ship k >= 2 always (below 2 the behaviour visibly stops being a quorum). Make T and k respond to context from fields the spec already has: high local Danger or high Muster => lower T and lower k (commit early and sloppily, the classic panic push); quiet ground with only Patrol and Distress => raise T and raise k (deliberate, high-agreement commit). Tune to hit a target cascade time of ~1-2 s per chamber, which is the spec's stated feel.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS an instrumented, falsifiable curve: sweep k in {1,2,5,9} in a fixed test vector and the fraction of agents that commit to the CORRECT (loudest) of two competing chambers should rise while chamber-wide commit time rises ~20%. FORBIDS silent regression to linear recruitment: if a build shows commit probability roughly proportional to neighbour count, k has drifted below 2 and quorum is not actually implemented, regardless of what the code claims.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has one knob (a per-agent threshold). This gives two orthogonal knobs with a published shape and a published cost curve, plus a hard validity floor (k>=2) that doubles as a regression test.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2689713/

### H3. Call-off cross-inhibition — a committed agent that meets a rival commitment goes UNCOMMITTED, never switches directly
**What the source says.** Seeley et al. (Science 2012, abstract via PubMed) showed scouts advertising one site deliver inhibitory 'stop signals' specifically to scouts advertising OTHER sites: 'each scout targets scouts reporting sites other than her own', and 'cross inhibition between populations of scout bees increases the reliability of swarm decision-making by solving the problem of deadlock over equal sites.' Pais et al. (PLOS ONE 2013, full text) formalise it as dx_i/dt = r_i(v_i) - rho_i*x_i - beta_i*x_j*u + noise, where u is the uncommitted pool and beta is cross-inhibition strength, and derive the deadlock-breaking bifurcation at beta_c = 2*(rho_0 + beta_0)/v, i.e. beta_c is inversely proportional to option VALUE: for two equally-valued options, low value + low beta leaves a stable deadlock, high value breaks it. Engineering follow-through (arXiv 2509.07561v2, N=100 simulated, 50 physical robots, scaling tested at N in {50,200,400}): direct-switch voting collapses under mild stubborn-agent bias while cross-inhibition holds cohesion to 'eta <~ 0.4' and 'eta <~ 0.17' for two bias types, and 'decision speed under cross-inhibition remains nearly constant across swarm sizes' whereas direct-switch slows as N grows; moderate noise can even IMPROVE cross-inhibition accuracy 'by eliminating the suboptimal attractor'.
**Proposed game rule.** Give each committed agent a target chamber id. When two agents in the same chamber are committed to DIFFERENT targets, each has a chance per contact to knock the other back to uncommitted (never to convert it). Uncommitted agents are the only ones recruitable. Set the knock-back rate as the beta knob and scale target 'value' from the target chamber's Distress+Noise, so beta_c ~ 1/value falls out: two mediocre targets leave the group deliberately stalled and patrolling, two hot targets get a decisive commit.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the enemy force never splits 50/50 between two comparable threats when both are hot — it picks one and mass-commits, and the choice can flip run to run. PREDICTS deliberate indecision as a FEATURE: when the player is quiet and there are two faint disturbances, the force stalls (deadlock) instead of dribbling agents into both. PREDICTS decision latency roughly flat as the garrison grows from 5 to 40 agents. FORBIDS a single stubborn/scripted agent hijacking the group: robustness holds to a stubborn fraction well above any single unit.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 asserts 'Multiple simultaneous breaches ... split enemies evenly' as a flat rule. Cross-inhibition replaces the flat rule with a mechanism that produces even splitting only when the targets are near-equal AND high value, and produces decisive massing otherwise — which is the difference between a horde that dilutes itself and one that feels like it made a decision. It also supplies the spec's missing 'no order given' consensus and a published robustness envelope against a loud outlier.
**Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22157081/ ; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0073216 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07561v2

### H4. Sticky commit — the release level sits below the commit level (state hysteresis with no memory)
**What the source says.** Couzin, Krause, James, Ruxton & Franks (J Theor Biol 2002, read in full via pdftotext) swept one individual-level parameter (zone-of-orientation radius r_o) up and then back down with everything else fixed. Going UP: near r_o = 1 the group is an unstructured swarm; 'As r_o increases towards 1.5, the group exhibits a rapid behavioural transition and enters the torus phase'; 'Beyond 2.5, however, the group enters a polarized configuration'. Coming back DOWN the group does NOT retrace: 'Group polarization does not decrease, and angular momentum remains low and the group does not adopt the torus conformation. As r_o decreases below 1.5, however, the polarization rapidly disappears'. Verbatim conclusion: 'the transition between behavioural states depends on the previous history (structure) of the group, even though individuals have no explicit knowledge of what that history is.'
**Proposed game rule.** Two levels per state transition, never one. Wake when the gated activity estimate exceeds T_commit; return to idle only when it falls below T_release, with T_release ~ 0.5-0.6 * T_commit. Same for garrison membership, for hold-at-the-doorway, and for the spotter's Infiltrate/Transmit switch. No timers, no stored history — the asymmetry alone produces group-level memory.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that a chamber which has cascaded once stays committed through a signal dip that would never have woken it, so a player who stops shooting for two seconds does NOT get an instant reset. PREDICTS that identical field values produce different behaviour depending on which way the fight has been trending — observable by logging (field value, state) pairs and finding two populations at the same value. FORBIDS threshold chatter: no agent should flip idle/alert more than once per few seconds at a steady field value; if the log shows flicker, the release level is wrong.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25's Stand-down is a 5-second timer ('local signal stayed faint ~5s'). A timer is a per-agent clock that has to be stored, reset, and desynchronised. A second threshold is stateless, cheaper, produces the same anti-flicker guarantee, AND produces collective memory the timer cannot: the whole group's state depends on the path the fight took, not just where it is now.
**Source.** https://jmvidal.cse.sc.edu/library/couzin02a.pdf (Couzin et al. 2002, J. theor. Biol. 218:1-11; read locally with pdftotext)

### H5. Contact-rate density estimate — small chambers feel crowded, and blocked contact reads as empty
**What the source says.** Pratt (Behavioral Ecology 16(2):488-496, 2005; abstract read verbatim) tested how agents actually detect a quorum. Verbatim: 'I show that ants perceive the achievement of a quorum through their rate of direct encounters with nest mates at the site. When ants in a crowded site were prevented from tactile contact with nest mates, they recruited by tandem runs, as though to an empty nest. Furthermore, when the encounter rate was raised independent of population, by reducing the size of the candidate nest, ants started to transport at a significantly lower population. The switch occurred at the same encounter rate regardless of nest size, whether the rate was measured as the mean over the entire visit or as the inverse of the latency until the first encounter.' Also verbatim: 'Ants cease monitoring quorum presence after switching to transport, coincident with an abrupt shortening of visit duration by approximately 2 min, which may be interpreted as the time required for quorum detection.' Corroborating fetch (Franks et al., Sci Rep 5:11890) reports quorum thresholds in the range 4-18 agents per site with larger colonies using larger thresholds and ~2 min to assess encounter rate — note this range comes from a fetch summary of that article, not a verbatim quote I confirmed.
**Proposed game rule.** Do not derive the density estimate from a headcount or from a scalar field. Each agent keeps a decaying counter of CONTACT EVENTS (another agent entering its personal radius) and compares contacts-per-second to its threshold; equivalently, use inverse-latency-to-first-contact, which is cheap and the source says works equally well. Chamber area then modulates commit for free: the same eight agents read as a mob in a corridor node and as a scattering in a hall. Cap the sampling window at ~2 s of game time and stop sampling once committed.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS chokepoints, stairwells and small polygons cascade first and are where garrisons form; big halls need visibly more bodies before anything commits. PREDICTS the pheromone scrubber and any keep-apart force are ALSO anti-quorum tools — spreading agents apart lowers contact rate and starves the commit even at constant headcount. PREDICTS a hard player lever: a cordon that physically prevents agents from meeting makes a fully occupied room read as empty and reinforcements keep being called as if nobody is there. FORBIDS area-blind behaviour where a 4x-larger polygon behaves identically at the same agent count.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's signal fields are one scalar per polygon with no notion of area or of who is standing next to whom, so chamber geometry currently cannot influence coordination at all. Contact-rate sensing makes level geometry a first-class tactical variable using only the find/senses API the spec already mandates, with no new field and no new coupling.
**Source.** https://academic.oup.com/beheco/article-abstract/16/2/488/297922 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4495386/

### H6. Recall runner — a wounded survivor carries a directed damping packet back to whoever is rallying to the place that hurt it
**What the source says.** Two university reports on the 'stop signal' work (Nieh lab). Mechanism: 'A bee delivers a stop signal by giving another bee a brief, vibrational pulse, usually through a head-butt'; duration 'about a tenth of a second'; vibration 'about 380 times a second'. Production is triggered by RECEIVING aggression, not by delivering it: 'Bees foraging for food that attacked other bees or experienced no aggression did not produce stop signals.' Magnitudes: during aggressive competition victims increased stop-signal production 'more than 40 times'; exposure to alarm scent 'an average of 14 times'; a simulated bite (mechanically pinched legs) 'an average of 88 times'. Effect on the receiver: the signal 'decreases their recruitment', so 'fewer nest mates go to the dangerous food site', letting the group 'reduce or cease recruitment to the dangerous food patch in proportion to the danger experienced'. The signal also grades with threat: 'Attacked foragers reduced their waggle dancing and produced stop signals that increased in pitch according to predator size', and the larger attacker triggered 'higher pitched stop signals that were more effective at stopping waggle dancing'.
**Proposed game rule.** An agent that takes damage in chamber C and survives switches to RECALL: it withdraws one to two hops and, on each contact with an agent committed to C, applies a damping packet that pushes that agent back toward uncommitted, with strength scaled by the damage it took (a ~6x span between a graze and a near-death hit is well within what the source shows). Recall runners expire after a handful of deliveries. The packet is delivered by contact, not by field, so it is directional and finite.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a genuinely new player tactic that no current rule produces: WOUNDING and letting go actively suppresses the flow into a chamber, while killing cleanly leaves nobody to carry the recall and the stream keeps coming. Directly instrumentable — compare inflow rate to a kill-box under a wound-heavy loadout vs a one-shot-kill loadout. FORBIDS the infinite conveyor of bodies into an established turret line: the group throttles its own commitment in proportion to damage taken, which is exactly the 'combat can resolve in the enemy's favour, but they are not suicidal' feel the spec asks for. PREDICTS that a player who kills every wounded straggler measurably raises the reinforcement rate against himself.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has Danger (emitted only on a KILL, no spread, very slow fade) — a place-scalar with no carrier and no dose. This adds (a) a signal generated by SURVIVING damage rather than by dying, (b) a carrier agent the player can intercept or fail to intercept, and (c) inhibition aimed at agents already committed to a specific chamber rather than a blanket area penalty. It is also the negative-feedback half the spec is missing: every field listed in the Appendix is excitatory except Danger, and Danger cannot damp an already-committed push.
**Source.** https://biology.ucsd.edu/about/news/article_021110.html ; https://today.ucsd.edu/story/biologists_discover_sophisticated_alarm_signals_in_honey_bees

### H7. Dilution of attention — N comparable targets divide the density estimate and postpone the commit
**What the source says.** Seeley & Visscher (Behav Ecol Sociobiol 2004; abstract read verbatim) ran the decisive manipulation: 'In paired trials, we presented each of four swarms once with five nest boxes close to each other at a site and once with a single nest box. The multiple nest boxes caused the scouts visiting the site to be dispersed among five identical nest cavities rather than concentrated at one. We observed long delays in the start of piping and the start of takeoff in the five-nest-box trials relative to the one-nest-box trials.' The design point is that total scout traffic and total site quality were unchanged; only the CONCENTRATION of scouts per cavity changed, and that alone delayed the commit signal and the group's departure.
**Proposed game rule.** Because commit is density-per-target (candidates 1 and 5), any set of N comparable attractors in the same neighbourhood automatically divides the per-target estimate by ~N. Turn this into a player tool: several cheap decoy emitters, or several small structures, placed in a cluster of adjacent polygons split the searching agents across targets and push the commit out. No new subsystem — it is a consequence of doing the density estimate per target instead of per area.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a monotone, plottable relationship: time-to-cascade in a fixed test vector should grow with the number of comparable attractors, and total inflow should be unchanged (the agents are all still there — they are just spread). FORBIDS the degenerate outcome where planting more decoys simply attracts more total enemies; the mechanism must DELAY, not amplify. Gives the player a legible counterplay against the anti-turtle flank breach: dilute the disturbance across several polygons rather than concentrating it in one.
**Beyond the current spec.** Additive to the spec's building economy, but new as a coordination rule: DESIGN_7_25 has no mechanism by which the player can manipulate the enemy's DECISION TIME as opposed to its routing. This is the cheapest available 'a truly advanced player will intuit how the system communicates with itself and manipulate it' payoff named in the Adversary constitution.
**Source.** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-004-0814-5

### H8. Quorum gate before recruitment — without it, bigger forces fragment EXPONENTIALLY worse
**What the source says.** Zhao, Su & Lynch (invited paper, MIT/Northeastern; full PDF read locally) analysed a colony-relocation model with pairwise recruitment ('tandem runs') but NO quorum rule, i.e. quorum size set to N so it never fires. Their Theorem 1 bounds the probability of the population converging on one site: the consensus probability C for a given tolerance is 'upper bounded by 4 exp(-(eps_0^2 N)/2), which decreases to 0 exponentially fast as N increases', and they note 'the smaller Delta is, i.e., the more stringent the consensus, the lower is the upper bound'. Their own summary: 'without quorum sensing, the only form of recruitment, tandem runs, does speed up the emigration process, but our results show that emigrations would still have a high probability of splitting among multiple new sites'. They extend the result from two candidate sites to k sites.
**Proposed game rule.** Build order matters: the quorum gate must land BEFORE or WITH Relay-recruit, not after. Concretely, an agent may only be recruited toward a target while it is UNCOMMITTED, and it may only become committed by crossing the quorum curve — pairwise recruitment alone must never be able to commit an agent. Also scale the threshold with the local population (larger garrisons need proportionally more agreement) rather than using one global constant.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a specific, testable regression: if a build ships Relay-recruit without the quorum gate, larger garrisons will fragment across targets MORE than small ones, and the fragmentation gets exponentially worse with garrison size. That is measurable in one farm run — log, per push, the fraction of committed agents heading to the modal target as a function of garrison size; a downward curve in that plot is the signature. FORBIDS the intuition that more agents will naturally produce a more coherent-looking response; the opposite is true without the gate.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 lists Quorum and Relay-recruit as two peer bullet points in a list of behaviours to build, with no stated dependency. This supplies the dependency and the reason, and it explains a failure the project has already observed in the field (07-20 to 07-23: 'the coordination never got a chance to show', reinforcements never converging) as something other than bad luck.
**Source.** https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10324427 (Zhao, Su & Lynch, 'Lack of Quorum Sensing Leads to Failure of Consensus in Temnothorax Ant Emigration'; PDF read locally with pdftotext)

### H9. Pace-matched propagation — fast signals only travel through tightly-clustered portal neighbourhoods, slow signals travel along the shortest chains
**What the source says.** Nature Communications 2022 (read via PMC8931172) shows the simple-vs-complex character of a spreading behaviour is set by its PACE, with no threshold anywhere in the model. Leader-follower consensus dynamics dx_i/dt = sum_j w_ij x_j(t) + w_i0 u(t) with a leader driven at u(t)=sin(omega t). On small-world graphs with N=240 nodes and average degree 16: at low driving frequency (omega <~ 1e-2) spreading effectiveness tracks the average shortest path (Spearman rs ~ -1 with path length) — simple contagion; above omega ~ 1e-2 effectiveness instead tracks the clustering coefficient (rs ~ +1) — complex contagion. Confirmed on 10 physical robots (9 followers, 1 leader) across random, ring and caveman topologies all at average degree 4, leader frequency 0.03-0.3 Hz, with the clustered 'caveman' topology strongest in the 0.06-0.2 Hz band. Verbatim: 'the nature of the contagion - simple or complex - is tightly related to the intrinsic pace of the behavior that is spreading.'
**Proposed game rule.** Stop treating all six-plus signal fields as the same kind of object on the same graph. Fast-cycling signals (Noise, Muster) should only be allowed to build across portal neighbourhoods with high local clustering (polygons whose one-hop neighbours are also connected to each other); slow signals (Distress, Danger, Patrol) should propagate along shortest chains regardless of clustering. Implementation is a per-signal spread rule keyed on a precomputed per-polygon clustering coefficient of the portal graph — a static level property, computed once at world build.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS which chambers can ever cascade, from level geometry alone and before any agent runs: a long hub-and-spoke corridor carries slow alarm fine but CANNOT sustain a fast rally, while a dense ring of interconnected polygons can. That is a level-design lever and a debugging oracle — if a rally fires in a low-clustering region, the propagation rule is wrong. FORBIDS the assumption that tuning fade constants alone controls reach; the same fade constant gives qualitatively different reach on different parts of the portal graph.
**Beyond the current spec.** The Appendix table assigns each signal an independent spread/fade rate but never links a signal's timescale to the topology it has to cross, so Muster's 'very exponential dropoff, fades VERY FAST' spec is currently untunable in any principled way — it will behave differently in every wing of the world for reasons nobody can predict. This gives the missing rule and turns the portal graph itself into the coordination substrate.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8931172/

### H10. Persistent per-agent initiator trait — a small, fixed minority starts most cascades
**What the source says.** Mugica, Torrents, Cristin, Puy, Miguel & Pastor-Satorras (arXiv:2203.05473, full text read locally) tracked N=40 fish at 20 fps over 12,000-frame series and defined avalanches as trains of consecutive frames with at least one agent turning past a threshold angle. Cascade sizes and durations are heavy-tailed with fitted exponents tau_s ~ 2.03 and sigma_s ~ 3.03 for size, tau_t ~ 2.33 and sigma_t ~ 1.59 for duration. Crucially the propensity to START one is a persistent individual trait, not noise: leadership probability (fraction of the avalanches an agent participates in that it also initiated) 'can take values up to 0.60, indicating that some fish initiate more than half of the avalanches in which they participate', significantly above a shuffled null at 99% confidence, while 'some fish never initiate an avalanche' at large thresholds. Complementary modelling (PMC4632542) shows heterogeneous acceptance thresholds are functional, not just variance: with N=100 agents, default quorum 0.5N (tested 0.25N-0.5N) and conversion rate 0.1/min, varying the fraction of high-threshold agents (H from 0 to ~0.4) improved speed AND accuracy together in some regimes — no trade-off — whereas 'speed-accuracy trade-offs when we vary the quorum threshold are robust against parameter variation'.
**Proposed game rule.** Make the spec's 'per-agent-varying threshold' a PERSISTENT trait rolled once at spawn and stored on the agent, spanning a wide range (a hair-trigger tail near 0.25 of nominal and a stolid tail near 0.5+), not a per-tick random offset. Mix threshold types deliberately within a garrison rather than sampling one distribution — the mix itself is the tuning knob, and the modelling says a mixed population can beat a uniform one on speed and accuracy simultaneously, which sweeping the threshold alone cannot.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a heavy-tailed histogram of cascade sizes from a real run — most calls fizzle at 1-3 agents, rare ones take a wing — which is directly checkable against the ~power-law shape and is the honest signature that quorum is working rather than everything-or-nothing firing. PREDICTS an emergent player skill with no code to support it: identifying and killing the specific hair-trigger agents in a garrison measurably lowers cascade frequency, while killing stolid ones does not. FORBIDS uniform thresholds (every chamber commits at once, all-or-nothing, no tail) and forbids per-tick threshold noise (no stable individual identity, so the assassination tactic never becomes learnable).
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says 'per-agent-varying' but does not say persistent, does not say wide, and does not say mixed — three choices that decide whether the behaviour reads as a swarm with characters in it or as uniform noise, and only the persistent-wide-mixed version yields the heavy-tailed cascade distribution and the targetable-instigator tactic.
**Source.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05473 (full text via arXiv PDF, read locally with pdftotext) ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4632542/

## LANE I — Repulsion, spacing, and negative gradients (the kin field)

### I1. Gate marks, not chamber marks — the No-Entry stamp lives on the portal, is twice as strong as the attract mark, and decays at the same rate
**What the source says.** Pharaoh's-ant foragers deploy a repellent trail mark as a 'no entry' signal on unrewarding routes (abstract, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16306981/ — full Nature text paywalled, not quoted). The free follow-up measured it: the mark is laid most strongly JUST AFTER a bifurcation on the unrewarding branch (test squares were cut 3 mm past the fork, 10 x 10 mm), it is localized near the bifurcation rather than along the route, its initial effect on branch choice is -49% (ratio 1.98) versus +25% (ratio 1.50) for the short-term attractive mark, the two decay GRADIENTS are statistically indistinguishable (t=0.17, df=9, p=0.87), and because the repellent starts ~2x higher it stays effective more than twice as long: no-effect reached at 78 min vs 33 min. Background traffic ranged 12-117 agents/min; natural trails are ~5 mm wide, apparatus 10 mm to eliminate congestion effects. A control showed the effect is not an artefact of general territorial marking. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/46214/1/RobinsonPheromoneDecayInsSoc.pdf
**Numbers.** -49% vs +25% initial branch-choice effect; no-effect at 78 min vs 33 min; equal decay gradients (p=0.87); mark localized within ~3 mm of the fork; 12-117 agents/min background flow
**Proposed game rule.** Add ONE edge-valued field to the substrate: kin_noentry[portal], written by an agent as it commits to an exit, decaying with the same time constant as the attracting mark but initialised at 2x its magnitude. An agent choosing an exit scores each portal as attract(next_chamber) - 2 * kin_noentry(portal). Spotters descending to plant garrisons, and patrollers spreading out, use the same read. Concretely: on commit, write 1.0 to the portal you leave through and 2.0 to every OTHER portal of the chamber you just left (the 'I have taken this fork, the others are now the unrewarding branch for me' inversion is not needed — mark what you rejected is the ant's actual pattern: the mark sits on the branch that did not pay).
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts: two spotters entering the same chamber within one decay window leave through different portals >~2/3 of the time, and garrison plant sites separate by >=2 hops without any global knowledge. Verifiable in a real run by logging portal choices per chamber. FORBIDS implementing kin repulsion as a per-chamber scalar: with a chamber scalar the agent standing in the marked chamber is repelled by its own mark and thrashes at the doorway; the ant data says the signal is only ever read at the decision point, never while standing on it.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25's whole signal table is chamber-valued scalars (one value per polygon). Every one of the eight fields is a node value; none is an edge value. ### I2. Crowd sensing by contact count that turns DOWN your own recruitment emission — no repellent field required

### I3. Priority budget, not weighted sum — keep-apart outranks bunch-together by allocation, and influence falls off as 1/d^2
**What the source says.** Reynolds' original paper states the behaviours 'in order of decreasing precedence: 1. Collision Avoidance: avoid collisions with nearby flockmates 2. Velocity Matching 3. Flock Centering.' On combining them: weighted averaging is 'a precarious interrelationship that is difficult to adjust'; if two requests 'lie in approximately opposite directions, they will largely cancel out under a simple weighted averaging scheme' — the agent makes a tiny turn and hits the wall; and averaging 'fly north' with 'fly east' into 'fly northeast' is explicitly called a bad idea. The fix is 'prioritized acceleration allocation... based on a strict priority ordering': requests are accumulated in priority order until the accumulated magnitude exceeds the agent's maximum acceleration, and 'the last acceleration request is trimmed back'; lower-priority urges 'might be temporarily unsatisfied.' Falloff: an early version weighted attraction/repulsion linearly with distance and produced 'a bouncy flock action'; switching to inverse square gave 'a more natural, better damped flock model', matching Partridge's schooling measurements that influence is 'inversely proportional to the square or the cube of the distance.' Neighbourhood = radius plus an exponent, and Reynolds notes it should be forward-weighted in proportion to speed. Crowded starts produce a 'flash expansion.' https://www.red3d.com/cwr/papers/1987/SIGGRAPH87.pdf
**Numbers.** 3 behaviours, strict precedence; influence ~1/d^2 (fish data: d^-2 to d^-3); neighbourhood = radius + exponent, forward-weighted by speed
**Proposed game rule.** Implement flocking as a per-tick acceleration BUDGET equal to the agent's max accel: fill it in the order keep-apart -> match-velocity -> close-up -> field-gradient -> objective, trimming the request that overruns and dropping the rest. Weight neighbour influence by 1/d^2, with a forward-biased neighbourhood whose radius scales with current speed.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts: no more mutual cancellation stalls at doorways (the classic 'two agents jitter in a portal' bug), and no spring-like bouncing between clumped agents. In a real run, log per-tick how much of the accel budget keep-apart consumed: in a surge through a breach it should saturate at 100% and close-up should get zero. FORBIDS the spec's literal wording — 'keep-apart weighted above bunch-together' is a weighted sum, which is the exact scheme the primary source says fails in critical situations.
**Beyond the current spec.** Spec line 205 says keep-apart is 'weighted above' bunch-together. This replaces a weight with a strict allocation order plus a trim rule, and supplies the falloff exponent (inverse square, not linear) that the spec does not specify at all.
**Source.** https://www.red3d.com/cwr/papers/1987/SIGGRAPH87.pdf

### I4. Topological neighbourhood: react to your nearest ~6-7 friendlies regardless of distance, and keep the movement axis clear
**What the source says.** 3D reconstruction of flocks of up to 2600 individuals showed interaction range is topological, not metric: anisotropy of neighbour placement decays to the isotropic value at n_c = 6.5 +/- 0.9 SE, and this is constant across flocks whose sparseness differs by more than 2x (nearest-neighbour distance r1 from 0.68 m in the densest flock to 1.51 m in the sparsest). r_c correlates with r1 (R^2 = 0.78, p = 0.00072) while n_c^-1/3 does not (R^2 = 0.00021, p = 0.97). 'The interaction between two birds 1 m apart in flock A is as strong as that between two birds 5 m apart in flock B.' Within one fixed metric range there were 10 individuals in the densest flock and 1 in the sparsest. Nearest-neighbour placement is strongly anisotropic with 'a striking lack of nearest neighbours along the direction of motion.' Topological interaction 'is indispensable to maintain flock's cohesion against the large density changes caused by external perturbations, typically predation.' https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.1916
**Numbers.** n_c = 6.5 +/- 0.9 SE; flocks up to 2600; r1 = 0.68-1.51 m; same-strength interaction at 1 m vs 5 m; 10 vs 1 neighbours inside one fixed radius
**Proposed game rule.** Both keep-apart and the kin read use the k nearest friendlies with k = 6 (chosen by hop distance then euclidean within chamber), never a fixed radius. And make the exclusion zone anisotropic: the forbidden region is along the agent's own velocity axis, so agents stack side-by-side and leave the lane ahead empty. At a breach mouth this means arrivals fan sideways instead of queueing into the aperture.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts: spacing behaviour is identical during a 3-agent trickle and a 30-agent pour — no retuning of radii per encounter size. Predicts breach mouths do not log-jam, because the agent standing in the mouth is repelled by the one behind it along the motion axis. Verifiable: measure mean nearest-friendly distance during trickle vs pour; a metric rule makes it collapse under load, a topological rule holds it. FORBIDS a single tuned separation radius, which the data says must be wrong at either high or low density.
**Beyond the current spec.** Spec line 205 says 'gap scales to agent size' — a metric rule. The measurement says gap should scale to LOCAL DENSITY via a fixed neighbour count instead. Directly serves spec line 39 ('enemies must physically enter the level through the breach — no log-jamming in the opening... ### I5. Depot dispatch and the too-many-friends exit both run off one leaky contact integrator (tau ~ 25 s), and headcount = rate x trip time
**What the source says.** A closed-loop model of colony outflow, fitted to field data: an idle agent inside the depot integrates brief contacts with returning agents as ds/dt = -s/tau + k*lambda_in with k = 0.3 stimulus units per contact and tau = 0.41 min (~24.6 s); when s crosses a bifurcation threshold the agent activates. Observed steady-state return rates ~0.25 agents/s (one colony) and ~0.65 agents/s (another) on the same day, computed with a 300 s sliding window. Round-trip delay modelled as chi-square with mean D = 2-15 min. The number of agents outside is E[Q] = r* x D (steady-state dispatch rate times mean trip time). A critical volatility c* = 0.5287 separates trivial from non-trivial steady states. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6294393/
**Numbers.** k = 0.3 per contact; tau = 0.41 min (~24.6 s); rates 0.25 and 0.65 agents/s; window 300 s; trip time D = 2-15 min; E[Q] = r* x D
**Proposed game rule.** Give the marrow ONE state variable per gate: s decays with tau = 25 s and jumps +0.3 per returning/arriving signal (a spotter transmission, an agent crossing back). Dispatch fires when s crosses a per-agent-varying threshold. Never count live agents: the number in the field is dispatch rate x mean survival/trip time, so bound the pour by setting the rate, not by a census. Use the SAME integrator inverted on the spotter: contacts with friendlies charge a 'leave' integrator with the same 25 s constant, so a spotter that meets ~3+ friendlies inside half a minute abandons the chamber and pushes further behind the line.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts a specific, checkable dynamic: after the player kills the agents in a chamber, arrivals stop, s decays to threshold in ~25 s, and the pour tapers rather than stopping instantly — a visible 'wave' shape in the arrival log. Predicts steady-state field population tracks rate x trip time, so lengthening the walk from breach to player raises the standing enemy count with no change to the reserve rules. FORBIDS a global live-agent counter in the depot (the spec forbids coupling anyway); the depot never needs to know how many are out.
**Beyond the current spec.** Spec has 'finite reserve that regenerates slowly' and a per-agent-varying quorum threshold, but no dispatch-rate control law and no relation between reserve spend and standing enemy count. This supplies both, with fitted constants, and doubles as the contact-rate rule Lane I asked for.

### I6. A field can only be navigated if it fades slower than it varies across space — and gradient-following nets at most 0.63 of move speed
**What the source says.** An open-access 2025 analysis of gradient-following in time-varying fields: non-dimensional drift saturates at v_d_inf = 0.63 V (V = swim speed), i.e. even under ideal conditions net progress up a gradient is at most ~63% of raw speed. Direction sensing is inhibited when the field's temporal change dominates its spatial change, |dc/dt| >> ||grad c||, with an identified critical sensing threshold kappa_c = 0.3014; the memory window used is 4 tau with tau ~ 1 s, matching measured response windows. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12423644/ . Supporting mechanics: agents too small to compare two points in space instead compare concentrations over time along their path; run ~1 s, tumble ~0.1 s, mean reorientation ~70 degrees; repellents increase tumble rate, attractants extend runs (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run-and-tumble_motion, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotaxis).
**Numbers.** drift saturates at 0.63 x speed; failure when |dc/dt| >> ||grad c||; kappa_c = 0.3014; memory 4 tau, tau ~ 1 s; run 1 s, tumble 0.1 s, 70 deg mean turn
**Proposed game rule.** Adopt a substrate law: any field an agent NAVIGATES by must satisfy fade-per-second << typical contrast-per-hop. Compute both at runtime and assert. Under that law, Muster ('fades VERY FAST', very exponential per-hop dropoff) is a rally/aggregation trigger only and must never be used as a steering gradient; Distress (slow spread, slow fade) and Danger (very slow fade) are the only legitimate climbing/descending fields, and the kin field must be given a fade slow enough to steer on. Second, budget spotter travel at 0.63x its move speed when it is descending the kin field or climbing danger — infiltration timings and the 45 s breach timer should be set against that, not against straight-line speed.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts: if you try to steer garrison placement off a fast-fading field, agents will random-walk (measurable as near-zero net displacement per second along the gradient despite full-speed movement) — the exact 'reinforcements never reached the player' symptom already recorded in the spec's own history note. Predicts a hard ceiling: a spotter told to descend kin and climb danger cannot cross N hops faster than N/0.63 hop-times. FORBIDS adding any new fast-fading field with a navigation role.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec assigns spread/fade rates per field but never states which fields are steerable. It currently instructs agents to climb Distress, refuse high Danger, and descend kin — three simultaneous gradients — without a rule saying a fast-fading field is unusable as a gradient. This gives the criterion and the speed penalty.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12423644/

### I7. Repulsion-based spreading has a density ceiling: above ~2% area occupancy it is worse than random walking
**What the source says.** Physical swarm study in a 3 x 2 m arena with repellent virtual marks only (projected light), N = 64 and N = 324 real agents plus simulation to N = 400. Decay was exponential with r = 0.005 (~140 s to threshold; variants r = 0.010 -> 69.3 s and r = 0.0025 -> 277.2 s) and a binary detection threshold at 0.5. At N = 64 the repellent rule improved area coverage 36% vs 30% and collective exploration 90% vs 72% against a plain random walk. At N = 324 (54 agents/m^2, ~4.6% physical occupancy) 'there is no ultimate advantage to either area coverage or collective exploration for using stigmergy', and beyond roughly 2% physical occupancy the marked-avoidance rule became inferior to random walk. The avoidance action itself is minimal: on detecting a mark, turn left or right at random for 0.5 s then move forward 0.5, 1 or 1.5 s with equal probability. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6894587/
**Numbers.** N = 64: coverage 36% vs 30%, exploration 90% vs 72%; N = 324 = 54 agents/m^2 = ~4.6% occupancy: no advantage; ceiling ~2% occupancy; decay ~140 s (69.3 s / 277.2 s variants); avoid action = 0.5 s random turn + 0.5/1/1.5 s forward
**Proposed game rule.** Gate the kin/no-entry rule on local occupancy: compute agents-per-chamber-area, and when the chamber's occupied area fraction exceeds ~2%, suppress the avoidance rule and fall back to plain wander plus keep-apart. Practically: spotters and patrollers use kin repulsion (they are sparse); a surge pouring out of a breach does not. Also adopt the avoidance ACTION verbatim — random turn for a fixed short interval then a randomized forward leg — instead of computing a repulsive vector; it is the cheapest correct implementation and it is the one that was actually measured to work.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts a peak-then-flatten curve: garrison spread quality (mean pairwise hop distance between plants) improves with the kin rule at low spotter counts and stops improving above the density threshold. This is a free experiment — sweep agent count in a purpose-built vector map and plot it. FORBIDS the assumption that more agents plus repulsion equals better coverage; above the ceiling the mark field is saturated everywhere and carries no information.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec proposes the kin field unconditionally. This says the mechanism has a validity range and gives the number, plus a decay constant (~140 s) in the same order as the spec's 45 s breach timer, and an implementation of avoidance that avoids gradient-vector math entirely.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6894587/

### I8. Even coverage from local information: sit at the centre of the ground that is closer to you than to any other agent — but your perception must reach twice your cell radius
**What the source says.** For a group of mobile agents covering a region, the optimal partition for fixed positions is the Voronoi partition; the locally optimal configuration is the centroidal one, where each agent sits at the centroid of its own cell. The control law is u_i = -k_prop (p_i - C_Vi) — move toward your own cell's centroid — and both continuous and discrete-time versions provably converge to the set of centroidal configurations. Crucially the computation is local: an agent needs only its Voronoi neighbours, and the paper gives the exact locality condition — 'Provided R_i is twice as large as the maximum distance between p_i and the vertexes of W(p_i, R_i), all Voronoi neighbors of p_i are within distance R_i', so R_i,min = 2 max ||p_i - q||. The scheme is asynchronous, with recomputation triggered when a neighbour becomes active or a new active agent becomes a neighbour. https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0212212
**Numbers.** control law u_i = -k_prop (p_i - C_Vi); R_i,min = 2 x max distance to own cell vertices; converges to centroidal configuration in both continuous and discrete time; asynchronous with neighbour-triggered recomputation
**Proposed game rule.** Garrison placement rule with no new field: a settling agent claims the set of chambers to which it is the hop-nearest garrison, and walks toward the 'centroid' of that set (the chamber minimising summed hop distance, weighted by chamber area or by Distress). Because agents sample only their own chamber and one hop, the locality condition bites: target garrison spacing must be at most ~2 hops, or make the claim explicit by having each garrison stamp its own chambers with a Garrison-reach value carrying the owner id and hop count, so an agent 1 hop out can still tell whose ground it stands on.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts garrisons converge to a near-even tiling of the map with dead ground minimised, from purely local moves, and that adding a garrison re-shuffles only its immediate neighbours (asynchronous, local recomputation) rather than triggering a global reshuffle. FORBIDS asking for garrison spacing greater than twice the perception range: with 1-hop sampling and a 4-hop spacing target the partition each agent computes is simply wrong, and the observable failure is garrisons drifting and never settling.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says garrisons are emergent and should 'spread out' but gives no settle criterion and no stopping rule — an agent descending a kin gradient forever never plants. This supplies a fixed point (you are at your cell's centroid), a convergence guarantee, and the perception-vs-spacing inequality that the spec's 1-hop sampling rule collides with.
**Source.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0212212

### I9. Boundary doubling and a switching (not linear) avoidance response — dead ground between garrisons is a feature worth 25-40% of the map
**What the source says.** A spatially explicit model of pack territories, matched to field data: neighbouring groups leave ~2 km wide buffer zones ('no-mans-lands') that 'may comprise as much as 25-40% of the available area'; territories run 125-310 km^2; only a few members of a 5-15 member group mark at all; a traveller encounters a mark roughly every 2-3 minutes; and marking frequency 'approximately doubles near territorial borders, giving rise to bowl-shaped distributions... with the raised edges of the olfactory bowl located at the territorial boundaries.' The analytic result: the qualitative form of the territories depends on the response function, and 'realistic territories in two spatial dimensions require switching of the movement AND scent marking behavior in response to foreign scent marks' — a convex/linear response (Holling type II analogue) is not enough; you need a sigmoidal switching response (type III analogue) in both channels. https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~mlewis/publications/22Lewis%201997%20JMB.pdf
**Numbers.** buffer ~2 km wide = 25-40% of area; territories 125-310 km^2; marking ~doubles at borders; one mark encountered every 2-3 min; only a few of 5-15 members mark; switching (sigmoid) response required in BOTH movement and marking
**Proposed game rule.** Two rules. (1) Response shape: an agent's turn-away response to the kin mark is a threshold sigmoid, not proportional — below the threshold it ignores kin entirely (so garrisons can be built shoulder to shoulder when needed), above it, it turns hard. (2) Reactive marking: an agent that detects a foreign-garrison mark DOUBLES its own kin emission on the spot. That single coupling is what makes boundaries sharp and stable rather than smeared. Accept and design for the resulting dead ground — target ~25-40% of chambers unclaimed between garrisons, and put the good salvage there.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts hard, stable garrison borders with a quiet corridor between them that the player can learn to route through — and that garrison edge chambers carry roughly twice the kin value of interior chambers (a 'bowl' profile), directly checkable by dumping the field. FORBIDS a linear/proportional repulsion response: the source's analysis says linear response gives the wrong qualitative pattern in 2D (blurred, non-territorial), which would show up as garrisons that never crisply separate.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's Garrison interior/reach fields already encode inside-vs-edge, but nothing says the EDGE emits harder, and nothing specifies the response curve. This supplies both, plus a design budget for how much of the map should be unclaimed, and it gives the cordon/containment mechanic a natural target: hold the buffer, not the interior.
**Source.** https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~mlewis/publications/22Lewis%201997%20JMB.pdf

### I10. When two agents want the same ground, one flips the other to ITS target rather than standing both down
**What the source says.** A model comparing four inhibitory interactions among foragers in a switching environment — indiscriminate stop-signalling, discriminate stop-signalling, self-inhibition, and 'direct switching' (the inhibiting agent 'switches opposing nestmate to their preference'). Direct switching wins: it 'allows for continually foraging, rather than other strategies' interruption by an uncommitted stage, which must rely on recruitment to restart', and it 'attains the highest levels of consensus and adaptivity'. Counter-intuitive result: in the single-target case with a changing environment the optimal recruitment strength is beta = 0 — 'recruitment does not benefit the swarm... Reinforcing the majority opinion via recruitment is detrimental once the environment changes.' Rapidly changing or low-quality environments need STRONGER inhibition; rich, slow environments need weaker. Only indiscriminate stop-signalling shows a consensus/adaptivity trade-off; the other three do not. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03061 . The underlying report that an inhibitory signal exists and is triggered by peril at a site is cited there as Nieh 2010 Curr Biol and Seeley et al. 2012 Science; the lab page confirms the signal 'inhibits the waggle dance' and 'reduces recruitment to a specific food location', with higher-frequency signals more effective (https://labs.biology.ucsd.edu/nieh/neg_feedback_communication.html).
**Numbers.** 4 inhibition variants compared; direct switching best on both consensus and adaptivity; optimal recruitment beta = 0 in single-target switching environments; stronger inhibition required at short switch period T and low target quality
**Proposed game rule.** Spotter conflict resolution: when a spotter meets another spotter already tasked to a chamber it also wants, it does not stand down and it does not idle — it overwrites the other's target with its own current target and both keep working, one moving on to the next-best chamber it perceives. Second rule: scale inhibition strength to volatility — while the player is moving fast and the front is shifting, raise inhibition (agents re-task each other aggressively); when the map is static, lower it so garrisons consolidate. Third: when the field is churning, cut recruitment weight toward zero and let private perception drive commitment, so the force does not pile onto ground the player has already left.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts: no dead time between a spotter abandoning a target and acquiring a new one (no 'uncommitted' idle state), measurable as time-in-idle per spotter approaching zero. Predicts that against a fast-moving player, a high-recruitment configuration will visibly mass on stale ground — the exact 'enemies pile up where the player was' failure — and that turning recruitment down fixes it. FORBIDS resolving spotter contention by stand-down, which needs a fresh recruitment event to restart and therefore costs a full reaction cycle.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's selection rule (line 50) is 'if I am not the weakest class in the room, keep fighting; else summon' — a stand-down rule with no handoff when the tasked agent's target is invalidated, and no volatility coupling. This replaces it with a transfer-of-task, and it warns that the spec's relay-recruit positive loop is actively harmful against a mobile player unless damped.
**Source.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.03061

### I11. Failure-gated dispersal marks: the agent that got stuck is the one that tells others to go elsewhere
**What the source says.** An open-access study of a swimming micro-agent shows a repellent signal whose PRODUCTION is gated on the producer's own stress/growth-arrest state (the producing enzyme and the matching detector are co-transcribed under a stress-response regulator, expressed during growth arrest rather than at high density), and whose DETECTION triggers a documented 'run-away response' in others of the same population. Response range 0.01-1 mM; producers release millimolar amounts. The authors frame it as a combined 'fight and flight strategy': the same substance suppresses competitors while dispersing the producer's own population to 'move away and explore more favourable niches'. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10390336/
**Numbers.** detector and producer co-regulated under the stress regulator, expressed during growth arrest not at high density; repellent response over 0.01-1 mM; producers secrete millimolar amounts
**Proposed game rule.** Gate kin-mark emission on FAILURE, not on presence. A spotter emits the kin/no-entry mark only when it fails: it could not find a safe plant site, it got interrupted mid-transmission, or it took damage in that chamber. A garrison agent emits it when its chamber is already saturated. Everyone else emits nothing. Result: the field carries information ('this ground did not pay') instead of merely marking occupancy, and it stays sparse enough to remain readable — which is exactly what the density-ceiling result demands.
**Predicts / forbids.** Predicts the kin field concentrates on chambers where spotters actually failed (player-held, turret-covered, recently fought over), so later spotters route around the player's cordon without any explicit knowledge of turrets — and a player who kills a spotter cleanly and quickly leaves LESS mark than one who wounds and chases it. FORBIDS every-agent-always-emits, which saturates the field (see the density-ceiling candidate) and destroys its information content.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's kin field (line 62) is presence-based: friendlies emit, callers climb away. Presence-based marking saturates and tells you nothing about whether the ground is worth taking. Failure-gated emission makes the same field double as the 'player-scented / do not enter' signal the spec needs for infiltration steering (line 53), with one channel instead of two.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10390336/

### J1. Threshold reinforcement: the actual update rule for adaptive counter-biasing, with a numeric window where a mix emerges at all
**What the source says.** Theraulaz, Bonabeau & Deneubourg 1998 give a complete decentralized specialization model with no central controller. Each agent i holds a response threshold θ_ij against each task-j stimulus, and engages with probability T_ij(s_j) = s_j² / (s_j² + θ_ij²) — a Hill function of exponent 2 that is near 0 when s << θ, near 1 when s >> θ, and exactly 0.5 at s = θ. The threshold updates by a LEARNING coefficient ξ and a FORGETTING coefficient φ: 'θ_ij → θ_ij − ξΔt if i performs task j within Δt, and θ_ij → θ_ij + φΔt if i does not perform the task within Δt', combined as θ_ij → θ_ij − x_ij ξΔt + (1 − x_ij) φΔt where x_ij is the fraction of time spent on the task; θ is clamped to [θ_min, θ_max]. Stimulus grows at a fixed rate and is consumed by work: ∂_t s_j = δ − (α/N) Σ_i x_ij. An active agent quits with probability p per unit time, so mean time on a task before giving up is 1/p. The sweep is the key result: holding ξ + φ = 11 and varying the forgetting rate φ, 'When φ < 0.4, all individuals are specialists because the forgetting rate is small'; 'For 0.4 ≤ φ < 2, the number of specialists for each task decreases... differentiation is observed in this region'; 'For φ > 2, forgetting is quick and no specialization is observed.' Figure 1/2 defaults: N = 100, m = 2, ξ = 10, φ = 1, p = 0.2, θ init 500, θ range [1, 1000], noise σ = 0.1. They also predict a removal experiment: pull the specialists out, non-specialists lower their thresholds and take over, and whether they STAY specialists after the originals return depends on how long the removal lasted.
**Proposed game rule.** Make the marrow's dispatch mix a threshold-reinforcement table, not a hand-tuned weighting. Rows = defense agent classes (mite, husk, spitter, brute, warden, shrieker, flicker). Columns = a small fixed set of intruder-tactic signatures (turret-camping, close-quarters brawling, long-range poking, kiting through portals, structure-planting, hole-shelling). Each cell holds θ. When the marrow dispatches class c against signature s and that wave produces damage on the intruder, θ[c][s] -= ξ·Δt; every tick a class is NOT dispatched against s, θ[c][s] += φ·Δt, clamped to [θ_min, θ_max]. Selection at pour time: sample each class with probability θ-driven T(s) = s²/(s²+θ²) where s is the accumulated evidence for that signature. Ship ξ = 10, φ = 1 (φ/ξ = 10%), sitting squarely inside the published differentiation band of roughly 4%–22%. Use the same Hill-2 form for the quorum wake threshold in the Appendix table — it is the same math, and per-agent θ variation gives the 'per-agent-varying threshold' the spec already asks for for free.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS, and it is measurable in a farm run: log the dispatched class histogram per wave. Against a bot that only camps turrets, the anti-structure classes' share of the mix should climb monotonically over roughly 4–6 waves and then plateau (θ hits θ_min); against a bot that alternates tactics every wave the histogram should stay near-flat. PREDICTS the removal result too: kill every spitter for two minutes and brutes should start being dispatched into the anti-structure role — the collective backfills without any code path for backfilling. FORBIDS instant adaptation (θ_max−θ_min divided by ξ sets a hard floor on adaptation time) and FORBIDS permanent lock-in (θ is clamped, and φ pushes back up whenever the tactic stops). It also forbids the two failure regimes explicitly: if you tune φ/ξ below ~4% the mix over-specializes and never recovers — every run ends up looking identical; above ~22% nothing ever specializes and counter-biasing is invisible. Those are two falsifiable, runnable settings.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 lists 'Adaptive counter-biasing — learn how this intruder fights, bias later reinforcement mixes toward counters' as one line with no mechanism, no state, no rates, and no tuning guidance. This supplies the state (one float per class×signature), the update rule, the sampling rule, the clamp, published default coefficients, and — the part you cannot guess — the narrow ratio window in which a differentiated mix emerges at all, plus the two named failure regimes on either side of it. It also unifies with the quorum threshold the spec already wants, so this is one mechanism serving two ratified behaviors instead of a parallel pathway.
**Source.** https://static.ias.edu/pitp/archive/2012files/18.pdf

### J2. Dose flips the sign: heavy repeated pressure DAMPS the response, light repeated pressure AMPLIFIES it
**What the source says.** Ifrim et al. 2014 ran a systematic reprogramming protocol on human responder cells: 24 h exposure to a single ligand, washout, 5 days rest as plain medium, then a second 24 h stimulation, measuring output signal levels. The headline is that the SAME ligand drives opposite long-term programs depending only on concentration. 'Monocytes were functionally programmed for either enhanced (training) or decreased (tolerance) cytokine production, depending on the type and concentration of ligand they encountered.' At high concentration the surface-receptor ligands (100 µg/ml, 100 ng/ml, and 10 µg/ml for the three tested) 'induced a' damped later response. Then: 'Systematically lowering the amount of [the TLR2 ligand]... strongly modified the monocyte reprogramming characteristics' and at 1 µg/ml it 'resulted in a tolerance status... upon [heterologous] secondary stimulation' while, upon re-stimulation with the SAME ligand, the pre-activated cells 'were programmed to produce an increased amount of IL-6 compared to that produced by nonactivated (RPMI) control cells.' And: 'decreasing the amount of [the other two ligands] during the preactivation period of monocytes led to the opposite effect of tolerance, which is a proinflammatory status represented by significantly elevated levels... upon restimulation.' Titration ranges spanned ~10 orders of magnitude (e.g. 100 µg/ml down to 0.01 pg/ml). Crucially the low doses were sub-threshold at the time: 'None of the first incubations with low doses of ligands induced measurable amounts of proinflammatory cytokines... after the first 24 h' — the biasing happened with no visible immediate reaction. Which receptor family was engaged also determined the sign 'regardless of the adaptor molecules that were used to signal', and lowering the dose did NOT reverse the program for three of the cytosolic ligands.
**Proposed game rule.** Give every tactic signature a per-chamber exposure INTEGRAL, not a counter. On each pour, read the integral accumulated since the last pour: below a low band, the marrow trains up (θ decreases for the counter class, per candidate 1); above a high band, the marrow DAMPS — it stops dispatching into that chamber at all and re-routes the mix elsewhere. Concretely: a player who parks in one chamber and dumps a huge volume of one tactic pushes the integral into the high band, and the reinforcement stream stops feeding that chamber and starts building garrisons behind him instead. A player who uses the same tactic lightly and repeatedly, below the visible-response floor, gets an escalating tailored counter. Note the sub-threshold detail: the low-dose exposures produced NO immediate reaction but still set the later program — so the biasing must accumulate even from disturbance too small to trigger a wake, which means the player gets no immediate feedback that he is teaching the enemy.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the anti-farm behavior the design wants without a special-cased anti-farm rule: a bot that grinds one chamber with one tactic will, after crossing the high band, see arrivals into that chamber fall to zero while total dispatch stays constant (measurable — count pours by chamber, not just totals). PREDICTS that quiet, low-intensity repetition is the DANGEROUS play, not the safe one. FORBIDS the naive monotonic design 'more use of a tactic ⇒ stronger counter' — the response curve is non-monotonic in exposure, with the peak counter arriving at moderate exposure. FORBIDS reading the effect off a single scalar 'aggro' number: the sign depends on which signature accumulated, and the source explicitly shows sign is set by which channel was engaged, not by total magnitude.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's anti-turtle answer is a single extreme Distress threshold that opens a spotterless flank breach — one blunt punishment with an admittedly 'extreme' trigger. This adds a graded, always-on mechanism operating far below that threshold, and it is the opposite sign from what anyone would guess: heavy pressure buys you local quiet at the cost of the enemy re-routing, while light persistent pressure buys you a tailored counter. It also gives 'counterplay reward' its missing dimension — the counter's strength is a function of exposure INTENSITY as well as repetition, so there are two knobs the player can manipulate, which is exactly the 'advanced player intuits how the system communicates with itself and manipulates it' clause in the Adversary constitution.
**Source.** https://journals.asm.org/doi/pdf/10.1128/cvi.00688-13

### J3. Drawdown and residual: the counter fades ~90–95% but never returns to zero, so second exposure starts elevated
**What the source says.** Two fetched reviews give the full shape of the response curve. On magnitude and drawdown: 'naïve precursors increase in prevalence by more than 10,000 times' during a first response, the traditional model involves 'an extensive contraction in cell numbers (~90–95%), leaving a residual stable memory T cell pool', and that residual pool is not the original baseline — 'memory pools increase the numbers of antigen-specific T cells by up to 200–1000 times' relative to the starting precursor count. Persistence of the residual is long: estimated half-life 'between 8 and 15 years' for one class and 'between 8 and 12 years' for another, with detectable persistence 'for up to 75 years' in some individuals. The composition of the residual also shifts with age: 'around 50% of the... memory populations express CD62Lhi at d60 after infection, compared with 90% being CD62Lhi by d500' — i.e. the surviving pool drifts toward the long-range/recall-capable subtype over time. On the speed side, kinetics are sharply asymmetric: first commitment is slow — cells enter 'their first S-phase within 30 h' — but once committed the population doubles fast, 'The doubling rate averages at 6–8 h with some studies demonstrating a peak doubling time of 4.5 h, or even 2 h', and 'dividing as quickly as once every 4 h' at peak.
**Proposed game rule.** Model every counter-bias entry as peak + residual, not as a single decaying float. When a fight in a signature ends, decay the bias by 90–95% over a short window (call it 60–90 s of quiet), and PERMANENTLY floor it at 5–10% of its peak for the rest of the run. Second use of the same tactic therefore starts from an elevated floor and climbs faster, which is the 'faster and larger second response' the lane is chasing. Mirror the kinetic asymmetry in dispatch: a long fixed lag from first evidence to first biased pour (the marrow's 'commitment' delay — slow, and a real window for the player to break contact), then a geometric ramp once committed (each subsequent wave in that signature scales by a fixed factor per interval until it hits the reserve cap). Also mirror the composition drift: the residual should be enriched in the LONG-RANGE classes over run time, so late-run re-engagements are answered with a different silhouette mix than early-run ones, even at the same bias level.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a specific, plottable signature in a farm run: bias-vs-time per signature is a sawtooth with a rising floor, and time-to-first-counter-wave on the Nth engagement with the same tactic shrinks with N while time-to-first-counter on a fresh tactic stays at the full commitment lag. PREDICTS that breaking contact during the commitment lag cancels the whole escalation — a real, learnable tactical out. FORBIDS a pure exponential decay to zero (which would make repetition costless after any pause) and FORBIDS instant ramp (the first wave against a new tactic must be slow and generic). Falsifier: if the measured time-to-counter is flat across repetitions, the residual floor is not being applied.
**Beyond the current spec.** 'Counterplay reward — repeating one tactic makes the future counter increasingly effective; switching tactics measurably slips it' is one sentence in the appendix with no decay model at all. Wave 1 punted precisely because 'decays' is not a spec. This gives the exact fraction that fades (90–95%), the exact thing that does not (a 5–10% floor that is nonetheless 200–1000x above the naive baseline in the source), the asymmetry between slow commitment and fast ramp with real numbers, and a drift in WHICH classes survive in the residual — which turns a scalar difficulty knob into a visibly changing enemy roster over a run.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3515894/

### J4. The switching law: response decrement is exponential in repetitions, specific to the exact stimulus, fully recovers after a rest, and re-decays FASTER the next series
**What the source says.** Boisseau, Vogel & Dussutour 2016 ran 416 single-celled organisms across a 9-day protocol (n = 200 control, 140 and 70 treatment) crossing a 13 mm bridge to reach food. Baseline: control organisms 'entered the bridge' and crossed 'within about an hour'. First exposure to a repellent (4 mM quinine): they made contact 'only after 2.5 h and it took them 4 h to cross the bridge by way of a very narrow pseudopod'; the second repellent (1 mM caffeine) was worse — 'almost 5 h' to enter. Repeat the SAME stimulus daily for 5 days and the aversion collapses: by the sixth presentation they 'behaved like the C slime moulds offered a plain agar bridge for 6 days in terms of shape and pseudopod size' and 'crossed the bridge twice as fast', and critically 'the decrease in responsiveness is shown as an exponential function of the number of repellent presentations.' Specificity is explicit: organisms 'facing a different repellent from the one they habitually encountered behaved like the C slime moulds facing the repellents for the first time. They showed a strong aversive behaviour' — no transfer between the two channels. And forgetting: 'P. polycephalum learns to ignore a stimulus when the stimulus is repeated and spontaneously recovers when it is withheld for 2 days', with one group not fully recovered after 2 days. Following that paper's citation [17] out to the underlying formal description, Rankin et al. 2009 enumerate ten characteristics, four of which are directly rate laws: (3) POTENTIATION — 'After multiple series of stimulus repetitions and spontaneous recoveries, the response decrement becomes successively more rapid and/or more pronounced'; (4) FREQUENCY — 'More frequent stimulation results in more rapid and/or more pronounced response decrement, and more rapid spontaneous recovery'; (5) INTENSITY — 'Within a stimulus modality, the less intense the stimulus, the more rapid and/or more pronounced the behavioral response decrement'; (8) DISHABITUATION — 'Presentation of a different stimulus results in an increase of the decremented response to the original stimulus'; and (9) the dishabituating stimulus itself wears out with repetition.
**Proposed game rule.** Apply this to the Adversary's reaction to NON-LETHAL disturbance — the bait/lure layer, which is the player's main tool for pulling a garrison off a chamber. Per (chamber, disturbance-kind) pair keep a decrement counter d. The quorum wake threshold for that kind scales as θ·exp(k·d), d increments on each disturbance that produces no casualty, and d decays back over a quiet interval — scale the 2-day recovery to roughly 40–60 s of run time. Enforce Rankin's four rate laws literally: specificity — a decrement on 'rifle fire in chamber 12' does nothing to 'explosion in chamber 12' or 'rifle fire in chamber 9'; potentiation — persist a per-kind series counter so the SECOND time the player runs the same bait routine it dies out in fewer repetitions than the first; frequency — spamming faster both kills the bait faster and restores it faster (fast-learned is fast-forgotten); intensity — a quieter disturbance is ignored sooner than a loud one; dishabituation — introducing a genuinely novel disturbance kind resets the decrement on the OLD kind too, and that reset itself gets weaker each time you use it.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a hard, verifiable ceiling on lure-farming: the same bait works about 5 times, and the count should be visibly smaller on the second series in the same run. PREDICTS an exponential, not linear, falloff — plot arrivals-per-bait against repetition number in a farm run and it should be a clean exponential; a linear falloff means the implementation is wrong. PREDICTS that a player who alternates two bait kinds keeps both alive far longer than a player who alternates none, and that the alternation trick itself degrades. FORBIDS infinite kiting with one lure; FORBIDS a global 'they got bored' scalar, because the decrement is strictly per-chamber and per-kind — a fresh chamber or a fresh weapon is a fresh start. FORBIDS the naive symmetric design where fast repetition and slow repetition decay at the same rate.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has a 'Stand-down' rule (a hunting agent whose local signal stayed faint ~5 s exits combat) but nothing that adapts across repetitions — the enemy is exactly as gullible on the tenth bait as the first, which is precisely the exploit an advanced player will find and grind. This adds the missing repetition axis with a published functional form (exponential in presentation count), a published recovery time to scale, a published specificity constraint that stops it collapsing into a global boredom meter, and — the surprising one nobody would invent — potentiation across series, which means the enemy gets better at not falling for it every time you try, and frequency-dependence, which means the impatient player burns his own tool faster.
**Source.** https://pedagogie.ac-toulouse.fr/svt/sites/default/files/traam_donnees_scientifiques/blob_causse/boisseau_et_al_2016-1.pdf

### J5. Bounded ordered evidence ledger: fixed capacity, newest-first, oldest evicted, and not shared between depots
**What the source says.** In single-celled organisms, past attackers are recorded as fragments captured from attacker genetic material and written into a genomic array. The structure is a bounded ordered stack, not a set: 'There are usually fewer than 50 units of the repeat-spacer sequence in a CRISPR array' and 'New spacers are usually added at the beginning of the CRISPR next to the leader sequence creating a chronological record of viral infections.' Entry size is tightly constrained: repeats '28 to 37 base pairs', entries 'typically 32 to 38 bp (range 21 to 72 bp)'. Write rate under sustained attack is slow and countable, not continuous: 'In one week, S. thermophilus strains acquired up to three spacers when challenged with a single phage.' And the ledger is emphatically LOCAL, not a population-wide broadcast: 'In the oral cavity, a temporal study determined that 7–22% of spacers were shared over 17 months within an individual while less than 2% were shared across individuals.' Acquisition also requires dedicated machinery separable from the response machinery — knocking out the two acquisition proteins 'stopped spacer acquisition, without affecting CRISPR immune response'.
**Proposed game rule.** Give the CORE a fixed-capacity ordered ledger — 8 to 16 slots — of intruder tactic signatures. A courier that reaches the hub pushes its signature onto the FRONT; the tail entry is evicted. Write rate is deliberately slow: at most a few new entries per minute of sustained contact, so the ledger is a coarse record of what the player has been doing lately, in order. The dispatch mix (candidate 1) reads the ledger with front-weighted priority. Two consequences to build deliberately: (a) the separable-machinery result says the WRITE path and the RESPOND path must be different subsystems — killing couriers stops learning but does not stop fighting, which is exactly the design's 'spotter is the vulnerability' logic extended to the learning layer; (b) the <2% cross-lineage sharing says each emergent garrison keeps its OWN small ledger and the hub keeps the master — a garrison that never had a courier reach the hub fights the generic mix. Destroying the core wipes the master ledger, which stacks a real second reward onto the existing core-kill status effect.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS an exploitable, legible rule the player can actually discover: cycling through more than ledger-capacity distinct tactics flushes the record — the enemy literally cannot hold more than N things about you at once. PREDICTS that two different garrisons in the same run respond differently to the same tactic, depending on whether a courier from that area ever got home. FORBIDS unbounded accumulation and forbids a global omniscient enemy model. FORBIDS 'the enemy already knows' the moment a tactic is used — there is a courier round trip and a slow write rate between using a tactic and it being in the record. Verifiable: dump the ledger contents in the farm log per wave and check both the eviction order and that garrison ledgers diverge from the master.
**Beyond the current spec.** 'Evidence relay — messenger agents carry evidence to a coordination chamber where it accumulates (feeds the counter-biasing)' names the actors but not the store. 'Accumulates' with no capacity is the road to an enemy that eventually knows everything, which would kill the counterplay loop. This gives the store a shape (ordered stack), a size, an eviction policy, a write rate, and — the design gift — a bounded-capacity flush exploit that is discoverable through play and turns tactic-cycling from a vague 'switching slips it' into a countable rule. It also gives the core kill a second, non-numeric payoff beyond the couple-of-minutes downtime already specced.

### J6. Couriers and a temporary assessment chamber that is built on demand and torn down
**What the source says.** Evidence does not teleport to the coordination site — carriers physically walk it there and the coordination site is a transient structure with its own lifetime. On the search economics at the site: matching agents are rare, 'the fraction of T cells specific for any antigen is about [1%–10%]', carriers 'scan about 300 randomly migrating T cells per hour' under random mixing, rising roughly threefold to '~900 unique T cells per hour at maximum chemotactic strength' when a short-range attractant gradient is added; searchers move at about '11.0 μm/min' randomly and '12 μm/min' with the gradient — i.e. the ~3x throughput gain comes almost entirely from BIASED ROUTING, not from moving faster; the bias is only detectable when searchers are 'less than 100 μm away from their nearest DC'. On the site itself: the assessment structure 'start[s] to expand four days following immunization', polarizes into two functionally distinct zones 'a week after immunization' — a fast-division zone and a selection zone — and the whole structure is temporary: 'These zones dissolve after 10 days of GC development which ends after about 3 weeks', with the dividing agents inside running cycles 'as short as only five hours'.
**Proposed game rule.** Make evidence relay a physical round trip with a killable carrier and a killable destination. A defense agent that takes damage from a recognizable tactic drops out of combat, becomes a courier, and routes home — down the Danger gradient, up the Garrison-reach field — carrying one signature. On arrival it pushes to the ledger (candidate 5). When the hub receives its first courier for a new signature, it builds a TEMPORARY assessment chamber adjacent to the core: it exists for a bounded lifetime, it is where the mix re-weighting actually happens, and while it stands the counter-bias sharpens; destroy it and the bias freezes at its current level and starts drawing down. Two hard rules from the numbers: the courier must be biased-routed rather than sped up (the 3x came from routing, not speed — so give couriers gradient-following, never a movement buff), and the attractant gradient guiding the courier home should be SHORT range, effective only within about one hop, so couriers are genuinely findable and interceptable mid-route rather than magnetically snapping home.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS three independent player counters to the learning layer, each verifiable in a run: kill the wounded agent that disengages (deny the write), intercept it en route (deny the write later), or push through a live breach and destroy the assessment chamber (freeze the bias). PREDICTS that a player who kills cleanly and leaves no wounded survivors learns nothing is learned about him — which is a coherent reason for the design's 'aggressive perfect play avoids reinforcements entirely' clause to extend to the learning layer. FORBIDS instantaneous or telepathic evidence transfer, and FORBIDS the assessment site being a permanent fixture of the hub — it is built on demand and dissolves, so a player who invades a hub that has never seen him finds no such chamber at all. Falsifier: if killing every disengaging agent for a full run still produces a biased mix, the relay is being bypassed somewhere.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says messenger agents carry evidence to a coordination chamber, full stop — no route, no interception, no structure, no lifetime, and no reason the chamber is anywhere in particular. This makes the coordination chamber an on-demand, temporary, destructible feature of the hub area (which the spec lists as an entirely open design unit), gives couriers a concrete routing rule reusing the existing fields rather than a new pathway, and — the counter-intuitive constraint — says the throughput gain must come from routing bias and not from making couriers fast, which keeps them catchable and preserves the 'the spotter is the vulnerability' shape the design already committed to.
**Source.** https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1002763

### J7. Negative marks: twice the amplitude of positive marks, the SAME decay rate, placed at doorways and not along the path
**What the source says.** Robinson et al. 2008 measured decay of the behavioural response to two short-lived trail markers at a bifurcation in Pharaoh's ants. 'Our results show that the repellent pheromone effect lasts more than twice as long as the attractive pheromone effect (78 min versus 33 min).' The mechanism behind the longer life is the surprise: 'The gradients of the decay rates of the responses to the short-term attractive and the repellent pheromones are highly similar (t-test t=0.17, df=9, p=0.87), so the longer lasting effect of the repellent pheromone is not due to a slower decay, but due to its significantly higher initial effect (t-test t=2.87, df=9, p<0.05).' Amplitudes: 'The attractive pheromone had an initial ratio of 1.50, equivalent to a response +25%... In contrast, the repellent pheromone had an initial ratio of 1.98 which would be equivalent to an effect of −49%.' Placement is spatially specific, not smeared: the repellent 'is found most strongly at bifurcations... so can be used to alert ants to a nearby branch in the trail' and is detectable 'up to 30 mm in advance', causing increased zigzagging on approach. The system runs three markers at two timescales in one medium: a non-volatile long-term network marker that is 'sufficiently long-lasting to allow trails to be re-located and reused even after several days of non-use', plus the two volatile short-term ones, and 'Both volatile pheromones decay within two hours, allowing the area to be re-explored to detect renewed food sources.' The corresponding formal update law is τ_xy ← (1−ρ)τ_xy + Σ_k Δτ_xy^k with selection p ∝ τ^α · η^β, and the max-min variant bounds τ to [τ_min, τ_max] with reinitialization to τ_max 'to prevent stagnation'.
**Proposed game rule.** Three corrections to the ratified signal table, all cheap. (1) Danger should NOT get its own 'very slow fade' constant — give it the SAME decay rate as Trail/Patrol and roughly 2x the emission amplitude. It will then outlast them by a factor of ~2.4 naturally, with one decay code path instead of two. (2) Emit Danger at PORTAL granularity, not chamber granularity — mark the specific doorway an agent died crossing, and let agents sample it one hop out so they hesitate and re-route BEFORE committing, which is where the design's 'enemies corner-peek turrets' behavior should come from. (3) Add the missing third tier: a very-slow non-volatile 'known ground' marker on the portal graph that persists for the whole run, so re-invading agents re-locate previously-used lanes without re-exploring. Bound every field to [min, max] per the max-min rule and reinitialize a chamber's fields to max when a garrison is destroyed there.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the specific, filmable behavior the director will look for: an agent approaching a turret room should visibly slow and zigzag one hop out, then either wait at the doorway emitting Muster or take the second-best portal — no code path for 'peeking' needed, it falls out of doorway-granular Danger. PREDICTS lanes self-organize and persist across the whole run because of the third tier. PREDICTS the negative mark dominates decision points while positive marks dominate route-following, so a single strong turret closes ONE doorway rather than making a whole region radioactive. FORBIDS tuning Danger persistence by slowing its decay — the source says explicitly that longer persistence came from amplitude and not from rate, and the rates were statistically indistinguishable. FORBIDS unbounded fields.
**Beyond the current spec.** The Appendix table gives Danger 'no spread, very slow fade' and Patrol 'no spread, slow fade' — two hand-picked decay constants that will have to be co-tuned forever. This says: one decay constant, two amplitudes, and you get the 2.4x persistence ratio for free with a measurement behind it. It adds portal-granular emission, which the current per-chamber scalar design cannot express and which is what makes doorway-holding and corner-peeking emerge rather than be scripted — worth noting the project already tried and reverted a hand-coded corner-peek behavior. And it adds a whole-run persistence tier the table is missing entirely, which is what makes reclaimed ground get re-reclaimed along the same routes.
**Source.** https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/46214/1/RobinsonPheromoneDecayInsSoc.pdf

### J8. Latent readiness is free; standing readiness is ruinously expensive — and readiness spreads one hop through the air
**What the source says.** van Hulten et al. 2006 measured the fitness cost of two different defensive postures in Arabidopsis. Standing (constitutively activated) defense is very expensive: in unchallenged plants, direct activation 'reduced seed production by 31%' at 60 mg/L of one agent and 'by 37%' at 200 mg/L of another; relative growth rate fell by '44%' and '39%' in 3–4 week-old plants and by '41%' and '71%' in 6–7 week-old plants. The primed (latent, not-yet-activated) posture is essentially free: priming treatments 'did not affect seed production' and '5-10 mg/L BABA priming did not lead to statistically significant reductions in RGR'. Under actual challenge the latent posture wins outright: unprimed controls had seed production 'reduced by 38%', while primed plants 'produced the highest amount of seeds of all P. syringae-inoculated plants' and 'displayed the highest growth rates', with priming 'almost as effective... as induction of direct defense'. Mutants locked into the two postures confirm it: the constitutively-primed mutant showed no significant seed reduction, while the constitutively-activated mutant 'displayed strongly reduced RGR, irrespective of its age' and 'produced significantly less seeds'. The companion review states the resulting design logic directly — 'Due to the ecological costs of priming, priming is reversible' — and adds that the latent state has range: it 'can spread not only [to] systemic plant parts but even to neighbouring plants via volatile signals and/or following generations via epigenetic mechanisms', with durability 'up to at least several weeks' in the classic result.
**Proposed game rule.** Hard architectural rule for the marrow: counter-biasing is a LATENT modifier on the dispatch mix and must cost zero marrow reserve. It changes WHICH classes come, never HOW MANY, never their stats. Standing readiness — pre-stationing counter-class guards in a chamber before contact — costs reserve at a continuous drain, so the hub only pays it when the Distress integral for that chamber is high, and it abandons the posture (releases the guards) when Distress resolves. Second rule from the review: let the latent bias diffuse ONE hop through the existing field substrate with no courier at all — a chamber whose neighbour holds a biased posture inherits a fraction of it. That gives biased-mix contagion around a hot area for free, using the field system the design already committed to, and it explains diegetically why the chambers adjacent to a fight are already 'wrong' when you push into them.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that an idle or slow-playing player never faces inflated enemy numbers — the counter appears as a change in composition on contact, not as a global difficulty ramp. PREDICTS a measurable reserve signature: reserve drain per second should be flat while the bias is latent and step up only when the hub commits to pre-stationing. FORBIDS the easy and wrong implementation where 'the enemy learned' means 'the enemy got a stat buff' or 'more of them come' — that is the standing posture, and the numbers say it costs 31–44% of throughput, which in game terms is reserve the marrow does not have. FORBIDS permanent readiness: because it costs, it must be reversible and must actually be released when the stimulus resolves, which is the thing that makes the player's disengage-and-relocate play pay off.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has a finite slow-regenerating reserve and a counter-biasing line, but never connects them — nothing says what learning COSTS. Left unspecified, counter-biasing will drift into a difficulty multiplier, which contradicts the design's own 'rebalance via kinematics, not damage inflation' and 'a status effect the player applies to the enemy' framing. This supplies the cost model with real ratios, forces the bias to be compositional rather than quantitative, and adds one-hop contagion of the biased posture through the field substrate — a mechanism the spec does not have and which the 'kin field' and 'garrison reach' plumbing can carry with no new system.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1459400/

### J9. Two memory tiers with an order-of-magnitude gap, and only the slow tier survives agent turnover
**What the source says.** The same measured system runs fast and slow stores side by side with very different lifetimes. In the trail system, the two volatile markers lose their effect in 33 and 78 minutes and 'Both volatile pheromones decay within two hours', while the third, non-volatile network marker 'can be detected after several days' and lets trails 'be re-located and reused even after several days of non-use' — a roughly 100x gap in the same medium, described by the authors as the colony's 'long term memory of where the colony has been foraging'. The same split shows up in the trained-response literature with an explicit mechanism for why: local training of the frontline agents 'is often assayed days to weeks post-exposure and tends to wane with local turnover, especially in mucosal sites', whereas training held at the PRODUCTION-ORGAN level 'is typically assessed weeks to months after exposure and can persist long-term', with 'innate memory [that] can persist up to ~1 year in some contexts, consistent with an HSPC reservoir of epigenetically imprinted progenitors', sustained by 'continuous generation of primed myeloid progeny' from the depot. In an invertebrate the effect on a second exposure is similarly durable and quantified: protection 'can last up to 22 d'; offspring of primed parents showed 'a 26% reduction in the level of larval mortality'; inheritance ran to 'the F2 but not the F3 generation'; and it is not free — priming 'is metabolically costly to induce and maintain', with primed females laying 'fewer eggs'.
**Proposed game rule.** Split the learning layer explicitly by lifetime and by where it lives. FAST TIER: the per-chamber scalar fields — seconds to tens of seconds, held in the level, wiped when the agents standing in a chamber die. SLOW TIER: the marrow ledger and the class×signature threshold table — minutes to whole-run, held at the depot, immune to losing every agent in the field. The stated causal rule is the important part: local memory dies with local turnover, depot memory does not. So killing every agent in a region must genuinely reset that region's fast tier (giving the player real, immediate, visible reward for a clean sweep — the design's containment reward) while leaving the slow tier untouched (so the reinforcements that arrive later are still biased, and the ground still re-arms). And because the depot continuously stamps new agents with the current bias, freshly-poured agents should carry the counter-bias while agents already in the field keep whatever they were poured with — so a wave is a snapshot of what the marrow believed when it opened the breach.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a legible two-speed feel: clear a room and the local pressure genuinely drops within seconds; come back in two minutes and the composition of what has re-occupied it is still tailored to you. PREDICTS that agents already in the level do NOT retroactively upgrade when the marrow learns — mixed-generation waves, verifiable by tagging each spawned agent with the bias vector active at its pour. FORBIDS a single global scalar 'the enemy has learned' with one decay rate — that is the design that makes clearing a room feel pointless (nothing resets) or makes the enemy amnesiac (everything resets). FORBIDS the slow tier being reachable by any amount of field-level killing: the only ways to touch it are the courier interception, the assessment chamber, and the core kill.
**Beyond the current spec.** The Appendix table is entirely fast tier — every listed signal fades in seconds and lives in a chamber. Nothing in the spec states where durable knowledge lives or what survives a wipe, which is exactly why 'containment reward' ('fully clearing and cordoning an area clears it permanently') and 'the ground re-arms' read as being in tension. The two-tier split resolves that tension mechanically: cordoning kills the fast tier permanently for that ground, while the slow tier at the depot is untouched and is why the ground re-arms if the cordon leaks. It also supplies the rule that a poured wave is stamped with the bias at pour time, which is a cheap implementation and a strong legibility win.
**Source.** https://elifesciences.org/articles/108276

### J10. Saturation is the failure mode: an un-capped shared medium makes the swarm 10x slower, not smarter
**What the source says.** Reid et al. 2012 showed a brainless organism using its own deposited trail as an externalized spatial record — it avoids ground it has already covered, which is what lets it escape a U-shaped trap that is a standard robotics navigation test. Reported quantitatively (PNAS full text returned HTTP 403; these figures are from a news report of the study, labelled as secondary): with one branch of a Y pre-coated in the organism's own deposit, '39 out of 40 molds avoided that branch and went the other way' — a 97.5% avoidance rate, so the marker is close to a hard gate rather than a soft bias. But the same marker becomes a liability when the environment is already saturated: in a dish that already contained trails, 'the mold would take almost 10 times longer than if the petri dish was clean', whereas on a clean surface it 'often tracked close to the most efficient route around the walls'. The corresponding algorithmic fix is standard: bound the marker to an interval, 'the range of possible pheromone amounts on each trail is limited to an interval [τ_max, τ_min]' with edges reinitialized to τ_max 'to prevent stagnation', under the update law τ ← (1−ρ)τ + ΣΔτ.
**Proposed game rule.** Every scalar field and every learned quantity gets a hard [min, max] clamp and a saturation guard, and the guard is instrumented, not silent. Specifically: cap Danger and Patrol per chamber; when a chamber cluster's aggregate Danger has been pinned at max for more than a fixed interval, reinitialize that cluster's Danger toward the ceiling-release value so routing can re-explore — the direct analogue of reinitializing to τ_max to break stagnation. Log a saturation counter per field per farm run. Also take the 97.5% number seriously: a strong negative marker should be near-absolute at the moment of deposit, not a gentle weighting — a doorway that just killed three agents should be genuinely refused by a lone agent, which is what forces the doorway-holding and Muster-gathering behavior the spec wants.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the exact shape of a bug this project has already lived through — 'reinforcements never reached the player.' Un-capped fields in a long fight produce agents that mill about, refuse every route, and never arrive; the diagnostic signature is roughly an order-of-magnitude increase in time-to-arrival with total dispatch unchanged, plus fields pinned at their ceiling. That is a concrete, checkable hypothesis to test in a farm run: instrument time-from-pour-to-contact and field saturation together. FORBIDS shipping any field, ledger entry, or threshold without a clamp. FORBIDS treating a strong negative marker as a mild preference — at deposit it should be near-absolute, and the way agents get through it is by gathering enough force to cross together, not by the marker being weak.
**Beyond the current spec.** The Appendix specifies emission, spread and fade for every signal but no bounds on any of them, and the tick-delta decay scheme it describes (decay computed from the delta since last update) makes accumulation cheap and unbounded by construction. This adds the missing clamp, the anti-stagnation reinitialization, an instrumented saturation counter, and a numeric expectation (~10x arrival-time inflation) that turns 'reinforcements aren't arriving' from a mystery into a measurement. Given that the project's rebuild notes record dead reinforcements as a previously-misdiagnosed root cause, keeping field saturation permanently in the hypothesis space is cheap insurance.
**Source.** https://news.mongabay.com/2012/10/brainless-slime-mold-uses-slimy-memory-to-navigate/

## LANE K — allocation across simultaneous demand sites, and reading a hidden system from its surface

### K1. Gate latency is the reserve gauge (time-to-first-agent, not agent count)
**What the source says.** A full dynamical model of a depot that must hand cargo from returning collectors to a limited pool of receivers puts one scalar at the centre of everything: S(t), the 'search time' a returning agent needs to find a free receiver. S(t) is a function of the ratio of waiting agents to available receivers, and every downstream decision keys off it. Fitted parameter table (values sourced to Seeley's measurements): single interaction time ss = 5 s (range 2 s -> 7 s); half-maximal search time mS = 10 s; search-time coefficient k = 4 (steep sigmoid); dispatch rate fr = 0.0010 s^-1; stand-down rate fs = 0.0002 s^-1; round-trip time fa = 15 min (range 0.5 -> 20 min); receiver hold time rs = 20 min. Escalation is a hard step function: dR/dt = U(S(t) - mT) * (omega*S^m)/(S^m + mT^m) — nothing at all happens to receiver recruitment until S(t) exceeds threshold mT. The paper's own summary of the regime change: 'Once this occurs the search time increases and recruitment is inhibited, leading to much slower changes in population.' https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.3311 . The general inference tool: L = lambda*W, 'not influenced by the arrival process distribution, the service distribution, the service order, or practically anything else'; worked example L = 10 x 0.5 = 5, and inverted W = L/lambda = 2/10 = 0.2 — i.e. an unobservable interior population is computable from two things measurable at the boundary. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little%27s_law
**Proposed game rule.** The marrow never displays its reserve. It leaks it through gate latency. Two numbers per breach, both computed from reserve R: (a) open-to-first-body latency L0 = base * (1 + (R_full/R)^k) with k=4 so it is flat and then explodes — at full reserve first body in ~0.8 s, at 25% reserve ~4 s, at 5% reserve ~15 s and visibly stuttering; (b) inter-arrival gap inside a wave scales the same way. Nothing else changes: agent kinds, health, speed all stay identical, so latency is the ONLY channel. Because a breach is a real portal the player can stand at, timing it is free and diegetic. Corollary the model demands: escalation is a step, not a ramp — the hub's 'open a second breach' behaviour must not fire at all until latency crosses a single hard threshold mT, then must ramp fast.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: log (reserve, L0) over a real run and the curve is monotone and steep — a player (or bot) that measures 3 consecutive gaps > 4 s can invade with >80% probability of finding the reserve near zero, and the doctrine metric 'time from first long gap to reserve exhaustion' becomes a measurable lead time. FORBIDS: reading the reserve off wave SIZE or off enemy composition (must be held constant, else the latency channel is redundant and the k=4 steepness is wasted); forbids any HUD reserve bar; forbids a linear latency ramp (a linear ramp is unreadable against the noise of one agent walking slower than another).
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 says 'the brief window between reserve exhaustion and the portal closing is the player's chance to invade' and that the hub is 'gated on the player noticing' — but specifies NO observable at all. This supplies the observable, the functional form, the steepness that makes it legible, and the reason it cannot be spoofed: latency is generated by the same reserve arithmetic that drives the economy, so it can never lie.
**Source.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.3311 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little%27s_law

### K2. Return-gated dispatch — a gate is fed in proportion to what comes back through it
**What the source says.** In a distributed collector system with no spatial signalling and no controller, the rate at which agents leave the depot is a running accumulator driven by RETURNS, not by demand: alpha_n = max(alpha_{n-1} - q*D_{n-1} + c*A_{n-d}, alpha), where each departure D decrements the rate and each return A increments it. Fitted/used values: 'alpha, the baserate of foraging, was very small, equal to 0.01 ants per second'; 'We set q to 0.05'; d set to 0 for the reported simulations; c (the effect of one returning agent on outflow) swept from 0.01 to 0.25. Observed system throughput ranged 0.1–1.2 returns/sec, and the model covered 0.15–1.2 departures/sec. The authors note 'a lag in the recovery of the rate of outgoing foragers in response to recovery of the rate of returning foragers.' https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1002670 . IMPORTANT PROVENANCE CORRECTION: I fetched this paper specifically to check the widely-repeated claim that it draws a network-congestion-protocol analogy. It does not. The paper contains no such comparison; that framing exists only in press coverage. I therefore report the accumulator, not the analogy.
**Proposed game rule.** Give every open breach its own dispatch accumulator alpha_b, updated on events only: -q each time a body steps through outbound, +c each time a body steps back inbound (a breach is already specified two-way, and 'a crossing is anything crossing the line'). Floor at a trickle rate so a gate never hard-locks. Tune so a gate whose agents all die decays from peak to floor in ~20–30 s, and a gate whose agents survive and return climbs over ~15 s. Return traffic must be a real behaviour: agents that lose contact with the intruder and stand down (spec already has a 5 s stand-down) walk home through the breach.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who builds a clean kill-zone on breach A sees A's flow throttle itself down within half a minute while breach B intensifies — the enemy 'gives up' on a killing field WITHOUT any code that reasons about killing fields. It also mechanises the spec's undefined phrase 'closes when it is no longer useful to the agents the hub spawns': useful == alpha_b above floor. FORBIDS: an infinite meat-grinder gate; forbids the failure where the hub keeps feeding a breach the player has perfectly solved. Verifiable in a real run: plot alpha_b against measured kill rate at each breach; the correlation must be strongly negative.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec defines breach closure by a 45 s uncrossed timer, by reserve exhaustion, and by 'no longer useful' — the third condition has no definition. This is the definition, and it is event-driven off crossings the spec already tracks, so it needs no new sensing. It also inverts the naive design: the HOT gate is the SUCCESSFUL one, not the oldest and not the noisiest.
**Source.** https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1002670

### K3. Flicker before failure — the depot stutters before it stops
**What the source says.** A review of generic early-warning signatures: as a system nears a threshold, 'recovery rates decrease smoothly to zero as the critical point is' approached, which shows up as rising variance and rising lag-1 autocorrelation. The paper's worked stochastic example gives both regimes numerically: far from the threshold, s.d. 0.016 with correlation 0.76; near it, s.d. 0.091 with correlation 0.90 (a ~5.7x rise in spread and a jump in memory for the same forcing). Separately it describes flickering: 'smaller transient excursions to the vicinity of an alternative state precede the upcoming major shift', statistically visible 'as increased variance and skewness as well as bimodality'. Caveats stated plainly: 'detection of increased autocorrelation may require long time series'; false negatives occur when a shift 'was not preceded by a gradual approach to a threshold'; and 'a spatial pattern contains much more information than does a single point in a time series, in principle allowing shorter lead times'. https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/2009/scheffer2009a.pdf . The companion methods paper shows how cheap this can be made if you know the system's structure: an impending collapse was flagged from only 'fifteen observations of population size and birth rate', giving 'ample warning'. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3271022/
**Proposed game rule.** Below ~20% reserve the marrow FLICKERS instead of degrading smoothly: a breach opens, emits 1–2 bodies, and reseals within a couple of seconds; then re-opens 10 s later somewhere near. Wave size variance rises ~5x while the MEAN barely moves (that is the whole trick — the mean is a bad indicator, the spread is a good one). Audio/visual: the breach aperture pulses irregularly instead of holding steady. Because the spec's fields are per-chamber scalars, the same signature is available spatially: near exhaustion the Muster field goes patchy/bimodal across neighbouring polygons instead of smooth.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: an aborted-open event is the loudest, cheapest tell in the game and it appears at ~20% reserve, i.e. tens of seconds of real lead time before the invade window — verifiable by logging (reserve, wave-size variance, abort count) and checking that variance/aborts rise while mean wave size is flat. FORBIDS: a smooth linear fade-out of reinforcements as the reserve drains (unreadable, and it wastes the strongest available signal); forbids relying on mean wave size as the tell. Also forbids over-claiming: the review is explicit that autocorrelation needs long series, so the shipped tell must be the flicker COUNT (an event the player can see once), not an autocorrelation estimate.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's entire hub raid is gated on the player noticing an off-screen process, with no designed perceptual signature. This gives one that is (a) unmistakable in a single occurrence, (b) generated by the reserve arithmetic rather than scripted, and (c) has a stated lead time so the invade window is reachable on foot rather than being a coin flip.
**Source.** https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/2009/scheffer2009a.pdf ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3271022/

### K4. Reserve-inverted split — even division is the RICH-state behaviour, concentration is the POOR-state behaviour
**What the source says.** Colonies given two IDENTICAL demand sites do not split evenly by default; positive feedback drives them onto one. The surprising measured result is which way quality pushes it: testing three source qualities (0.1 M, 0.5 M and 1 M), 'Contrary to prediction, we found that symmetry breaking decreased as the quality of two identical food sources increased' — richer sites gave a MORE even split, poorer sites a more winner-take-all split; 'individual ants can modulate their response to pheromone trails which may lead colonies to exploiting resources more evenly when in a resource rich environment.' https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27784955/ (abstract only — the Springer full text is behind an auth redirect and I quote no figures from it). The matching baseline this deviates from: distribution 'proportional to the amount of resources available in each' patch, with the standard empirical deviation being undermatching — 'proportionately more individuals in the least profitable patch and a shortage at the richest patch'. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_free_distribution
**Proposed game rule.** Make the split a function of reserve, not a constant. Define spread s = clamp(R / R_full): at high reserve the hub allocates near-proportionally across all open breaches (undermatched — deliberately over-serve the quiet gate slightly); as reserve falls, allocation sharpens until below ~30% reserve it is winner-take-all on the single highest-alpha gate and the others stall at the trickle floor. Implement as a softmax over per-gate weights with inverse temperature beta = beta0 / s.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: the split ITSELF becomes a second reserve readout, independent of latency — 'three breaches all lightly fed' means the marrow is fat; 'two gates gone quiet and one gate screaming' means it is nearly dry and the invade window is close. It also makes a two-front fight early-game and a one-front fight late-game, which is the correct pacing shape. FORBIDS the spec's literal rule: an unconditional even split. Verifiable: log per-gate throughput share vs reserve; share entropy must fall monotonically with reserve.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 states multiple simultaneous breaches 'split enemies evenly', full stop. The measured behaviour of shared-pool recruiters is that an even split is unstable under positive feedback, and the direction of the quality effect is counter-intuitive. Replacing the constant with a reserve-driven spread costs one line of arithmetic and buys a second, redundant, independent legibility channel — which matters because the whole hub raid hangs on the player reading one hidden number.
**Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27784955/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_free_distribution

### K5. Two-choice dispatch over deliberately stale, per-gate-jittered demand, with an aging floor
**What the source says.** A distributed dispatcher choosing among servers from a bulletin board that is T seconds out of date: 'although choosing the shortest queue is best when information is current (T = 0), for even very small values of T the strategy performs worse than randomly selecting a queue, especially under high loads.' The named failure: 'all the tasks that arrive over those T seconds will go only to the small set of servers that appear lightly loaded on the board, overloading them while other servers empty. The system demonstrates what we call herd behavior' — the paper's own analogy is a supermarket announcing 'Aisle 7 is now open', after which 'Very often Aisle 7 quickly becomes the longest queue.' Sampling exactly two candidates and taking the better of the two 'performs substantially better than even three choices over a large interval of values of T'. And the fix for oscillation is randomness, not freshness: when each arrival sees information of age X ~ exponential with mean T rather than a synchronized age T, arrivals 'make different choices. Hence the herd behavior is mitigated'. Simulations at lambda = 0.5 and 0.9, mu = 1.0, n = 100 and n = 8 servers; the choice strategies matched the analytic system to within 1–2%. http://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/dec/tech_reports/SRC-TN-1998-002.pdf . The underlying bound: with one random choice max load is log n / log log n; sampling d and taking the least loaded gives log log n / log d + Theta(1). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balls_into_bins_problem . The anti-starvation floor: 'the priority of a process should increase as it waits'; worked example on a 0–511 scale, '+1 every 15 minutes, then in more than 32 hours the process will age to 0 priority'. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_(scheduling)
**Proposed game rule.** The marrow's dispatch policy, in three clauses: (1) it reads each gate's demand from a snapshot whose age is drawn per-gate from an exponential with mean ~4 s — never a synchronized global refresh; (2) for each body it samples exactly TWO open gates and sends it to the better one — never argmax over all gates; (3) each gate accrues +1 dispatch weight per 10 s it has received nothing, so a gate the policy has ignored eventually gets served regardless. This is also the correct model for the SPOTTERS, which the spec already forbids from knowing hub state: a spotter picks between two candidate plant sites it can sense, on stale local field values, never the global best.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: no visible thundering-herd — you never see the entire reinforcement stream slam onto one gate, empty it, then slam onto another in a 2-cycle. The aging clause predicts that a gate the player has left completely alone will still eventually produce a trickle, so 'ignore the far breach' is never free. FORBIDS: any global argmax over gates or chambers anywhere in the dispatch path (the source is explicit that this is worse than RANDOM once information is even slightly stale); forbids a synchronized field-update tick shared by all gates — the staleness must be jittered per gate, because de-synchronizing the information age is precisely what kills the oscillation.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has no allocation policy and no anti-oscillation story, and its signal fields are updated per-chamber on a shared tick with decay computed from a delta — exactly the synchronized-staleness structure this literature identifies as the herd generator. This is a two-line change (jitter the sample age; sample two, not all) with a named, measurable failure it prevents. It also answers the wave-1 'oldest gate hottest' proposal precisely: age should be a starvation FLOOR, not the ranking function.
**Source.** http://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/dec/tech_reports/SRC-TN-1998-002.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balls_into_bins_problem ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_(scheduling)

### K6. No-entry marks live on DOORWAYS and outlast the come-here marks 2.4x
**What the source says.** Direct measurement of how long each of two opposing route markers keeps changing behaviour in a live junction network: 'the repellent pheromone effect lasts more than twice as long as the attractive pheromone effect (78 min versus 33 min)'. And the initial strengths differ too: 'the initial effect of the repellent pheromone [is roughly double that of] the attractive pheromone (48 % versus 25 % above control)'. The two decay at approximately the same RATE; the asymmetry is in initial amplitude and therefore in time-to-baseline. Functional split stated by the authors: the negative marker acts at decision points while the positive marker 'guides ants along the entire trail'. Trial traffic for scale: 'Background flow ranged from 12 – 117 ants/minute'. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/46214/1/RobinsonPheromoneDecayInsSoc.pdf . The underlying discovery this measures: a negative route marker used as a 'no entry' signal to mark an unrewarding path, deposited at branch points. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16306981/ (abstract only)
**Proposed game rule.** Add ONE per-portal scalar (not per-chamber): Refusal, emitted onto the specific doorway an agent was crossing when it took damage or died, with no spread at all, initial amplitude ~2x that of the positive routing signal and a half-life ~2.4x longer. Routing agents subtract Refusal from a doorway's attractiveness at the moment of choosing which portal to commit to; it never affects behaviour inside a chamber. Because the spec's Trail mechanic already marks committed doorways positively, this is its exact mirror and reuses the same storage.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a cordon becomes MECHANICAL rather than aspirational — a crossing the player has held and killed at accumulates Refusal, so spotters route around it instead of trickling into it, and 'held ground stays held' emerges from the field instead of from a special case. It also predicts observable lane formation: traffic self-organises away from three or four marked doorways onto one, which the player can read off the map. FORBIDS: modelling danger only as a per-chamber scalar (a chamber-level Danger value cannot express 'this room is fine, that ONE door into it is a killing floor', which is exactly the geometry of a turret covering a doorway); forbids giving the negative signal the same lifetime as the positive one — the whole point is that refusal outlives attraction, so a cleared route stays refused after the fight's excitement has faded.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's Danger is per-chamber, no spread, very slow fade — right instinct, wrong resolution. Moving refusal onto the PORTAL is what makes the spec's own 'containment reward' and 'cordon of held crossings' buildable, and the 78/33 min and 48%/25% pair give a ratified tuning ratio instead of a guess. Everything else about Danger stays.
**Source.** https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/46214/1/RobinsonPheromoneDecayInsSoc.pdf ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16306981/

### K7. Wave shape encodes hop-distance to the depot
**What the source says.** For any agent that must carry output from a fixed home base to a remote work site, round-trip cost reshapes the delivery: 'animals from birds to honeybees have been found to take larger loads when foraging further from their central place', and foragers 'should be choosier and more likely to specialize on the most profitable food sources when foraging further from their central place'. Also: within a single trip, agents 'become progressively less choosy as time spent foraging increases'. The calibration warning is as useful as the rule — real systems miss the theoretical optimum badly: merlins switch strategy 'at around 1/50 of the distance predicted by a model (Rands et al. 2000)', and in the chipmunk test 'neither the actual sizes of the loads nor the function describing the relationship between travel distance and load size is accurately explained by theory'. https://chittkalab.sbcs.qmul.ac.uk/2018/WoodgateChittka_2018_CentralPlaceForaging.pdf
**Proposed game rule.** Let h = portal-hop distance from the marrow chamber to the breach's host chamber. Wave batch size scales with h and inter-wave interval scales with h, so total throughput is roughly flat but the GRANULARITY is not: a breach cut 3 hops from the marrow delivers small parties every few seconds; one cut 9 hops delivers a big lump every 20 s. Second clause from the same source: the further the gate, the more selective the mix — distant gates get only high-value classes (heavy units), near gates get whatever is on hand.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who has fought at three breaches can rank them by how close each is to the hub — lumpy, heavy, infrequent waves mean 'this gate is far from the core; the OTHER one is the short way in'. That turns 'find the hub' from luck into inference from ordinary combat experience, with no new UI. FORBIDS: identical wave cadence at every breach (which would make all gates informationally equivalent and destroy the inference); forbids choosing the invasion gate at random. Verifiable: log (h, mean batch size, mean gap) per gate over a run and confirm both scale with h.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says the hub is reachable only through a live breach and is 'gated on the player noticing', but every breach is otherwise interchangeable, so a player who notices still has no way to choose WHICH one to dive. This makes gate choice a skill expression. Note the source's own caution: real systems deviate from the optimum by large factors, so tune this by feel and do not derive the constants.
**Source.** https://chittkalab.sbcs.qmul.ac.uk/2018/WoodgateChittka_2018_CentralPlaceForaging.pdf

### K8. Demand-signal gaming — allocate on delivered throughput, not on shouted demand
**What the source says.** When a supplier allocates scarce output in proportion to the demand each site reports, the reporting itself becomes strategic: under rationing, 'the buyer acts on this knowledge by placing larger orders in hopes of getting closer to the actual desired quantity', which injects false demand upstream. The amplification this produces is large: 'a fluctuation in point-of-sale demand of five percent will be interpreted by supply chain participants as a change in demand of up to forty percent' (an ~8x distortion). Other named amplifiers: order batching, and lead-time variability causing every level to hold extra buffer, 'compounding variations at each supply chain level'. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullwhip_effect
**Proposed game rule.** Two consequences. (a) DEFENSIVE: the marrow must NOT allocate in proportion to a live per-chamber demand signal (Distress/Muster), because those are emitted by agents whose survival depends on getting reinforced — that is a proportional-rationing loop and it will amplify noise ~8x into runaway waves. Allocate instead on realized past throughput at each gate over the last N seconds (the return-gated accumulator of candidate 2). (b) OFFENSIVE, deliberately kept: because the ANTI-TURTLE flank-breach rule is a threshold on raw Distress, the player can farm a decoy site to manufacture a huge demand signal and pull the flank breach to a place and time he controls. Keep that loop; just make sure it is the ONLY place demand-proportional allocation appears.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: no runaway reinforcement spiral from a single hot fight — a 5% wobble in local combat intensity cannot bloom into a 40% swing in dispatch, because dispatch is keyed to what actually came back, not to what was requested. And it makes the spec's own 'bait the enemy into attacking an impenetrable fortress' explicitly reachable. FORBIDS: dispatch weight = f(Distress) anywhere in the hub economy; forbids order batching (the hub must dispatch continuously, not accumulate then dump), since batching is listed as an independent amplifier on top of the rationing loop.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has a demand-driven flank-breach threshold and a separate reinforcement economy but never separates them, and never notices that reinforcement-proportional-to-distress is a positive feedback loop with a known instability. This names the instability, gives the fix (allocate on realized throughput), and preserves the player-facing exploit the director explicitly wants.
**Source.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullwhip_effect

### K9. Bounded scatter on the transmitted position — imprecision is the feature
**What the source says.** An agent-based study of location transmission fidelity in a 1,000-agent colony, sites at 400 / 750 / 1,000 / 1,250 / 1,500 / 1,750 / 2,000 m, 2 / 5 / 7 / 10 sites, 8-hour runs, 20 runs per condition, testing transmitted angular error of 0, 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 degrees. Natural error is 'ranged between 10–15° at the lower margin'. Results: 'When the variation was 5° or less, only the waggle dance colony was the best colony under all conditions tested' — but that precision came with a failure mode, 'many foraging bees completely failed to switch feeders'. At 10° the transmitting colony 'remained the best performer'; at 15° results were 'highly mosaic'; 'When the variation was 30° or 60°, the waggle dance colony did not perform the best.' Conclusion: 'A 10–15° variation in the conveyance of food-source information might be optimal for guaranteeing foraging success under a dynamically changing environment... a naturally selected trade-off between risk and success.' https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3935192/
**Proposed game rule.** The spotter transmits a position; the marrow cuts the breach at that position PLUS bounded scatter — roughly a 10–15 degree cone about the transmit point as seen from the marrow's link, resolved onto the portal graph as: same chamber ~60% of the time, an adjacent chamber otherwise. Keep the scatter small enough that the spotter's placement still dominates (the source is explicit that 30–60 degrees destroys the advantage). Same rule for the second and third breach a spotter plants: they land near, not on, the intended spots.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: breaches never stack on the identical tile, so multi-breach fights are spatially spread by construction rather than by a de-duplication hack; and a player who camps the exact spot he saw a spotter transmit from is rewarded but not made immune — roughly 4 in 10 breaches open one chamber over, which forces him to hold a small area rather than a single pixel. FORBIDS: a pixel-perfect ambush breach; forbids scatter large enough (>1 hop typical) that the spotter's careful 'as close to the player as is safe' placement stops mattering — the source shows the wide-error condition is strictly worse.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's spotter 'communicates/transmits a position' with implied exactness, and separately requires that breaches not log-jam and that the player be rewarded for catching the transmit. Exact transmission makes the reward binary (camp the spot, deny everything). Bounded scatter turns it into a graded reward and — the surprising part — the source shows the imprecise system OUTPERFORMS the precise one in a changing environment, so this is not a nerf, it is what keeps the enemy adaptive.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3935192/

### K10. Cross-suppression to break a two-gate tie
**What the source says.** A short vibratory inhibitory signal, 'typically lasting approximately 100 ms at approximately 380 Hz' (a later measurement: '407 Hz for 147 ms'), delivered directly onto agents advertising a site. It is triggered by congestion and by bad experience at a site: 'As a feeding station grew more crowded, the rate of stop signaling within the colony increased', and 'Bees that had experienced danger or competition at a food source are more likely to perform the stop signal toward other foragers waggle-dancing for the same food source.' Effect on the target: they 'are more likely to leave the dance floor' and 'their average dance length is shorter'. **Proposed game rule.** Two clauses, both cheap. (a) SELF-suppression from congestion: an agent that finds its target chamber already crowded emits a brief suppressor that decrements the dispatch weight of the gate it came from — the depot stops shovelling into a jam without anyone measuring the jam. (b) CROSS-suppression to break ties: when two gates are within a few percent of equal weight, agents committed to gate A decrement gate B (and vice versa), which forces the near-tie to collapse onto one gate in a few seconds instead of chattering. Direction matters and is the non-obvious bit: for choosing between sites the suppression is aimed at the OTHER site, not at one's own.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: two equally-hot breaches resolve to one dominant gate within seconds rather than alternating; and log-jamming at a breach mouth self-corrects because congestion feeds back as suppression — which is exactly the failure the spec calls out ('no log-jamming in the opening') and tries to solve purely by making the aperture wide. FORBIDS: a stable exact 50/50 between two gates (deadlock), and forbids the depot continuing to push bodies into an already-crowded destination. Verifiable: seed two gates with identical weights and confirm the split diverges within ~5 s rather than oscillating.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has only positive signals in its table (Distress, Danger as avoidance, Noise, Trail, Patrol, Muster, Garrison) — every one of them either recruits or repels by location; there is no signal an agent aims at ANOTHER AGENT'S decision. This adds the one missing sign, and it is the specific mechanism that makes a tied allocation resolve instead of oscillate. It is also the correct fix for the aperture jam, which the spec currently addresses only by geometry.

### L1. Latency-rank leadership (the leader is whoever reacts first, and never knows it)
**What the source says.** GPS logging of free-flying and homing flocks of up to 10 birds (~15 km displacement) measured, for every pair, the delay at which one individual's heading change is copied by another. About two-thirds (63%) of pairwise comparisons produced clearly directed edges at correlation threshold Cmin=0.5. Mean directional-correlation delay was 0.37 s (±0.27 SD) at Cmin=0.5 and 0.32 s (±0.20 SD) at Cmin=0.9. The resulting follow-graph was almost perfectly transitive: only 3 of 15 flights contained directed loops, and the proportion of edges pointing the same direction averaged 0.99 (±0.03 SD). Rank correlated strongly with spatial position: mean projected distance along the direction of motion vs. hierarchical order, Pearson r=0.863, n=9, p=0.003 — high-rank individuals sit toward the FRONT. Rank was stable across contexts: per-individual delay in free vs. homing flights correlated r=0.797, n=8, p=0.018. There is a lateralization effect too: response is faster to a partner on the left (mean tau_left − tau_right = −0.23 s, s.d. 0.27, flight 1). No individual signals anything; leadership is purely a reaction-time asymmetry. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.5394 (author preprint of the 2010 Nature paper; read in full)
**Proposed game rule.** Give every Adversary agent a per-instance REACTION LAG drawn once at spawn, ~0.15–0.60 s, when it re-reads its one-hop signal-field sample. An agent does not act on this tick's field values; it acts on the values it sampled LAG seconds ago (a tiny per-agent ring buffer of its own last few samples). Nothing else changes — no leader flag, no follow links, no broadcast. Emergent consequence: in any local cluster reacting to the same Muster/Distress change, the lowest-lag agent commits to a doorway first and everyone else's committed heading converges onto his, delayed. Define the emergent garrison anchor as: the chamber occupied by the agent with the lowest LAG among agents currently in a Garrison-interior polygon, evaluated only when a garrison forms. Bias initial position so low-lag agents drift to the front of a push (they commit to the next portal sooner, so front position falls out for free — do not code it separately; verify r>0.5 between lag rank and mean along-push position). Vagrant/lacuna get HIGH lag on purpose (they are followers, they hang back, and that is why they survive to transmit).
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS, verifiable in one run by logging (agent, lag, chamber, committed portal, t): (a) the empirical follow-graph — who changed heading after whom within 1 s — is >90% transitive, i.e. an acyclic order emerges without being coded; (b) the same agent leads across separate, unrelated engagements (per-agent lag is fixed, so cross-fight rank correlation should be high, the r=0.797 analogue); (c) low-lag agents are on average nearer the front of a push. FORBIDS: any leader-designation message, any 'I am the leader' state, any follower list. It also forbids instant collective turns — a whole garrison cannot pivot in under ~one lag; the player gets a real reaction window when he flanks, and that window is measurable.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 defers Leader AI and says only 'leaders have no idea they have followers, and whatever room the leader happens to be in emerges as the base.' This supplies the actual mechanism that makes that true — one scalar per agent, zero coupling — and it makes 'which room?' answerable and reproducible instead of arbitrary. It is also the cheapest possible implementation: a sample delay, not a behavior tree.
**Source.** https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.5394

Conditional probability of depositing while at an occupied site was 10x the probability of collecting there — a positive feedback: material begets material. The driving field is a physical gradient in a ~2 mm boundary layer (comparable to body size ~1 mm); the authors state pheromones are NOT necessary to explain the early deposition patterns — the structure itself is the coordinating signal. URL: https://elifesciences.org/articles/86843 — Definitional framing: coordination through traces left in the environment, 'stimulation of workers by the performance they have achieved', versus the broader category of behavior evoked by the evidence of work already performed. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stigmergy
**Proposed game rule.** Garrison fortification with no architect and no build orders. An agent in a Garrison-interior polygon that is idle deposits one barricade unit at the position within the polygon where the spatial gradient of the Garrison-interior field is steepest — which in a polygon/portal world is always a PORTAL MOUTH (the field's discontinuity is the doorway; the interior/reach split in the spec already encodes exactly this). Make deposition self-reinforcing at 10:1 — an agent standing at a spot that already holds barricade is 10x more likely to add than to move on — so a chokepoint that gets its first block gets walled fast while random spots never start. Sub-rule from the same finding: prefer the LATERAL EDGES of a portal (the ~30% stronger-gradient analogue), so barricades grow inward from the doorway jambs and leave a narrowing slot rather than a solid wall — the garrison ends up with a firing slit it can shoot through and the player must widen.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS, checkable from a screenshot of any mature garrison: barricade material sits at doorways, essentially never in room centers; doorways narrow from the sides inward over time; and the count of fortified doorways per garrison equals the number of portals on its interior/reach boundary, not a designer constant. Killing the anchor does NOT stop construction (no leader is involved) — a specific falsifiable difference from a leader-driven design. FORBIDS a build-order system, a blueprint, and a construction leader.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec asserts 'a garrison is emergent, not an object' and that a leader 'directs them to fortify a room he chooses' — those two sentences are in tension, because directing is an object-like command. This resolves the tension: the field's own geometry picks the build sites, the leader does nothing, and the Garrison-interior/reach distinction the spec already defines becomes load-bearing rather than decorative.
**Source.** https://elifesciences.org/articles/86843

### L7. Capacity plateau, not a jam (refuse-to-enter throughput control)
**What the source says.** 170 experiments yielding 612,000 flow/density pairs on bridges 5, 10 and 20 mm wide, with source colonies of 400 to 25,600 individuals. Flow rose linearly with density below ~8 individuals/cm2 and then stayed FLAT at ~10 individuals/cm/s with no sign of declining even at occupancy 0.8 — no traffic jam ever formed, and observed density never exceeded 18/cm2. Comparable human crowds decline above ~40% occupancy. The mechanisms: individuals avoided ENTERING an already-crowded bridge (regulation at the entry, not on the bridge), almost never reversed once committed (U-turn probability 0.01), and each contact cost 0.24 s of crossing time; entry rates ran 2.69–5.05 individuals/s depending on bridge width. URL: https://elifesciences.org/articles/48945
**Proposed game rule.** Two rules on the existing portal graph, replacing any special-casing at the breach mouth. (1) ENTRY GATE: before committing to a portal, an agent samples the occupancy of the destination polygon (bodies * body_area / polygon_area, a 'find' API query, one hop — legal). If occupancy > 0.5 it does not commit; it re-picks among its other one-hop options, or holds. Regulation happens at the doorway, never inside. (2) NO-REVERSE: once committed to a portal traversal, U-turn probability ~0.01 per decision tick — an agent that has entered the room commits, which is what makes the flow steady instead of oscillating. (3) CONTACT COST: each agent-agent contact adds ~0.25 s of delay rather than a bounce — crowding degrades speed smoothly instead of producing shoving. Scale the breach mouth's throughput budget with its aperture width the way entry rate scaled with bridge width (roughly 2.7 to 5 agents/s across a 4x width range — i.e. clearly sublinear in width, so widening a breach buys much less throughput than it looks like it should).
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: arrivals/second through a breach saturate at a plateau and STAY there under pressure instead of collapsing; a plot of flow vs. polygon occupancy is linear-then-flat with the knee near occupancy ~0.4–0.5; and agents visibly queue on the far side of a portal rather than clumping in the aperture. FORBIDS the log-jam the spec explicitly bans, without any breach-specific code. Also forbids the opposite failure — a hard entity cap at the mouth — because the limiter is a local occupancy read, so a wide room genuinely admits more.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says 'DO NOT add any special casing around entry; simply ensure the breach is wide enough' — but width alone does not prevent jams, it just moves them. This gives the general, non-special-cased rule (destination-occupancy gating + commitment + contact time cost) that produces the jam-free behavior the spec demands, on every portal in the world, with published knee and plateau values to tune against.
**Source.** https://elifesciences.org/articles/48945

### L8. The garrison IS the wall (body-built base, and the settled/moving cycle)
**What the source says.** Some colonies build no excavated nest at all: they construct a living structure from their own bodies, individuals gripping each other's legs into an organized mass. The interior is not solid — it contains numerous passages and 'chambers' holding food, larvae, eggs and, at the protected center, the queen. Personnel are layered by age: older workers form the exterior layer, younger workers sit inside. Under threat, soldiers gather at the TOP SURFACE of the mass. The site is not permanent: during the nomadic phase a new temporary structure is built almost daily (~15 days), followed by a stationary phase of about 20 days. Colonies can exceed 15 million individuals. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ant
**Proposed game rule.** Make the emergent garrison literally its own fortification and give it a duty cycle. (1) BODIES AS WALLS: settled garrison members occupy fixed anchor positions ringing the interior polygon's portals and, while settled, take reduced damage from the front arc and do not chase — they ARE the barricade. Breaking a garrison means killing the wall; there is no structure to destroy afterward, satisfying 'a garrison is emergent, not an object'. (2) LAYERING: the toughest/most-blooded members occupy the outer ring (highest contact score outward), the freshest sit interior — a garrison peeled from the outside gets softer, so committing to a breakthrough is rewarded. (3) THREAT RESPONSE: when Noise in the interior crosses a threshold, interior members surge to the contacted portal rather than the whole ring dissolving — the far side of the ring holds. (4) DUTY CYCLE: a garrison alternates SETTLED (~90–120 s of hold-and-fortify) with MOVING (relocate the anchor along the kin/danger gradients), roughly a 3:2 settled:moving ratio (20:15 day analogue). Cleared ground therefore does not stay clear passively — but a garrison in its moving phase is the player's cheap window to erase it.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: garrison positions drift over a long run instead of ossifying (log anchor chamber over time — expect a relocation every ~2–4 minutes if not engaged); assaulting a settled garrison head-on costs strictly more than intercepting one mid-move (measure player HP lost per garrison destroyed, settled vs. moving); and the outer members die first and are the tougher ones. FORBIDS immortal static camps, forbids a garrison entity with hitpoints, and forbids the 'kill the boss, camp evaporates' shortcut.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has garrisons planted by spotters and then implicitly permanent, with no life cycle and no physical realization beyond 'agents settled in a base'. This makes the garrison tangible to the player (a visible wall of bodies), gives the containment/cordon reward a real clock (garrisons must MOVE to spread, so a cordon starves them at a measurable rate), and supplies the outer-ring/inner-core damage grammar that makes assaulting one feel like breaking a shell.
**Source.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ant

### L9. Aperture sized to the body (solo-plug gates vs. group-flow gates)
**What the source says.** Across 89–99 species surveyed phylogenetically, one caste defends nest entrances by using its head as a living door, and head width sets the exact size of the entrance defended; head width spans a four-fold range across species. Two distinct strategies map onto aperture size: 'disc' and 'dish' morphs specialize on entrances that fit A SINGLE head (solo-blocking), while 'square' and 'dome' morphs defend entrances much larger than one head by GROUP-blocking. Colonies nest in pre-existing bored cavities they did not make, so the aperture is inherited from the environment, and the fit between defender head and the pre-existing hole determines defensive performance against usurpers, with consequences for expansion into further cavities. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7104247 — The general category is defending a burrow by using one's own body as the barrier (term coined 1927), documented in ants, aphids, frogs and spiders. In one aphid study 173 gall openings were examined and 90.8% were plugged, 'with no space among the guarding soldiers'. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phragmosis
**Proposed game rule.** Make aperture width, in units of defender body radius, a first-class property of every gate in the game — breach mouths, hub entrances, and the Portable Hole. Three bands: (a) aperture < 1 body: only mite/shrieker-scale bodies pass, and ONE guardian body parked in it seals it completely (a solo plug — the player must kill the plug before anything can pass, in either direction); (b) 1–2 bodies: single-file, guarded by a rotating plug, throughput ~1 agent per 1.5 s; (c) >= 3 bodies: cannot be plugged by one body, requires a group-block ring of 3+ agents standing in the aperture, and flows freely otherwise. Assign hub entrance apertures deliberately: the hub's outer gates are band (c) group-blocked halls, its deep gates before the CORE are band (a) solo plugs — so the last approach is a series of single-body doors, each of which is a duel. Target 'no space among the guarding bodies' as the visual: a plugged aperture should read as completely filled, not as a gap with an enemy near it.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: the player can be hard-stopped by exactly one correctly-placed body, and can himself hard-stop pursuit by killing something in a narrow aperture (corpse or his own turret occupying it); and hub throughput per gate is predictable from aperture width alone — measure agents/second past each gate and it should fall into three discrete bands, not a continuum. FORBIDS enemies phasing/overlapping through an occupied narrow gate, and forbids the Portable Hole becoming a safe firing slit — the spec already wants hole-capable pursuers, and band (a) gives the geometric reason why only small bodies come through.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec fixes the Portable Hole's aperture rule ('passes only small bodies') and asserts breaches must be 'wide enough', but nowhere makes aperture a measured quantity with defensive consequences. This turns width into the hub's primary tactical grammar — wide halls to fight in, narrow plugs to break — and it is the concrete answer for 'defended entrance geometry' in the open hub unit.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7104247

### L10. Guard fraction with a real price (5–20% standing, and what it costs to raise it)
**What the source says.** In the reference literature 'the soldiers constitute 5–20% of a typical insect colony'. Experimental manipulations held total group size at 20 and varied composition across 18:2, 16:4, 14:6, 12:8, 10:10 and 0:20 — i.e. 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 50% and 100% dedicated defenders — showing the ratio is a tunable colony parameter, not a fixed one. Dedicated defenders in these systems cannot feed themselves and depend on being fed by workers, so the caste is energetically costly and raises the effort demanded of the rest. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9207448/ — Separately, a defended home site stratifies duty by position: the older/experienced hold the exterior layer, the youngest sit interior, and dedicated defenders converge on the exposed surface only when threatened. URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ant
**Proposed game rule.** Define the hub's guardian ring as a FRACTION of the hub's live population, not a fixed spawn list: baseline 15% standing guard (inside the published 5–20% band), the other 85% being dispatchable reserve. Make the fraction dynamic and costly, driven by the fields the spec already has: sustained Distress reaching the hub raises the guard fraction toward 30–40%, and every point of guard fraction subtracts directly from dispatch capacity — a hub that is being invaded stops sending reinforcements out into the level. Guards are fed from the same finite reserve, so a high guard fraction also slows reserve regeneration. Distribute guards by depth per the layering rule: veteran guardians outward/shallow, freshest deepest, and dedicated guardians converge on whichever gate has contact instead of standing evenly.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a measurable strategic seesaw that the player can drive: log breach-open rate in the level while the player is inside the hub — it should visibly collapse, because the same population cannot both guard and deploy. Also predicts that a player who repeatedly pokes the hub and withdraws suppresses level-wide reinforcement pressure at the cost of a hardening hub — a real, discoverable trade. FORBIDS a hub with an unlimited independent garrison AND unlimited dispatch (the standard 'infinite spawner plus boss guards' failure), and forbids guard count being a designer constant.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has a finite reserve and a 'ring of guardian agents' but treats them as separate systems — the ring is scenery, the reserve is economy. Coupling them through one fraction makes invading the hub tactically meaningful BEFORE the core dies (pressure alone throttles the reinforcement layer), which extends the spec's 'core kill is a status effect' idea into a continuous knob rather than a binary, and it gives the guardian ring a number instead of a vibe.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9207448/

- COMPUTE BUDGET — not one lane costs its mechanism. ## Strongest candidates, by the critic's judgment

- Denominator gate — an agent wakes on the ACTIVE FRACTION it can sense, not on absolute signal level (Lane H) — I verified the fitted coefficients (0.302 / -1.421 / -0.126, 154-fish school) and the paper's own verdict that fractional beats numerical contagion; it directly REJECTS the model DESIGN_7_25 line 203 specifies, and buys automatic modulation by group size, spacing and chamber occupancy with zero new signals.
- Inverted mix at contact — the roster at the depot is not the roster that shows up (Lane G) — verified verbatim ('At the site of combat, the major workers outnumber the minors (although the reverse is true for the colony at large)'); gives the wholly-undesigned hub area an identity derived from the combat system rather than invented, and makes the spec's 'outnumbered and undergunned' feel a perception skill rather than a stat.
- Gate latency is the reserve gauge (Lane K) — I verified the entire parameter table including the k=4 steepness and the U(S-mT) step; it supplies the ONE observable that DESIGN_7_25 line 68/71 hangs the whole hub raid on and never specifies, and it cannot lie because it is generated by the same reserve arithmetic.
- Crowd sensing by contact count that turns DOWN your own recruitment emission (Lane I) — verified (5.6-fold, 0.055 vs 0.315, 45% after 10 contacts) including the paper's explicit statement that it downregulates a positive signal rather than emitting an inhibitory one; delivers what spec line 62 asks the kin field for using zero new fields and without pushing holding agents off ground they are meant to hold.
- Five-percent informed wave (Lane L) — verified thresholds (5% -> ~90% arrive, 10% -> no split, 2.5% -> fails); answers the unasked question of what a dispatched reinforcement actually KNOWS without violating the one-hop law, and converts spotter-killing from a binary counter into a graded one.
- Idle reserve with one-way replacement (Lane G) — verified in full (0.607 inactive, 20% removal, active replaced within a week, inactive never replaced); it is the mechanism that makes the spec's own thesis — 'containment, not extermination, is the practical strategy' — TRUE as a simulation consequence instead of a hope, with a discoverable breakpoint.
- Priority budget, not weighted sum (Lane I) — from the Reynolds primary, with the author's explicit argument that weighted averaging fails when requests oppose; it corrects DESIGN_7_25 line 205's literal wording ('keep-apart weighted above bunch-together'), supplies the missing 1/d^2 falloff, and kills the doorway-jitter stall the project will otherwise hit.
- Top-heavy depth grammar (Lane L) — every number verified against the BioOne excavation (half the area in the top quarter, -25 to -40% per decile, spacing peaking at 70-80% depth, single trunk, banded stores); it is a codeable generator for the single largest open design unit, and its inverted-veterancy rule is a real, non-obvious design gift.
- Flicker before failure (Lane K) — the only candidate in the fleet that specifies what the PLAYER PERCEIVES, with a stated lead time; it makes the invade window reachable on foot instead of a coin flip, and its 'variance rises ~5x while the mean stays flat' framing correctly identifies that the obvious indicator (wave size) is the wrong one.
- Sticky commit — the release level sits below the commit level (Lane H) — replaces the spec's per-agent 5 s stand-down timer (line 206) with a stateless second threshold that is cheaper, cannot chatter, and produces group-level memory a timer cannot; the honest flag that it extrapolates from a group-STRUCTURE transition rather than a commit/abandon decision is exactly the right disclosure.
- Two-choice dispatch over deliberately stale, per-gate-jittered demand (Lane K) — the Mitzenmacher result that shortest-queue is WORSE THAN RANDOM under even slight staleness is a genuine surprise, and it indicts a structure DESIGN_7_25 already has (a shared per-tick field update), predicting a named failure — thundering-herd oscillation between breaches — before it is built.

# The open questions

Answer each with a recommendation you would ship, its mechanism, what it
predicts and forbids, and your odds it survives a real playtest.

1. **The CORE as a system of agents.** The spec says the CORE is "an organ, not
   a single static NPC" -- a system of agents, with regeneration, that is a real
   game area to fight through. What are its parts? What does the player attack,
   in what order, and what does each kill actually change? The research offers
   two attack surfaces with opposite time constants (producers deep inside
   versus the dispatch machinery at the hub's own mouth), an anchor lattice
   where destroying the structure displaces producers rather than killing them,
   and caretaker agents that rebuild it. Do you take that shape, and how does it
   avoid becoming a boss fight with extra steps?

2. **Reserve economy.** Size, regeneration, dispatch latency, and what happens
   when demand outruns supply. The research proposes a closed-loop governor
   driven by how empty the depot is (`regen = base * (1 + K*(1 - stock/cap))`)
   that never reads Distress, Noise, or kill counts -- which would satisfy our
   decoupling rule by construction. It also proposes that under sustained drain
   the marrow ships degraded "runt" agents, making the reserve state legible off
   the enemies' bodies instead of through a HUD. Do you endorse both? What are
   the numbers?

3. **Core-kill as a status effect.** The director has ruled that destroying the
   CORE takes the reinforcement layer offline for **a couple of minutes** and is
   a tactical tool the player applies before assaulting a fortified area -- not a
   run-ending event. Given your answer to (2), what values make a couple of
   minutes fall out of the economy rather than being hardcoded as a timer? What
   should the player observe during the blackout, and at its end?

4. **Dispatch and crossing.** Pour cadence, aperture, how the mouth's exact
   position is chosen, whether a used gate becomes self-amplifying, and what
   makes a gate stop being used. Hard constraints: the connection carries NO
   scaling factor, the aperture is simply wide enough that agents walk through on
   normal AI, and there is NO special-casing around entry. The research proposes
   the spotter transmits a *polygon* and the marrow resolves the seam by argmax
   over local agitation, drawn from a patch inventory precomputed at world build
   whose sites never grow in number, only in size. Does that hold up?

5. **The two scout classes, vagrant and lacuna.** They have only ever been
   placeholders. What differentiates them mechanically -- movement, sensing,
   where each is dangerous? The research suggests one that creeps along
   boundaries and can sense through a sealed portal without crossing it, versus
   one that moves in the open; and a transmit split into a cheap interruptible
   handshake and an expensive rooted broadcast. What is the exposure window
   during transmit, and how does the player learn to recognize it?

6. **Held ground and garrisons.** The director has ruled garrisons are emergent:
   agents settle around a leader (e.g. a Warden) who fortifies whatever room he
   is in, and leader AI is deferred -- for now leaders do not know they have
   followers. **One research lane argues the leader concept should be deleted
   entirely**, with any room converting whoever stands in it once its
   Distress-over-time integral crosses a threshold, and settled agents becoming
   weak fighters but very loud emitters (so the player who reads threat ratings
   ignores exactly the unit he should kill first). Adjudicate: leaderless
   conversion, the director's leader-anchored version, or both composed?

7. **Signal damping and cloaking.** The player's counterplay is a pheromone
   scrubber that severely damps all chemo-signals, and a pod-linked cleaning
   robot that clears splatter, piles corpses for the pod's roots, and emits a
   couple of components as salvage. Doctrine: an uncloaked base eventually gets
   found and purged, and that is intended. How should damping work numerically
   so cloaking is a real strategic choice rather than a mandatory tax? Does a
   scrubbed region read as suspiciously quiet to the enemy -- a hole in the
   signal field that is itself a signal?

8. **Where the reinforcement stream becomes the fight.** The spec's intent is
   that for most of a run, standing and fighting reinforcements is a better use
   of ammo and time than hunting the source, and that a competent player manages
   how much aggro he draws. What makes the stream fun to fight rather than a
   chore to grind? What is the pacing shape of a single breach's pour, and how
   should it feel different from a fight the player started himself?

9. **Anything we have not asked.** Name the load-bearing assumption in this
   design that you think is most likely wrong.
