# Research packet: the marrow

Assembled for an external consult. Two things are inside:

1. **Sourced mechanism candidates** from a six-lane deep-research fleet. Each
   lane was required to actually fetch its sources (no writing from memory),
   walk links two hops deep, harvest quantitative detail, cite only what it
   fetched, and report dead ends. Every mechanism has been renamed into
   game-systems vocabulary; the source links are bare provenance.
2. **An adversarial critic's assessment of that research.** The critic
   independently re-fetched a sample of cited pages and checked the quoted
   figures against them. It confirmed most, and caught several real defects --
   a direct quotation attributed to a page that does not contain it, a rate
   derived by converting a stock fraction into a per-unit-time figure, a
   load-bearing ratio that is an uncited encyclopedia claim, and one lane that
   built its headline recommendation on an absence of evidence that a different
   lane had already filled with sources. Those are flagged INLINE below, on the
   candidates they indict.

**How to read this.** The candidates are inspiration, not authority. A flagged
number is not automatically wrong, but it must not be used as a tuning anchor
without saying that it is unverified. Where two lanes disagree on the same
quantity, both figures are given and neither has been adjudicated -- those

their own right.

The full game spec (`DESIGN_7_25.md`) is inlined separately in this consult and

# Research candidates

## LANE A — reserve depot economics and dispatch under demand

### A1. Depot-occupancy governor (production is driven by how EMPTY the depot is, not by how loud the alarm is) — with rebound overshoot
**What the source says.** The density-feedback study shows the production line is regulated by the standing stock in the depot itself, not by the alarm. Correlation between depot stock and production-line proliferation rate was R = −0.84 to −0.94 (P ≤ 0.01) for the top tier, −0.78 to −0.94 for the middle tier, −0.88 to −0.93 for the assembly tier — and it held across BOTH the draining and the refilling phase. A graded artificial drain (0.1–100 µg dose ladder) with NO alarm stimulus at all produced R = −0.89 (P ≤ 0.01) between drain depth and production rate at day 2 — i.e. emptying the depot alone ramps production with the alarm pathway entirely absent. Two demand-signal pathways with different shapes were separated: an alarm-driven spike (70 pg/ml → 2.5 ng/ml at 6 h, decaying to 0.7 ng/ml by day 2 — a ~36x spike that loses two-thirds of its height in under two days) and a deficit-driven pathway that is slower, sustained, and independent of the alarm pathway. Refill OVERSHOOTS: depot stock peaked at day 6 for a light drain and day 8 for a heavy drain, with the heavy drain producing 'even greater rebound' — above baseline — and full return to steady state at ~day 12, roughly twice the drain window. Production-tier proliferation peaked at day 1 and was back to naive levels by days 4–6, i.e. the ramp finishes long before the stock does.
**Proposed game rule.** Replace 'finite reserve that regenerates slowly' with a two-term closed-loop governor on the CORE:

  regen_rate = base * (1 + K_deficit * (1 - stock/cap)) + alarm_term
  alarm_term = A * exp(-t_since_peak / tau_alarm), tau_alarm ≈ 25 s
  K_deficit ≈ 2.5, so an empty depot produces ~3.5x the rate of a full one.

Critically: the deficit term needs NO knowledge of the player. The CORE never reads Distress. It reads its own shelf. This keeps the CORE decoupled from the signal fields exactly as IMPLEMENTATION CORRECTIONS demands, and it means the reserve economy still works if every spotter is dead.

Add the overshoot: once stock crosses back above ~70% of cap, the governor does not stop at cap — it runs to overshoot_cap ≈ 1.35 * cap and then bleeds back down to cap over a window ~2x the length of the drain. Overshoot stock is spent first (LIFO), so it manifests as a fatter-than-normal wave.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: (a) a player who drains a breach to ~40% and then walks away returns 3–5 minutes later to a depot that is fuller than it started, and the next wave is measurably bigger — half-draining is punished; (b) production ramp rate is observable as wave INTERVAL shortening (breach re-call cadence tightens) before wave SIZE changes, because the ramp peaks long before the stock refills; (c) the deficit ramp still runs when the player is nowhere near a spotter. FORBIDS: a constant reinforcement trickle; forbids reserve level being a pure function of player aggression. VERIFIABLE IN A RUN: log stock/cap and regen_rate per tick; the stock trace after a heavy drain must show a peak above 1.0 and a decay back, not an asymptotic approach to 1.0.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 specifies only 'a finite reserve that regenerates slowly.' That is an open-loop constant and it makes farming a breach strictly free once you can out-DPS the trickle. The governor makes the reserve a control system with a punish-the-half-measure shape, and — the load-bearing part — it derives that behaviour from the depot's own state, so the CORE stays fully decoupled from the spotters and the signal fields.
**Source.** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0019957

### A2. Depot sizing law — the reserve is held at ~28:1 against the standing field force, and the outage from killing the production organ is DERIVED from the finished-goods queue length, not chosen
**What the source says.** Encyclopedia-depth source gives the standing distribution across the three compartments as 28 : 1 : 25 (held in depot : in transit : already deployed in terrain). The kinetics review gives the depot holding ~6 × 10^11 units against a total in-transit pool of 65 × 10^7 units/kg (~4.5 × 10^10 for a 70 kg body) — a ~13:1 depot-to-in-transit ratio — with full-rate output of 5 × 10^10 to 10 × 10^10 units/day, i.e. the depot holds 6–12 DAYS of full-rate production. The finished-goods queue (the post-production, pre-dispatch holding stage) has a transit time of 4–6 days. The surge review gives the whole pipeline as 10 days at steady state. So the finished-goods queue is 40–60% of the total pipeline. Individual unit lifetime once deployed is short: half-life 6–8 h, disappearance half-life ~7 h.
**Proposed game rule.** Hard numbers for the marrow, all derived from one anchor. Let S = the standing live-enemy count the world targets (say S ≈ 40).

- reserve_cap ≈ 28 * S ≈ 1100 dispatch-units. Roughly 12 minutes of continuous full-rate output.
- base regen: cap / 12 min → ~1.5 units/s. Full-rate emergency output ~3.5x that (see candidate 1) → ~5 units/s, so the depot can sustain ~40 live agents against a player killing ~5/s, and no faster.
- Deployed-agent decay: an agent that has been out of the breach and out of contact for a long time should retire back through the gate rather than standing in an empty room forever — half-life ~7 h of a 10-day pipeline maps to ~3% of the outage window, i.e. ~8 s of no-contact before a lone agent starts drifting home. (This is already half-specified as 'Stand-down ~5 s' in the appendix; make stand-down route to the nearest live breach.)
- THE OUTAGE IS DERIVED. Killing the CORE destroys the production line but not the finished-goods queue. That queue is 40–60% of a 10-day pipeline. Map the 10-day pipeline to a ~4-minute in-game rebuild and the queue is ~2 minutes. That is EXACTLY the 'couple of minutes' the director specified — so implement it as: core_kill sets stock = −(0.5 * cap) and disables regen until a rebuild timer (~4 min of pipeline) elapses at 1/1 rate, with the first breach re-opening the instant stock crosses 0, i.e. ~2 min. The number stops being arbitrary and becomes a consequence of pipeline length.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a full drain of a fresh depot requires ~10–12 minutes of sustained maximum-rate engagement — a player CANNOT drain it in one firefight, which forces the 'establish a foothold and grind it' play the director wants; and the post-core-kill blackout measures ~2 min ±20 s in a real run without anyone tuning a magic constant. FORBIDS: a reserve small enough to exhaust incidentally; forbids the outage length being a free parameter someone can quietly re-tune.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec asks for 'finite', 'slow-regen', 'deep negative', and 'a couple of minutes', and says 'tune deep-negative depth × regen rate to that spec' — i.e. it hands us two free parameters and a target. This candidate collapses both free parameters into one anchor (pipeline length) and shows the director's 'couple of minutes' is the self-consistent answer, plus it supplies the reserve_cap number the spec leaves blank.
**Source.** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrophil

### A3. Demand-driven quality collapse — sustained pressure makes the depot ship half-finished agents, and you can READ the depot's balance sheet off the enemies' bodies
**What the source says.** Under sustained load the pipeline is compressed, not merely accelerated: total maturation drops from 10 days at steady state to 48–72 h (a ~3.5–5x compression), and the final finishing stage specifically drops from 24 h to 12–16 h. The shipped population's composition changes accordingly: half-finished forms rise from a normal <5% to as much as 45% of everything in transit. Those half-finished units are measurably worse — 40% reduced effectiveness against targets — and are internally lopsided (payload occupies 67% of internal volume vs 32% in finished units, i.e. all ammunition and no machinery). 35% of the production floor deviates into an alternate emergency line. The surge review independently confirms the surge output is 'functionally immature ... with impaired' navigation and target handling. The switch is fast at the top: the emergency production program's master regulator rises 8-fold within 30 min of the alarm and binds 1,352 sites within 2 h.
**Proposed game rule.** Wave COMPOSITION becomes a function of recent drain rate, and it is the primary HUD-free readout of the reserve economy.

  half_finished_fraction = clamp(0.05 + 1.0 * (drain_rate_ema / base_regen - 1), 0.05, 0.45)

A 'runt' variant of each Corrupted-Testator class: −40% damage, degraded gradient following (samples the field at a coarser cadence so it visibly wanders and mis-takes doorways), takes second-best routes far more often, but costs the depot only ~1/4 of a finished unit — so under heavy drain the depot ships MORE bodies of WORSE quality per unit of reserve. Silhouette must read as unfinished at a glance (asymmetric, over-loaded with ordnance, under-built in the limbs — the 67%/32% ratio is a literal art direction note: all gun, no chassis).

Also implement the 30-minute-to-8-fold switch as a fast composition step: the mix flips within ~5 s of drain_rate crossing threshold, but flips BACK slowly (~45 s), so quality lags recovery.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: (a) after ~2 minutes of holding a firebase against a breach, ≥30% of arrivals are visibly runts and the player can SEE that he is winning the economy war with no UI; (b) runt-heavy waves are more numerous and less dangerous per body, so the fight changes texture rather than just scaling; (c) if the player disengages, quality recovers slower than quantity — the wave after a lull is small but sharp. FORBIDS: a fixed wave composition table; forbids the reserve economy being invisible to a player who never opens a menu.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says nothing at all about what comes out of a breach changing over time — the reinforcement stream is composition-static. This makes the reserve level diegetically legible (a standing project law: the player should be able to intuit how the system talks to itself), and it gives the 'stand and fight the stream' loop, which the spec calls 'the fight' for most of a run, an actual arc instead of a flat treadmill.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12722637/

### A4. Two-gate dispatch — flow through a breach is a ratio of a dominant HOLD pressure against a modulatory PUSH pressure, and the alarm moves both at once
**What the source says.** Dispatch is not a spawn timer; it is a tug-of-war between two opposed signals, and they are NOT symmetric. Removing the PUSH signal leaves agents stuck in the depot: 65.3% ± 7.6% retained vs only 25.0% ± 3.5% reaching the field (P < 0.0001), a dispatch index falling from 1.84% ± 0.32% to 0.57% ± 0.18% (P = 0.02) — a ~3x retention penalty. Under an emergency demand signal at 1.5 h, normal agents mobilised 2.3-fold while PUSH-deficient agents showed NO significant increase — 'maximal responses require [PUSH] signalling.' Chemically cutting the HOLD signal alone gave a 3.8 ± 1.2-fold mobilisation within 1 HOUR, but completely failed to move PUSH-deficient agents (dispatch index 13.0% ± 3.27% vs 0.53% ± 0.21%, P = 0.02) — so cutting HOLD does nothing without PUSH. Directly activating PUSH gave 3.8 ± 0.5-fold, but only 'partially' in HOLD-deficient agents. Removing BOTH gives constitutive, unstoppable mobilisation identical to removing HOLD alone: HOLD is dominant. The alarm signal moves both terms simultaneously and in opposite directions — it suppresses the HOLD emitters (the depot floor) while inducing the PUSH emitters (the gate structures themselves) 2.7-fold ± 0.3.
**Proposed game rule.** Model the marrow's dispatch as two scalars on the hub, never as a rate constant:

  hold  = HOLD_base * (1 - alarm_norm)        # depot floor, dominant
  push  = PUSH_base * (1 + 1.7 * alarm_norm)  # emitted by the BREACH MOUTH itself
  dispatch_rate = clamp(push / max(hold, eps) - 1, 0, max_rate) * min(stock, ...)

Because the alarm divides one and multiplies the other, response is superlinear — 3.8x within one 'hour' unit maps to a hard surge, matching 'reinforcements surge and climb.' Key structural consequence: PUSH is emitted BY THE BREACH, not by the player's noise. So a breach that has been open longer has a higher local push and pours faster; a freshly opened breach trickles. This is why multiple simultaneous breaches must split evenly (spec) but the OLDEST breach should still be the hot one.

Player counterplay maps cleanly: the pheromone scrubber attacks PUSH (modulatory) so it throttles the surge but never stops the trickle — because HOLD is dominant and only the CORE controls HOLD. Killing the CORE is the only thing that stops flow entirely. That asymmetry is a designed, sourced reason why damping cannot substitute for invasion.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: (a) throttling a breach with a damper cuts surge rate by ~60–75% but leaves a floor trickle that never reaches zero — a scrubbed room is quieter, never safe; (b) breach output ramps over its lifetime rather than being flat from the first second; (c) two breaches of different ages pour at different rates even with the same reserve behind them. FORBIDS: a breach that spawns at a fixed cadence; forbids damping being a hard off-switch.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has NO dispatch model — breaches open and pour until the reserve runs out. This supplies the actual flow equation, explains why the countermeasure building is a throttle and not a kill switch, and gives the multiple-breach case a principled asymmetry (age-dependent output) instead of a flat even split.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2898597/

### A5. Three-tier dispatch latency ladder — and the counter-intuitive LULL that precedes the pour
**What the source says.** Latency after a demand signal is not one number, it is a ladder. Transit through the depot itself is ~10 min; the finished-goods queue is 4–6 days; the production line is 10 days at steady state. The demand-signal review gives release as 'rapid (within 4–24 h) and sustained' — so first-arrival latency and steady-flow latency differ by ~6x. The alarm signal's own dynamic range: baseline <30–163 pg/mL, engaged 30–3,199 pg/mL, ~20-fold. Dose sets the ceiling: peak response was 5-fold at low dose and 15-fold at high dose. AND THE SURPRISE: after the demand signal is injected, the field count first DROPS — a nadir of 0.38 ± 0.09 × 10^9/L at 30 minutes — BEFORE it rises, peaking at 6 h and returning to near-baseline by 24 h. The system pulls agents off the field and re-stages them before it floods.
**Proposed game rule.** Three dispatch tiers behind one breach, each drawing from a different bucket:

  T0 GUARD RING  — agents already standing at the hub's gate. Arrive 2–4 s after the breach opens. Small hard cap (~6). This is the burst that punishes standing in the doorway.
  T1 DEPOT STOCK — the bulk. First arrival ~15 s, steady flow by ~25 s, sustained. Drawn from `stock`.
  T2 FRESH BUILD — only reachable once the governor has ramped. First arrival ~2.5 min. Always half-finished (candidate 3). This is what a player who camps a breach for four minutes is actually fighting.

And implement the pre-pour lull, scaled from the 30 min nadir in a 24 h cycle → ~3 s in a ~40 s wave cycle: for ~3 s immediately after a spotter completes Transmit, ALL Adversary agents within 2 hops of the transmit point disengage and re-stage toward the breach site. Local pressure visibly drops right before it spikes.

Also: cap wave magnitude by the disturbance that called it. A quiet call gets a 5x wave, a loud call gets 15x — dose sets ceiling, so 'how much noise you made' is not just whether you get a wave but how big it can possibly be.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: an experienced player learns the ~3 s lull as the tell that a call landed and uses it to reposition or to sprint the transmit site — a skill expression that emerges from the economy rather than from a UI warning. Also predicts that the first 4 seconds of a breach are disproportionately dangerous (guard ring) and that minute 3 onward is disproportionately easy but endless. FORBIDS: a monotonic call→ramp→flood curve; forbids wave size being independent of how the call was provoked.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's spotter state machine ends at one state, 'Pour', with no internal structure, and gives the breach no arrival-latency model at all. The lull in particular is the kind of rule nobody invents at a desk — the system going QUIETER as a reinforcement tell is far better than a klaxon, and it is exactly the 'intuit how the system communicates with itself' payoff the Adversary constitution asks for.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2852428/

### A6. The alarm outlives the supply — and a loud enough decoy drains the depot with no fight at all
**What the source says.** Under a 100x-heavier load, the depot fell to 'less than 10% of the baseline number' by day 3, while the demand signal 'remained elevated despite reserve exhaustion' — the system kept screaming at full volume with an empty shelf ('strong immediate induction, but neutrophil depletion was not averted'). Worse, 50–70% of the remaining depot stock was destroyed IN PLACE at days 2–3 rather than being dispatched. Under the survivable load the depot was never depleted at all and stock was actually ELEVATED at days 7 and 9 (the overshoot of candidate 1); outcome was 100% survival. And the finding with the most game leverage: a pure decoy — an alarm ligand with NO actual threat behind it — caused >80% depot reduction on its own, with up to 40% of depot stock lost in place, and converted a fully survivable engagement into a lethal one. Target counts in the terrain ran one to two orders of magnitude higher when the depot was drained, and correlated negatively with remaining depot stock.
**Proposed game rule.** Two rules.

(A) EXHAUSTION HAS A TELL. When stock hits 0, spotters do NOT stop calling — the spec already forbids spotter→hub coupling, so a spotter cannot know. It transmits, the hub reaches through, and the breach ABORTS: the mouth opens, flickers, and seals in ~1.5 s with nothing coming out. That failed-breach animation IS the player's read that the reserve is dry and the invasion window is open. It costs nothing architecturally because it falls straight out of the required decoupling.

(B) DECOY DRAIN. Reserve is spent on DISPATCH, not on kills. Anything that produces a call spends reserve — including calls the player provoked deliberately somewhere he is not. Concretely: a player who runs up Noise in a far corner (a sacrificial turret cluster, a deliberately loud firefight in an empty wing, or the flank-breach turtle bait the spec already describes) drains the depot without ever engaging the arrivals. Add a small in-place loss term too: when dispatch demand exceeds available flow for a sustained period, a fraction (target ~15%/min, sourced from the 50–70%-over-a-day in-place loss) of remaining stock is lost outright — the depot degrades faster than it is consumed once it is under-supplying.

(C) Corollary the source forces on us: the ENGAGED depot is worse off than the untouched one, but the LIGHTLY-engaged depot ends up richer than baseline. Light pressure feeds the enemy. There is no safe amount of poking.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: (a) a run log will show reserve reaching 0 while spotter call attempts continue and breach-abort events fire — the abort count is a directly observable metric; (b) a player who spends 3 minutes making noise on dead ground and then charges will find the reserve materially lower than a player who fought the same duration head-on, because the in-place loss term compounds; (c) light, intermittent harassment leaves the depot ABOVE where it started. FORBIDS: reserve being a pure function of enemies killed; forbids exhaustion being a silent state the player has to guess at.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec makes exhaustion silent ('when the reserve is exhausted it stops opening portals') and then requires the player to notice the invasion window — but gives him nothing to notice. This supplies the tell for free, and adds a whole non-combat strategic lane (drain by decoy) that fits the director's 'a truly advanced player will manipulate how the system communicates with itself' goal. It also gives a mechanical reason why timid poking is punished, reinforcing the anti-turtle design.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2678456/

### A7. The damper REDISTRIBUTES pressure, it does not delete it — the sourced reason the scrubber cannot be a turtle button
**What the source says.** The counter-intuitive result buried in the dispatch tug-of-war study: agents lacking the PUSH signal are cell-intrinsically STUCK in the depot (65.3% retained), but the whole organism shows the OPPOSITE — an excess of agents in circulation — 'due to impaired tissue emigration creating a feedback loop.' Blocking deployment locally did not lower total force; it raised total output, because undeployed stock feeds back into the governor. The two emitters are also spatially separate and oppositely regulated: the HOLD emitter is the depot floor, the PUSH emitter is the gate structure, and the alarm suppresses the first while inducing the second 2.7-fold ± 0.3. Damping is therefore an attack on ONE emitter at ONE location, never on the system's total output.
**Proposed game rule.** The pheromone scrubber suppresses local PUSH inside its radius. It does NOT reduce global dispatch. Implementation: dispatch demand suppressed inside a scrubbed area is added to a redistribution budget that is re-issued to the highest-eligible unscrubbed polygon within N hops — pressure MOVES. Combined with the deficit governor (candidate 1), a heavily scrubbed base also means the depot's consumption falls, its stock climbs, and the eventual unscrubbed contact is fought against a fuller depot.

So the honest scrubber contract: a scrubbed base is quiet and stays hidden (which the spec already wants — 'unscrubbed bases eventually getting found and purged is intended'), at the cost of (i) heavier pressure on every forward position you have NOT scrubbed and (ii) a fatter depot behind whatever you eventually attack. Scrubbing is a cloak, not a shield.

Second, sharper use, from candidate 9's arrest mechanic: because damping is spatially local, a damper placed AWAY from the base — on the approach — makes arriving agents lose the gradient and stall in the wrong polygon. The scrubber is offensively useful as a route-breaker, not just defensively as a cloak.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: instrument total dispatch-units-per-minute globally; adding scrubbers must leave that number flat or HIGHER while local arrivals inside the scrubbed radius fall sharply. If global dispatch drops when scrubbers go up, the redistribution is not wired and the build is a turtle button. That is a crisp pass/fail test on a real run. FORBIDS: scrubber count monotonically reducing enemy activity; forbids a stack of scrubbers being a win condition.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec gives the scrubber only an upside — 'severely damps ALL chemo-signals in its area' — with no systemic cost, which makes stacking them strictly dominant and directly undercuts the anti-turtle intent stated three sections earlier. This is the missing cost term, and it comes with a one-number verification test.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2898597/

### A8. The crossing gate runs both ways at roughly 1-in-4, and reverse traffic is what defines the invasion window
**What the source says.** Measured directly: under stimulus, '26.4% of all crossing events' were in the REVERSE direction (n = 6, P = 0.040) — reverse traffic is not an exotic edge case, it is a quarter of all gate throughput. The gate seam only becomes reverse-passable in a window: seam-protein loss was detectable 'as early as 30 min' after the local signal, sustained up to 8 h, and back to baseline by 24 h — so reverse passability is ~33% of the gate's active lifetime, opening after a delay, closing on its own. In a second model, up to 80% of agents recruited to a site went back toward the origin within 24 h, with only 3% lost in place — reversal, not attrition, was the DOMINANT clearance route, so a site emptying out does not mean you killed anything. Reversed agents come back CHANGED: a durable altered profile (one adhesion marker high, one navigation receptor low), a higher output capability, and they go on to cause damage at distant sites they were never dispatched to. Agents that had already engaged a target stayed put — engagement suppresses reversal.
**Proposed game rule.** (a) Losing agents ROUTE HOME rather than dying. Target ~25% of a breach's lifetime throughput as inbound-to-hub. Trigger: an agent below a health threshold, or one knocked back off its approach (the universal knockback in the combat canon becomes a routing input, not just a stagger), heads for the nearest live breach and crosses. Agents currently in contact with the player do NOT reverse — engagement pins them, which is exactly the source's finding and is also good combat feel.

(b) THE INVASION WINDOW IS A GATE PROPERTY, NOT A SPECIAL CASE. A breach becomes reverse-passable only after it has been open ~1/3 of its lifetime and stops being reverse-passable near the end. This gives the player's crossing a principled, learnable timing rule that is the SAME rule the enemy uses — no player-specific carve-out, which the spec's decoupling law demands.

(c) RETURNED VETERANS. An agent that reverse-crossed re-enters the depot with a 'tissue-experienced' tag: distinct silhouette detail, higher output, and a counter-bias toward whatever damage type nearly killed it. It redeploys through a DIFFERENT breach. This is the cheapest possible implementation of the appendix's 'Adaptive counter-biasing' and 'Evidence relay' — the evidence is carried by the body that survived, no messenger class needed.

(d) Consequence for the containment reward: a room emptying out is ambiguous. 80%-reversal means the player must distinguish 'I killed them' from 'they went home,' which is precisely the uncertainty that makes cordoning feel like real work.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: on a recorded run, ~20–30% of breach crossings are outbound; the player will visibly see wounded enemies break contact and run for the hole; kill-count will diverge from spawn-count by roughly that fraction and the gap is NOT a bug. FORBIDS: enemies fighting to the death; forbids the two-way breach being a player-only affordance; forbids a special-cased 'player may enter' timer.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec declares the breach two-way and lets the player cross back, but nothing else ever uses the return direction — so two-wayness is a player affordance with a bespoke timer. This makes reverse traffic a live enemy behaviour the player observes long before he thinks about invading, turns knockback into a routing mechanic, and delivers the appendix's counter-biasing/evidence-relay goals with zero new subsystems.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4504024/

### A9. ARREST is what makes a wave effective — more aggression from the reinforcements makes them WORSE, and this is why waves must form a cordon instead of a funnel
**What the source says.** The headline result inverts the obvious assumption. Agents with their self-limiting brake removed travelled roughly twice as far (displacement 1.7–3.2 mm vs 1.0–1.5 mm over 4 h), moved faster in the late phase (90–180 min) where controls had already arrested, formed LARGER clusters, and dominated controls in head-to-head competition — and they were WORSE at their job: significantly higher target counts, 'higher counts of free [targets] outside of the clusters and a significant increase of [target] growth.' The stated cause: unbraked agents 'lacked arrest phases and migrated at high speed out of clusters again,' so the containment ring kept breaking — 'a breach in the swarm-dependent barrier.' The brake is receptor fatigue on the agents' OWN relay signal: the very signal that recruits them, once locally saturated, shuts their movement down. Arrest is defined operationally as instantaneous speed < 2 µm/min. Cluster stabilisation happens 30–75 min after the event; early recruitment resolves in the first 40 min; test geometry used 130 µm patches spaced 400 µm apart.
**Proposed game rule.** Give every dispatched agent an ARREST state driven by the field it is itself emitting into. Also: a garrison is just agents in permanent arrest state — the spec already says 'a garrison is emergent, not an object,' and arrest IS the emergence rule. No new object needed.

### A10. Signal priority is a DECAY-RATE difference on the agent's own accumulator, not a weight table
**What the source says.** How an agent choosing between two overlapping gradients decides which to obey: the high-priority (true-target) signal produces a response that stays elevated for MORE THAN 2 MINUTES after peak, while the low-priority (relay/recruitment) signal decays back to baseline in ~20–40 seconds after peak — at one tested concentration the relay response lasted only ~10 seconds. Both signals are read through the same summation; only the decay differs. The decay is governed by exactly two phosphorylation sites on the relay receptor's tail, and mutating BOTH (T308A/S310A) abolishes prioritisation entirely — the agents then move with 'random directionality' in competing gradients. So priority is an emergent consequence of two time constants and nothing else. Independently, movement style differs: high-priority guidance produces straighter paths with higher directional persistence; relay guidance produces more direction changes and lower persistence — and that difference survives the decay mutation, so path style and priority are SEPARATE knobs.
**Proposed game rule.** Move part of the signal model out of the polygon and into the agent. Each agent keeps a small per-field internal accumulator with a per-field decay constant, samples its own polygon and the one-hop neighbours (per the spec's constraint), and steers on the SUM of accumulators. Set:

  tau(Distress) ≈ 2.0 s, tau(Noise) ≈ 2.0 s   # target fields, persistent
  tau(Muster) ≈ 0.3 s, tau(Trail) ≈ 0.4 s     # relay fields, fast-decaying
  tau(Garrison-reach) ≈ 0.5 s

No priority table, no weights. Relay fields dominate when the agent is far and the relay is being actively re-broadcast; target fields win as soon as the agent is close enough to sample them directly, because they persist across sampling gaps and the relay does not. Then set path style INDEPENDENTLY: steering on a target field uses high directional persistence (straight, committed); steering on a relay field uses low persistence (weaving, searching). That is the visible difference between an enemy hunting you and an enemy answering a call, and it costs one extra float.

The falsification test is handed to us: set all tau equal and the agents must visibly lose directionality in overlapping fields. If they don't, the field stack is not actually driving navigation and something else is.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: enemies answering a Muster call move in searching, weaving paths; the same enemy switches to a straight committed line the instant it has direct Distress/Noise — a legible behavioural switch with no state machine. Also predicts a specific A/B: equalise the tau values and directional coherence in mixed-field polygons must collapse measurably. FORBIDS: a hand-maintained field priority matrix; forbids priority being a function of signal MAGNITUDE (a very loud relay signal must still lose to a faint target signal).
**Beyond the current spec.** The appendix's field table specifies spread and fade PER ROOM and notes Muster 'fades VERY FAST' — but there is no mechanism by which fast fade produces subordination, and no per-agent adaptation at all. This makes the table's fade column load-bearing, delivers 'high-priority overrides' without a priority system, splits path-style from priority as two independent knobs, and comes with its own built-in falsification experiment.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10680494/

### B1. Two-key gate: the wall calls you over, but only a committing agent can unlock the seam — and the unlock is CACHED at the seam
**What the source says.** A study tracking individual mobile agents crossing a two-layer barrier under flow found two distinct signal molecules, ~90% identical in structure, doing completely non-redundant sequential jobs. Signal A is emitted by the BARRIER itself and governs approach: blocking it cut adhesion and along-wall crawling by >62% and cut completed crossings by ~84%. Signal B is emitted by the CROSSING AGENTS THEMSELVES, released only once an agent has latched onto barrier-bound signal A; blocking B had no effect at all on approach or crawling but produced 'almost total inhibition' of actual crossings, and raised the ABORTED-crossing rate from ~2% of crawling agents to ~15%. Blocking both together was no worse than blocking either alone — proof the steps are sequential, not parallel. Critically, the barrier carries a non-signalling receptor concentrated exactly at the seams between wall panels whose only job is to RETAIN signal B, forming a junctional 'depot'. Remove that depot and >40% of agents that entered a seam aborted and reversed back into the transport compartment (vs <10% in controls); total throughput fell ~48%. One complete crossing of the inner layer took ~6 min. Source describes this as a self-guided response: the crosser makes the signal that opens its own exit.
**Proposed game rule.** Give every breach TWO scalar gauges instead of one open/closed flag. (1) APPROACH — written into the target polygon by the marrow when it accepts a spotter's transmitted position; it governs whether agents on the hub side will path to the mouth at all. (2) KEY — deposited AT THE MOUTH by each agent that actually commits to a crossing, decaying with the standard field decay. Rules: an agent may only begin a crossing if KEY > 0 OR it pays the full slow first-crosser cost (~4x normal transit). Each completed crossing adds +1 KEY (cap ~6). While KEY > 0, transit is fast. Abort rule: an agent mid-crossing when KEY hits 0 aborts and returns to the hub side — set baseline abort at 2%, rising to 15% when KEY is starved. Player countermeasure: the pheromone scrubber does not close the breach, it destroys the KEY cache — with the cache gone, ~40% of agents entering the mouth reverse back into the hub and total pour rate drops ~48%. That is the scrubber's real mechanical identity in this system.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a first-agent-through that is visibly slower than all following agents; a pour that accelerates for the first ~6 arrivals then plateaus; a measurable ~50% drop in arrivals-per-minute when a scrubber is placed adjacent to a live breach, WITHOUT the breach closing; visible reverse traffic (agents walking back in) under scrubber pressure. FORBIDS: killing the very first crosser being merely 'one kill' — it must reset the gate to slow mode; also forbids a scrubber acting as an off-switch. VERIFIABLE IN A RUN: log arrivals-per-minute vs KEY value; expect a monotone relationship and expect first-crossing latency ≈ 4x steady-state latency.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 has the breach as a binary hole with a 45s uncrossed timer and a reserve gate. It has no notion of throughput RAMP, no abort mechanic, no directionality, and — most importantly — the pheromone scrubber currently has no defined interaction with breaches at all (it only damps chemo-signals generically). This gives the scrubber a specific, legible, numerically tunable effect on the reinforcement layer, and gives the player a way to REVERSE a pour rather than only fight it.

### B2. Site selection by local wall agitation, not central choice — and the first crossing makes that exact site hotter
**What the source says.** High-resolution tracking of agents crossing a barrier made of tiled panels found that crossings do not happen randomly along the seams. Agents choose seams that show elevated local membrane agitation (dynamic protrusions ~2.5 µm tall, flap- or finger-shaped, present on ONE of the two panels flanking the seam and not the other). ~50% of agents crossed immediately on reaching a seam; the other ~50% crawled ALONG the seam for at least 20 s hunting for a better spot, and ~40% changed direction at the seam, exiting underneath the neighbouring panel rather than the one they arrived at. Sites where agents actually exited had significantly higher agitation than sites where the same agents crawled past without exiting (p=0.0305). The decisive finding: agitation at a site increased SIGNIFICANTLY AFTER THE FIRST AGENT CROSSED (p=0.0006), and subsequent agents reused that same exit site. Suppressing the actin-branching machinery that generates the agitation cut crossings by ~50-60% (p=0.0288, p=0.0079). Artificially forcing local agitation raised crossings ~40-50% (p=0.0483) and raised the NUMBER of active seams per panel (p=0.0035). One crossing completes in ~120 s. Also: short exposure to flow (5-20 min) raised agitation, but sustained exposure (30-60 min) returned it to baseline.
**Proposed game rule.** The spotter transmits a POLYGON, not a point. The marrow then resolves the actual mouth position by sampling every candidate seam (portal edge / wall segment) in that polygon and picking argmax of a per-seam AGITATION scalar. Agitation seeds from local signal values (Noise + Distress on that edge's neighbours) plus a small per-seam noise term, so the same call in the same polygon does not always cut the same hole. Then: every completed crossing adds +agitation to THAT seam (decaying ~30s half-life), so the chosen site self-amplifies and stays chosen — one hot mouth, not a spreading rash. Give arriving agents the 50/50 split: half commit at the mouth immediately, half sidestep along the wall up to ~2s before emerging, and ~40% emerge facing a different direction than the mouth normal (this alone kills the 'conga line out of a doorway' read the director forbade). Sustained pressure at one seam should decay its agitation back toward baseline after ~45-60s of continuous use, forcing the marrow to re-resolve to a different seam in the same polygon — the mouth WALKS.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: two calls on the same polygon in one run produce mouths at different wall segments; a mouth in heavy use drifts to a neighbouring segment after ~1 minute; killing agents before any of them completes a crossing leaves the site cold and the next call re-rolls elsewhere. FORBIDS: a central 'pick the spot nearest the player' rule; forbids the mouth being a fixed property of the polygon; forbids all agents streaming out along the mouth normal in single file. VERIFIABLE: log seam-id per crossing; expect a heavy-tailed distribution (one seam takes most crossings within a window) but a moving mode across the run.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says the hub 'reaches through and opens the breach at the transmitted spot' — a point chosen centrally. This replaces that with local field-value site selection plus positive feedback, which is exactly the open sub-question ('how the exit SITE is selected by local signal on the wall rather than chosen centrally'). It also supplies the anti-logjam behaviour the director demanded WITHOUT special-casing entry: the 40%-turn and 50%-sidestep rules disperse the exit naturally.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8437435/

### B3. Fixed patch inventory: the number of possible gate sites never changes — only their SIZE — and the agents that use one widen it by contact
**What the source says.** Mapping of a barrier's outer shell across five different terrain types found pre-existing THIN PATCHES where the structural material is deposited at only ~19-31% of average intensity. These patches are not rare and not random: they cover 10.3±1.0% of the wall surface in one terrain and 18.3±2.8% in another. Median patch area is 8±1 µm² in one terrain and 28±2 µm² in another, with a heavy tail (range ~1-74 µm², up to ~192 µm²); in the tight terrain 80% of patches fall in 1-10 µm², in the loose terrain only 35.4±8.9% do. The patches align spatially with gaps in the shell layer behind them (9±2 µm² and 17±3 µm² respectively) and with the seams between wall panels in front — a three-layer aligned corridor. Under sustained load, mean patch area grows +78.7% and +82.1% (two different structural components), and the fraction of patches larger than 10 µm² rises from ~15.4% to 29.3-46.4%. The companion study measured the shell gaps directly: 9.70±0.30 µm² unstimulated vs 16.98±0.43 µm² stimulated (~75% enlargement). Decisively: gaps that had an agent in them were LARGER than gaps that did not; removing >89% of the mobile agents left the gaps small; and agents held back so they could not physically touch the shell produced NO extra widening. The survey review states the rule flatly: under load the gaps 'increase in size, but not in number.'
**Proposed game rule.** Precompute, at world build, a fixed PATCH INVENTORY per polygon: sample its wall perimeter and mark 10-18% of its length as candidate patch segments, sized from a heavy-tailed distribution (80% small, a rare few 5-10x larger). This inventory NEVER grows during play. Breach aperture width = current size of the chosen patch. Aperture governs SIMULTANEOUS crossing slots: small patch = 1 agent at a time (drip), large patch = 3-4 abreast (surge). Growth law: sustained call pressure on a patch grows it ~+75-80% over its baseline toward a per-patch cap; ADDITIONALLY each agent that physically completes a crossing adds a small increment — but ONLY on contact, i.e. only completed crossings widen it, never mere pressure. Corollary the sources force on us: kill agents while they are IN the aperture and the widening stops dead. So the player's optimal play against a fresh breach is to stand ON the mouth and deny completions, keeping it a 1-wide drip; letting even a few through converts it to a 3-wide surge.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a breach that starts as a trickle and becomes a flood only if the player lets agents through; aperture width is a visible, readable property (the hole literally gets wider); a polygon's set of possible mouth locations is stable across an entire run and learnable by the player. FORBIDS: a breach spawning extra mouths under pressure (pressure must never increase mouth COUNT, only size); forbids infinite widening (per-patch cap); forbids aperture growth in a polygon where the player kills every crosser at the mouth. VERIFIABLE: log aperture width vs cumulative completed crossings; expect width flat when completions ≈ 0 even under maximum call pressure.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says only 'ensure the breach is wide enough' and treats aperture as a static geometric fix for log-jamming. This makes aperture a dynamic, player-contestable resource with a pre-seeded map geography, and answers the open question 'do agents squeeze through one at a time or widen the aperture' with a concrete answer: BOTH, sequentially, and the transition is the player's to prevent.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2797906

### B4. Two-stage gate where the SECOND stage is the slow one — the throughput cap lives behind the mouth, not at it
**What the source says.** Timed tracking of agents crossing a layered barrier found the two layers take wildly different times. Getting through the inner panel layer takes ~4-7 minutes. Getting through the outer shell takes far longer: ~20 minutes of crawling along the shell's structural elements hunting for a gap, plus ~11 minutes to actually squeeze through it, with total wall-breach times of 15-40 minutes. The outer shell is explicitly the rate-limiting step — 'up to 40 minutes compared to 4 minutes required for penetration of the [inner layer].' ~70% of the observed breaches used gaps in the 1-52 µm² size range. A separate two-layer reconstruction found that crossing the first layer LICENSES the second: agents that had never crossed a layer got through the outer layer at 8.8%, whereas agents that had just crossed the inner layer got through at 19.2% — a ~2.2x improvement, attributed to the crossing act itself upregulating the agent's grip hardware. Single inner layer alone passes 63.7%; the two-layer stack passes 37.7%.
**Proposed game rule.** Model the breach as MOUTH + ANTECHAMBER, not a single teleport. Scaling the 4-7 : 20-40 minute ratio (~1:5) into game seconds: an agent takes ~1.5s to appear in the antechamber (a shallow visible pocket on the level side of the mouth) and ~7.5s more to work its way out of the antechamber into the contested polygon. The antechamber has the aperture's slot count, so it is a queue with a hard service rate — this IS the pour rate limiter and it should be the single number we tune, not a spawn cooldown. The antechamber is fully visible and shootable from the level side: a breach is therefore a kill-box with a slow conveyor, which is what makes 'stand and fight the reinforcement stream' the intended play. Licensing rule: an agent that has completed one crossing this run carries a +2.2x modifier to its NEXT crossing time — so agents that backflow into the hub and come out again arrive noticeably faster the second time.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: sustained arrival rate at a breach is bounded by antechamber service rate and is INSENSITIVE to how big the marrow reserve is (reserve controls duration, not rate); a player camping the mouth sees agents queued and staggered rather than a burst; total throughput of a 2-stage gate is roughly 60% of what a 1-stage gate would pass. FORBIDS: burst-spawning N enemies at once on breach open; forbids reserve size affecting instantaneous pressure. VERIFIABLE: plot arrivals/min vs reserve remaining — expect a flat plateau that falls off a cliff at exhaustion, not a proportional curve.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec asks 'how dispatch and crossing should be rate-limited' and answers only 'bounded by the reinforcement economy' and 'only so many breaches open at once.' This puts the rate limit in a specific physical place with a specific ratio, decouples RATE from RESERVE (a distinction the spec currently conflates), and turns the breach mouth into the intended standing-fight arena rather than a spawn point.
**Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22615129/

### B5. Self-extinguishing pour: agents go deaf to the rally signal when they are soaked in it, which is what caps the surge and lets the gate close
**What the source says.** A study of what stops a mass convergence found the stop is RECEIVER-SIDE, not emitter-side: a damping enzyme desensitizes the receptors for the agents' OWN self-secreted rally attractant once local concentration is high. Only receptors for intermediate/rally-type attractants desensitize; receptors for the actual end-target attractant do NOT. With damping intact, agents converged then slowed and arrested between 90-180 min; the fraction moving slower than 2 µm/min ('arrest coefficient') was roughly DOUBLE that of damping-deficient agents. Damping-deficient agents kept moving, with total displacement of 1.7-3.2 mm versus 1-1.5 mm for controls over 4 hours — about twice as far. The counter-intuitive outcome: damping-deficient agents formed LARGER piles but performed WORSE — their cordon was 'discontinuous', the target escaped and grew, while normal agents formed a smaller, continuous, static cordon that actually contained it. A related receptor study found the same receiver switches signalling arms at low (10 nM) vs high (100 nM) signal, so the response is genuinely non-monotone in signal strength.
**Proposed game rule.** Every agent carries a per-agent SATURATION counter that integrates the Muster field it is currently standing in. Above a per-agent-varying threshold it stops responding to Muster entirely (it goes deaf to rally) but keeps responding fully to Trail/Noise (the intruder itself). Consequences, all of which the spec wants: (1) A breach pour self-limits — once ~8-12 agents are packed around a mouth, further hub-side agents no longer read the mouth as a destination, so the marrow stops dispatching to it and the breach closes 'because it is no longer useful to the agents,' with a real mechanism rather than a timer. (2) Saturated agents convert from mobile attackers into a STATIC cordon — they stop and hold, which is exactly the garrison behaviour the spec wants and gets it for free. (3) Deliberately make the cordon better than the mob: a static saturated ring should actually hold ground (deny spotter passage) better than the same agents streaming, so the enemy's self-limiting is a genuine tactical strength, not a handicap.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: enemy density near a mouth plateaus at a finite value regardless of reserve remaining; agents at the plateau visibly STOP and hold rather than chase; a player who retreats out of Trail range from a saturated cluster does NOT get followed by the whole cluster. FORBIDS: unbounded pile-up at a hot breach; forbids Muster being a monotone 'more is better' recruiter; forbids the breach closing on a bare timer. VERIFIABLE: count agents within 1 hop of a mouth over time — expect a hard asymptote; and expect mean agent speed inside the cluster to fall below a threshold while speed outside stays normal.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec asserts a breach 'closes when it is no longer useful to the agents the hub spawns' but supplies no mechanism for that state. This is the mechanism, and it doubles as the emergent-garrison rule the spec currently hand-waves ('a garrison is emergent, not an object'). It also gives Muster a principled saturation term, without which Muster is an unbounded positive feedback loop.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8926156/

### B6. Grip curve: a gate cannot anchor in DEAD-QUIET ground — and it tears itself apart under too much load
**What the source says.** Direct force measurements on the grapples that let a moving agent latch onto a wall under flow found a genuinely counter-intuitive law: at LOW applied load, increasing the load INCREASES bond lifetime (a 'catch' regime); past a critical load, lifetime collapses (a 'slip' regime). Measured critical load is 47.9-61.8 pN across variants; maximum bond lifetime is ~0.2 s. Because of this, adhesion has a FLOW THRESHOLD — below a minimum flow the agents fail to latch and their motion becomes saltatory and intermittent. Skid velocity is non-monotone in flow: ~45 µm/s at 0.2 dyn/cm², falling to a minimum of ~15 µm/s at the ~0.8 dyn/cm² optimum. Anchor density modulates the whole law: below ~10 anchors/µm² adhesion becomes discontinuous, and above ~300 anchors/µm² the low-flow threshold DISAPPEARS ENTIRELY — a dense enough wall latches agents even at zero flow.
**Proposed game rule.** Make breach viability non-monotone in local disturbance, with three bands. (1) BELOW a minimum combined Noise+Distress flux, a called breach fails to anchor: the mouth flickers open and agents cannot commit — they emerge partway and slide back. A polygon the player has genuinely gone quiet in cannot host a working breach even if a spotter transmitted it. (2) In the MIDDLE band, higher disturbance = faster commitment and shorter antechamber transit (this is where all normal play lives). (3) ABOVE a critical load — sustained extreme disturbance, roughly the spec's turtling threshold — the gate TEARS: the mouth destabilizes and collapses early, dumping whatever is in the antechamber and closing. Anchor-density modifier: in a polygon with high Garrison-interior signal (the enemy's own held ground), the low-flux threshold is waived — the enemy can open gates into their own quiet ground at will. That is precisely the 'reclaiming ground you didn't hold' behaviour the spec asks for, derived rather than bolted on.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: sneaking (staying under the flux floor) denies breaches in ground you are moving through, WITHOUT killing spotters — a stealth-flavoured counterplay the game does not currently have; and extreme sustained farming in one room causes gates there to collapse rather than escalate forever. FORBIDS: 'more noise is always more enemies' — the response curve must have a peak; forbids a breach opening into a polygon at zero local signal UNLESS it is enemy-held ground. VERIFIABLE: sweep a bot's noise output across a range and plot successful-breach rate — expect a hump, not a ramp. If it is monotone, this rule is not implemented.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's response curve is monotone everywhere: more disturbance → more reinforcements, until an extreme threshold triggers the flank breach. This adds a LOW-end cutoff (giving the quiet-play style a real mechanical reward, which currently only exists as 'aggressive perfect play') and reframes the extreme-turtling punishment as a gate FAILURE mode rather than an extra gate — arguably a better reading, and it can coexist with the flank breach as its precursor tell.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2634935/

### B7. Backflow and dissemination: an over-pressured gate loses its one-way property and returning agents seed the fight somewhere else on the map
**What the source says.** A survey of agents that cross a barrier and then RE-CROSS IT BACKWARD found the phenomenon is common, not exceptional: in one model as many as 80% of agents that had been recruited to a disturbance site subsequently migrated back toward the transport compartment; in another model ~15% of all observed crossing events were reverse-direction. The trigger is the rally signal itself at high concentration — it induces an enzyme that CLEAVES the specific junction molecule that made the gate directional, so an over-driven gate loses its polarity. Timing: agents accumulated within 2 h, peaked at 6 h, the reversal window ran 6-12 h, and the site cleared by 24 h. The decisive systems consequence: reversed agents carry the disturbance with them — from one origin site, disturbance was subsequently detected at three distinct distant sites, converting a local event into a multi-site one. The companion two-signal study gives the same number from the other direction: strip the gate's retention cache and >40% of agents entering the seam reverse (vs <10% control).
**Proposed game rule.** When a polygon's Distress integral exceeds a high band (below the flank-breach threshold, above normal combat), the live breach in that polygon FLIPS POLARITY: agents in the polygon begin streaming back through the mouth toward the hub, and the outbound pour stalls. Each agent that backflows carries a disturbance packet that the marrow re-deposits as APPROACH value on a DIFFERENT polygon — biased toward polygons with player Trail or player structures, i.e. behind the player. Concretely: over-farming one chokepoint does not just spawn a flank breach in the same room, it relocates the war to your base. Tune so ~15% of arrivals backflow under normal heavy contact (ambient, a visible tell), rising toward 80% at the extreme band. Also: this makes 'shooting through the breach' properly two-way — the spec already wants the breach to be a real two-way door, and backflow is what makes the enemy use that door in the enemy's direction.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who camps one breach and farms it will, after a bounded period, see arrivals fall AND see a new call land in a polygon behind him; ambient reverse traffic through any hot mouth at a low rate. FORBIDS: infinite safe farming at a single mouth; forbids over-pressure being punished only locally. VERIFIABLE: instrument backflow events per breach and correlate with next-call polygon distance from the player's base — expect calls to move TOWARD player structures after a high-Distress episode.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's anti-turtle answer is a single extreme-threshold flank breach — a binary, rare punishment. This gives a continuous, always-slightly-visible pressure with the same intent, and it costs the player GROUND rather than just spawning a fight, which fits the spec's core thesis ('this is a war, the enemy does not simply let you have ground you took but didn't hold') far better than a local punish does.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11107488/

### B8. Two reserve pools: a small pre-stocked surge pool with instant release and recycling, plus a large slow-manufactured sustain pool
**What the source says.** The wall's grapple hardware comes in two classes with completely different supply chains. Class one is held PRE-BUILT in storage granules and pushed to the surface within ~3-5 minutes of a stimulus, with no manufacture required; it is subsequently pulled back in and RECYCLED into the same store for reuse. Class two is entirely manufacture-dependent, requires up to 3 hours to reach peak availability, and then sustains for ~4 hours. Separately, the same survey found the firm-latch receptor is present at 100-1000x the density of the skid receptors — i.e. the approach hardware is the scarce one and the hold hardware is abundant, not the other way round.
**Proposed game rule.** Split the marrow reserve into READY and BUILT. READY: small (say 8-12 agent-units), released with near-zero latency on the first call after a quiet period, and it REGENERATES BY RECYCLING — agents that backflow through a breach (see candidate 7) return their unit to READY, so the enemy literally recovers what you let escape. BUILT: large, but with a long lead time (~3x the ready pool's cycle) before it starts contributing, and once flowing it sustains for a long stretch. Behaviour this produces: the first call of an engagement is a fast small punch; if the player kills that punch quickly and leaves, nothing more comes; if the fight lasts past the lead time, the BUILT stream arrives and does not stop. Exhausting READY but not BUILT produces a LULL — a false 'I won' moment ~10-20s before the sustained stream. And the invade-the-core window the spec wants is precisely BUILT-exhaustion, not READY-exhaustion, so it is a rare, earned window rather than a routine one.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a measurable bimodal arrival profile — an early burst, a lull, then a sustained stream; short engagements that never see the second wave; letting agents retreat alive measurably shortens the enemy's recovery time before the next call. FORBIDS: a single scalar reserve with one regen rate; forbids reserve exhaustion being reachable by winning one quick fight. VERIFIABLE: log arrivals/min across a 3-minute engagement — expect a two-humped curve with a trough, not a single decaying exponential.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec specifies one finite, slowly-regenerating reserve. A single pool cannot simultaneously deliver 'fast response to a call' and 'exhaustible only after a long fight' — those want different rates. Two pools resolve that tension, produce the lull that makes the invade-the-core window dramatic, and add a genuine incentive to finish wounded enemies (denying recycling) that the spec does not currently have.
**Source.** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK53380/

### B9. Chamber-class admissibility: only one geometric class of polygon can host a gate at all
**What the source says.** A survey of where in a branching transport network agents actually latch and cross found the answer is extraordinarily narrow. 39% of all agents passing through one class of conduit segment were skidding along the wall (i.e. engaged with it), versus only 0.6% in the immediately upstream class. That class carries a 10-fold higher density of the firm-latch receptor than either the upstream or downstream classes, and the density peaks specifically at segments ~25 µm in diameter — larger and smaller segments both carry less. Crossing into the surrounding terrain is documented as occurring in that one class ONLY, with no crossing in the upstream class at all. Same wall material, same agents, same flow — the geometry alone decides.
**Proposed game rule.** Gate admissibility is a static geometric property of a polygon, computed at world build: a polygon can host a breach only if its size falls in a mid band (peak admissibility at a target scale, falling off both above and below), with a hard cutoff outside the band. Huge halls and tiny closets are BREACH-PROOF. Give the roughly 65:1 ratio real teeth — an inadmissible polygon should be effectively immune, not merely disfavoured. Render this diegetically: admissible walls carry a visible material tell so the player can read a room's breachability on sight without a HUD element. This makes room choice a first-class tactical decision (fall back to the big hall; build the pod in a closet) and gives the cordon rule a shape: you cordon the ADMISSIBLE polygons, and the inadmissible ones are free corridors.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who learns to fight in inadmissible rooms takes zero called breaches there and must be flushed out by ground assault instead; base sites in inadmissible polygons never get raided by breach, only by patrol. FORBIDS: a breach appearing in any polygon a spotter can reach — the spotter must transmit an ADMISSIBLE polygon, which constrains and shapes spotter pathing. VERIFIABLE: log the polygon of every opened breach across a run and check the size distribution is tightly banded; if breaches appear across the full size range, the rule is not live.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says a breach is cut into 'an ordinary existing chamber' with no constraint at all, which means every room is equally dangerous and the player has no map knowledge to accumulate. This adds a learnable spatial grammar to the whole reinforcement layer at essentially zero implementation cost, and gives the containment reward a concrete definition (cordon the admissible set).
**Source.** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK53380/

### B10. Docking collar plus the off-seam minority: the wall grips the crosser (a vulnerability window), and ~1 in 10 agents cuts its own private hole elsewhere
**What the source says.** Imaging of the moment of crossing found the WALL actively builds a structure around the crossing agent rather than passively yielding: it raises a ring of vertical projections that encircles at least 240° of the agent's circumference and remains attached through the whole passage, staying anchored even at late stages. The passage aperture itself is 0.5-2 µm in diameter. Preventing the collar from forming cut crossings 3-4 fold. The same work quantified the route split precisely and found it is AGENT-CLASS-DEPENDENT: 5±2% of one agent class, 7±1% of a second, and 11±4% of a third crossed straight THROUGH a wall panel rather than at a seam between panels — a small but consistent minority cutting a private hole in a place the seam-based gate logic would never predict.
**Proposed game rule.** Two rules. (a) COLLAR: while an agent is in the mouth it is visually gripped by the breach edge and is IMMOBILE and unable to attack for the crossing duration, and takes full damage. This converts the forbidden 'log-jam in the opening' into a deliberate, readable kill window — the director's rule was no special-casing that BLOCKS entry, and this does not block it, it just makes the transit state legible and punishable. Removing the collar (player damping the mouth) should cut crossings 3-4x, matching candidate 1's scrubber effect and reinforcing it. (b) OFF-SEAM MINORITY: make the private-hole fraction a per-CLASS stat, not a global constant — small fast classes ~5%, mid classes ~7%, and the spotter classes ~11%. Those agents do not use the mouth: they emerge from an arbitrary wall segment of the polygon, offset from the mouth. This is the cleanest possible answer to 'which is the weaker spotter' — differentiate VAGRANT and LACUNA on exactly this axis: vagrant is the low-percentage, mouth-using, cheap-and-numerous caller; LACUNA is the high-percentage one that arrives through its own hole away from the mouth, which is why it is the one that 'deliberately tries to slip past' the cordon the spec describes.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player watching a mouth still occasionally gets flanked by an agent that never came through it, at a rate that differs measurably by enemy class; agents in transit can be killed at a much higher rate than agents in the open. FORBIDS: 100% of arrivals appearing at the mouth; forbids a cordon of turrets on the mouth being a complete answer. VERIFIABLE: log arrival position vs mouth position per class — expect a small, class-specific tail of arrivals >2 units from the mouth, and expect the lacuna tail to be roughly 2x the vagrant tail.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec explicitly lists 'vagrant & lacuna have only ever been placeholders; design them for real (stats, silhouettes, behaviors, which is the weaker)' as an open unit and separately wants enemies that 'deliberately try to slip past.' This supplies a single measured mechanism that answers both at once and ties the two spotter classes to the crossing system rather than making them arbitrary stat blocks.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2172560/

## LANE C — signal damping, active resolution, signature cloaking, and post-fight salvage

### C1. Salvage-fed reserve: the corpse field IS the marrow's economy
**What the source says.** In a large distributed system with a finite circulating material pool, the depot is refilled almost entirely by breaking down its own expended units, not by external supply. Measured split: 20–21 units/day recovered from expended agents versus only 1–2 units/day taken in from outside; salvage supplies 90–95% of the entire pool's turnover; normal losses are 1–2 units/day (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6316009/). A second source gives the stock-vs-flux ratio: the deployed population holds 2–3 g, static stores ~1 g, daily delivery 20–25 mg/day, but the in-flight carrier holds only 2–3 mg at any instant — i.e. the amount actually in transit is ~1/1000 of the stock (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8235187/). Companion load figure: 200–300 billion units are turned over and cleared per day system-wide, and unrecovered ones do not stay neutral — they break down and leak, which escalates the alarm signal instead of clearing it (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9157040/).
**Proposed game rule.** Replace the marrow's flat regen timer with a two-term law: reserve_regen = salvage_rate * (agent corpses recovered through a live breach or by hub-side collectors) + trickle, with salvage tuned to roughly 10–20x trickle (matching the 20:1 measured split). Every Adversary corpse left on the floor is unbanked reserve. Enemy agents in a non-contact state route to nearby corpses and drag them toward the nearest live breach — a visible, interruptible corpse convoy. The player's cleaning robot consuming a corpse is therefore direct reserve denial, and the pod's roots eating a corpse pile permanently removes that mass from the enemy economy. The 'deep negative after a core kill' resupply from the home universe runs at the TRICKLE rate only — which is exactly why the outage lasts minutes: at 1/20 the normal rate you cannot climb out fast. Corpses that are neither salvaged nor cleaned for T seconds 'spoil': they stop being salvageable by either side and convert into a slow persistent Distress/Danger emitter in that polygon.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a run where the player kills enemies and lets the cleaning robot process the bodies shows a measurably slower breach cadence than a run with identical kill counts and no cleanup — this is directly instrumentable (breaches/minute vs corpses-cleaned/minute). PREDICTS contested corpse fields and visible convoy behaviour, giving the player a new interception target that is not a spotter. PREDICTS killing enemies near a live breach is economically WORSE for the player than killing them deep in his own ground, because the convoy is short. FORBIDS reserve regeneration being a constant a designer types in; forbids corpses being inert scenery; forbids the player being able to farm kills at a firebase with no economic consequence (the spoil rule punishes farming with rising Distress — which feeds straight into the existing extreme-Distress flank-breach punishment).
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 says only 'the marrow holds a finite reserve that regenerates slowly' and gives regen no physical source. This gives regen a source the player can attack, converts the cleaning robot from a cosmetic FOB-tidier into a strategic weapon, supplies the actual reason the deep-negative outage lasts a couple of minutes rather than an arbitrary timer, and gives the spoil timer as a farming disincentive the spec currently only handles with the extreme-turtle flank breach.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6316009/

### C2. Two-tier signal law: relay chatter saturates, target signature never does
**What the source says.** Agents converging on a target amplify the pull by re-broadcasting their own relay signal, and would in principle recruit without bound. A dedicated saturation governor inside each agent shuts down its sensitivity to relay signals after cumulative exposure — but ONLY to the agent-generated relay signals, never to signals emitted by the target itself. Measured: sensitivity to the two agent-generated relay signals is governed and blunted; sensitivity to the two target-emitted signals showed 'minimal or statistically nonsignificant differences'. Timeline: from 0–30 min the governed and ungoverned agents behave identically; divergence appears at 90–180 min. Governor-removed agents show twice the displacement (1.7–3.2 mm vs 1.0–1.5 mm for normal agents) — they keep roaming instead of arresting. Arrest was quantified as the percentage of instantaneous speed samples below 2 µm/min. Pre-saturation used 0.1 µM or 1 µM of relay signal for 30 min; step stimuli were 50/100/200 nM. The counter-intuitive payoff: removing the governor makes clusters LARGER but WORSE — governor-removed populations had significantly higher escapee counts outside their own clusters and were impaired at picking up targets at all (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8926156/).
**Proposed game rule.** Split the signal table by emitter class and give only one class a per-agent saturation governor. Muster and Distress (agent-generated relay) accumulate an exposure integral per agent; once the integral crosses a per-agent-varying threshold, that agent stops climbing those two gradients and switches to hold-position/arrest for a cooldown. Noise and Trail (player-generated signature) are exempt and never saturate. Cadence tuned to the measured shape: no visible difference in the first ~20% of a fight, divergence at ~60% — i.e. a fight's first wave behaves greedily, later waves plateau. Add the arrest metric as a real instrument: log the fraction of agent speed samples below a low-speed cutoff per polygon, which is the direct analogue of the published arrest coefficient and lets a run be verified numerically rather than by eye.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a garrison self-caps: standing and fighting reinforcements at a firebase plateaus at a bounded agent count instead of dragging the map in, which is exactly the 'reinforcement stream itself is the fight' pacing the spec asks for. PREDICTS the specific counter-intuitive, falsifiable result that DISABLING the governor produces a bigger swarm that is worse at containing the player — more agents, more gaps, more escapes past the cordon. That is a real A/B test with a non-obvious expected sign. PREDICTS the player can starve a fight by going quiet (signature signals decay) but cannot starve it by killing agents (Distress from a killed agent still recruits, but saturates). FORBIDS all-map death balls; forbids a single loud fight emptying every distant polygon.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has 'Stand-down' — a hunting agent whose LOCAL SIGNAL STAYED FAINT for ~5 s exits combat. That only ends cold fights. It has nothing that terminates a HOT fight, so the current design has no upper bound on pile-on except the reserve. This adds a saturation-arrest that caps hot fights, and adds the emitter-class asymmetry (agent-relay signals saturate, player signature does not) which the spec's signal table does not distinguish at all.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8926156/

### C3. The scrubber is a consuming sink with finite throughput, not a multiplier — and a sink is also a repeller
**What the source says.** Dedicated damping units do not attenuate a field; they physically remove signal quanta and destroy them. Key mechanistic facts, all measured: (1) the sink is signalling-SILENT — binding produces no internal response at all, no state change, no action; it is purely a consumer (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10405735/). (2) It cycles CONSTITUTIVELY, not on demand — it traffics inward spontaneously whether or not anything is bound, so it has a fixed throughput independent of load (same source). (3) It is BROAD-SPECTRUM: one sink type clears at least 11 distinct signal species, with binding strengths spread over ~10–180 nM — i.e. the same damper handles many signal channels but with a wide spread in effectiveness per channel (same source). (4) Placement matters more than density: the broad sink sits on BOUNDARY surfaces — the sinusoids, venules and lymphatic walls that traffic must pass through — not scattered through open ground (same source). (5) Deleting the sink entirely raises ambient signal only 3.5x for one channel and 2x for another, and not measurably for a third (same source) — so a sink sets a floor, it does not zero the field. (6) The consumption machinery is independent of the sensing machinery: full removal of the usual sensing adaptor did NOT affect scavenging of either signal, in double-knockouts (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5777261/). (7) Cycle timings from that primary: surface reappearance measurable at 15 and 45 min for one signal, absent at early timepoints for another, converging by 3 h; test concentration 50 pM; one signal showed biphasic decay with rapid initial consumption in minutes, the other single-phase. (8) Mounting the sink on a NON-migrating bystander agent measurably reduces attraction of nearby responders, tested at a 1:1 bystander-to-source ratio over 3 h and 12 h (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11811810/).
**Proposed game rule.** Implement the pheromone scrubber as SUBTRACTIVE with a throughput cap, never as a multiplier. Each tick it removes min(capacity_per_tick, field_value) from its own polygon and from every polygon one portal away — capacity is a single shared budget spent across ALL signal channels, so a scrubber fighting six loud channels damps each one poorly. Effectiveness per channel is deliberately uneven (mirror the ~18:1 spread in binding strength): one scrubber is strong on two channels, weak on the rest. Floor, not zero: a scrubber can reduce a polygon's steady-state level by roughly 2–3.5x, never to nil. Throughput is constant and load-independent, so the failure mode is SATURATION: a big enough fight overruns the budget and the field leaks through at full strength. Placement law follows the boundary result — a scrubber on a portal/crossing covers both sides and is worth far more than one in the middle of a room, which directly rewards the spec's cordon doctrine. And the second-order effect: because a sink consumes, the field slopes DOWN toward it, so agents standing at the edge of scrubbed ground are steered away from the scrubber. A scrubber is passively a repeller as well as a damper — free, emergent, and it explains diegetically why a cloaked base is also an unattractive one.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a scrubbed base goes quiet under light pressure and audibly/visibly LEAKS under heavy pressure — verifiable by logging per-polygon field values against scrubber capacity during a large fight. PREDICTS scrubbers on portals outperform scrubbers in room centres by a measurable margin (same component cost, different placement). PREDICTS agents idling just outside scrub coverage drift outward rather than inward. FORBIDS 'scrubber = signals off'; forbids one scrubber covering every channel; forbids the scrubber being a percentage modifier (a percentage modifier cannot saturate, and saturation is the entire interesting behaviour).
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says only 'severely damps ALL chemo-signals in its area' — no math, no coverage shape, no failure mode. This supplies: subtractive-with-throughput instead of multiplicative; one shared budget across channels so ALL-channel damping is genuinely weak per channel; saturation as the designed failure; hop-1 coverage; portal placement as the strong play; a 2–3.5x floor rather than silence; and the unasked-for repeller side effect.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10405735/

### C4. Locate-score doctrine: cloaking is destroying the signal at the source, and it alone decides whether you spread or get purged
**What the source says.** The single strongest result in this lane. An agent that destroys the recruitment signal around itself does not merely delay discovery — signal destruction is BOTH NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT for survival and spread. Transplanting only the signal-destroying machinery into an otherwise harmless, always-cleared organism converted it into one causing systemic collapse within 24 h, with successful colonisation of distant sites (upper airway survival p=0.001, liver p=0.012, spleen p=0.0003, node spread p=0.0015); removing that same machinery from an aggressive strain crippled its spread (survival p=0.0019, node spread p=0.0001) (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20158613/). The destroyer targets a structurally conserved site shared across several signal species, so one tool covers a band of channels rather than one channel. Second source: cloaking is never a single trick — one organism carries around 40 distinct evasion factors; ~75% of field isolates carry a surface coat; several factors work by wrapping the entity in HOST material so it reads as friendly rather than by hiding; and the receptor-blockers are strongly SELECTIVE, one measured at 1.1 nM against one recruit channel but 35.4 nM against another — a 32:1 preference, so no single blocker covers the board (https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/microbiolspec.gpp3-0061-2019).
**Proposed game rule.** Make the spec's 'unscrubbed bases eventually get found and purged' a real number. Every polygon carries a persistent LOCATE score that increments on every combat event there (shots fired, agent killed, structure placed) and decays extremely slowly — slower than Danger. Spotter garrison expansion and the flank-breach selector both read LOCATE, not instantaneous Distress. A scrubber does not damp LOCATE; it prevents its accrual while active and while unsaturated. Consequence: a base that never fights accrues nothing and is never targeted; a well-defended base that wins fight after fight accrues heavily and is eventually raided — the counter-intuitive doctrine falls straight out. Selectivity: give each scrubber a 32:1 strong-band/weak-band split, so a base that expects mixed pressure needs two or three differently-tuned scrubbers, not one. Offensive use: a scrubber planted next to an enemy garrison suppresses the garrison-interior signal that needs two fronts to meet, breaking the FOB's own coordination — the player can un-fortify a boss room without shooting it.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a measurable time-to-discovery for an unscrubbed forward base that scales with combat volume there, and effectively unbounded time-to-discovery for a quiet one — instrumentable as LOCATE vs wall-clock. PREDICTS an offensive scrubber measurably degrades an enemy garrison's cohesion (garrison-interior coverage should collapse). FORBIDS one scrubber making a base permanently invisible; forbids cloaking being a passive aura that works while you are shooting through it at full volume. PREDICTS the specific tell the director wants: the player who fights hardest at his FOB is the player who gets raided.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec states the purge doctrine as intent with zero mechanism, and gives no reason a base ever gets found. This supplies the accrual variable, makes the scrubber's job preventing accrual rather than hiding a value, adds band-selectivity that forces multiple scrubbers, and adds an offensive application of the same building against enemy garrisons — which the spec does not contemplate at all.
**Source.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20158613/

### C5. Dispatch gate: occlude versus tear down — a core kill destroys the gate machinery, so the outage outlasts the cause
**What the source says.** The system has a single global signal that closes the storage depot's export gate, and it has TWO distinct modes with wildly different recovery profiles. Mode A (reversible occlusion): the signal blocks the gate's outward-open conformation — instant, and it releases the moment the signal clears. Mode B (teardown): the signal drives the gate machinery inward and destroys it, so export cannot resume until the machinery is REBUILT. The measured numbers make the point: the closing signal itself is cleared from circulation within MINUTES, but the export block lasts 24–48 h, because recovery is gated on rebuild time, not on the signal. Gate structures were observed internalised within 1 h of exposure and essentially fully destroyed by 24 h. Effect outlasts cause by roughly three orders of magnitude. Ambient level of the closing signal in an unstressed system: ~2–20 nM (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8235187/).
**Proposed game rule.** Give the marrow two independent stop conditions with different recovery laws instead of one reserve number. OCCLUDE (reversible, instant both ways): dispatch pauses whenever the hub-side gate is physically obstructed — corpses stacked in the mouth, a player standing in the aperture, another wave still crossing. Recovers the instant the obstruction clears; this is the natural rate-limiter on crossings and needs no timer. TEARDOWN (irreversible, rebuild-timed): killing the CORE destroys the dispatch machinery. The outage length is then a REBUILD timer, not a function of reserve depth — set it to the spec's couple of minutes directly, and let reserve refill independently underneath it. This decouples two things the spec currently couples: 'how much reserve is left' and 'is the layer online'. Crossing capacity should also be a stock-vs-flux ratio, not a pool size: the depot holds a large stock but the amount in transit at any instant is a tiny fraction of it (measured ~1/1000), so cap simultaneous in-transit agents per breach at a small hard number regardless of how deep the reserve is.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the reinforcement layer stays down for a fixed, predictable window after a core kill regardless of what the player does next — so the player can plan the window and use it, which is exactly the spec's 'a core kill is a status effect you apply' framing. FORBIDS the exploit of farming the reserve back up (or the enemy farming it) to shorten the outage. FORBIDS the layer snapping back the moment the player leaves the hub. PREDICTS breach throughput is visibly capped and self-throttles when a mouth is congested, without any special-case anti-logjam code — which the spec explicitly demands ('DO NOT add any special casing around entry').
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec ties the outage to 'deep-negative depth × regen rate', which is two coupled knobs producing one hard-to-tune number and is gameable by manipulating reserve. This replaces it with an uncoupled rebuild timer that tunes to 'a couple of minutes' in one step, and adds the reversible-occlusion mode as the natural crossing rate-limit the spec asks for but does not specify. Also supplies the in-transit cap as a ratio rather than an arbitrary constant.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8235187/

### C6. Cleanup ramps and corpses spoil: the cleaning robot as a self-accelerating short-range program
**What the source says.** Cleanup is not a flat service rate. Processing one corpse measurably PRIMES the cleaner for the next: material stripped from corpse #1 is converted into the very machinery used to grab corpse #2 (a documented metabolic chain ending in the actin-remodelling step that performs the grab). Numbers from the primary: after processing, the cleaner emits three distinct short-range boost mediators; production peaks at 1 h and 24 h for one, 3 h and 48 h for the others; they act at 1 nM, with endogenous output measured at ≈0.15–0.54 nM; priming takes effect within 15 MINUTES; the three act SYNERGISTICALLY — combined effect exceeds any individually; and they work both on the producer itself AND on neighbours, with the measured neighbour effect confined to within 21 µm — a hard, short radius (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870015/). Load figures and the failure mode from the companion review: 200–300 billion units cleared per day system-wide; cleaners routinely stack MULTIPLE corpses at once because corpses far outnumber cleaners; and uncleared corpses do not stay neutral — they break down secondarily and leak their contents, which ESCALATES the alarm signal rather than letting it fade (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9157040/). Peak corpse burden after an event was measured at 3–4 h post-event, i.e. the debris load peaks well AFTER the fight ends (same primary). From the resolution review: cleanup-boosting output is absurdly potent relative to recruit-suppressing output — 10 pM raised clearance by ~90%, while suppressing fresh recruitment took a much larger dose for a ~40% effect (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4515371/).
**Proposed game rule.** Cleaning robot gets three rules. (1) RAMP: each corpse processed raises its processing rate for the next, decaying back over time — so cleaning a big pile promptly is far more efficient than trickle-cleaning, and component yield scales with the ramp. Tune the ramp to kick in fast (priming inside ~15 s of game time, mirroring the 15-min biological latency scaled down) and to cap at ~3x. (2) SHORT RADIUS: the robot emits a 'settled' effect confined to its own polygon, no spread — it suppresses new Distress deposition there and buffs any second cleaning robot inside that radius, superlinearly (two robots in one room beat two robots in two rooms). This is the direct transplant of the 21 µm neighbour bound, and it makes robot placement a real decision. (3) SPOIL: a corpse unprocessed for T seconds converts from salvage into a persistent Distress/Danger emitter — it makes the room LOUDER than if the fight had never happened. Combine with the observation that debris load peaks after the fight ends: schedule the spoil timer so the cleanup window opens right as the player wants to push on, forcing the hold-vs-advance choice. Asymmetric potency: give the robot a strong cleanup ramp and a WEAK direct recruit-suppression (roughly the 90% vs 40% split) — the robot is a salvage/cloaking tool, not a combat damper. That is the scrubber's job.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS component yield per corpse rises sharply with cleanup density — a player who wins one big fight and cleans it out-earns a player with the same kill count spread thin. PREDICTS a base that wins fights and does NOT clean becomes noisier than one that never fought, and gets found faster (couples directly to the locate-score candidate). PREDICTS two robots in one room beat two robots split across rooms, measurably. FORBIDS the cleaning robot being flat-rate cosmetic dressing; forbids corpses being neutral; forbids the robot doubling as a signal damper.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec says cleanup 'emits a couple of components' and lists visual behaviours. This supplies the rate law (ramp with cap), the hard short radius and the superlinear co-location bonus, the spoil timer that gives cleanup urgency and a real failure state, the after-the-fight timing that creates the hold-vs-advance decision, and the potency asymmetry that keeps the robot's role distinct from the scrubber's.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870015/

### C7. Wind-down by departure: agents leave through the breach rather than dying, and the crossing wears out from use
**What the source says.** A response does not end by its agents being killed or by quietly evaporating — the overwhelming majority WALK BACK OUT. Measured: >80% of agents that responded to a site subsequently departed it; in another model the on-site population fell by more than 90% between the 12 h and 24 h marks while only ~10% expired locally. Departure is not an order; it is triggered by (a) saturation of the agent's own sensitivity and (b) destruction of the crossing's ANCHOR structure — the agents themselves release material that degrades the junction they entered through, so the door they used falls apart from having been used. There is also an explicit RETENTION signal that actively pins agents in place; removing the retention signal releases them, and raising the outside attractant level is sufficient on its own to drive departure. Departed agents keep an altered marker profile afterwards but were shown to respond and fight just as effectively on a SECOND call. Route home is two-phase and predetermined: site → transit → depot (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9686117/).
**Proposed game rule.** Give committed reinforcement agents a return path. When a polygon's Noise stays below threshold for T seconds, agents in the wind-down state route to the nearest live breach and cross OUT, refunding ~50% of their reserve cost to the marrow. Retention: any agent in contact with the player emits a short-range retention quantity that pins nearby agents in place and blocks wind-down — so breaking contact cleanly is what lets the enemy recover, and staying in contact denies it. That gives the player two opposite valid strategies with different economics: disengage to cool the area (enemy recovers reserve), or maintain contact to pin the force forward (enemy cannot bank the refund). GATE WEAR as the third breach-closing condition alongside the spec's 45 s uncrossed timer and reserve exhaustion: every crossing chips the breach's structural integrity, so a heavily-used breach closes EARLY. Pinning a breach open by repeatedly crossing it therefore has a hard cost — it wears the gate down — which resolves the spec's open worry that shooting through a breach to keep it alive is unbounded. Returned agents come back at full effectiveness on the next call but visibly marked (scarring/altered silhouette), so the player can tell a veteran from a fresh dispatch.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the map does not accumulate orphaned enemies after the player disengages — agent counts in cleared polygons should fall toward zero without the player killing anything, verifiable in a run log. PREDICTS reserve recovers measurably faster after a clean disengage than after a grind at contact. PREDICTS a breach used hard closes sooner than an idle one, so shoot-through-the-portal pinning self-terminates without a special rule. FORBIDS 'kill everything' being the only way to reduce pressure; forbids agents standing around forever in stand-down.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's Stand-down rule exits an agent from combat state but leaves it with nowhere to go, and the spec explicitly flags that breach-pinning is 'bounded by the reinforcement economy, not the timer' without saying how. This makes the reserve two-way (departure refunds), gives stand-down agents a destination, adds retention-pinning as a player-controlled economic lever, and supplies use-based gate wear as the missing third closing condition.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9686117/

### C8. Jamming: a damped signal fragment becomes an active blocker, and stripping stickiness stops deposition
**What the source says.** Signal destruction is not binary — the fragments matter, and some of them are weapons. Multiple independent cutting families converge on the same tiny 2–5 unit window at the head of the signal molecule, and the products fall into three sharply different classes: (1) INERT — chopping elsewhere leaves the signal fully active (one cut product 'retains full chemotactic activity'); (2) DEAD — 'lack of chemotactic activity, no induction of Ca2+ influx'; (3) BLOCKER — the truncated fragment still binds the sensor but cannot trigger it, and it now COMPETITIVELY INHIBITS the intact signal. Documented blocker products include four distinct cases across three signal families, each explicitly described as an antagonist that inhibits the full-length signal's own recruiting. A separate, fourth class removes the signal's ability to STICK to the ground: one cut product showed 'no heparin binding' — it can no longer be immobilised in place, so it cannot form a standing gradient even though it is still in the area. There is also a documented feedback loop: rising signal levels induce production of the cutter that destroys them, i.e. the damper is auto-scaling (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6890590/).
**Proposed game rule.** Split the scrubber's effect into three explicit outputs instead of one subtraction. A fraction of every unit the scrubber consumes is converted into a stored NEGATIVE 'jam' quantity held in the polygon, which subtracts from any signal deposited there for a while — so scrubbed ground does not merely read quiet, it CANCELS a spotter's transmission attempted inside it, and the cancellation persists briefly after the scrubber is destroyed (a killed scrubber leaves a fading dead zone, not an instant restoration). A second, separately-tunable output STRIPS STICKINESS: inside coverage, the player's Trail is emitted but cannot be deposited into the polygon's store, so it is sensed by an agent standing in the room but leaves no followable track — local detection still works, long-range tracking fails. That is the exact distinction the sources draw between blocking the sensor and blocking the ground attachment, and it maps cleanly onto the existing per-polygon scalar fields (one touches the value, the other touches the write). Third: auto-scaling — scrubber throughput rises with the local field it has been eating, so a scrubber in a hot area is stronger than one idling, which stops scrubbers from being dead weight in quiet ground.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a spotter that reaches scrubbed ground and completes a full Transmit produces a WEAKER or failed breach rather than a normal one — so the player has a second answer to spotters besides shooting them, and a specific verifiable event to log (transmit completed, breach not opened). PREDICTS an agent standing in a scrubbed room still notices the player directly but cannot follow him out of it. PREDICTS killing a scrubber does not instantly restore the field. ### C9. False beacon: a decoy that outbids the marrow for a spotter's transmission

### C10. Two-axis marking with a hard engagement cliff: quiet beats defended, and half a cordon is worth nothing

### D2. RUPTURE THRESHOLD (a garrison holds only while replacement rate >= loss rate; drop it below the line and the shell bursts, dumping its stored pool)
**What the source says.** Direct dose-titration of the wall-builder supply produced a graded, measurable rupture threshold rather than graceful degradation. Supply was cut by 47%, 67%, and 84%; the observed rule was 'the smaller the supply, the sooner the development of core breakdown.' At 47% supply reduction the containment broke down at day 4 vs day 6 for full supply. Raising supply the other way by +175% cut the extracellular-breakout indicator by 41% at day 7. Stated model: containment integrity depends on replacing dying held agents fast enough; when replacement falls below a critical rate, dying agents are not absorbed by arrivals, they rupture and release the entire contained population into open ground where it grows unchecked. A separate source measures the loss side of that ledger: ~80% of held agents die within 24 h in the active case.
**Numbers.** supply cut 47% / 67% / 84% -> monotonically earlier rupture; rupture at day 4 vs day 6 (a 33% earlier failure for a 47% supply cut); +175% supply -> -41% breakout; ~80% of held agents lost per 24 h
**Proposed game rule.** Every garrison keeps an explicit ledger: replenish_rate (agents/s the marrow dispatches to it, itself capped by reserve) vs loss_rate (agents/s the player is killing there). A garrison also holds a STORED POOL — agents that arrived and settled but are parked out of sight in the polygon's interior (the reason a boss room 'has more in it than you can see'). Rule: if loss_rate > replenish_rate continuously for 12 s, the garrison RUPTURES: the entire stored pool (typically 6–12 agents) spawns at once at the polygon's edges, in one burst, and the garrison drops a tier. Below that, chip damage does nothing but get replaced. Scale it off the measured ladder: a 47% deficit ruptures at ~0.66x the time a 25% deficit does.

### D3. THE RING FEEDS WHAT IT SURROUNDS (inside a formed cluster, each kill converts arriving agents into committed ones at ~2–3:1; the containment structure is where the threat grows)
**What the source says.** Quantitative live tracking of aggregate formation found the counter-intuitive result that the containment structure ACCELERATES the thing it forms around. Mechanism: the contained unit induces recruitment of new agents to the aggregate AND induces rapid short-range motion of those agents once inside; that motility lets multiple arrivals efficiently find and absorb held agents that are dying, so each dying agent converts several arrivals — 'rapid, iterative expansion.' Measured: ~80% of held agents die within 24 h, and on average two or three new arrivals become converted for every single dying agent. Crucially, expansion does not begin until aggregation has occurred: counts did not rise until clustering at 3–5 days, and formation itself is fast once started — an aggregate can form within 2–3 days of the seed reaching deep terrain. Removing the recruit-and-stir signal cut deaths by ~50% and recruitment to ~25%, and the contained unit lost its growth advantage entirely. The primary aggregate then SEEDS SECONDARY aggregates by emitting a loaded carrier agent that walks out and founds a new one elsewhere; dissemination was tracked across ten subjects.
**Numbers.** 2–3 conversions per death; ~80% turnover / 24 h; expansion starts only after clustering at 3–5 d; formation in 2–3 d; signal knockout -> 50% fewer deaths, 25% of recruitment; secondary seeding tracked in 10 subjects
**Proposed game rule.** Two rules, both keyed to whether the polygon has an active Garrison-interior value. Outside a garrisoned polygon, a kill emits Distress/Danger as today (no conversion). (b) SEEDING: a garrison that has held tier 2+ for 90 s emits ONE loaded settler that walks out and nucleates a second garrison elsewhere, biased down the kin-field away from its parent. **Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: the player who stands and fights in a room the enemy has settled sees enemy count RISE with his kill count until the dispatch cap; the same fight one polygon away sees it fall. PREDICTS: ignoring a garrison for 90 s costs you a second garrison somewhere behind you — 'cleared ground re-arms' gets a specific, traceable causal chain instead of ambient respawn. FORBIDS: farming a boss room for salvage; FORBIDS: a garrison being a static, containable number of units.

### D4. BURN THE EXIT TICKET (an agent that settles destroys its own ability to sense the return gradient — permanent, one-way, and only available in a short window after arrival)
**What the source says.** Residency is not a flag, it is self-mutilation. Mobile agents navigate back out of terrain by climbing an EXIT gradient that is high in the transit network and low inside terrain; they read it with a specific receptor. An agent that commits to staying expresses a surface protein that binds that receptor and drives its internalization and degradation — the settler destroys its own exit-gradient sensor, permanently loses the ability to sense the way out, and cannot rejoin the mobile pool. Structural work confirms the binder acts directly on the receptor and forces its internalization. The commitment window is short and early: the retention effect operates within a 16 h window on arrival, and defects established by day 7 persist through the whole later memory phase. Strikingly, the requirement is TERRAIN-DEPENDENT, not universal: in some terrains the mechanism is required (3–5 fold fewer settlers without it, at all late timepoints) and in others it is entirely dispensable — settlers there stay by other means. Once settled they persist for months to years with no further stimulus, and were still patrolling 18 months after the original event.
**Numbers.** 16 h commitment window; 3–5x settler deficit where the mechanism is required; dispensable in other terrains; persistence to 18 months without further stimulus
**Proposed game rule.** Garrisoning is a one-way state transition with a cost to the ENEMY, executed inside a short window. When an agent has been in a nucleating polygon for a continuous SETTLE_WINDOW (~20 s, scaled from the 16 h window), it may convert to GARRISON state: it permanently loses its Muster-gradient sense — it will never answer a call, never join a raid, never retreat, never reinforce a breach again — and it is DEDUCTED FROM THE MARROW'S RESERVE LEDGER for good (it is off the books, no longer a dispatchable body). It gains: never stands down, ignores the 5 s stand-down rule, and defends its polygon at higher aggression. Terrain-dependence is the design gift: make the window mechanism REQUIRED in open polygons (interruptible by the player keeping the polygon hot) and dispensable in dead-end / high-cover polygons, where agents settle regardless — so map shape decides where garrisons are cheap to prevent and where they are not.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who re-enters a polygon within 20 s of enemies arriving prevents the garrison entirely, and instrumenting 'settle attempts vs completed settles' should show that directly. PREDICTS: every garrison the enemy plants makes the next wave measurably thinner — garrisoning and waves compete for the same finite reserve, so a heavily garrisoned map has weak waves and a wave-heavy map has thin garrisons. That is a real strategic dial the player can push either way. FORBIDS: garrison units retreating, rallying, or being pulled off to answer a breach; FORBIDS: garrisoning being free for the enemy.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has garrisons emerging with no cost and no interruption window, and separately has a finite marrow reserve — the two never touch. Coupling them makes both legible: the reserve now visibly drains into ground the enemy is holding, which is the mechanical statement of 'this is a war over territory, not a spawn faucet.' ### D5. PER-POLYGON HOLD CURVE WITH A HEAVY TAIL (ground clears independently, one seed per node, with a slow rising hazard rate — and pulling the cordon reactivates ~70% of it)
**What the source says.** Tracking of many independent containment nodes in the same subject found: each node arises from a SINGLE founding unit; nodes follow completely independent trajectories with respect to internal counts, composition, and outcome; the spread of contained-population size across nodes within one subject spans 10,000-fold. Withdrawing the maintenance signal that holds established containment together caused reactivation in ~70% of quiet subjects — established containment DISINTEGRATES without continuous upkeep, it is not a finished wall.
**Numbers.** one founder per node; 10,000-fold spread within one subject; self-clear 0% @4wk -> ~10% @11wk -> >30% @6mo; 40/60 outcome split from a ~25-unit seed; ~70% reactivation on withdrawal of upkeep
**Proposed game rule.** Replace the binary 'cordoned area is permanently clear' with per-polygon state and a rising hazard. Each polygon carries held_time and a HELD flag. FORBIDS: 'sector cleared' toggles; FORBIDS: identical clear times across polygons; FORBIDS: cordon integrity being fire-and-forget.

### D7. ONE RESIDENT RAISES THE WHOLE DISTRICT — AND EMPTIES THE NEIGHBOURS (garrison contact triggers a zero-sum reallocation, measurable as a hole elsewhere)
**What the source says.** A single settled agent that detects a target does not fight it — it ALARMS. Within 12 h of local detection, gate-signal expression on the local entry vessels rose 5.5-fold, plus a broad set of other recruitment signals; the result was a 5.5-fold accumulation of mobile agents in that terrain. Second, the accumulation is explicitly ZERO-SUM and was measured on the other side of the ledger: while the terrain gained 5.5x, the central store dropped 2.4-fold and the transit pool dropped 5.4-fold. Removing the single relay signal the resident emits abolishes the whole cascade — one agent's one signal is the entire trigger.
**Numbers.** 12 h to 5.5x gate-signal upregulation; 5.5x local accumulation; 2.4x depletion of the central store; 5.4x depletion of the transit pool; recruited agents target-nonspecific; single relay signal is necessary and sufficient
**Proposed game rule.** When a garrisoned agent makes contact, it does not call the marrow (garrison agents have burned their exit ticket and are off the marrow's books — candidate 4). Instead it opens its polygon's crossing gates for ~30 s: every uncommitted agent within 2 hops — patrollers, spotters in transit, agents en route to some other objective, regardless of task — is re-tasked here, ~5.5x local density. And the drain is REAL and accounted: adjacent polygons measurably empty (target ~2.4x thinner one hop out, ~5.4x thinner in transit lanes) for the duration. PREDICTS: spotters in transit get yanked off their calls by a garrison trigger, so aggroing a garrison suppresses breach calls for ~30 s. FORBIDS: enemy density staying uniform under pressure; FORBIDS: garrison alarm creating units out of nothing.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's Muster is purely additive — it calls agents toward a fight but nothing in the spec ever REMOVES agents from anywhere. Making the response zero-sum turns the whole signal system from a spawn amplifier into a resource-allocation puzzle the player can manipulate, which is precisely the stated goal that 'a truly advanced player will intuit how the system communicates with itself and manipulate it to his advantage.' ### D8. PAYLOAD BINDING (heavy payloads stick in the shell and never reach the core; light ones pass — penetration is a per-weapon coefficient, not a damage number)
**What the source says.** Whether an effect reaches the interior of a sealed containment structure is governed by BINDING to the shell material, not by dose. Measured free (unbound, therefore mobile) fractions across 36 payloads span five orders of magnitude: the heaviest, most complex payloads sit below 0.01% free (essentially none of it ever arrives), one common payload 5.13%, another 29.3%, intermediate classes 8–16%, and the smallest simplest payloads above 99.9% free. Direct spatial imaging confirms the consequence: 12 h after a single full dose, the payload was localized in the cellular RIM of the structure with very low levels in the sealed core — distribution inside a single structure is inhomogeneous and payload-specific. A predictive rule was fitted across 279 payloads: an index of (lipophilicity + number of aromatic rings) below 4 gives >67% probability of >10% free fraction. In short: mass + structural complexity predicts failure to penetrate, with a correlation of R^2 = 0.82–0.95 across models and surrogates.
**Numbers.** free fraction range <0.01% to >99.9%; specific values 5.13%, 8–16%, 29.3%; index <4 -> >67% chance of >10% free; model R^2 = 0.82, surrogate R^2 = 0.88–0.95; rim-vs-core imaging at 12 h
**Proposed game rule.** Give every weapon and every building effect a PENETRATION COEFFICIENT into shelled volumes (encysted rings, mature garrisons, and the CORE's guardian ring), computed from the projectile's own physical properties — mass x AoE radius, i.e. the game already has both numbers. Heavy/explosive payloads (charged Tau, grenades) bind on the shell: they hurt shell agents at full value and deliver ~5% to anything behind it. Light fast payloads (rail, uncharged shots) pass at ~30–99%. This is not a nerf — it is a routing rule, and the shell must be visibly the thing eating the damage. Consequence for the hub: the guardian ring cannot be blown through with the biggest gun; the CORE fight requires either a light-payload weapon or physically breaking the ring first, which is what makes the guardian ring a puzzle rather than an hp wall.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who brings only heavy AoE into the hub does zero core damage and can verify it — core hp curve flat while ring agents die. PREDICTS: two distinct viable hub loadouts (break the ring with heavy, or thread the ring with light), which is exactly the 'entire game area unto itself' texture the hub needs. FORBIDS: one dominant loadout clearing all shelled content; FORBIDS: the guardian ring being solved by raw dps.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec specifies the hub as 'the most heavily defended place in the world' and 'VERY hard' but gives no mechanism — currently that can only be implemented as more hp and more units, which is the boring answer. A penetration coefficient makes the ring geometrically meaningful, is one multiplier in the existing damage path, and creates a real reason the player must prepare a loadout before taking a breach into the hub (which reinforces the existing 'you must bring a way back' lesson).
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5028112/

### D9. LURE / REDIRECT (the player chooses where the enemy garrisons, but only during a live wave — the placement window is narrow)
**What the source says.** Where settled agents end up is not fixed by the terrain — it can be dictated externally by applying the attract-signal locally. Applying an attract-signal topically to chosen terrain pulled mobile agents into it and they PERSISTED there: elevated at 4 weeks and, critically, showing no decline between 4 and 12 weeks. Protection at that site went from 36.3% to 100%, and 57% -> 100% at the 10–12 week challenge. Two constraints matter more than the headline: (1) the pull only works during a NARROW WINDOW — it was most effective during the active-mobilization phase (day 5) and recruitment fell off sharply at later timepoints even though the agents still carried the receptor, so the redirect requires an already-mobilized population; (2) the redirected agents established permanent residence WITHOUT any local target present — the source explicitly confirms the terrain was clear of the triggering agent — so where they settle is decided purely by the signal, not by where the fight was.
**Numbers.** 36.3% -> 100% and 57% -> 100% outcome swing; residency stable from 4 to 12 weeks (no decay); pull effective at the day-5 mobilization phase, ineffective later; no local target needed for settlement
**Proposed game rule.** Second mode for the scrubber, and the far more interesting one. The scrubber damps signal (deny). A LURE EMITTER (or the scrubber running in inverted polarity, one building, two modes) instead BROADCASTS Garrison-interior into its own polygon. Settlers within 2 hops that are looking for garrison ground settle at the lure instead of where they intended — and once settled they never leave (they burned the exit ticket, candidate 4), so the lure permanently collects the enemy's territorial expansion into a killbox of the player's choosing. The narrow-window constraint is what keeps it honest: the lure only captures settlers while a wave is actively mobilized in the sector (Muster above threshold). **Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: with a lure live during a wave, garrison nucleation should concentrate at the lure polygon at a measurable rate vs. control runs, and the rest of the sector should stay clean far longer — directly testable by logging nucleation sites with lure on/off. FORBIDS: pure-denial being the only counterplay to territorial loss; FORBIDS: the lure functioning as a free permanent enemy-magnet outside combat.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's only listed countermeasure to being found and purged is damping ('you must actively cloak the chemical signature of any base you mean to keep') — a purely defensive, subtractive tool. ### D10. VITRIFICATION (a cordon held long enough becomes permanent and impassable — at the cost of the ground going economically dead, and with a dormant seed left inside)
**What the source says.** Long-standing containment structures mineralize: the wall agents themselves drive the deposition (via an enzyme they express locally), converting the structure into a hard, inactive, permanent feature. This is described as the marker of the healed/inactive phase. Frequencies: 46% of subjects showed mineralized nodes; 40% of subjects diagnosed a year earlier had them; 40.9% of subjects with scarring developed them; and 80% of single-cavity cases. Two hard constraints attach. First, mineralized nodes are NOT necessarily sterile — residual contained units persisted alongside the mineralized structure even after full clearance treatment. Second, mineralization is not free: large mineralized structures physically obstruct neighbouring passages and cause their own downstream failures. Elsewhere the same structure's endgame is the opposite failure: a shell-dissolving agent secreted by the wall builders themselves degrades the surrounding matrix and breaks the structure open, releasing 10^7–10^9 contained units — the shell is a bomb if the dissolving signal wins.
**Numbers.** 46% / 40% / 40.9% / 80% mineralization frequencies across cohorts; mineralized nodes can still hold live units; contained population at burst 10^7–10^9
**Proposed game rule.** The permanent-clear outcome from candidate 5 has a visible terminal state and a price. A polygon that stays held past the hazard draw VITRIFIES: the seams glaze over, enemy agents will not path through it at all (permanent, no re-arm, no cordon upkeep needed) — but the polygon goes economically dead: no salvage regenerates there, pod roots cannot extract from it, and it no longer counts as a source of components. Rate it near the measured frequencies: roughly 40–46% of long-held polygons vitrify, ~80% for dead-end single-entrance polygons (which is a nice map-shape incentive). And leave the trap: ~1 in 5 vitrified polygons retains a DORMANT seed — if an adjacent polygon is ever lost, it cracks and fires one late garrison from ground the player had written off. Symmetrically, an ENCYSTED player firebase (candidate 6) whose ring is left alone too long can be dissolved from outside by the ring's own builders and burst inward all at once — the wall around you is a countdown, not a stalemate.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a long run should produce a map with visibly glazed dead corridors that are safe and worthless, so the player is pushed forward instead of nesting — and that is checkable by counting vitrified polygons and salvage income over run length. PREDICTS: a player who thinks he owns the map still gets one surprise from behind (the dormant seed) at a rate around 20%. FORBIDS: infinite safe farmable territory; FORBIDS: 'held ground' being strictly better than contested ground in every dimension.
**Beyond the current spec.** Additive to the containment reward, but it closes the loop the spec leaves open: as written, 'fully clearing and cordoning an area clears it permanently' is a pure win with no downside, which means the optimal strategy converges on slow total map lockdown — exactly the turtling the spec elsewhere tries to punish. Vitrification makes permanent safety and economic productivity mutually exclusive, so total lockdown starves you, and the dormant-seed rule keeps even owned ground slightly alive.

## LANE E — THE PRODUCTION ORGAN AS A SYSTEM OF AGENTS (the CORE)

### E1. TWO STOCKS, ONE INCOMPRESSIBLE PIPE — surge comes only from the depot, never from the factory
**What the source says.** A substantial review of a large distributed production-and-dispatch system separates the producer population into a DIVIDING tier set and a NON-DIVIDING maturation/holding tier set, and measures the transit time through the non-dividing portion at 5–6 days. The review states flatly that this maturation lag is 'inherently slow' and that 'no data on disease-induced shortening of this period have been accurately described' — i.e. demand does NOT speed the pipe up. It therefore concludes that the early surge of agents seen the moment a threat appears 'is caused by recruitment of cells from an already differentiated pool', not by new production. The same system holds 5–10× more finished agents parked in the depot than are in transit at any moment, and dispatches ~10^11 finished units/day. A separate primary paper times the two phases apart: depot dumping peaks at 6 hours, upstream production only starts climbing at day 1–2, and the depot does not refill until days 6–8. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022826/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3104996/
**Proposed game rule.** The marrow reserve stops being one scalar and becomes TWO stocks with different physics. READY = finished agents standing in the depot; IN_PIPE = an array of N tier-slots holding unfinished agents. Dispatch through a breach draws ONLY from READY. A production tick advances IN_PIPE one slot every T seconds and T is a CONSTANT — no demand signal, no player behaviour, no difficulty setting may shorten it. Demand may only raise the INTAKE rate at slot 0. Suggested scaling (source ratios preserved, 1 day ≈ 12 s of run time): 5 pipe slots at 12 s each = 60 s end-to-end lag; READY cap ≈ 5–10× the number of agents the map can hold simultaneously. Consequence to implement deliberately: after a big call empties READY, there is a TROUGH of ~60 s during which almost nothing crosses, followed by a delayed second wave.
**Predicts / forbids.** FORBIDS: any code path where raising pressure/difficulty produces more enemies within the same second; forbids the CORE being modelled as a single number that goes down when you shoot it. PREDICTS a three-phase signature that must be visible in a real run log of crossings/sec: (1) fast burst as READY drains, (2) a measurable trough of ~55–65 s where crossing rate falls near zero even though the enemy 'wants' to send more, (3) a delayed second wave as the pipe delivers. PREDICTS that destroying the CORE does NOT stop arrivals for ~60 s — the in-flight slots still deliver. Verification: plot READY and IN_PIPE and crossings/sec on one timeline; if there is no trough, the rule is not implemented.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 §Marrow economy has one quantity: 'a finite reserve that regenerates slowly'. That gives a monotone drain with no rhythm. Splitting it into a spendable depot plus an incompressible pipe creates the run's tactical clock for free — the trough IS the player's window to push, and the 'output continues after the organ is hit' behaviour the director asked for falls out of the structure instead of needing a special case.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022826/

### E2. DEMAND IS SENSED AS DEPOT EMPTINESS, NOT AS COMBAT
**What the source says.** A primary experimental paper establishes that the control variable driving a central production organ's output rate is the DENSITY OF FINISHED AGENTS SITTING IN THE DEPOT — not any detection of the threat. Reported numbers: inverse correlation between depot stock and upstream producer activity of R = −0.84 to −0.94 (P ≤ 0.01); a graded-depletion dose series produced PROPORTIONAL responses with R = −0.89, which the authors call density-sensing rather than threshold-detection; upstream activity rises within 1 day of depot depletion; depot rebound at days 6–8; baseline back at days 4–6. Four separate controls show the trigger is emptiness and not the enemy: (a) emptying the depot by a method that generates minimal disturbance produced a response 'indistinguishable' from a real threat; (b) the response survives removal of the threat-recognition receptor pathway entirely (the pathway a real threat uses to raise the signal is NOT the pathway an empty depot uses); (c) it survives removal of the identity-recognition machinery; (d) it survives removal of the adaptive/learning subsystem. Sustained elevated output after the first dump is explicitly attributed to depot emptiness rather than to ongoing threat. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3104996/
**Proposed game rule.** CORE intake rate at pipe slot 0 is a pure function of its own deficit and contains NO term referencing the player: intake_rate = k * (READY_cap - READY_now) / READY_cap. Nothing else — not kills, not Distress, not player hp, not run time. Whatever spends the depot, by any mechanism, is what makes the CORE build more. Set k so a fully drained depot refills in ~90–110 s if nothing is dispatched (matches the source's day-6-to-8 rebound against a day-1 onset, scaled). This is architecturally decoupling-compliant: the CORE reads one number it owns and never queries the player or any other system.
**Predicts / forbids.** FORBIDS the entire class of 'the enemy ramps because the player is doing well' difficulty hacks, and forbids any player-referencing term in the regen equation. PREDICTS, and this is the discriminating test: a player who pins a breach open and farms the stream is TRAINING THE FACTORY — intake climbs proportionally to what he kills, with no aggro term involved. PREDICTS the converse: a player who avoids all contact leaves READY at cap, deficit ≈ 0, intake ≈ 0, and the CORE idles. Verification on a real run: scatter intake_rate against READY deficit; it must be a straight line through the origin with |r| > 0.85, and a multiple regression adding player kill count or Distress must add ~zero explanatory power.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 specifies passive slow regen plus a separate Distress-threshold anti-turtle punishment (the flank breach). Depot-deficit sensing gives the anti-farm behaviour for free, without a punishment, without a threshold, and without the CORE knowing the player exists — and it is a strictly better fit to the ratified Adversary constitution ('responds to disturbance, not identity'). It also means the flank-breach threshold no longer has to carry the whole anti-turtle load.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3104996/

### E3. THE GUARDIAN RING IS THE DEPOT — dispatch and defence draw on the same stock
**What the source says.** Two sources independently give the parked-versus-deployed ratio of a large agent reserve. A primary paper measures 1.84% ± 0.32% of the finished agent population in transit under baseline conditions — i.e. ~98% is parked in the depot at any moment — and shows the depot can be flipped open to release 3.8× ± 1.2 within 1 hour, or 3.8× ± 0.5 within 30 minutes under a direct release challenge. An encyclopedia-depth article gives the standing distribution across depot:transit:deployed as 28:1:25. A third source describes a rare, very large, immobile producer class that docks DIRECTLY against the exit vessel and extrudes 2,000–5,000 finished units through the wall without them ever crossing the depot floor; these producers are 1 in 10,000 of the organ's population, 50–100 µm across (10–15× a normal unit), and accumulate up to 32 copies of their internal template before releasing. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2898597/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutrophil , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megakaryocyte
**Proposed game rule.** Delete the idea of a separate hand-authored 'guardian roster' for the hub. The guardian ring IS the READY depot rendered as live entities standing in the vault arena. Dispatching to a breach physically walks those entities out of the room. The vault's defensive strength therefore equals its depot stock, and every crossing weakens it. Additionally place 1–2 EXTRUDERS: immobile, very large, very rare producers docked against the vault-side breach mouth that push agents through in bursts of 2–5 without them traversing the vault floor, holding up to 32 charges. Extruders make the vault dangerous to approach from the breach mouth even when the floor is empty, and are the only thing still shipping while the guard ring is out.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a strong ANTI-correlation between on-map reinforcement count and vault garrison strength, which the player can deliberately manufacture: bait a mass call (or hold a fortress and let them come), then dive the breach into a nearly-empty vault. FORBIDS the vault being uniformly hard regardless of map state, and forbids the player ever finding the vault at full strength immediately after fighting a large wave. Verification: log vault occupancy and on-map reinforcement count each second across a full run; correlation must be strongly negative (target r < −0.7). If the vault is equally defended before and after a wave, the rule is not wired.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 describes 'a ring of guardian agents' as a static fixture and separately says the hub is 'reachable ONLY through a live breach, and VERY hard'. Those two facts are in tension — the player can only arrive through a breach, which is exactly the moment the ring should be deployed elsewhere. Unifying ring and depot resolves the tension into the best strategic lever in the whole system, at zero extra content cost, and it turns the hub raid into a thing you SET UP rather than a thing you survive.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2898597/

### E4. FOUR-TIER ORGAN — few permanent sources, a wide amplifier band, a finisher band, a fat depot
**What the source says.** Sources give a consistent shape: a very small, very slow, permanent source pool feeding a fast-dividing amplifier band feeding a non-dividing finishing band feeding a large parked depot. Numbers harvested: the source population fires on average once every 40 weeks (range 25–50 weeks); the amplifier band contains ~2×10^9 units of which 50% divide EACH DAY, against a total organ population of ~10^12; the finishing band takes 5–6 days and cannot divide at all; the depot holds 5–10× the in-transit population (28:1 by another measure); total dispatch is ~10^11–5×10^11 finished units/day out of a 3.7 kg organ that is ~5% of total system mass. A tier-count claim of 'up to five divisions' in the amplifier band appears in secondary summaries, but see dead ends — the reviews I fetched explicitly refuse to commit to a division count. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022826/ , https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21343613/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow
**Proposed game rule.** The CORE is four tiers of ENTITY, all individually killable, arranged by depth in the vault. TIER 0 SEEDS: 6–8 entities, deepest chamber, never dispatched, never move, fire one downstream unit + one replacement on a very long timer (~one per 6–10 min of run time — the 40-week ratio scaled against a 60 s pipe). TIER 1 AMPLIFIERS: each seed output becomes an amplifier that splits ~4× over ~90 s → ×16 per seed output; roughly half the amplifier population is mid-split at any moment (the '50% divide each day' figure). TIER 2 FINISHERS: cannot split, mature on the fixed 60 s pipe. TIER 3 = READY depot = the guardian ring (candidate 3). Ratio target: depot population ≈ 25–30× on-map reinforcement count. Kill economics fall out: killing FINISHERS is nearly free damage with no throughput effect (they were arriving anyway); killing AMPLIFIERS collapses throughput within ~2 min but the depot still supplies the map; killing SEEDS is the only permanent damage, and they are the deepest and best defended.
**Predicts / forbids.** FORBIDS the CORE ever being one high-HP NPC, and forbids uniform damage value across the vault — where you shoot must matter more than how much you shoot. PREDICTS a legible depth gradient the player can learn without a tutorial: shallow kills feel unrewarding, deep kills feel decisive. PREDICTS specific measurable curves — amplifier kills should cut steady-state dispatch rate with a ~90–120 s lag and a magnitude of roughly (killed/total)×16 units of lost throughput; finisher kills should show ~zero effect on dispatch rate 3 minutes later. Verification: three scripted runs killing only tier 1, only tier 2, only tier 0, comparing dispatch rate at T+180 s.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 lists 'The CORE as an organ — a system of agents with regeneration and its own defense, not a static NPC' as an OPEN design unit with no content. This is the missing content: a concrete tier count, an amplification factor per tier, a population ratio between tiers, and a per-tier kill-value gradient that makes the vault's spatial layout mechanically meaningful rather than decorative.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022826/

### E5. KILL THE ANCHORS, NOT THE PRODUCERS — output stops because producers unmoor and wander off
**What the source says.** Production in this class of organ does not happen at arbitrary locations; it happens in CLUSTERS held together by named physical attachments to a stationary support entity. One source describes the cluster unit as '1 or 2 central support entities closely surrounded by a minimum of 3' maturing units, progressing through '4 to 5 rapid divisions' while docked; the anchor supplies growth signals, raw material (iron), waste disposal, and even transfers working power plants down physical nanotubes into the docked units. Attachment is by specific named pairs (MAEA, VCAM1–α4β1, ICAM-4–αV, EphrinB2–EphB4, CD169–CD43); blocking one pair 'significantly suppresses' cluster formation, and deleting another 'disrupts the formation process'. A second source shows that when the ANCHOR degrades rather than the producer, the producer fails anyway: young source units 'could not be maintained nor undergo' production when transplanted into an aged support environment, and loss of physical attachment causes producers to lose identity and differentiate away. A third source reports that deleting the anchor's retention signal causes the producers to MOBILISE — they leave the organ and enter circulation. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11932602/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell_niche , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4459580/
**Proposed game rule.** Tier-1 and tier-2 production only runs at ANCHOR structures: stationary, unarmed, moderate-HP support entities, 1 anchor per cluster, each holding 3–5 docked maturing agents (visible, tethered, non-combatant while docked). Killing an anchor DOES NOT kill its docked agents — they undock, instantly become ordinary low-tier combat agents, and attack. The cluster's production slot goes to zero and stays zero until a seed grows a replacement anchor (~40 s). So the anchor is the efficient target and the docked mob is the price. Anchors are cheap to kill and visually obvious (they are the thing everything else is attached to), which is what makes the vault readable at a glance.
**Predicts / forbids.** FORBIDS 'shoot the biggest HP bar' as a winning vault strategy. PREDICTS an explicit, verifiable risk/reward trade: every anchor killed produces an immediate local spike of +3 to +5 hostile agents AND a lasting throughput cut. PREDICTS that a player who kills docked agents first (before the anchor) faces a smaller spike but wastes time, since the anchor just re-docks new ones. Verification: instrument anchors_destroyed against dispatch_rate over the following 120 s; each anchor kill must produce a step down in dispatch rate, and a matching hostile-count spike within 2 s of the anchor dying.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 has no notion of internal structure inside the CORE at all — no support entities, no distinction between 'the thing producing' and 'the thing holding the producers in place'. This is what turns the CORE from a room full of enemies into an ORGAN with parts that have different jobs, and it supplies the vault fight's core decision (spike now for throughput later) without any scripting.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11932602/

### E6. THE CROSSING GATE IS A TUG-OF-WAR, AND IT FILTERS BY MATURITY
**What the source says.** Two independent controls govern whether a finished agent may leave the organ, and they pull against each other. A primary paper measures: baseline 1.84% ± 0.32% of the population in transit; remove the RELEASE signal receptor and it falls to 0.57% ± 0.18%; remove the RETENTION signal receptor and release becomes constitutive; remove BOTH and release is still constitutive — retention dominates. Blocking retention pharmacologically gives a 3.8× ± 1.2 rise within 1 hour; a direct release-signal challenge gives 3.8× ± 0.5 within 30 minutes; but with the release receptor missing, blocking retention produces NO release at all (0.53% ± 0.21% versus 13.0% ± 3.27%) — the two signals are genuinely antagonistic, not independent. Under system-wide stress the release ligand rises 2.7× ± 0.3 while the retention signal falls to 47% ± 3% of basal. Separately, the exit wall is a hard MATURITY FILTER: 'only mature units contain the membrane proteins required to attach to and pass' the wall — unfinished units physically cannot cross even if they want to. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2898597/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow
**Proposed game rule.** Breach crossing rate = clamp(release_pressure − retention_pressure). RETENTION is emitted by the CORE's anchors and scales with how empty the depot is (a low depot holds its guards home). RELEASE is the Noise/Distress arriving through the breach mouth from the map side. Both must be nonzero for anything to cross — zero release means no crossings no matter how full the depot, which is the mechanical statement of 'if it calls and nothing is happening, nothing comes'. Calibration from the source: baseline outflow ≈ 1.8% of depot per dispatch window; a maximal call moves that ~3.8× within 30–60 s; a strike on the CORE drops retention to ~47% of basal. HARD RULE at the gate: only tier-3 (finished) agents may cross a breach. Tier 0/1/2 entities physically cannot pass, in either direction.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS the counter-intuitive moment that makes this worth building: hitting the CORE COLLAPSES RETENTION AND FLOODS THE MAP BEFORE IT STARVES IT. Damage the organ and outflow spikes to roughly 2× for ~30–60 s, then falls off a cliff. A player who dives the vault without a firebase behind him gets punished by his own success. FORBIDS half-finished trash ever leaking through a breach, so the player never sees a weak arrival and the breach always reads as dangerous. FORBIDS crossings when the map is quiet even at full depot. Verification: script a CORE strike at a known time in a real run and read the crossings/sec trace — a spike-then-collapse must be visible; a monotone decline means the retention term is missing.

### E7. DORMANT INNER SANCTUM — a protected deep pool that contributes nothing until the depot hits zero
**What the source says.** Modelling of the source pool splits it in two with sharply different rates. Dormant fraction: 30–45% of the pool, firing once every 149–193 days. Active fraction: 55–70%, firing once every 28–36 days. Under stress BOTH fire at approximately once every 10 days — a ~15–19× speedup for the dormant fraction and ~3× for the active. After the stress is withdrawn it takes about 10 days for the dormant fraction to regain dormancy — the wake is fast, the return to sleep is slow. A second review gives the dormant fraction as 15–30% and its interval as ~145 days (~5 firings in an entire system lifetime), states the dormant pool 'does not significantly contribute to daily production under steady-state conditions', has 'the highest long-term repopulation capacity' of anything in the system, and is DAMAGE-PROTECTED precisely because it is not cycling — dormant units survive damage that destroys cycling ones, and deliberately waking them before a damage event is described as fatal. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2745578/ , https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/hematology/articles/10.3389/frhem.2024.1401713/full
**Proposed game rule.** Split the 6–8 tier-0 SEEDS into ACTIVE (≈60%, outer vault chamber, normal HP, feeds the pipe) and DORMANT (≈40%, innermost chamber, contributes ZERO throughput at steady state, and takes heavily reduced damage while dormant). Dormant seeds wake only on two conditions: READY hits zero, OR active seeds drop below ~30% of their starting count. Woken seeds fire at ~15× their dormant rate — i.e. they match active rate — and stay awake for 90 s after the last triggering condition clears (the 'slow to sleep again' asymmetry). While awake they lose the damage reduction. This is the direct answer to 'what fraction of the organ must survive to regenerate': the dormant chamber is that fraction, and it is ~40%.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that a player who kills every ACTIVE seed sees output stop, feels like he won, and then meets a delayed hard second surge as the sanctum wakes — with the sanctum's guards now mobile. PREDICTS the ONLY way to change a run permanently is to reach the innermost chamber, which gives the vault a genuine final room. FORBIDS the player permanently disabling the organ from the doorway or by attrition across deaths. FORBIDS the reduced-damage state being permanent — once awake, the sanctum is killable, so the correct play is to force the wake and then push. Verification: a run that kills all active seeds must show dispatch resuming within ~20 s of the depot reaching zero, sourced from the sanctum, with sanctum damage-taken per shot jumping at the wake instant.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 asks 'what fraction of the organ must survive for it to regenerate' as an open question and gives no answer. This answers it with a number (≈40%), gives that fraction a PHYSICAL LOCATION (the innermost chamber), gives it a mechanical reason to be damage-resistant that is not arbitrary armour, and creates a scripted-feeling second-phase vault fight from pure system rules with no boss script.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2745578/

### E8. DOWNTIME IS A DOSE CURVE, NOT A TIMER
**What the source says.** A clinical-scale dataset of 271 rebuilds of a destroyed production organ from a transplanted seed pool measures recovery as a function of SEED DOSE. Median time for the fast output line to come back: 10 days (range 8–24). Median for the slow output line: 11 days (range 7–40) — note the slow line's range is far WIDER. Higher seed dose correlates significantly with faster recovery for both lines (r = −0.32, P < 0.001 and r = −0.27, P < 0.001). Dose groups compared were below 5×10^6 per unit mass (38.1% of cases) and at-or-above (61.9%). Critically: ZERO total failures — 'no engraftment failure occurred in any patient'. The organ always came back; only the SPEED varied. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7775700/
**Proposed game rule.** Replace the fixed 'a couple of minutes' downtime constant with recovery_time = base / max(1, surviving_seed_count). Preserve the source's two-line shape: the DEPOT refills on a tight, predictable schedule (narrow range — say 55–70 s of jitter band), while the AMPLIFIER band refills on a loose, unpredictable one (wide range — 50–150 s). So after a CORE kill the player gets a reasonably reliable estimate of when trash resumes, and a genuinely unreliable one for when the heavy stuff resumes. And, per the source's zero-failure result: the layer ALWAYS comes back — there is no seed count at which the organ is permanently dead short of killing every seed including the sanctum.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS run-to-run variance in downtime that is explained by how deep the player got before he had to run, not by RNG — the reward for a deeper raid is a longer window, continuously. FORBIDS a fixed timer, and forbids the downtime being a fixed reward for a binary 'core killed' event. PREDICTS the asymmetry is felt: players will learn to trust the 'when do the small ones come back' estimate and distrust the 'when do the big ones come back' estimate. Verification: across ≥20 recorded raids, scatter downtime against seeds killed; must be monotone decreasing, and the variance of heavy-agent-return time must be materially larger than the variance of light-agent-return time.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 says 'the reinforcement layer goes down for a couple of minutes (tune deep-negative depth × regen rate to that spec)' — one constant to be hand-tuned. This replaces a magic number with a dose-response the player can steer, and it makes 'how much of the vault did you clear before you bailed' into a continuously-valued decision rather than a binary. It also supplies a principled reason the layer can never be permanently switched off, which the spec asserts but does not mechanise.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7775700/

### E9. THE AMPLIFIER BAND BROADCASTS ITS OWN RECRUITMENT SIGNAL AND OPENS A SHORTCUT
**What the source says.** A primary paper identifies a SECRETORY SUBSET of the intermediate amplifier tier — ~31% of that tier at steady state — which, on stimulus, emits the very same signals that drive production of its own downstream lineage (a list including the principal production-driving signals of the whole system). Its emitted medium ALONE induced downstream production from naive upstream units 'in the range of full cytokine stimulation', i.e. the tier can drive the organ almost as hard as the organ's own top-level controller. The signal is LOCAL: these units sit 143 ± 82 µm from the nearest source units, close enough for local diffusion. Timing after the finished-agent depot is emptied: this subset expands at days 2–3, and moves measurably CLOSER to the source pool by day 2. Functionally it creates a BYPASS ROUTE that skips an intermediate stage entirely — the paper describes it as 'a self-reinforcing amplification compartment' producing an 'accelerated burst'. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10140385/
**Proposed game rule.** Designate ~1/3 of tier-1 AMPLIFIERS as BROADCASTERS. While alive and stimulated (depot below ~30%), a broadcaster emits a short-range field inside the vault that (a) raises nearby seeds' emit rate and (b) unlocks a SHORTCUT edge letting tier-1 output skip tier 2 entirely. Shortcut agents arrive ~40% sooner but ship at reduced hp and damage — visibly 'green', a different silhouette tint. Broadcasters migrate toward the seed chamber ~25 s after a depot crash (mirroring the day-2 approach) and their count grows ~30 s later. Range is deliberately SHORT — a broadcaster only boosts seeds in its own chamber, so vault layout determines how much it can amplify.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS a legible composition shift the player can read WITHOUT a HUD: during a sustained siege, breach output visibly degrades in quality while increasing in rate — that is the tell that the organ is running hot and the depot is empty. FORBIDS a static enemy mix across a run. PREDICTS broadcasters are the highest-value tier-1 target (killing one both cuts seed rate and closes the shortcut), giving the amplifier band internal target priority rather than being an undifferentiated mob. Verification: histogram arriving-agent quality in 30 s buckets across a long run; the mean must drop measurably after the first depot crash and recover as the depot refills. If the mix is flat, the rule is not wired.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 lists 'adaptive counter-biasing' and 'evidence relay' as aspirational learning systems — expensive, hard to verify, and easy to get wrong. This produces an observable, dynamic enemy-mix change from SUPPLY-SIDE PRESSURE alone: no learning model, no memory of the player, no coupling to the player at all, and it is trivially verifiable from a run log. It is the cheap version of the effect the spec wants.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10140385/

### E10. FIELD PRODUCTION SITES — when the organ is suppressed, held ground starts manufacturing
**What the source says.** When the central production organ is unable to operate — because it has been scarred over, damaged, or otherwise disabled — production RELOCATES and restarts at improvised secondary sites rather than simply stopping. The listed triggers are: severe loss of finished units activating the system's homeostatic machinery, organ dysfunction, organ damage, and system-wide disturbance. Site priority is ordered: one large filter organ is the commonest secondary site, then the liver, then the lymph nodes, and 'in extremis' a long list of opportunistic locations including thymus, heart, adrenal glands, kidneys, pleura, skin, nerves, spinal canal and retroperitoneal tissue. A separate source notes secondary-site clusters reorganise under stress, with anchors transferring working power plants into docked units to accelerate their proliferation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extramedullary_hematopoiesis , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11932602/
**Proposed game rule.** While the CORE is dead or the reserve is negative, any emergent garrison on the map that meets a size threshold (≥N agents settled, per the existing emergent-garrison rule) grows a FIELD ANCHOR at its centre — visible, slowly forming, unarmed — and begins producing low-tier agents locally at a small fixed rate (say 1 per 20 s per garrison, capped). Site priority mirrors the source ordering: the largest garrison first, then smaller ones, and only under a long suppression do marginal sites activate. Field anchors are killable in a few seconds and do not regrow while the CORE is back online. A field anchor is the SAME entity class as the vault anchors (candidate 5) — one implementation, two placements.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS that 'kill the core, then sprint across the map' is punished exactly in proportion to how much fortified enemy ground the player left behind him, and is rewarded in proportion to how well he cordoned — which is the containment reward the spec already wants, now with teeth. PREDICTS a specific verifiable log signature: during the downtime window, spawn-source attribution must show nonzero garrison-origin spawns IF AND ONLY IF garrisons exist; a fully cordoned run must show exactly zero. Verification: two scripted runs of the same seed, one cordoned and one not, comparing spawns during the identical downtime window.
**Beyond the current spec.** DESIGN_7_25 says a core kill takes 'the reinforcement layer offline for the duration' — a flat global effect. This gives that window a texture that varies with how the player has played, and it directly couples the CORE system to the garrison/containment system that already exists in the spec, using the shared substrate rather than a new pathway. The spec's scrubber currently reads as a strength/radius tuning problem ('damps ALL chemo-signals in its area'); a flood-damper is a different verb with a different, self-limiting failure mode, and it makes the spec's own line 'unscrubbed bases eventually getting found and purged is intended' arrive by a mechanism rather than by a probability roll.

### F2. Payload cliff — Transmit carries an evidence magnitude, and below a sharp threshold the dispatch NEVER fires no matter how long the broadcast runs
**What the source says.** The primary study varied the amount of evidence a stationary agent carried and measured the responders' behavior. The result is a cliff, not a ramp: 'DCs pulsed with peptide concentrations of at least 200 pM triggered ... phase two–like interactions,' while at half that dose 'DCs that had been pulsed with 100 pM ... did not promote long-lasting T cell contacts over at least 12 h.' A 2× reduction in payload converts 'full commitment' into 'nothing ever happens, even given 12 hours.' The per-contact payload counts are given as ~12.7 versus ~313 units for the two dose regimes. The engagement also has a fixed three-stage shape: ~8 h of brief serial contacts, then stable locked contacts beginning at 6–8 h and lasting ~12 h, then at ~24 h a return to short contacts.
**Proposed game rule.** Transmit is not a timer that either completes or is interrupted. The spotter accumulates a PAYLOAD scalar during Infiltrate — incremented per second of line-of-sight on the player, per player weapon discharge heard within 1 hop, per friendly death witnessed, per player-built structure seen. On reaching the transmit spot it broadcasts, and the hub's response is a step function of payload at the moment the broadcast resolves: below threshold P_min, the broadcast completes and NOTHING OPENS. At or above P_min, a breach opens; above 2·P_min, the breach opens with a larger first surge. Set the two regimes an exact factor of 2 apart so the cliff is teachable. Give the broadcast the three-stage shape: stage A (~2.5 s) wind-up, spotter still mobile-ish and can abort; stage B (~6 s) LOCKED — fully stationary, fully exposed, this is the whole counterplay window; stage C the spotter unlocks and walks off. Killing it in A or B denies the call; killing it in C denies only the NEXT call.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a player who kills a spotter's witnesses and breaks line of sight suppresses reinforcements WITHOUT ever finding the spotter — a second, non-violent counterplay axis. PREDICTS: run logs will show completed broadcasts that produced no breach, and their count should be non-trivial (target roughly 20–40% of broadcasts in careful play, near 0% in loud play); if that number is 0% the payload accumulator is saturating instantly and P_min is set wrong. FORBIDS: reinforcement volume being a pure function of elapsed time. FORBIDS: a spotter that saw nothing calling in a full wave. FALSIFIER: if breach counts correlate with run duration as tightly after the change as before, payload is not gating anything.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's Transmit is binary and time-shaped: 'stationary and fully exposed for the whole broadcast,' interruptible or not. It has no notion of the call carrying CONTENT, so the only counterplay is a kill inside a window. The payload cliff adds an information-denial counterplay that the spec does not have anywhere, and it gives the director's stated intent — 'aggressive perfect play avoids reinforcements entirely' — an actual mechanism: aggression suppresses reinforcements because dead witnesses carry no payload, not because of a special-cased rule. The three-stage shape also fixes an ambiguity in the spec: right now 'the whole broadcast' is the exposure window, which makes long broadcasts strictly worse for the enemy; splitting it means only the middle segment is worth the player's bullet.

### F3. LACUNA — the sitter: samples the NEXT polygon through a closed doorway without entering it, and the doorway stays sealed
**What the source says.** The primary study documents resident agents that stay put and instead extend probes THROUGH a sealed boundary to acquire material from the far side. The barrier is not breached to do it: the study reports the boundary's integrity was unaltered by the sampling, describing the mechanism as capture 'through' the sealed junctions rather than across a broken one. The framing the authors use is 'preemptive' — the response begins against material that has not crossed the boundary at all. A companion review of the same class notes the probes reaching all the way to the outermost layer. In a separate primary study of a related stationary class, probes average 19 µm (SE 0.58) with some exceeding 60 µm, and probe tips move at up to 40 µm/min while the agent's body moves at only 2–3 µm/min — the sensing apparatus is more than ten times faster than the body (PMC327133).
**Proposed game rule.** Make this the lacuna's defining verb and the weaker of the two spotter classes. The lacuna is nearly immobile (body speed ~0.35× Commando), hugs walls, and its primary action is PROBE: it plants itself at a doorway and extends a visible tendril one hop into the adjacent polygon for ~3 s, harvesting that polygon's find/senses result — player position, structures, danger — WITHOUT its body entering. Payload accrues from probing. Critically, the probe must not count as a crossing for any other system: it does not trip a turret's engagement test (turrets fire on bodies), it does not deposit chalk in the probed polygon, and it does not raise Noise there. The tendril itself is the tell — a thin lit filament through the doorway, visible to a player who is looking at the doorway, invisible to one who is not. Counter: a turret whose facing covers the doorway MOUTH (not the volume beyond it) severs the probe; the pheromone scrubber's flood makes probe results useless.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: a cordon built the obvious way — turrets covering the interiors of the polygons behind the line — will leak. Areas the player believes are cordoned will still get breached, and the run log will show the responsible lacuna never entered any cordoned polygon. That is the intended lesson, and it is verifiable: log 'polygons entered' per spotter and check that successful raids on cordoned ground have entry counts of zero. PREDICTS: turret placement shifts from covering rooms to covering doorframes. FORBIDS: 'I cleared this area and put a turret in it, therefore it is held' — holding requires denying observation of the doorway, not denying passage through it. FORBIDS: the lacuna being an effective fighter or an effective long-distance traveler.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec asserts a containment reward — 'Fully clearing and cordoning an area clears it permanently, as long as nothing slips through the cordon' — and separately asserts that 'some types deliberately try' to slip past. Both are stated as outcomes with no mechanism, and as written the cordon is a solved problem the moment the player owns enough turrets. This gives the cordon a real, learnable hole that is not just 'a fast enemy ran past.' It also answers the open sub-question 'design vagrant and lacuna for real' for one of the two classes by giving it a verb no other agent in the game has, rather than differentiating the two classes by hit points.

### F4. VAGRANT — the roamer: power-law patrol legs with a PER-AGENT exponent drawn from a spread, and a visible collapse into slow confined search on contact with a lead
**What the source says.** The motility review tabulates measured crawl speeds of agents patrolling along the inner boundary of transit lanes, and the numbers scale with lane width: narrow lanes ≈9–17 µm/min (kidney ≈9, mesentery ≈9, lung ≈10, dermis 17), wide lanes far faster (carotid 36 µm/min — the review states this is 3× the narrow-lane figure — femoral 12 µm/min). Track segments: ≈80 µm in ≈9 min, ≈200 µm in ≈20 min, 249 µm in 14 min, and 134 µm in 4.7 min in the wide lane. Under alarm conditions the motion COLLAPSES: 2.3 µm/min diffusive falling to 0–1 µm/min confined; another condition falls 6.7 → 1.5 µm/min over 20 hours. The shape changes with the mode — agents 'appear round' during fast flights and 'alternate between elongating, while crawling in a more meticulous manner by an amoeboid movement, and being round during flights.' The review explicitly frames this as a two-mode search: 'exploration' (fast, undirected) switching to 'exploitation' (slow, informed). It also warns that the obvious metric is useless: 'straightness seems less informative in describing PMo crawling, as both random and confined walks can show similar values,' with straightness ranging 0.2–0.7 across all conditions. The search-strategy math it appeals to gives an inverse-square step-length exponent (α = 2) as optimal for sparse randomly placed targets without memory, notes a 2020 result that this holds in 2D/3D only under narrow conditions, notes that under INTERMITTENT search (detection only during pauses between jumps) inverse-square is 'the only intermittent Lévy process that is highly efficient with respect to all target scales without the need for any adaptation,' and gives a collective-search optimum of α* ≈ 3 − log k / log D for k searchers at target distance D — with the striking practical note that 'sampling uniformly from the interval (2, 3)' across group members is near-optimal at ALL distance scales, implying 'different members of the same group follow different search patterns.'
**Proposed game rule.** The vagrant is the roamer and the stronger of the two spotter classes. Its Infiltrate leg lengths are drawn from a power law P(l) ∝ l^−α, with α FIXED PER AGENT AT SPAWN, sampled uniformly from [2.0, 3.0] — so a group of vagrants is heterogeneous by construction: the α≈2 ones make long straight runs deep behind the line, the α≈3 ones grind over local ground. One knob, one uniform draw, and squad behavior stops being homogeneous without any leader logic. Detection is INTERMITTENT: the vagrant only runs its find/senses query during the pause between legs, so a long fast leg is genuinely blind — it can and should run straight past the player. Speed scales with the polygon's width class: ~3× faster crossing wide open chambers than tight corridors, which puts the wide-open parts of the map on the vagrant's preferred route and makes narrow ground genuinely safer to move through. On acquiring any lead (Trail scent, a sighting, chalk), the vagrant COLLAPSES into exploitation mode: speed drops to roughly 1/5, legs get short, and its silhouette elongates — an unmistakable long-range read that this one has your scent, and the player's cue to kill it before it starts accruing payload.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: with per-agent α spread, two vagrants spawned together end up at very different distances from the spawn point after 60 s; without it they clump. Directly checkable in the farm: log displacement variance across a cohort. PREDICTS: vagrants will visibly run past the player during long legs, and that will look like the old 'runs past enemies' bug — it is not, and the discriminator is whether it was mid-leg (blind) or mid-pause (should have seen). Instrument the pause/leg state in the log or this WILL be misdiagnosed. PREDICTS: mode collapse gives the player a legible 'it has my scent' tell at a distance. FORBIDS: using straightness/path-directness as the metric for whether patrol behavior is working — the source states directly that confined and random walks score alike on it; use displacement variance and area-covered-per-minute instead. FORBIDS: a vagrant that is simultaneously fast and observant.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec's patrol layer is 'patrolling agents climb the Distress gradient, looking for trouble, refusing to enter rooms with high Danger' plus a Patrol scent that marks searched ground — i.e. purely reactive, purely gradient-following, with no model of search at all when there is no gradient to climb (which is most of a quiet run). This supplies the missing behavior for the no-gradient case, and does it with a tunable that has a published optimum rather than a hand-picked number. The per-agent exponent spread also delivers, for free, something the spec explicitly defers: the 'Raid (multi-approach fan)' behavior currently needs a special rule ('~1/3 of committed searchers take their second-best route'); heterogeneous α produces fanning as a side effect of the population rather than as a hardcoded fraction. And the measurement warning is load-bearing — it names in advance the metric that will lie to us.
**Source.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8485030/

### F5. Census law — the spotter population divides in place at a crawl, the CORE never makes one, and a wiped region gets exactly ONE outside restock wave, late
**What the source says.** The primary persistence study measures how much of a local resident population is replaced from the central production site over long timescales, and the answer is essentially none: '<3% of LCs in the skin were of donor origin 8 weeks after transplantation; this percentage did not increase over 18 months,' and in a shared-circulation pairing 'no mixing of LCs was detectable for at least 6 months.' The population sustains itself by slow division in place: 'only 5–10% of LCs incorporate BrdU 10–12 days after systemic administration,' which the source itself renders as roughly 0.4–0.8% per day. After a wipe the recovery is slow and biphasic: recovery takes '4–5 weeks,' outside recruits are 'detected 2–3 weeks after' the damage, and blocking the recruits' homing route leaves '75% fewer' at 14 days. A companion review agrees on the division rate ('approximately 2-5% of LCs express Ki-67 and are in cell cycle in the steady state'), describes the two-wave restock ('a transient monocyte wave' then reconstitution 'four to six weeks later'), and — importantly — reports there is no privileged replicating sub-class: 'attempts to define a subset of LCs that act as local stem cells have been unsuccessful suggesting that mature LCs have an intrinsic ability to enter cell cycle when required,' with 'equal-sized patches' of terrain colonized by the progeny of multiple individuals (PMC4685746).
**Proposed game rule.** Give the spotter economy its own numbers, wholly separate from the marrow reserve. (a) The world spawns a fixed spotter census at init, distributed over polygons. (b) The CORE never produces one — spec already says this; hold the line. (c) Spotters replace themselves by SPLIT: any living spotter, when its polygon has been quiet (no Noise, no Danger) for a sustained period, has a low per-second chance to split into two, capped at the polygon's initial census. Tune the rate so a region regrows from half-strength to full in roughly 4–5 minutes of quiet — the compressed analogue of the source's 4–5 weeks — and so that the fraction of the census in 'about to split' state at any instant is ~2–5%. There is no special breeder unit: every spotter can split, so decapitation strategies do not exist. (d) A region wiped to ZERO gets exactly ONE outside restock wave, and it arrives LATE — around half the full regrow time (the 2–3 week analogue) — as a small group walking in from the highest-chalk neighbouring region. One wave, once, per wipe. After that the region is only repopulated by splits from neighbours walking in. (e) A region wiped to zero AND cordoned gets nothing.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: total extermination is a real but impractical strategy exactly as the spec wants — the split cap means killing spotters faster than they split is winnable locally, and the single restock wave means it is not permanent unless the cordon holds. Verifiable: farm run with a scripted total wipe of one region should show breach calls in that region drop to zero, then resume once at wave-arrival time, then stay at zero if the cordon holds. PREDICTS: the census is a monotone-decreasing pressure gauge over a long run and should be logged as such. FORBIDS: a spotter ever emerging from a breach. FORBIDS: killing one 'queen' collapsing the spotter economy. FORBIDS: infinite grinding of spotters for resources. FALSIFIER: if the census is flat or rising across a full run despite heavy killing, the split rate is wrong by an order of magnitude.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec states the outcome — 'The Adversaries are a finite population. Exterminating that population permanently disables called breaches — intended, and in practice near-unachievable' — but supplies no regeneration mechanism at all, which means as written the population is either strictly monotone decreasing (so a patient player wins by attrition, contradicting 'near-unachievable') or has an unspecified respawn (contradicting 'finite'). Split-in-place with a per-polygon cap resolves that contradiction with two numbers. The 'no privileged breeder' finding is the part nobody would invent at a desk: the obvious design instinct is a spotter-hive or a nest object to destroy, and the source says the real system deliberately has none, which is exactly the spec's own 'no commander to kill' constitution applied one level down.

### F6. Widest-gap commit — the spotter probes several doorways at once, always takes the widest, and if it is interrupted mid-commit it TEARS APART
**What the source says.** The research summary of the 2019 pathfinding result describes agents that do not path by planning. They physically insert probes into 'several adjacent pores' simultaneously and use their own rigid core as 'a kind of mechanical gauge or ruler' to measure which opening is largest; 'whenever the cells had a choice, they used the bigger pores, i.e. the path of least resistance.' Commitment is the core passing through the chosen opening — after which the probes still inserted in the rejected openings MUST be retracted, or the agent will 'become entangled or even fragmented.' The retraction is coordinated by an internal scaffold that trails the core; when the experimenters destroyed that scaffold, 'the cells entangled because the supernumerary protrusions had not retracted,' the leftover probes 'kept moving forward through other pores' in multiple directions at once, and the agent would 'fracture into pieces and died.' A separate source on the same class of agents notes that this whole style of movement needs no cutting tools and no adhesion — entry into an opening 'requires neither tool' (PMC2806457), with agents inserting a projection and then swelling until the gap widens.
**Proposed game rule.** Replace the spotter's doorway choice with a physical commit. At any junction the spotter extends probes into the 2–3 candidate doorways SIMULTANEOUSLY (visible tendrils, ~1.5 s) and then commits to the one scoring highest on a WIDTH-like metric — portal aperture width first, chalk second, danger as a veto. During the probe-and-commit it is at half speed and cannot turn. Then the payoff rule: if the spotter takes damage or is knocked back (all weapons push, per combat canon) DURING the commit window, its rejected probes do not retract, and it FRAGMENTS — dies outright and drops a small chalk deposit at that junction. This turns junctions into designated kill spots with a bespoke death, and it makes universal knockback matter against a non-combat unit. Corollary for the player's engineering layer: since selection is width-first, narrowing an aperture (a building, a corpse pile the cleaning robot has not yet eaten, a dropped kit) demotes that doorway in every spotter's ranking — a player can steer spotter traffic by making one route physically wider than the others, without blocking anything.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: after the player narrows one route in a two-route junction, the fraction of spotters taking the other route should move sharply — measurable, and a clean A/B in the farm. PREDICTS: a measurable fragment-kill rate at junctions specifically, distinct from ordinary kills. FORBIDS: a spotter reversing direction inside a junction (it is committed). FORBIDS: any spotter needing to chew through or destroy geometry to travel — movement is aperture-limited, never destructive, which keeps 'a breach is an absence of material, it cannot be killed' consistent with how everything else moves. FALSIFIER: if route-share is unchanged after a width change, the width term is being drowned by chalk and the weights are wrong.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec has no route-selection model for the spotter beyond field gradients ('descending the chemotaxis gradients,' 'climbs the danger field to a peak'), which means at any junction where the fields tie, the choice is arbitrary and the player cannot influence it. Width-first selection makes the player's PHYSICAL construction — where he drops things, how wide he leaves a gap — an input into enemy routing, which is exactly the spec's stated ambition that 'a truly advanced player will intuit how the system communicates with itself and manipulate it to his advantage,' extended from the chemical layer to the geometric one. The fragmentation death is additive flavor with a real hook: it gives knockback a use against something you cannot out-damage.

### F7. Pressure-valve crossing — the breach mouth passes bodies only while the far side is more crowded than the near side, so the player's own occupation of the mouth clamps it shut
**What the source says.** The mechanics review describes the entry gates of the collection network as overlapping leaflets of boundary material forming 'microscopic one-way valves,' termed primary valves. The opening rule is a pure pressure comparison: the leaflets 'open when PL < Pi' (lumen pressure below outside pressure) and 'close when intralymphatic fluid pressure exceeds the interstitial fluid pressure.' Entry happens at the junction-free tip of the flap, so the gate opens 'without disrupting junctional integrity' — the seal is never broken, the flap just lifts. Anchoring filaments run out into the surroundings and mechanically pull the flaps open as the surroundings load up. Gate calibers are given: 5–15 µm collapsed versus 15–30 µm when filled in one preparation, 25–50 µm in another, 35–70 µm in human skin — the aperture is itself a function of load. Downstream, contractile segments 'propel lymph forward when outflow pressures are as high as 12 cm H2O when an inflow pressure of 1 cm H2O is imposed,' against a normal operating range of 1–5 cm H2O.
**Proposed game rule.** Give the breach mouth a pressure state instead of a timer-only state. Define near_load = (hostile bodies + projectiles crossing) on the level side within the mouth's radius over the last 2 s; far_load = agents queued on the hub side. The mouth's aperture width is a function of (far_load − near_load), clamped: strongly positive → wide open, agents pour; near zero → narrow, one body at a time; negative → clamped, nothing crosses in EITHER direction until it recovers. Render the leaflets — the mouth visibly dilates and irises down, so the player can read dispatch pressure from across the room. Consequences that fall straight out: standing in the mouth and shooting into it CLAMPS the breach and stalls the wave, which is a legitimate and heroic tactic that also traps the player at the choke point; and the aperture being narrow when pressure is low is precisely 'no log-jamming,' since a narrow mouth only admits a trickle it can actually pass. The spec's rule that the mouth closes 'when it is no longer useful to the agents the hub spawns' becomes literal: far_load = 0 for a sustained period IS the uselessness condition, no separate check needed.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: mouth-camping becomes a real strategy with a real cost, and run logs should show players who hold the mouth taking more damage per reinforcement killed than players who fight at range — if it is strictly better, the clamp is too strong. PREDICTS: no pile-ups at the mouth, because aperture and queue are coupled by construction rather than by a spawn-spacing hack. FORBIDS: the player casually walking back through a breach that is actively pouring — he must first suppress the far side, which makes the spec's 'bring a way back' lesson arrive as a physical rule instead of a timer roll. FORBIDS: any special-case anti-jam code around the entry, which the spec explicitly bans. FALSIFIER: if aperture never actually reaches the clamped state in a real run, the near_load term is too weak to matter and this is decoration.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec defines the breach as two-way and unconditional, closing on a 45 s uncrossed timer or on reserve exhaustion, and separately warns 'DO NOT add any special casing around entry; simply ensure the breach is wide enough.' A width that is a function of pressure satisfies that instruction better than a fixed width does — it is one rule, not a special case, and it makes the width self-tuning. It also converts three separate spec clauses (no jamming, closes when not useful, the player can cross back) into consequences of a single scalar comparison, which is a net reduction in mechanism count.

### F8. Dispatch by chance encounter — the hub FINDS calls by sampling, it never receives them, and the rate limit lives in the walk rather than in the hub
**What the source says.** The primary study measures how a stationary hub agent and mobile partners meet, and the answer is that nothing steers them together. The partners 'did not accumulate around DCs' and showed 'symmetrical' velocity distributions approaching and departing, which the authors call 'stochastic' rather than directed encounters — there is no gradient pulling anyone toward the coordination point. Despite being purely random, the throughput is enormous: one stationary hub agent achieves '4,630 ± 670 contacts per hour,' with a '3.4 min' median contact duration and roughly 250 contacts held concurrently, over a surface of 1,800–2,400 µm² with a mean contact patch of 8 µm². The kinematics are lopsided: the stationary agent's body moves at 2–3 µm·min⁻¹ (mean 2.7) while the mobile partners move at >10 µm·min⁻¹ and probe tips reach 40 µm·min⁻¹, with probes averaging 19 µm (SE 0.58) and occasionally exceeding 60 µm. The bottleneck is therefore never the meeting rate at the coordination point — 4,630/hour is one contact every 0.78 s — it is everything upstream of it.
**Proposed game rule.** Implement Transmit exactly as the decoupling law demands, using this as the model. The spotter does NOT message the hub and cannot read it. It deposits a CALL TOKEN in its polygon (position + payload + timestamp, decaying). Independently, on its own clock, the marrow runs a sampling loop: every tick it draws one polygon at random weighted by that polygon's Noise, and if a live token is found there with payload ≥ P_min AND the marrow's reserve permits AND fewer than N breaches are open, it opens a breach at the token's position. Expected latency from broadcast to breach is therefore exponentially distributed with a mean set by ONE global knob (the sampling rate), not by a per-spotter timer — which gives natural variance and means two simultaneous calls do not resolve simultaneously. And take the source's structural lesson literally: do NOT put the rate limit at the hub sampler. Make the sampler fast (mean latency a few seconds) and let the real bound come from upstream — how long the walk to a safe transmit spot takes, and how many spotters are alive. That is the difference between reinforcement pressure that responds to the player's actions and reinforcement pressure that is a fixed global drip.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: breach latency after a broadcast is variable, not constant — and if the player kills the spotter AFTER the broadcast but BEFORE the sample lands, the breach still opens, because the token outlives its carrier. That is a specific, checkable, and initially surprising outcome. PREDICTS: reinforcement rate tracks living-spotter count and travel distance, so a player who pushes the front forward feels pressure rise with distance from his cordon. FORBIDS: any spotter→hub reference, any hub state read by a spotter — the spec's IMPLEMENTATION CORRECTION #2, structurally enforced rather than remembered. FORBIDS: 'the hub is on cooldown' as the reason nothing came; the spec's own phrasing, 'if it calls and nothing is available, nothing comes,' becomes the literal implementation. FALSIFIER: if breach rate is flat across runs with wildly different spotter populations, the bound is sitting in the hub sampler after all and needs to be moved.
**Beyond the current spec.** The spec correctly demands decoupling and states 'Only one agent transmits at a time and only so many breaches open at once, so the reinforcement rate is bounded' — but a one-transmitter-at-a-time global lock is itself a coupling (something must arbitrate it) and is exactly the kind of global gate that makes pressure feel like a metronome. Token-plus-sampler gets the same bound with no arbiter and no shared state, and relocates the rate limit to the place the source says the real bottleneck lives: transit, not dispatch. The token outliving its carrier is additional design content the spec does not have — it means killing the spotter is worth doing EARLY, not just eventually.

### F9. Mode exclusivity — an agent that has committed to summoning stops sensing, and the commit has a wind-up that must be paid again after every interruption
**What the source says.** The encyclopedia article on the mobile scout class describes the switch as a discrete state change on contact with something worth reporting: agents 'become activated' and 'begin to migrate,' upregulating the homing sensor that makes the journey possible. The corresponding review confirms the switch is an upregulation event tied to that activation, and notably describes no mechanism at all for reversing it or for shutting the guidance signal off — there is no decoy or scavenging mechanism in that review's account (PMC3986807). The general article also gives a population turnover figure for the mobile class of replenishment 'at a rate of 4000 cells per hour' with a residence of '10 to 14 days' over which the agent undergoes only a limited number of divisions. The broader literature framing that the same article carries is that the switch DISSOCIATES the two jobs: acquisition happens before the switch, engagement after, and the acquisition machinery is wound down at the switch — an agent does not do both at once.
**Proposed game rule.** Make Infiltrate and Transmit mutually exclusive modes with an asymmetric cost. On entering Transmit the spotter (a) stops accruing payload — whatever it has is what it broadcasts, forever, (b) stops contributing to and reading the airborne fields, so it becomes chemically silent and blind, and (c) stops depositing chalk. Getting knocked out of Transmit does not return it to a saved state: it drops back to Infiltrate with its payload FROZEN at its committed value, must walk to a new spot that satisfies the safety predicate, and must pay the full ~2.5 s wind-up again. Combine with the payload cliff: an interrupted spotter can never improve its call, only re-attempt the same one, so an interrupted-and-fled spotter is a strictly diminishing threat while it remains alive. Corollary the exclusivity buys us: a transmitting spotter cannot steer away from the player mid-broadcast, which is what makes 'stationary and fully exposed' an actual property of the agent rather than an animation lock.
**Predicts / forbids.** PREDICTS: harassing a spotter without killing it is a viable and distinct play — it permanently caps that spotter's call quality. Verifiable: log payload-at-broadcast per spotter and confirm interrupted spotters show flat payload thereafter. PREDICTS: a transmitting spotter contributes nothing to the field state, so a broadcast in progress is chemically INVISIBLE to the player's other read-outs — the only way to know is to see it. FORBIDS: a spotter that fights and calls in the same instant. FORBIDS: instant re-call after an interruption. FORBIDS: a spotter fleeing mid-broadcast. FALSIFIER: if interrupted spotters go on to open breaches at the same average strength as uninterrupted ones, the payload freeze is not wired in.
**Beyond the current spec.** Additive to the spec's Infiltrate → Transmit → Pour machine in structure, but it adds two things the spec leaves open. First, the spec never says what happens to an interrupted spotter — as written, a spotter that survives an interruption presumably just tries again with no penalty, which makes interruption strictly worse than a kill and therefore never worth attempting unless you can finish it. Freezing payload gives partial success a real payoff, which matters because in practice the player often CAN'T finish the kill. Second, chemical silence during broadcast is a small rule with a large consequence: it means the spec's own claim that 'the calling happens out of sight' holds even against a player who has learned to read the signal layer, preserving the spec's intent that beating the hub is 'an organic reward for paying careful attention' rather than for watching a meter.

## Strongest candidates, by the critic's judgment

- ARREST is what makes a wave effective (Lane A cand 9) / Self-extinguishing pour (Lane B cand 5) — the same mechanism found independently by two lanes from the same verified primary. Highest leverage in the fleet because it fixes a RECORDED failure of the last build ('the enemies just don't have any chance to react', reinforcements never reaching the player): a gradient-climbing wave funnels into a doorway and gets farmed, an arresting wave forms a ring the player must break, which is literally the director's 'falling back must be fought for, not free'. It also supplies the garrison emergence rule the spec hand-waves ('a garrison is emergent, not an object') at zero extra cost, and the counterplay — over-saturate a field remotely to stall a wave in the wrong polygon — is exactly the 'manipulate how the system communicates with itself' payoff the constitution asks for.
- Depot-occupancy governor (Lane A cand 1) / Demand is sensed as depot emptiness (Lane E cand 2) — same paper, two routes, matching figure-level numbers. Replaces the spec's open-loop 'finite reserve that regenerates slowly' with a closed loop whose control variable is the CORE's own shelf, so the CORE never reads Distress, never reads the player, and satisfies IMPLEMENTATION CORRECTIONS #4 and #8 structurally rather than by discipline. It also kills the whole class of 'ramp because the player is doing well' difficulty hacks, and the rebound-overshoot term punishes half-draining a breach — a behaviour nobody would invent at a desk.
- Dispatch gate: occlude versus tear down (Lane C cand 5) — the cleanest fix to a specific spec defect. DESIGN_7_25 line 69 hands over two coupled free knobs and a target ('tune deep-negative depth x regen rate' to 'a couple of minutes'). This uncouples them: the outage is a REBUILD timer, the reserve refills independently underneath it, so the number tunes in one step and cannot be gamed by manipulating reserve. The reversible-occlusion half doubles as the crossing rate-limiter the spec asks for and explicitly forbids special-casing.
- The damper REDISTRIBUTES pressure (Lane A cand 7) — the missing cost term on the pheromone scrubber, which as specced (line 99, 'severely damps ALL chemo-signals in its area') is pure upside and makes stacking strictly dominant, directly undercutting the anti-turtle intent stated 20 lines earlier. Ships with a one-number pass/fail test on a real run: global dispatch-units/min must stay flat or RISE when scrubbers go up while local arrivals fall. If global drops, the build is a turtle button. Pair with Lane C cand 3's throughput cap and saturation failure mode for the implementable form; note the two lanes disagree on the verb (see unsourced_or_suspect_claims) and that has to be adjudicated first.
- The guardian ring IS the depot (Lane E cand 3) — resolves a genuine internal contradiction in the spec at zero content cost. Lines 66 and 71 together say the hub is 'the most heavily defended place in the world' AND 'reachable ONLY through a live breach' — i.e. the player can only arrive at the exact moment the ring should be deployed elsewhere. Unifying ring and depot turns that contradiction into the best strategic lever in the design: bait a mass call, then dive an emptied vault. Turns the hub raid into something you SET UP rather than something you survive, and deletes the need for a hand-authored guardian roster.
- The crossing gate runs both ways at roughly 1-in-4 (Lane A cand 8) — makes the spec's two-way breach a live enemy behaviour rather than a player-only affordance with a bespoke timer. Wounded agents routing home turns the universal knockback (combat canon, line 25) into a routing input; engagement pinning agents in place is both the sourced finding and good combat feel; and 'a room emptying out is ambiguous — did I kill them or did they go home' is precisely the uncertainty that makes cordoning feel like work. Delivers the appendix's 'evidence relay' goal with no messenger class. Caveat: reconcile its ~25% target against Lane B cand 7's ~15% before tuning.
- LACUNA — the sitter that samples the next polygon through a closed doorway (Lane F cand 3) — the only candidate in 60 that gives one of the two named placeholder classes a verb nothing else in the game has, and it makes the containment reward (line 80) a real puzzle instead of a solved one. # The open questions

Answer each with a recommendation you would ship, its mechanism, what it
predicts and forbids, and your odds it survives a real playtest.

1. **The CORE as a system of agents.** The spec says the CORE is "an organ, not
   a single static NPC" -- a system of agents, with regeneration, that is a real
   game area to fight through. What are its parts? What does the player attack,
   in what order, and what does each kill actually change? The research offers
   two attack surfaces with opposite time constants (producers deep inside
   versus the dispatch machinery at the hub's own mouth), an anchor lattice
   where destroying the structure displaces producers rather than killing them,
   and caretaker agents that rebuild it. Do you take that shape, and how does it
   avoid becoming a boss fight with extra steps?

2. **Reserve economy.** Size, regeneration, dispatch latency, and what happens
   when demand outruns supply. The research proposes a closed-loop governor
   driven by how empty the depot is (`regen = base * (1 + K*(1 - stock/cap))`)
   that never reads Distress, Noise, or kill counts -- which would satisfy our
   decoupling rule by construction. It also proposes that under sustained drain
   the marrow ships degraded "runt" agents, making the reserve state legible off
   the enemies' bodies instead of through a HUD. Do you endorse both? What are
   the numbers?

3. **Core-kill as a status effect.** The director has ruled that destroying the
   CORE takes the reinforcement layer offline for **a couple of minutes** and is
   a tactical tool the player applies before assaulting a fortified area -- not a
   run-ending event. Given your answer to (2), what values make a couple of
   minutes fall out of the economy rather than being hardcoded as a timer? What
   should the player observe during the blackout, and at its end?

4. **Dispatch and crossing.** Pour cadence, aperture, how the mouth's exact
   position is chosen, whether a used gate becomes self-amplifying, and what
   makes a gate stop being used. Hard constraints: the connection carries NO
   scaling factor, the aperture is simply wide enough that agents walk through on
   normal AI, and there is NO special-casing around entry. The research proposes
   the spotter transmits a *polygon* and the marrow resolves the seam by argmax
   over local agitation, drawn from a patch inventory precomputed at world build
   whose sites never grow in number, only in size. Does that hold up?

5. **The two scout classes, vagrant and lacuna.** They have only ever been
   placeholders. What differentiates them mechanically -- movement, sensing,
   where each is dangerous? The research suggests one that creeps along
   boundaries and can sense through a sealed portal without crossing it, versus
   one that moves in the open; and a transmit split into a cheap interruptible
   handshake and an expensive rooted broadcast. What is the exposure window
   during transmit, and how does the player learn to recognize it?

6. **Held ground and garrisons.** The director has ruled garrisons are emergent:
   agents settle around a leader (e.g. a Warden) who fortifies whatever room he
   is in, and leader AI is deferred -- for now leaders do not know they have
   followers. **One research lane argues the leader concept should be deleted
   entirely**, with any room converting whoever stands in it once its
   Distress-over-time integral crosses a threshold, and settled agents becoming
   weak fighters but very loud emitters (so the player who reads threat ratings
   ignores exactly the unit he should kill first). Adjudicate: leaderless
   conversion, the director's leader-anchored version, or both composed?

7. **Signal damping and cloaking.** The player's counterplay is a pheromone
   scrubber that severely damps all chemo-signals, and a pod-linked cleaning
   robot that clears splatter, piles corpses for the pod's roots, and emits a
   couple of components as salvage. Doctrine: an uncloaked base eventually gets
   found and purged, and that is intended. How should damping work numerically
   so cloaking is a real strategic choice rather than a mandatory tax? Does a
   scrubbed region read as suspiciously quiet to the enemy -- a hole in the
   signal field that is itself a signal?

8. **Where the reinforcement stream becomes the fight.** The spec's intent is
   that for most of a run, standing and fighting reinforcements is a better use
   of ammo and time than hunting the source, and that a competent player manages
   how much aggro he draws. What makes the stream fun to fight rather than a
   chore to grind? What is the pacing shape of a single breach's pour, and how
   should it feel different from a fight the player started himself?

9. **Anything we have not asked.** Name the load-bearing assumption in this
   design that you think is most likely wrong.
